## The Discovery (1947)

- Muhammad edh-Dhib, young Bedouin of the Taʿamireh tribe, searching for stray goat
- Threw a rock into a cave, heard pottery breaking
- Returned days later, found tall clay jars with lids still sealed
- Inside: scrolls wrapped in linen cloth — leather and papyrus
- Seven initial scrolls from Cave 1
- Sold through antiquities dealers to Archbishop Samuel (Syrian Orthodox) and Professor Sukenik (Hebrew University)
- Recognition by John Trever at ASOR — compared script to Nash Papyrus
- Public announcement: April 26, 1948 (one day before Israeli independence)
- Subsequent searches 1947-1956: 981 manuscripts across 11 caves

**Note:** Like the Nag Hammadi story, the DSS discovery narrative has variants. John C. Trever reconstructed from interviews. The exact circumstances are disputed — how many shepherds, who entered first, exact date. But the core is established: Bedouin found scrolls in caves near Qumran, late 1946 or early 1947.

## The 40-Year Suppression

THIS IS A MAJOR FINDING for the thesis. The discovery is only half the story. What happened AFTER discovery is itself a pattern of institutional gatekeeping.

### The Cartel
- Jordan granted exclusive publication rights to a small team of scholars
- After 1967 war, Israel captured the scrolls but maintained the same restricted access
- Only ~40 scholars worldwide had access
- After 40 years, only 100 of ~500 scrolls had been published
- Hershel Shanks (BAR editor) called it "a small cartel of monopolists"

### Specific Example: MMT
- John Strugnell assigned the critical text MMT (letter from sect leader)
- Concealed its existence until 1984 — 37 years after discovery
- Wrote 500+ page commentary on 121 lines but refused to release the text
- Gave copies to friends who taught classes and wrote articles about it
- The text remained unpublished while insiders profited from exclusive access

### The Breakthrough (1991)
- Huntington Library in San Marino, CA held ~3,000 photographic negatives of virtually ALL scrolls
- Photos taken as preservation measure in case of war
- September 22, 1991: Director William Moffett announced unrestricted access
- Called it "the scholarly equivalent of breaching the Berlin Wall"
- "At one stroke, we liberate the scholars as well as the Scrolls"
- Israel Antiquities Authority threatened legal action, then backed down
- Research team expanded from 12 to 50 members

### Timeline of Suppression
- 1947: Discovery
- 1947-1956: More caves found, 981 manuscripts recovered
- 1950s-1960s: Small team assigned exclusive rights
- 1967: Israel captures scrolls, maintains restricted access
- 1984: Strugnell finally reveals existence of MMT (37 years hidden)
- 1989: Scholars publicly denounce delay as "disgraceful"
- 1991: Huntington Library breaks the monopoly
- 1990s-2000s: Full corpus gradually published
- 2011: Israel Antiquities Authority puts scrolls online via Google partnership

## Significance for the Research

The pattern is stunning: **discovery is not enough. The texts must also be FREED.**

1. The scrolls were found in 1947 but kept from most scholars for 40+ years
2. An institutional cartel controlled access — functioning exactly like the dragon pursuing the woman's seed
3. Liberation came through an unexpected channel (a California library's vault of photos)
4. The "earth helping the woman" — the photographs existed as a preservation measure and became the tool of liberation

This parallels the Nag Hammadi pattern:
- NHL: buried ~367 CE, found 1945, Jung Codex not fully published until 1975, Robinson's complete English edition 1977 (32 years after discovery)
- DSS: found 1947, cartel maintained 40+ years, Huntington broke monopoly 1991 (44 years after discovery)

**The texts survived burial. Then they had to survive the institutions that found them.**

The thesis should trace this double pattern: providence operates not only in the hiding and discovery but in the liberation from scholarly gatekeeping. The dragon pursues at every stage — underground, in the academy, in the publication process.
