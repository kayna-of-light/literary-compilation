# The Mirror and the Star: A Comparative Pneumatology of Disney's *Wish* and *Snow White* (2025)

## I. Introduction: The Schism of Spiritual Physics in the Centennial Era

The year 2023 marked the centennial of the Walt Disney Animation Studios, a cultural institution that has arguably served as the primary myth-maker for the Western world in the 20th and 21st centuries. Throughout its history, the studio's output has operated on a fundamental, almost metaphysical premise: that the internal world of emotion, intent, and character can be—and indeed must be—externalized into the visible world of form, movement, and environment. This premise, whether utilized intuitively by animators or consciously by storytellers, established a structural isomorphism with the theological system of Emanuel Swedenborg (1688–1772), specifically his **Doctrine of Correspondences**, which posits that natural forms are the "ultimate" effects of spiritual causes.

For decades, this "language of nature" remained consistent. A storm in *The Little Mermaid* was not merely a meteorological event but the externalized fury of a sea witch; the wilting rose in *Beauty and the Beast* was not a botanical anomaly but a chronometer of the soul's potential for regeneration. The animation medium itself, by its very nature, demanded a "physics of the soul" where gravity, light, and color obeyed the emotional and moral logic of the scene rather than the laws of Newtonian mechanics. This artistic necessity aligned the medium perfectly with Swedenborg's vision of a plastic, responsive spiritual world where affections manifest visibly as animals and landscapes shift instantaneously to reflect the interior state of the inhabitants.

However, the release of the animated feature *Wish* (2023) and the live-action adaptation of *Snow White* (2025) reveals a profound schism in this metaphysical tradition. While *Wish*, intended as a tribute to the studio's legacy, has been critiqued for performing a "theological inversion" or "profanation" of these principles (*The Inverted Influx*), analysis of the live-action *Snow White* indicates a surprising fidelity to the structural dynamics of Swedenborgian regeneration. This divergence presents a paradox: the film explicitly designed to celebrate the "magic" of the studio (*Wish*) appears to have hollowed out that magic by severing its vertical connection to the Divine, while the film most criticized for its "modern sensibilities" and departure from tradition (*Snow White*) has inadvertently reinforced the deep theological structures of the original fairy tale.

This analysis undertakes an exhaustive investigation into this divergence. It posits that *Wish* represents a shift from "Universal Correspondence" to "Cultural Metaphor" (*The Diffracted Light*) and Gnostic self-deification, effectively severing the link to the "Spiritual Sun." Conversely, the live-action *Snow White*—through its specific reinvention of characters like **Jonathan**, its handling of the **Magic Mirror**, its "Apple Orchard" backstory, and its musical score—inadvertently reconstructs a rigorous Swedenborgian drama regarding the destruction of the **the self** (selfhood) and the restoration of the "State of the Church." By applying the forensic tools of Swedenborgian exegesis—specifically the doctrines of **Influx**, **Vastation**, and **Use**—this analysis will demonstrate why the "modernized" live-action film may be spiritually more "traditional" than the studio's animated centennial celebration.

## II. Theoretical Framework: The Swedenborgian Substrate

To understand the magnitude of the divergence between *Wish* and *Snow White* (2025), one must first establish the "active substrate" of Swedenborgian thought that historically underpinned the Disney universe (*The Invisible Influx*; *The Animating Spirit: An Exhaustive Analysis*). This was not merely a stylistic choice but an operational philosophy that governed the "physics" of the animated world.

### 2.1 The Doctrine of Influx and the Illusion of Life

The core of Swedenborgian cosmology is the **Doctrine of Influx**. Reality is stratified into discrete degrees: the Celestial (Love), the Spiritual (Wisdom), and the Natural (Action). A fundamental tenet of this system is that life does not originate in the natural world; it flows into it from the Lord, who is the "Spiritual Sun." Forms in the natural world—whether human bodies, animals, or trees—are merely "receptacles" of this life. They do not generate life; they receive it.

In the "Golden Age" of Disney animation (e.g., *Pinocchio*, *Snow White* 1937) and the "Renaissance" (e.g., *The Lion King*, *Beauty and the Beast*), this doctrine was secularized as the artistic principle of "Inner Force driving Outer Form." Animators were taught that a character's movement (natural effect) must be driven by an internal intent or emotion (spiritual cause). If a character moved without this internal motivation, it was dismissed as "rubber hose" animation—mechanical, lifeless, and devoid of soul. This pedagogical mandate created a universe where the physical world was transparent to the spiritual.

### 2.2 Selfhood and the Problem of Self

The central antagonist in Swedenborgian theology is the **self** — the vivid sensation that we live, think, and act from ourselves. When the self confirms itself as the source of life, it becomes the "Love of Self," which is the root of all evil and the essence of Hell.

the self operates as a closed loop. It constantly seeks confirmation of its own existence, drawing all things toward itself as center. It cannot receive influx from the Divine because it has positioned itself *as* the source. This creates a spiritual "black hole"—a vacuum that appears to have substance but is, in reality, empty of all life and truth.

### 2.3 The True Wish vs. The False Wish

At the heart of this investigation lies a critical distinction: **The True Wish versus The False Wish**.

| Dimension | The True Wish | The False Wish |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Source** | Received (from the Divine, through prayer) | Self-Generated (from the self) |
| **Direction** | Vertical (up/down between degrees) | Horizontal (within the natural plane) |
| **Object** | The Good of Others / Conjunction with the Divine | Self-aggrandizement / Control |
| **Mechanism** | Influx (life flows *through* the vessel) | Closure (life is claimed *by* the vessel) |
| **Outcome** | Regeneration / Heaven | Vastation / Hell |
| **Disney Expression** | "I'm Wishing" (prayer) / "Waiting on a Wish" | "I'm a Star" (Stardust) / Wish Orbs |

A "true wish," in this framework, is fundamentally a prayer—a desire directed toward the Divine, seeking alignment with the Divine Will. It opens the soul to receive influx from above. A "false wish," by contrast, is a desire originating in the self, seeking to confirm the self as the source of good. It closes the soul into a loop of self-reference.

### 2.4 The "Fisher King" and the Moral Mirror

This substrate manifested most clearly in the "Fisher King" archetype, which correlates with Swedenborg's correspondence of the **Land** (Earth) with the **State of the Church**. In Swedenborg's exegesis, the physical state of the land in biblical prophecy—whether it is blooming, drought-stricken, or frozen—is never a description of agriculture but a precise diagnosis of the spiritual state of the people.

Disney films replicate this diagnostic topography with high fidelity. In *The Lion King*, the reign of Mufasa (Divine Truth/Law) maintains the "Circle of Life" (Divine Order), resulting in a lush kingdom. When Scar (Self-Love/Falsity) usurps the throne, the transformation of the landscape is immediate and total. The sun is obscured, the water (Truth) dries up, and the land becomes a skeletal wasteland. This is a perfect depiction of "Vastation"—the spiritual desolation that occurs when the connection to the Divine is severed by the rule of the self.

## III. The Anatomy of the False Wish: *Wish* as Gnostic Inversion

The film *Wish* (2023) serves as the primary case study for the abandonment or inversion of this system. Intended as a centennial celebration of the studio's history of "wishing upon a star," the theological mechanics it establishes represent a radical departure from the studio's historical metaphysics, effectively "profaning" the very tradition it seeks to honor.

### 3.1 The Star as "Stardust": Immanence vs. Transcendence

In the classic Disney canon, exemplified by *Pinocchio* (1940), the "wish" is a prayer directed upward to a transcendent source (the Blue Star/Blue Fairy). The puppet Pinocchio is "empty" and must earn his soul through moral regeneration ("brave, truthful, and unselfish"). This aligns with Swedenborg's view that the human Selfhood (self) is lifeless until animated by Divine Influx. The source of life is external and superior to the vessel.

In *Wish*, this cosmology is inverted. The song "I'm a Star" explicitly teaches that "we are our own origin story" and that the divine spark is inherent in the material constitution of the characters ("we're all just little nebulae in a nursery"; Michaels and Rice 2023). This is a **Gnostic** or **Pantheistic** framework where the divine is trapped in matter, rather than a **Theistic** framework where life flows *into* matter from a higher degree.

The implication of this shift is profound. It collapses the "Vertical Causality" of Swedenborg into a "Horizontal" plane. There is no need for regeneration, repentance, or influx from the Lord because the self is already divine. Swedenborg holds that nature has no life of its own—"nature also is dead, because it derives its origin from that sun" (*Divine Love and Wisdom* §§157–158)—and that to claim the Divine as one's own is the falsity of **the self** (Self-Love): "man's proprium is nothing but evil; wherefore he who attributes to himself what is Divine as his own not only defiles it but also profanes it" (*Apocalypse Revealed* §758). By celebrating the self as the source of magic, *Wish* inadvertently celebrates the very force (Selfhood) that acts as the antagonist in Swedenborg's theology.

### 3.2 The Wish Orb: The Externalization of the Ruling Love

The film introduces the concept of the "Wish Orb," a physical sphere containing a person's greatest desire. King Magnifico extracts these orbs and keeps them in his castle for safekeeping ("he keeps them safe," *Wish* 2023), causing the citizens to forget their wishes and live in a state of docile contentment.

In Swedenborgian psychology, every being is defined by a "Ruling Love" (Dominant Love). This is the gravitational center of the soul. All thoughts, actions, and memories organize themselves around this core desire. If the ruling love is removed, the person effectively ceases to exist or becomes a "spiritual zombie" until it is restored.

By treating the Ruling Love as an external object that can be given away, stored, or shattered, *Wish* commodifies the spiritual essence. In Swedenborg's system, the Ruling Love *is* the life of the person; it cannot be extracted without death. The film's mechanic suggests that the soul is a possession one *has*, rather than what one *is*.

Furthermore, King Magnifico stands in the place of a false god who demands worship ("Now, bow down to your king!", *Wish* 2023) in exchange for the "protection" of these wishes. This mirrors Swedenborg's description of **Babylon**—the profanation of good and truth for the sake of dominion: "the love of dominion over the holy things of the church from self-love" (*Apocalypse Revealed* §717). However, the film's solution to this tyranny is not to restore the wishes to a higher order (Divine Will) but to return them to the "self," reinforcing the closed loop of the self.

### 3.3 The "Hollow" State and the Closed Loop

Critics and analysts have frequently noted that *Wish* feels "hollow" or lacks the emotional weight of earlier films: "less a story and more a weird pastiche, with some pretty animation, but very little heart" (Nicolosi 2023). From a Swedenborgian perspective, this is the inevitable result of the **"Closed Loop of Self-Reference"**.

The mechanism of "Universal Correspondence" relies on the influx of the Infinite (the Divine) into the Finite (the Natural). This creates a sense of depth, mystery, and life. When the "vertical" connection to the Divine is severed in favor of the "self" (which is finite and dead in itself), the artistic product loses its "soul." It mimics the *forms* of correspondence—talking animals, magic sparks, musical numbers—but lacks the *influx* that gives them life. The Star in *Wish* is not a messenger of Divine Providence; it is a "mascot" or a reflection of Asha's own ego.

This reliance on "Cultural Metaphor" (horizontal references to Disney history) rather than "Universal Correspondence" creates a simulacrum of magic. The film references *Peter Pan*, *Bambi*, and *Snow White*, but these are Easter eggs (surface data) rather than spiritual resonances ("Disney Easter eggs aplenty"; Chiswell 2024). The result is a "Kingdom of Self," where the universe exists only to validate the ego's desires, a state Swedenborg identifies with the "Love of the World" separated from the "Love of Heaven."

## IV. The Apple Orchard and Jonathan: Production Theology in *Snow White* (2025)

In stark contrast to the "self-deification" of *Wish*, the 2025 live-action *Snow White* appears to reconstruct the rigorous moral and spiritual architecture of Swedenborg's universe. Despite modernizing elements—such as the removal of the Prince Charming archetype and the introduction of a new character named Jonathan—the film's plot mechanics, specifically regarding the **Mirror**, the **Queen**, the **Apple**, and the **Land**, align with the "Science of Correspondences" with high fidelity.

### 4.1 The "Johnny Appleseed" Backstory and the Doctrine of Use

In the 2025 film, Snow White is given a specific backstory: as a child, with her parents, the good king and queen, she would pick apples, "take them and make pies and go out into the village" (*Snow White* 2025). This "origin story" for Snow White—linking her foundational memories to an apple orchard—is striking in light of the "Johnny Appleseed" segment of *Melody Time* (1948), one of the most explicitly Swedenborgian texts in the Disney canon.

As detailed in companion documents (*The Apple and the Angel*), the "Johnny Appleseed" segment establishes:

1. **The Source of Blessing:** Johnny's "Blessings Three" are "LOVE, FAITH and the APPLE TREE" (*Johnny Appleseed* 1949)—not personal ambition but the gifts of Divine Providence.
2. **The Method of Use:** Johnny's purpose is to plant apple trees to "give" to others. He is a vessel of Use.
3. **The Definition of Luxury:** When Johnny protests that he has "none of the things I'll need out there," the angel answers that his seeds, his book and a pot for a hat are enough: "You got the stuff to see you through" (*Johnny Appleseed* 1948). Service, not comfort, aligning with the Swedenborgian rejection of the "Love of the World."

Snow White's backstory in the 2025 film mirrors this structure:

1. **The Source of Blessing:** The "good old days" of the orchard are associated with her father—an absent Divine source.
2. **The Method of Use:** The method of this alignment is *work*. "We'd pick apples, and we'd take them and make pies and go out into the village" (*Snow White* 2025). This is active charity. She is not sitting in the castle wishing; she is in the orchard working.
3. **The Definition of Luxury:** The Queen answers: "Pies are luxuries. They don't need luxuries. It confuses them" (*Snow White* 2025). This reveals the Queen's spiritual state. To the "natural man" (the Queen), spiritual goods (charity/pies) are useless luxuries. To the "spiritual man" (Snow White), they are essential sustenance.

### 4.2 The Theological Correction of the Apple

By giving Snow White this specific backstory, the filmmakers have effectively realigned her "ruling love" with the Doctrine of Use. In Swedenborgian correspondence, the "Apple" (and fruit trees generally) signifies the "Good of Life" or "Works of Charity" derived from Truth: "the good works done by men are compared to fruits" (*Heaven and Hell* §111), and in heaven the "trees bear fruit that are in accordance with the good of love" (*Heaven and Hell* §176).

* **The Queen's Apples:** The Evil Queen uses the apple as a vessel for death (poison). This represents the **Profanation of Use**—using the appearance of a good thing (a beautiful red apple) to destroy the soul. It is "faith separate from charity," or knowledge used for evil ends.
* **Snow White's Apples:** Snow White uses the apple as a vessel for nourishment (pie/charity). This represents the **True Use** of goods.

By linking Snow White to the apple *before* the poisoning (a change from the 1937 original), the film reclaims the symbol of the apple from the Fall (Eve) and realigns it with Redemption (Johnny Appleseed/Charity). This is a theological correction that posits "Good Works" (baking pies) as the foundation of the character's strength. Snow White is not "fairest" merely because she is beautiful; she is "fairest" because she is *useful*. She feeds the village. She manifests her internal love in external works.

### 4.3 Jonathan: The "God-Given" Truth of Doctrine

One of the most controversial decisions in the 2025 production was the replacement of the traditional "Prince" with a new character named "Jonathan," described as a bandit or a Robin Hood-like figure. Critics initially viewed this as a concession to modern feminism or a rejection of the romance trope. However, a structural analysis reveals a profound theological logic behind this specific nomenclature and characterization.

#### 4.3.1 The Etymology and Archetype

The name "Jonathan" is Hebrew (*Yehonatan*), meaning "Yahweh has given" or "God-given." In Swedenborgian exegesis, names in sacred texts (and by extension, in correspondential narratives) are never arbitrary; they signify the "quality" or "essence" of the character. The choice of "Jonathan"—a specifically biblical name—over a generic European royal name (like Florian or Henri) signals a shift in the character's function.

In the Bible, Jonathan is the son of King Saul (the corrupt, external ruler) but the sworn friend and ally of David (the true, spiritual king). The New Church reading of the story analyzes the biblical Jonathan as representing the truth of doctrine: he "represents those parts of the letter of the Lord's Word which are in full agreement with the spirit. From such passages doctrine must be drawn" (Worcester n.d.). He acts as a medium or a bridge. In the 2025 film, the kingdom is ruled by the Evil Queen (a Saul figure/Usurper). Snow White represents the "Church" or the "Soul" in exile. Jonathan, introduced as a "bandit" operating outside the Queen's law, represents "Truth" that has been alienated from the ruling structure but remains loyal to the true order.

#### 4.3.2 The "Thief" Correspondence and the Spoliation of the Egyptians

Jonathan is a thief who steals from the Queen's stores to feed the hungry: "My friends are hungry. I'm hungry. That's my excuse" (*Snow White* 2025). In Swedenborgian symbolism, "thieves" generally have a negative correspondence: "those who claim for themselves his merit and righteousness" (*True Christian Religion* §319). However, in the context of "saving the remains" (the poor/the good) from a "profane dominion" (the Queen), this action corresponds to the "Spoliation of the Egyptians"—the reclaiming of truths and goods that have been captured by evil, which "were to be taken away from them, and to be transferred to those who are of the spiritual church" (*Arcana Coelestia* §6914).

Jonathan enters the "closed" system of the Queen's tyranny to introduce a new element of hope, functioning as a disruptive force of truth in a kingdom of falsity. This characterization fundamentally alters the dynamic of salvation. Jonathan's role is to facilitate Snow White's agency, not to save her passively. This aligns with the Swedenborgian concept of "Cooperation." The soul (Snow White/Will) cannot be saved by "Faith Alone" (passive belief in a Prince). It must cooperate with "Truth" (Jonathan/Understanding) to fight against the self.

### 4.4 The Orchard: Design and Echo

The "Johnny Appleseed" connection is carried by the film's words, not by its design. Disney's production notes describe "the apple orchard" as a set built on Pinewood's Stage 2 and name the 1937 film and German and Swiss castles as the design sources; they say nothing of *Melody Time* or Mary Blair (Walt Disney Studios 2025). The words, though, echo the 1948 film: the opening song plants "a tender seed / Trusting something good will grow," "a beautiful abundance we share," under "a golden sky," and the finale sings "For we reap what we sow" (Pasek and Paul 2025), where Johnny had "seeds to sow" and a heavenly orchard with "apples there for everyone in the world to share" (*Johnny Appleseed* 1948). The image of an apple orchard lying "in snowy drifts and mounds" under blossom belongs to Eleanor Atkinson's Swedenborgian novel *Johnny Appleseed* (1915), whose orchard joins sleep, death and heaven (*The Apple and the Angel* §6.2). Reviewers found the orchard stylized—"faker than any video game cutscene" (Winkler 2025)—in a film whose palette "jumps from drab to garish depending on the scene" (Stradling 2025).

Mary Blair's style, characterized by "flatness" and "color as emotion," is a visual application of Swedenborgian aesthetics—seeing the spiritual in the natural, rather than the natural as a separate solid. The 2025 orchard does not cite it, but it serves the same function: the orchard is not just a location; it is a "sacred space" of memory and innocence, distinct from the "Vastated" landscape of the Queen's castle.

The film's use of color adheres to the "Correspondence of Colors":

* **Snow White:** Primary colors (Blue/Red/Yellow). Blue corresponds to Truth, Red to Love/Charity, Yellow/Gold to Goodness. She is the integrated soul.
* **The Queen:** Purple, Black, and Cold tones. Purple corresponds to the "Love of Dominion" (royal power abused). Black corresponds to falsity and the void.
* **The Orchard:** Described in reviews as having a specific, almost "fake" or "video game cutscene" quality (Winkler 2025). While intended as a criticism, this "unreality" aligns with Blair's rejection of realism in favor of "emotional truth." The orchard is a spiritual memory, not a geographical location.

## V. "Waiting on a Wish": Lyrical Exegesis and the Rejection of Gnostic Self-Deification

The most direct evidence for the theological coherence of *Snow White* (2025) is found in its central musical number, "Waiting on a Wish," written by Benj Pasek and Justin Paul (Pasek and Paul 2025). Where *Wish* offered the anthem "I'm a Star" as its statement of origin, *Snow White* offers a counter-thesis that operates as a deliberate rejection of the Gnostic model and an affirmation of the Vertical.

### 5.1 Verse-by-Verse Analysis

#### Verse 1: The Prayer Posture

> *Little girl at a lonely well / With the same little tale to tell / … / So she's dreaming all alone / Sharing secrets with the stone*
>
> *I'm waiting on a wish / Beneath a thousand treetops / And as the silver sky stops / I send a whisper to the water*

This opening establishes the **prayer posture**—the soul directing its desire outward, to the wishing well of the 1937 film, and waiting for an answer (Pasek and Paul 2025). The mechanism is clear: blessing does not originate in the self but is received. She does not declare what she is; she sends "a whisper to the water" and waits. This is the precise inversion of *Wish*'s "I'm a Star."

#### Verse 2: The Father's Daughter and the Miracle

> *Holding out for someday / Hoping somehow some way / I'll become my father's daughter*
>
> *Little girl by a wild wood / How she tries to be someone good / … / So will she rise or bow her head / Will she lead or just be led*
>
> *Holding out for someday / Hoping somehow some way / There comes a miracle to find me*

Snow White's wish is not for personal power or recognition. It is:
1. **"I'll become my father's daughter"** — **Conjunction with the Divine Good** (The Father). This is the alignment of the Will with the Divine Will. She is not seeking autonomy; she is seeking alignment. She wants to be *formed by* her origin, not to *replace* it.
2. **"To be someone good"** and to "lead," not "just be led" — the leadership the film will give her is a form of goodness, not of power.
3. **"There comes a miracle to find me"** — the decisive turn. What she waits for comes *to* her from outside; it is not generated from within.

The posture is *receptive* (the whisper, the waiting) but also *active* ("tries to be someone good"). This is Swedenborg's "as-if-from-self" principle: the regenerating soul must *act* as if from itself, while acknowledging that the power to act comes from the Lord.

#### "Good Things Grow": The Desire for Use

> *And we give to this land / With a generous hand / And the bounty will flow / Where the good things grow*

The film's opening song, sung under the good king and queen, gives the kingdom its law: "a beautiful abundance we share," a land where "we plant a tender seed / Trusting something good will grow" (Pasek and Paul 2025). This is the Doctrine of Use operating at the level of a whole people. Against the **the self's** scarcity mindset, which hoards because it believes itself to be the source and the center, the good kingdom gives "with a generous hand." The "more" it seeks is more to give, not more to receive. The influx of Divine Love creates a spontaneous desire to *be useful to others*, not to accumulate for the self—the state of **heavenly peace** that Swedenborg contrasts with the **worldly anxiety** of those ruled by self-love. When the Queen calls Snow White's pies "luxuries," she is refusing exactly this law.

#### The Bridge: The Explicit Echo Rejection

> *They say, "All you have to do / To make your wish come true / Is let it echo, echo, echo, echo / Your voice will carry through / And bring that dream to you / Just like an echo, echo, echo"*  
> *Well, I can hear the echo loud and clear / But I'm still waiting here*

This is the most theologically loaded passage of the song. The echo is the one that answered Snow White's "I'm Wishing" at the well in 1937; the "they" are the voices of that convention, and of the conventional Disney wisdom: "When you wish upon a star... makes no difference who you are... your dreams come true." In *Wish*, this is literalized: Asha's wishes are amplified by the Star mascot and the "echo" of the community singing with her. The power of the wish is directly proportional to its volume—a horizontal, democratic amplification.

Snow White **explicitly rejects this mechanism**. She can "hear the echo loud and clear"—she is aware of the cultural script that tells her wishing will be enough—"But I'm still waiting here." The echo is *not working*. This is a direct admission that the horizontal mechanism of self-amplification (the core theology of *Wish*) is insufficient. The wish requires something else: a response from *above*, not from within.

This is not passive despair; it is **theologically correct patience**. She is waiting for influx from the Vertical, which cannot be commanded by the loudness of the Horizontal echo. This posture is faith, not "faith alone" (passive) but "faith that waits" (receptive).

#### Final Verse: The Humble Self vs. The Stellar Self

> *Learn to be my father's daughter / Someone who could finally start / Start speaking with a fearless heart / Someone who just might be brave / Someone no one needs to save*

The final verse establishes Snow White's goal: to become "someone no one needs to save." This is the opposite of the 1937 model (passive faith requiring a Prince) but *also* the opposite of the *Wish* model (self-deification requiring no external source).

Snow White does not say "I am already brave" or "I have the power within me." She says she might be "someone who could **finally start**," someone who "**just might** be brave." This is the language of regeneration—a process of becoming, not a pre-existing state. "A fearless heart" is the activation of the Will (Heart) by Divine Truth; courage is not self-generated but the fruit of alignment with the Vertical. Swedenborg describes regeneration as a gradual transformation where the Lord remakes the person's interiors over time. It is not instant, and it is not self-generated.

### 5.2 The Structural Mechanics of Restoration

In addition to the lyrical content, the filmmakers introduced several narrative elements that reinforce the Swedenborgian structure:

#### The "Father's Daughter" Motif

Snow White explicitly seeks to be her "father's daughter," to return to the orchard, and to restore the kingdom to its former state. This longing for a lost "Golden Age" aligned with a benevolent Ruler/Father corresponds directly to the Swedenborgian concept of the **"Most Ancient Church"** and the "Heavenly State" before the Fall. The Father is not present in the film's action but is the absent source of all that is good. This is theologically correct: the Divine (Father) does not appear *as* a character competing with other characters; the Divine is the *source* from which all goods flow and the *standard* by which all evils are measured.

#### The Environment as Spiritual Barometer (Vastation and Restoration)

The live-action film retains the Swedenborgian "Fisher King" dynamic with striking clarity. Under the Evil Queen, the farmers are conscripted into soldiers: "She turned the farmers into soldiers, loyal only to her, and robbed the riches of the kingdom for herself" (*Snow White* 2025). The design marks the same change: the castle gate "is always open when it's the Good Kingdom … when the Evil Queen takes over, the gates come down" (Walt Disney Studios 2025). This is **spiritual vastation**—the draining of "Charity" (color/life) and "Peace" (farmers) by the "Love of Dominion" (soldiers/tyranny).

The restoration is instantaneous. When the Mirror is broken and the Queen consumed, the kingdom gathers to celebrate in a reprise of the opening song (Zachary 2025a): "And the strongest of roots / Bear the sweetest of fruits / For we reap what we sow" (Pasek and Paul 2025). This immediate restoration of the environment upon the removal of the ruling evil is a textbook example of Swedenborgian **Influx** returning to its proper order. The block (Selfhood) is removed, and Divine Life flows in again, healing the land.

## VI. The Mirror and the Queen's Destruction: Judgment and the Void

The central visual and narrative innovation of the 2025 film is its treatment of the Magic Mirror. Where the 1937 version presented the Mirror as a passive revealer of truth, the 2025 version elevates it to a site of judgment and, ultimately, consumption.

### 6.1 The Mirror as the Self and the Void

In the 2025 film, the Evil Queen's relationship with the Magic Mirror is central to the spiritual dynamics. The mirror represents the **Self-Reflection of The self**. The Queen gazes into it not to seek truth, but to confirm her own superiority ("fairest of all"). It is a closed loop of vanity.

The climax of the film involves the Queen destroying the Mirror. This act is pivotal. Shattering the mirror creates a "rift into a netherspace that appears dark and empty" (Zachary 2025b). She turns to ash and is sucked into this void (Shaw 2025).

This imagery is strikingly Swedenborgian. Swedenborg describes Hell not as a place of external punishment (fire and pitchforks), but as a state where the internal is a "void" or "abyss" because it is emptied of truths and goods. The "netherspace" behind the mirror corresponds to the **internal state of The self**. It is a black hole of self-absorption. The fact that the Queen is consumed by the mirror is a perfect visual representation of Swedenborg's teaching that "every evil carries its punishment with it, the two making one" (*Heaven and Hell* §509). the self consumes itself. She is not killed by Snow White (which would be external combat); she is consumed by the medium of her own vanity (internal vastation).

Furthermore, Swedenborg reads the potter's vessels that "shall be broken in pieces" as "the things which are of one's own intelligence, which are all falsities, and in themselves of no account" (*Apocalypse Revealed* §149). By shattering the mirror, the Queen destroys the container of her own self-image, revealing the "abyss" of her non-existence beneath.

### 6.2 The "Void" and the "Broken Mirror"

The imagery of the broken mirror in the 2025 film is particularly significant. When the Queen smashes the mirror, she is attempting to destroy the *Truth* (that Snow White is fairest). In Swedenborgian terms, to destroy Truth is to commit **Profanation**.

The result is not that the mirror ceases to function, but that it opens a **"Void."** This corresponds to the **"Hell of the Profaners"**—those who mix truth and evil. They are not merely "punished" by an external agent; they are "swallowed up" by their own interior state. The 2025 film's visual of the Queen turning to ash (total lack of spiritual life) and being pulled into the dark dimension is a profound representation of **Second Death**. It is a far more severe and spiritually accurate consequence than the cliff fall in the 1937 film, which could be interpreted as accidental or natural. The 2025 death is explicitly spiritual and internal.

### 6.3 The Mirror as *Speculum Vivum*

In contrast to the Queen's relationship with the Mirror (narcissistic self-confirmation), the Mirror's function in relation to Snow White is one of objective truth. The Mirror speaks the truth ("Snow White is fairest") regardless of the Queen's feelings. It represents a **Theistic** theology: Truth is objective, external, and often painful. It creates tension. It demands that the "Old Man" (Queen) be destroyed so that the "New Man" (Snow White) can rule.

The live-action film, by centering the Mirror and the Queen's struggle with it, retains the dramatic tension of **Judgment**. It acknowledges that there is an objective moral order that cannot be bribed or shattered without consequence. *Wish* tries to democratize magic ("everyone gets a wish"), effectively removing the hierarchy of spiritual degrees. *Snow White* maintains the hierarchy: The Queen falls because she violates the order; Snow White rises because she aligns with it (Father's Daughter/Jonathan's Partner).

## VII. Comparative Synthesis: The Mirror vs. The Star

The divergence between *Wish* and *Snow White* ultimately comes down to the symbols they choose to center: **The Star** and **The Mirror**.

### 7.1 The Star: The Failure of Humanism

In *Wish*, the Star is a cute, silent character—a "mascot" of the self. It validates Asha's desires without challenging them. It represents a **Humanist** theology: the divine is friendly, internal, and affirms our "truth." This leads to a spiritual flatness because there is no tension between the "Old Man" (Selfhood) and the "New Man" (Regenerate Soul). Everyone is already a "Star."

### 7.2 The Mirror: The Persistence of Theism

In *Snow White*, the Mirror is a terrifying, judgmental force. It speaks the Truth ("You are not the fairest") regardless of the Queen's feelings. It represents a **Theistic** theology: Truth is objective, external, and often painful. It creates tension. It demands that the "Old Man" (Queen) be destroyed so that the "New Man" (Snow White) can rule.

### 7.4 The Paradox of "Wokeness"

Ironically, the very elements of *Snow White* labeled as "woke" or "modern" by critics—Snow White's leadership, the active role of Jonathan, the removal of the "saving kiss" as the sole resolution—align her *more* closely with the Swedenborgian ideal of the **Regenerate Person**.

* **The 1937 Snow White** is the "Church in Expectation"—passive, waiting for Grace.
* **The 2025 Snow White** is the "Church in Act"—cooperating with Grace (Jonathan) to banish Evil (Queen).

Swedenborg argues that man must act "as if from himself" to shun evils. Passive waiting is "Faith Alone," which is spiritually dead. Therefore, the "girlboss" energy of the new Snow White, when viewed through this specific theological lens, is actually a correction of the "Faith Alone" passivity of the 1937 original. She is taking up the "Sword of Truth" (figuratively) and engaging in the work of regeneration.

## VIII. Intentionality and the Ghost in the Machine

Does this evidence prove that Marc Webb, Greta Gerwig, or Erin Cressida Wilson are practicing Swedenborgians? It is highly unlikely that they are card-carrying members of the New Church. However, the evidence suggests the existence of an "Active Substrate" of Swedenborgian thought within the "Disney Formula" that asserts itself whenever the studio engages seriously with fairy tales.

### 8.1 Greta Gerwig: The Theological Bricolage

Greta Gerwig, who worked on the screenplay (the Writers Guild credits the screenplay to Erin Cressida Wilson and gives Gerwig an "additional literary material" credit; Writers Guild of America West n.d.), has a known background in exploring theological themes. Her film *Lady Bird* is explicitly concerned with Catholicism, grace, and an economy of love; Gerwig thought, while writing it, "about what saints were like as teenagers" (Blondiau 2017). While she is not Swedenborgian, her "Catholic imagination" (sacramentalism, saints, tangible grace) predisposes her to "Vertical" storytelling.

When adapting *Snow White*, her instinct to give the character "agency" did not lead to secular nihilism (as in *Wish*) but to a "Theology of Works." She replaced "Passive Faith" not with "Self-Empowerment" but with "Active Charity" (the Pies). This aligns with the Swedenborgian critique of "Faith Alone" (Sola Fide). Gerwig likely accessed the "Appleseed" archetype via the studio's cultural memory, utilizing it as a shorthand for "American Goodness," inadvertently reactivating its sectarian roots. She intuitively understood that for Snow White to be a leader, she had to be a *server*.

### 8.2 Marc Webb: The Wholesome DNA

Marc Webb, the director, has said: "It was really important for us to honor the DNA of Snow White" (Harper 2025). A critic praised the film's "joyous finale" as "the type of wholesome and uplifting family entertainment that comes directly from old-school Disney DNA" (Rooney 2025). By attempting to "honor the DNA," Webb is essentially running the old code. The "DNA" of 1937 and 1948 *is* Swedenborgian (in this library's reading: Graham's pedagogy and the *Appleseed* production). Therefore, the more Webb tries to be "Disney," the more Swedenborgian the film becomes. His own account of the design is a return to the studio's first film: in the Walt Disney Archives he found "an early sketch of the prince in the dungeon," and "the storyline with Jonathan … really began with that one image" (Walt Disney Company 2025).

### 8.3 The "Woke" Paradox Resolved

Critics have focused on the "woke" elements of the film—Rachel Zegler's comments about the "stalker" prince, the diverse casting, the "girlboss" leadership arc. However, this analysis finds that these elements have ironically reinforced the "Vertical Spiritual Logic" of the film.

* **Rejection of the Stalker Prince:** By rejecting the "passive rescue" of the 1937 film, the creators have moved the narrative from "Faith Alone" (waiting for salvation) to "Cooperation" (working with Truth/Jonathan).
* **Leadership Arc:** By making Snow White a leader who "rallies her people" through service (pies), the film moves her into the "Doctrine of Use." She is not just being good; she is *doing* good. In Swedenborgian terms, this is a higher spiritual state. The "modernization" has inadvertently made the film more theologically rigorous.

---

## IX. Conclusion: The Unintended Reformation

The investigation concludes that the 2025 live-action *Snow White* provides structural proof of intentional (or at least structurally inevitable) Swedenborgian adherence. The film stands as a "theological correction" to the Gnostic humanism of *Wish*. Where *Wish* trapped the divine in the self, *Snow White* (2025) restores the mirror—not as a tool of vanity, but as a terrifying, vertical window into the ontological reality of the soul.

The evidence is cumulative and mutually reinforcing:

1. **The "Apple Orchard backstory"** links the heroine to the "Blessings Three" (Love, Faith, and the Apple Tree) of Johnny Appleseed, grounding her character in the "Doctrine of Use."
2. **The character of Jonathan** ("God-given") restores the Intellect-Will dynamic of Swedenborgian regeneration, replacing passive romance with active cooperation.
3. **The song "Waiting on a Wish"** explicitly rejects the "Echo" mechanism of self-amplification and affirms the Vertical posture of prayer—waiting for "a miracle to find me"—while "Good Things Grow" makes giving, not receiving, the law of the good kingdom.
4. **The Magic Mirror** is elevated to a site of Judgment, and the Queen's destruction by the Void is a precise visual representation of "Vastation" and "Second Death."
5. **The film's words** echo the 1948 *Johnny Appleseed*—seed, sharing, "we reap what we sow"—and its design returns to the studio's first film.

Snow White is not "fairest" because she is pretty; she is "fairest" because she is Useful. She bakes pies. She feeds the village. She manifests her internal love in external works. She waits for influx from above while acting "as if from herself" to restore the kingdom.

The creative leadership, in their attempt to "modernize" the story, has stripped away the "romance" (Horizontal) and replaced it with "alliance" and "judgment" (Vertical). They have replaced the "Prince" with "Truth" (Jonathan) and the "Cliff" with the "Void." In doing so, they have produced a film that is arguably more spiritually rigorous than the original it seeks to update.

The commercial fate of the film may be subject to the whims of the "culture war," but its metaphysical architecture stands as a testament to the enduring power of the Vertical Mirror. The "Magic" of Disney, it seems, cannot be sustained by "Stardust" alone; it requires the "Apple"—the tangible, edible fruit of a life lived in correspondence with the Divine.

As Disney moves forward, the lesson is clear: The "Magic" does not come from the *idea* of magic (The Star); it comes from the *obedience* to spiritual laws (The Mirror). To reanimate its soul, the studio must look less at its own reflection in the water ("I'm a Star") and more at the hard truth in the glass.
