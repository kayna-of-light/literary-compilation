## Correction to the library
*The Transmission of the Ancient Word* (Works Cited 29) cites "Gardiner, Karen. 'Theology within the Nonsense: Lewis Carroll and the Eschatology of State.' PhD diss., University of Bristol, 2018." No such thesis exists: the title, university and year are wrong. "Theology within the nonsense" is a phrase from Gardiner's conclusion (p. 297). *The Looking-Glass of Theology* cited her through a blog post.

## What it argues (her words)
- Conclusion: "there is ample evidence to suggest that Lewis Carroll's imaginative works contain theology within the nonsense, and that he was particularly influenced by the eschatology of F. D. Maurice and those writers and theologians connected to him through the Christian Socialist Movement (Charles Kingsley), the Cambridge Apostles (Farrar and Trench), the church at Vere Street (George MacDonald) and through wider Broad Church and Oxford interests (Jowett and Max Müller)" (p. 297).
- Maurice and Carroll "ultimately shared a belief that everlasting punishment is an inadequate and immoral axiom for an eternally loving God … The understanding of eternity as a state which is unrelated to time or space and the belief that there is something intrinsically childlike, relational and unified about that state, is a theme which is revisited insistently throughout Maurice and Carroll's writings" (pp. 298–299).
- On *Sylvie and Bruno*: "the author allows physical space to be manipulated and experienced in a multitude of ways depending on the spiritual state of the individual" (p. 271). Maurice, *Theological Essays*: when we live in a state of eternity we "break loose from the fetters of Time" (*TE* 432, quoted p. 271).
- On *Looking-Glass*: Alice, MacDonald's Diamond and Kingsley's Tom all "experience and observe the challenges that arise from living backwards"; Alice "is able to achieve progress" only "by walking away from where she wants to go" (p. 242).
- New evidence: Maurice's letters to Carroll in the Jowett controversy, transcribed in Appendix 2 ("March 1863"), lent by a Dodgson relative.
- Literature review (pp. 17–20): Morton Cohen (1995) held that Carroll did not blend religion and nonsense; Docherty (1995) traced mutual symbols in Carroll and MacDonald; Gabelman (2016) and Beer (2016) read theology and philosophy in the nonsense.
- **She never mentions Swedenborg** (whole-text search: 0 hits).

## Why it matters
It is the most thorough study of Carroll's theology, built on primary letters. It documents the route by which the content the library reads in Carroll — eternity as a state, not a duration; space bent by spiritual state; punishment as continuing choice — actually reached him: Maurice and the Broad Church, with MacDonald in the same circle. It finds no Swedenborg in that route.
