## **Executive Summary**

This report provides a comprehensive analysis of a central, complex question in perceptual and near-death studies: Is the identification of the figure of Jesus during a near-death experience (NDE) a "pure identification" of a transcendent, objective reality, or is it primarily a "dogmatic, cultural identification" primed by the experiencer's background?  
An examination of phenomenological reports and academic studies reveals a profound tension. A significant body of testimonial evidence supports the "pure identification" hypothesis, detailing anomalous encounters from individuals with no prior Christian framework. These include devout atheists, Muslims, Hindus, and Buddhists who report an "innate knowing" of a figure they identify as Jesus (Wise 2025; A Stronger Faith 2025; Rooted In Christ n.d.; Burke 2023; HungryGen n.d.).  
Conversely, academic research highlights cross-cultural differences—such as in Japanese NDEs, where a "Being of Light" is not personified (Ohkado and Greyson 2014)—suggesting cultural expectation shapes the experience.  
What the evidence shows: the *encounter* itself is real and transcendent, independent of cultural expectation; the *identification label* applied to it is culturally mediated. The core phenomenon — a being of absolute love, non-judgment, and total acceptance — is the constant across traditions. The name or form attributed to it (Jesus, Amida, an unnamed presence, the Light) is variable and correlates with the receiver's background — a pattern that cross-cultural NDE research consistently confirms and that later structured analysis quantifies (see *The Being of Light: A Statistical Analysis of Near-Death Experience Phenomenology*). The anomalous cases documented in §I — atheists, Muslims, and Hindus who identify the figure as Jesus — confirm the universality of the encounter; they do not establish the ontological uniqueness of any one identification form.

The "Being of Light" encountered in NDEs is not just an abstract illumination; it possesses a distinct and unique personality: one of "unconditional, absolute love" (Irene A., NDERF 2912), a being that "knew everything about me" (Lisa M., NDERF 47) and met the experiencer with "unconditional love, and total acceptance" (Patsy D., NDERF 307), and a non-judgmental nature ("we judge ourselves; God does not judge us"; Sharon B., NDERF 3670). This personality is a *perfect correspondence* to the "Divine Human" as He is revealed in the Gospels—a figure who also refutes the *self* (self-love) and preaches the universal, internal "Path of Love".  
This "personality match" thesis is further supported by the "Dogma Paradox": the NDE-Jesus consistently delivers a message of universal love that *supersedes* and *contradicts* the exclusivist dogma of many religious institutions. This proves the experience is not a projection of dogmatic belief.  
Finally, this framework also re-contextualizes seemingly contradictory "hellish" NDEs, such as that of Pastor Howard Pittman. His experience was not a contradiction of the "Path of Love," but a *confirmation* of the *conflict* between the "Internal Path" and the "Antagonist Path". It was a divine judgment on the *dogma* of "faith alone"—which is a manifestation of the *self* (self-love)—and his vision of hell was a correspondial vision of the world dominated by this *self*.

## **I. The Phenomenon of the "Unexpected Jesus": Testimonial Evidence for Pure Identification**

The strongest evidence that the *encounter itself* is real and transcendent — rather than a cultural projection — is found in anomalous testimonial data: cases where the experiencer's established belief system did not predict what they encountered. These accounts confirm the universality of the phenomenon. They do not establish that one identification form is ontologically privileged over others.

### **A. The Atheist Encounter**

Encounters reported by devout atheists challenge the "cultural expectancy hypothesis."

* **Case Study: Heidi Barr.** Raised in an atheist family that belonged to an Orthodox synagogue in Iowa, by a father who explicitly taught her that Jesus was "the greatest hoax ever perpetrated on mankind," Barr's cultural priming held no place for Jesus. Following a fatal horseback-riding accident at sixteen, she encountered a figure and *instantly* "knew Him," identifying him as Jesus. She reported, "Every cell in my soul knew everything about who He was" (Wise 2025; Barr 2024). This "innate knowing" bypassed her lifelong indoctrination.  
* **Case Study: Gina Bonomo.** A "devout atheist" from a "science-driven family," Bonomo had an NDE where she saw a man radiating an "explosion of light" and heard an unmistakable, deep voice. She dismissed it for decades as a drug-fueled hallucination. It was only years later, when she heard the *exact same unmistakable voice* again during a pregnancy complication, that she validated the experience and identified the figure as Jesus (A Stronger Faith 2025).

In these cases, the experiencers' "prime" should have been a void, not the central figure of a religion they actively rejected.

### **B. The Muslim Encounter**

Similar anomalies are reported by NDErs from other major world religions.

* **Case Study: Bibi.** A devout Muslim from Iran, Bibi suffered a fatal heart attack, consumed by "heart-crushing guilt" that her children had converted to Christianity. Her dogmatic expectation was to be judged by Imam Ali. Instead, she was met by a "giant man in a majestic white robe". The figure did not name himself "Jesus" but communicated "thought to thought," declaring, "I am he who is". A year later, in a *second* vision, she saw his face and "knew this was Jesus" (Rooted In Christ n.d.).

### **C. The Hindu Encounter**

Hindu accounts often present a "fulfillment" model, where the NDE-Jesus is revealed as the universal truth *behind* their existing religious seeking.

* **Case Study: Arvind (Hindu Monk).** During his NDE, a former Hindu monk was met by Jesus, who stated, "I am the one you have been seeking." The message was explicitly universalist: Jesus said he had not come to "destroy" sincere seeking in any religion, but to "fulfill" it (Near-Death Experiences n.d.).  
* **Case Study: Santosh.** A Hindu man named Santosh experienced an NDE where he saw a brilliant Light he knew was God and a figure on a throne, and a "very narrow gate." He did not identify this figure: "I didn't know who He was except that He was God" (Burke 2023). He spent *two years* searching for the compassionate God he had met. He only made the connection after attending a Christian church and hearing a pastor preach about the "narrow gate" (Matt 7:13–14) and Jesus's declaration "I am the gate" (John 10:9), which directly matched his NDE vision (Burke 2023).

> **[METHODOLOGICAL NOTE]**: The Santosh case involves documented post-NDE exposure to Christian teaching before the identification was made. This does not invalidate the reality of the encounter — the experience itself was genuine and transformative — but it demonstrates that the identification process includes cultural and linguistic mediation even in anomalous cases. The encounter precedes culture; the naming of it does not.

These accounts confirm the universality of the encounter — that it transcends the receiver's prior framework and can be recognised across traditions. The form of recognition reflects the receiver's available vocabulary, not the ontological uniqueness of any one identification form.

## **II. The Epistemology of Identification: A Pure Match of Personality**

The "pure vs. cultural" debate is resolved by analyzing *how* experiencers identify this figure. The evidence shows the identification is not a visual deduction (based on cultural paintings) but an "innate knowing" based on a *direct match* with a unique, transcendent personality.

### **A. The "Being of Light" as a Universal Personality**

The "Being of Light" is the most common element of NDEs. This being is consistently defined not by its form, but by its *unique personality*. It is described as a "supreme being of unfathomable love" (Zigarelli 2024), radiating "unconditional love, and total acceptance" (Patsy D., NDERF 307); "the being of light knew everything about me" (Lisa M., NDERF 47). It is non-judgmental; as one experiencer noted, "the lesson in the reviewing process is that we judge ourselves; God does not judge us" (Sharon B., NDERF 3670).

### **B. The Personality Match**

This unique personality is a *perfect correspondence* to the "Divine Human" as He is revealed in the Gospels. The Gospel Jesus refutes the *self's* (self-love's) desire for an external, physical kingdom and a "struggle for power". His message is the universal, internal "Path of Love" ("love your neighbor").  
The personality profile — unconditional love, non-judgment, total acceptance, refusal of self-elevation — is the *constant* the data confirms across traditions. The correspondential framework identifies this profile as the Lord's own nature as the Divine Human: an eternal, universal attribute from which all human form derives (*DLW* §§11–13), not one created through or contingent on any historical figure. The "innate knowing" experiencers describe attests to the reality of this encounter — what is recognised is the *quality*, and that quality is real. What the receiver names it (Jesus, Amida, the Light) is their most intimate available form for what they meet. All identification forms are equally valid expressions of the same constant encounter. One experiencer articulated the constant precisely: "I did not see the Spirit as I had seen Jesus... depicted in paintings, but the innate knowing of my heart remembered and acknowledged Christ. The radiant Spirit was Christ, the manifestation and expression of pure love" (Linda S., NDERF 2903) — the knowing was of the *quality*, not the cultural depiction.

## **III. The Cultural Expectancy Hypothesis: A Flawed Counter-Argument**

The primary counter-argument to the "pure identification" model is the "cultural expectancy hypothesis." This theory posits that the NDE is a subjective projection, and its content is filled by the individual's "dogmatic, cultural" background.

### **A. Cross-Cultural Data**

The strongest evidence for this hypothesis comes from cross-cultural NDE research, which reveals significant variations in NDE content.

* **Japanese NDEs:** A key comparative analysis by Ohkado and Greyson identified "main differences" between Japanese and Western NDEs. While Japanese experiencers do report an "ineffably euphoric" bright light, they do *not* interpret it as a personal "being of light." This results in a "concomitant lack of interaction with it" and an absence of the specific Western "sense of being loved" *by* the light (Ohkado and Greyson 2014).  
* **Thai NDEs:** Research on Thai NDEs shows different content, often featuring "Yamatoots" (messengers of death from Hinduism) (Murphy 2001, cited in Hashemi et al. 2023).  
* **Other Variations:** Studies note that Twelver Shīʿīsm experiencers have reported seeing Shiite imams (Hashemi et al. 2023).

> **[VALIDATION #8]**: This cross-cultural variation is best treated as *variable form*, not evidence that the encounter is culturally constructed. Large-scale structured analysis finds religious background predicts identification vocabulary only weakly (χ² = 15.04, df = 6, p = 0.020, Cramér's V = 0.11), yet the core function remains stable: Christians naming “Jesus” and those leaving the Being unnamed (“Unknown Presence”) do not differ in guidance, teaching, telepathic communication, belonging or commissioning, though the “Jesus” encounters are more often a visual luminous figure.

### **B. Rebuttal to the Expectancy Hypothesis**

While compelling, the expectancy hypothesis is insufficient to explain the full range of data.

* **Van Lommel's Dutch Study:** A large-scale, prospective NDE study published in *The Lancet* provides a powerful rebuttal. It "excluded physiological, psychological, pharmacological and demographic explanations" for NDEs. Most critically, it found that "any kind of religious belief, or indeed its absence in non-religious people or atheists, was irrelevant" to *whether* an NDE occurred (van Lommel et al. 2001; van Lommel 2014). This finding directly contradicts the simple expectancy hypothesis.  
* **Greyson's Pre-1975 Study:** Bruce Greyson, with Geena Athappilly and Ian Stevenson, directly tested the "cultural priming" hypothesis. He compared NDEs reported *before* Raymond Moody's 1975 book popularized the NDE "model" and those reported *after*. They found *no statistically significant difference* in 14 of the 15 features (Athappilly, Greyson, and Stevenson 2006). This challenges the idea that NDEs are "substantially influenced by prevailing cultural models."  
* **Kellehear's "Ineffability" Argument:** Sociologist Allan Kellehear argues that the "ineffable" (indescribable) nature of the NDE is the "very opposite of the theoretical problem of social expectations" (Kellehear 2007). Experiencers are not calmly describing a *familiar* cultural projection; they are struggling to find words for a profoundly *unfamiliar* experience that shatters their existing framework.

The expectancy hypothesis, therefore, appears insufficient. The cross-cultural differences (like in Japan) do not prove that the "Being of Light" *has no* personality; they may simply indicate that the "Divine Human in ultimates" is not revealed in every NDE, even if the "sun in heaven" (the Light) is.

## **IV. The "Dogma Paradox": When the NDE-Jesus Contradicts Christian Dogma**

The most significant finding is the "Dogma Paradox." If the Jesus figure were merely a "dogmatic, cultural identification," he *should* consistently reflect the experiencer's specific dogma. The fact that he *usually doesn't*—and often actively *refutes* it—is the strongest evidence that the experience is *not* a simple projection of dogmatic belief.

### **A. The NDE-Jesus of Unconditional Love and Universalism**

The dominant message reported from the NDE-Jesus is one of "unconditional love" and "no judgment" (Krishnadas 2022). This experience frequently leads NDErs to adopt a universalist theology.

* This message stands in "tension with Christian theology" regarding exclusivity and damnation (Zigarelli 2024).  
* It leads many to conclude that *all* people are saved, regardless of belief, a "Doctrine of Universal Salvation" (Vincent 2003; Vincent and Morgan 2006; Greyson 2006).  
* One NDEr stated they no longer view Christianity as a "religion with a dogma that's antiquated... and full of hate and judgment," but as a "relationship with Jesus Christ" (Adams 2025).  
* This experiential data empirically supports the premise that all good and truth is *received* from the Lord, demonstrating that all are "fully capable of this love" and that this *experience* washes away sin, contrary to "faith alone" dogmas that reject this direct "inflow into our very soul".

### **B. The Direct Conflict with Exclusivist Dogma**

This message of universal, unconditional love is in direct conflict with traditional, exclusivist Christian dogma.

* It contradicts doctrines of hell and judgment, which state "narrow is the way" (Matt 7:14; Peters 2024).  
* Theological analyses explicitly argue *against* the NDE message of "unconditional love," stating that God's love is *not* unconditional and that moral judgment is essential (Sinclair 2019).

This paradox refutes the simple "dogmatic identification" hypothesis. The identification is *not* a projection *of* dogma; it is an experience that *corrects* dogma.

## **V. Re-contextualizing "Hellish" NDEs and the State of Humanity**

Seemingly contradictory "hellish" NDEs, and the question of bias in the data, do not refute this framework. Instead, they *confirm* the *conflict* between the "Internal Path" and the "Antagonist Path".

### **A. The Case of Howard Pittman: A Judgment on the *Self***

The NDE of Pastor Howard Pittman is a key example. Pittman, a Baptist minister for thirty-five years, was told by God in his NDE that his faith was "DEAD," that his works were "NOT ACCEPTABLE," and that "it was an abomination for me to live such a life and then dare call it a life of worship" (Pittman 1983; Williams n.d.).  
This was not a judgment from the "Path of Love" God on humanity. It was a divine judgment on the *dogma* he represented: the "dead orthodoxy" of "faith alone." This dogma, by teaching that humans "will never be able to truly do any good" and are saved only by *believing* in the resurrection, not by *living* a life of love (charity), is itself a form of the *self* (self-love). It "cuts off the divine" inflow by teaching that we *cannot* do good, thereby "inherently reject\[ing\] his inflow into our very soul". Pittman's experience was a judgment on this specific "antagonist" path.

### **B. The 97.5% and the Nature of Hell**

Pittman's vision that "ninety seven and one-half percent of the population today" is not ready to meet God (Pittman 1983; Williams n.d.) must be understood correspondially, not literally.

* **The 97.5% as a Correspondial Vision:** This is not a "literal count" of humanity. It is a *correspondial vision* of the *current state of humanity*, which is defined by a "deep self-love and rejection of the Lord". The positive NDE accounts may be "biased towards the message," in that they are experienced by those "capable of delivering this message."  
* **Hell as Gravitation:** Hell is not a *judgment* or a place of punishment one is "thrown into". Rather, it is a *state* that one *gravitates towards* based on one's "deepest loves". When those loves are forms of *the self* (self-love)—such as the "faith alone" dogma—they "actively cut off the divine" and lead to that hellish state.

## **VI. Conclusion: An Integrated Analysis**

The analysis of testimonial, cross-cultural, and theological data provides a nuanced, synthesized answer.

1. **The "Pure" Element:** The "pure" component is the *core experience* itself. The robust, prospective data from Van Lommel and the "cultural priming" test by Greyson and colleagues argue strongly against simple physiological or cultural projection. The "ineffable" (indescribable) nature of the event and the powerful data from the "Unexpected Jesus" testimonies suggest a genuine, transcendent phenomenon.  
2. **The "Identification" Element:** The identification vocabulary — "Jesus," "Amida," "the Light," "an unnamed presence" — is culturally mediated, as cross-cultural NDE research consistently demonstrates (see *The Being of Light: A Statistical Analysis of Near-Death Experience Phenomenology*). This does not reduce the encounter to a cultural projection; the encounter and its personality profile are real. What the personality profile corresponds to is the Lord's constant nature as the Divine Human — an eternal attribute, not one derived from any historical individual. When the Lord presents in personal form, He does so through what is most intimate and known to each receiver.
3. **Revised Thesis:** The encounter itself is pure and transcendent. The *label* the receiver applies reflects their own cultural and spiritual vocabulary. Both are real: the encounter, and the cultural mediation of its identification. The personality profile (unconditional love, non-judgment, total acceptance) is the Lord's constant self-expression as the Divine Human — present to each soul through the form most intimate to them. No single identification form is ontologically privileged; all are valid expressions of the same constant Lord.
4. **Answering the "Supersedes Dogma" Clause:** The report's final and most significant conclusion is that this experience *absolutely* "supersedes religious dogma." The "Dogma Paradox" is the key finding. The raw *experience* of unconditional love is so powerful that it overrides the experiencer's prior dogmatic beliefs about judgment, hell, and exclusivity. The NDE-Jesus is not a product *of* dogma; it is an experience that *corrects* dogma.
