# NHL Research Query 2: Attribution and Protagonist Analysis

## Research Question

Analyze the attribution patterns across the Nag Hammadi Library. Many NHL texts present their teaching as a revelation or discourse attributed to, or received by, a specific named figure from the early Christian or Jewish tradition. This query aims to map those attributions systematically.

For each NHL tractate (NHC I–XIII plus BG 8502):

1. **Identify the primary authority figure**: To whom is the teaching, revelation, or narrative attributed? Who is the named recipient of secret knowledge, or the protagonist of the narrative?
2. **Identify secondary figures**: Which other named individuals appear in speaking roles, as interlocutors, or as recipients?
3. **Note any explicit statements about transmission**: Does the text claim a particular chain of transmission (e.g., "Jesus said to James," "the Savior revealed to John")?
4. **Note any polemical references**: Does the text explicitly criticize, exclude, or subordinate any named figure?

Present the results as:

### Table A: Attribution by Figure

For each named figure who appears as a primary authority or protagonist in at least one text, list:
- The figure's name
- All texts in which they appear as primary authority/protagonist
- All texts in which they appear as secondary figure or interlocutor
- All texts in which they are the subject of polemic (positive or negative)

Include at minimum: Jesus, James (specify which James if distinguishable), Thomas, Philip, John (distinguish son of Zebedee from other Johns where possible), Peter, Mary (specify which Mary if distinguishable), Paul, Seth, Adam, Shem, Norea, Silvanus, Hermes/Trismegistus, Zostrianos.

### Table B: Frequency Distribution

A simple count: how many texts feature each figure as primary authority?

## Output Requirements

- Base attributions on the text itself, not on scholarly speculation about authorship
- Where a text's protagonist is debated (e.g., the "I" of a first-person narrative), note the scholarly discussion
- Cite the relevant critical editions or translations used
- Use peer-reviewed scholarship for any interpretive claims about identity (e.g., whether "Judas Thomas" = "Thomas" = "Jude brother of Jesus")
- Report what the texts say; do not supply an overarching interpretive framework
