# Source 004 — Carroll, "Eternal Punishment" (*The Lewis Carroll Picture Book*, 1899)

**Citation**: Carroll, Lewis. "Eternal Punishment." In *The Lewis Carroll Picture Book*, edited by Stuart Dodgson Collingwood, 345–355. London: T. Fisher Unwin, 1899.
**Accessed**: 2026-10-23, full djvu text, archive.org `lewiscarrollpict00carruoft`. No mention of Swedenborg in the volume.

## What it is
Collingwood's headnote: of a planned volume of essays on religious difficulties Carroll "only lived long enough to finish one … the subject—Eternal Punishment—was one on which he felt very deeply".

## Key passages (verbatim)
- The three propositions: "I. God is perfectly good. II. To inflict Eternal Punishment on certain human beings, and in certain circumstances, would be wrong. III. God is capable of acting thus."
- Case C: "one who retains Free-Will and continues for ever to choose evil. In such a case Eternal Punishment would be infinite suffering, inflicted as punishment for infinite sin. I assume that the reader would not feel any difficulty in recognising the justice of inflicting continuous suffering as punishment for continuous sin."
- His conclusion on II: "the irresistible intuition that infinite punishment for finite sin would be unjust, and therefore wrong."
- On the word: the doctrine "depends largely, if not entirely, on the meaning given to one single word (αἰών). This is rendered, in our English Bibles, by the word 'eternal' or 'everlasting': but there are many critics who believe that it does not necessarily mean 'endless.'"
- His method: "my object has been, throughout, not to indicate one course rather than another, but to help the Reader to see clearly what the possible courses are".

## Why it matters
It is Carroll's own theology in his own words. It converges with Swedenborg on two points without naming him: the one who goes on choosing evil is in continuous suffering of his own continuing choice (cf. *HH* §547: man "is led into hell, not by the Lord but by himself"); and the question turns on whether "eternal" means duration (cf. *HH* §§162–163: in heaven "instead of years and days there are changes of state"). The word-point is Maurice's (source_008).
