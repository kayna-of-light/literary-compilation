# Source 017 — Aquinas, *Summa Theologiae*: the feature test, Thomist side

**Citation**: Thomas Aquinas. *Summa Theologiae.* Translated by the Fathers of the English Dominican Province. 2nd rev. ed., 1920. New Advent online edition (`newadvent.org/summa/1045.htm`, `1076.htm`, `1078.htm`, `1037.htm`, `1039.htm`). Cited by part, question and article.

**Accessed**: 2026-10-24.

## Passages

- **I q.45 a.7**: "in all creatures there is found the trace of the Trinity, inasmuch as in every creature are found some things which are necessarily reduced to the divine Persons as to their cause."
- **I q.76 a.1**: "inasmuch as the soul is the form of the body, it has not an existence apart from the existence of the body, but by its own existence is united to the body immediately."
- **I q.78 a.1**: "the whole corporeal nature is subject to the soul, and is related to it as its matter and instrument." The sensitive operations are performed "through a corporeal organ".
- **I q.37**: Love as the proper name of the Holy Ghost. **I q.39 a.8**: the appropriations, with power to the Father, wisdom to the Son and goodness to the Holy Ghost.
- **I q.82 a.3**: the intellect is higher than the will "simply and absolutely".

## How Delsarte's formulations compare

| Delsarte (store 002; Stebbins fragments) | Aquinas | Swedenborg (store 001) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trinity "present in all Creation" | Trace of the Trinity in all creatures (I q.45 a.7) | End, cause and effect in every created thing (*DLW* §154) | Shared; his vocabulary is Thomist |
| "The soul being the form of the body" | Verbatim formula (I q.76 a.1) | Not Swedenborg's formula | Thomist marker |
| Natural, intellectual, supernatural; light of sense, reason, grace | Nature and grace; natural and supernatural | Natural, spiritual, celestial | Thomist marker |
| Love highest of the three | Intellect higher than will absolutely (I q.82 a.3) | Celestial love above spiritual wisdom | Shared with Swedenborg (and the Augustinian-Franciscan line), against Aquinas |
| Trinity as "Love, Wisdom, Power" (Stebbins's English) | Power, wisdom, goodness; Love the Spirit's name | "Love, wisdom and power" (as Stebbins quotes him) | Ambiguous; the French original is not seen |
| Correspondence by function of the organ | Sensitive powers through a corporeal organ (I q.78 a.1) | Inflow "into the functions and uses of the bodily members" (*HH* §96) | Shared |

## Why it matters

Delsarte's frame carries Thomist markers that are not Swedenborg's. With Waille (store 003), this supports reading Delsarte's own route as Catholic-hermetic, not Swedenborgian.
