# Source 026: Clement and Origen's Genesis Readings — The Chain Sustained

**Status**: Gap C research. Grounded in primary texts and Daniélou's scholarly analysis.

## The Core Finding

Clement and Origen both sustained Philo's correspondential Genesis readings across two additional centuries in Alexandria. The chain is documented:

| Reader | Date | Adam | Eve/Woman | Paradise | Heaven | Method |
|--------|------|------|-----------|----------|--------|--------|
| **Philo** | 1st c. CE | Mind (nous) | Sense-perception (aisthesis) | Virtue / the soul | Mind (intelligible realm) | Lived practice + systematized |
| **Clement** | c. 200 CE | Potential for perfection | [Fall = premature desire] | [Implicit in allegory theory] | [Double creation: invisible + material] | Theory of concealment |
| **Origen** | c. 230 CE | Interior man, incorporeal | Soul (anima) — "woman" to spirit's "man" | Allegory of the soul | "First heaven is our mind" | Threefold sense systematized |

## Origen's Specific Genesis Readings (Primary Text)

From *Homilies on Genesis* (Heine translation, Fathers of the Church vol. 71):

**On the interior man (= Adam):**
> "No corporeal being can contain the image of God, but what has been made in God's image is the interior man, invisible, incorporeal, incorruptible, immortal."

**On man and woman as spirit and soul:**
> "Our interior man is composed of soul and spirit. The spirit is called man, the soul (anima) is called woman. If there is harmony between them, they unite frequently and beget sons which are good dispositions and salutary thoughts."

**On heaven as mind:**
> "First heaven indeed, which we said is spiritual, is our mind."
(Homilies on Genesis 1.2)

**On animals as dispositions:**
> "Birds and fishes signify those realities... dispositions of the soul and the thoughts of the beast."

**On digging for meaning:**
> "If one knows how to dig into the depth, he will find a treasure in the details."
(Homilies on Genesis 8.1)

### Daniélou's Assessment

Jean Daniélou, *Origen* (1955), the definitive scholarly study:
> "Origen took from Philo the idea of looking to Scripture for allegories applying to the moral life. This is particularly evident at the beginning of the homilies on Genesis, where the whole of creation is regarded as an allegory of the soul, as the macrocosm of the microcosm. Man and woman are the two parts of the soul."

This confirms: Origen's Genesis readings are NOT independent invention. They are the CONTINUATION of Philo's readings — the same key applied to the same text, producing the same output, 200 years later.

## Clement's Contribution (Primary Text)

From *Stromata* Book 5 (Ante-Nicene Fathers translation):

**The principle of concealment:**
> "All who have spoken of divine things, both Barbarians and Greeks, have veiled the first principles of things, and delivered the truth in enigmas, and symbols, and allegories, and metaphors, and such like tropes."

**Natural correspondence in the Tabernacle:**
> "The covering and the veil were variegated with blue, and purple, and scarlet, and linen... purple is from water, linen from the earth; blue, being dark, is like the air, as scarlet is like fire."

The Tabernacle elements correspond to the four elements, which correspond to four cosmic principles. Clement is reading the Tabernacle correspondentially — the same method Philo applied to Genesis and the Gospel of Philip applied to the Temple.

**On double creation:**
Clement assumed a double creation — one of an invisible world and the second being material creation, influenced by Plato. This is Philo's "two Adams" in Clementine dress: Genesis 1 = the invisible/spiritual creation, Genesis 2 = the material/natural creation.

**On why truth is hidden:**
> "All things that shine through a veil show the truth grander and more imposing."

Truth comes in types and images not as a limitation but as an AMPLIFICATION — the veil makes the content "grander." This is Philip's principle restated: "Truth did not come into the world naked, but it came in types and images."

## The Catechetical School Chain

The Catechetical School of Alexandria provides the institutional continuity:

| Head | Period | Contribution |
|------|--------|--------------|
| **Pantaenus** | c. 180-190 CE | Founded the school; reportedly traveled to India; established the allegorical tradition |
| **Clement** | c. 190-202 CE | Theorized the method; wrote Stromata defending allegory against both literalists and Gnostics |
| **Origen** | c. 202-231 CE | Systematized the threefold sense; applied it to the entire Bible; wrote the Hexapla |

This institutional chain — a SCHOOL with formal succession — sustained the correspondential method for 50+ years. The method wasn't just floating in the intellectual atmosphere; it was taught, transmitted, and defended through an identifiable institution.

When Demetrius (Rome's man in Alexandria) brought the school under episcopal control and eventually expelled Origen, he was capturing the institutional carrier of the correspondential method — domesticating it within the hierarchy.
