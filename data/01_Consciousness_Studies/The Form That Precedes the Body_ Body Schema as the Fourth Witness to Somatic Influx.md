# **The Form That Precedes the Body: Body Schema as the Fourth Witness to Somatic Influx**

## A Framework-Grounded Synthesis of Soul, Bodily Form, and the Neuroscience of Body Representation

---

> **Abstract.** The Divine Bricolage framework holds that the body follows the soul: celestial love or will proceeds through spiritual form into natural effects, and the body is the soul expressed at the degree of ultimates rather than an independent organism that generates inner life. The corpus has already assembled evidence for this direction across bodily formation, spontaneous remission, and terminal lucidity. This thesis brings that established ontology to a question left open in contemporary body-representation research: what is the relation between the body's morphology and the internal form by which a person experiences, locates, and acts through a body? The neuroscience reveals three distinguishable levels. Primary sensorimotor territory is reassigned according to functional use; fine representational structure can persist for decades without peripheral input; and the form model in premotor and posterior parietal cortex can include limbs that were never physically built. Own-body processing can also align with identity rather than birth-assigned morphology, with differences appearing in supramarginal and insular regions rather than only in primary sensory maps. Placed within the architecture of discrete degrees, these findings resolve how the soul's prior human organization reaches bodily expression: body schema is the plastic natural articulation through which interior form becomes available for ownership, posture, sensation, and action, while gross morphology develops through the more determinate laws of the natural degree. Genetics, embryology, and neural development are therefore not rival causes but the natural mechanisms of expression. The evidence establishes a cumulative case in which body-schema findings occupy the precise research space opened by the framework's existing evidence that the body follows the soul.

> **Keywords.** body schema, somatic influx, correspondences, phantom limb, aplasic phantom, cortical plasticity, discrete degrees, own-body processing, body image, birthmark correspondence, determinacy gradient, posterior parietal cortex, supramarginal gyrus, post-materialist consciousness

## 1. Introduction

*The Surface That Withholds Nothing* established the relation from which this thesis begins. At the **formation** of the body, trauma carried in the history of a soul can print onto a newly forming body as a birthmark: in the forty-nine Division of Perceptual Studies cases where a medical document — usually a postmortem report — verified the previous personality's wound, forty-three, or eighty-eight percent, showed a satisfactory correspondence between wound and mark. In the **course of a life**, a documented reorganisation of a person's governing orientation is followed by the disappearance of Stage IV disease, on a timescale and at a magnitude that exceeds expectation-mediated physiology. At the **end**, in terminal lucidity, a person whose neural substrate has been devastated by dementia can return to full coherence shortly before death. Three moments, three literatures, one direction: the body follows the soul. It is the soul's outermost expression, not the source from which the soul is generated.

This direction belongs to the framework's broader account of how anything comes into being. Reality unfolds through discrete degrees: love or will at the celestial degree, its articulation as understanding and form at the spiritual degree, and its completion in effects at the natural degree. The degrees are distinct in operation but continuous in causation. The natural does not copy an interior original from across a divide; it is that interior brought into ultimate expression through natural laws. Consequently, genetics, embryology, neurodevelopment, and cortical plasticity are not competitors to spiritual causation. They are its natural-degree history — the lawful means by which interior form becomes physical form.

The present thesis brings this established ontology to the contemporary science of **body schema**: the internal organisation through which a person owns, locates, moves, and experiences a body. The research gap lies there. Neuroscience has mapped cortical territories, digit representations, parietal form models, phantom limbs, and own-body processing in considerable detail. Yet these literatures are ordinarily read within a bottom-up frame in which the body supplies the data from which its internal representation is constructed. They are rarely placed beside evidence that bodily formation and reorganisation can follow an already existing state of the person, and they do not ask how a soul that is itself in human form would become expressed through a nervous system.

The case developed here is that body schema is the natural-degree articulation of the soul's own human form. The soul is not formless awareness placed inside a body. It is an organised receiving vessel: a unique configuration of love, understanding, biography, identity, and capacity. The corpus calls this configuration its spiritual geometry. At the natural degree, that interior organisation must become actionable — a body must be felt as one's own, its parts must stand in relation, sensation must be localised, and intention must become movement. Body schema is the neurological form of that work.

This does not mean that the natural body is passive material or that every feature of morphology reproduces an interior image. Each degree operates by its own laws. Gross sexual morphogenesis, limb number, skeletal architecture, and organ formation follow highly determinate developmental pathways. Body schema is comparatively plastic: it integrates multisensory information, reorganises with use, incorporates tools, and can persist when the morphology it represents is absent. The soul's form can therefore become legible most clearly at this more receptive level even where gross morphology follows a different natural pathway. The limit is part of how discrete degrees work, not a restriction imposed from outside the framework.

**The central finding of this thesis is that the neuroscientific literature, read for what it actually measured rather than for what its citation-trail asserts, has converged over the past decade on a three-level architecture of bodily representation in which the level that governs form is precisely the level that does not require a body.** Primary sensorimotor territory is reallocated to whatever body part assumes the missing part's function — this is the plasticity result, and it is real. But the fine representational structure *within* that territory persists unchanged for decades after amputation, including in a case where brachial plexus avulsion abolished all communication between the residual limb and the central nervous system. And the form model itself is not in primary sensorimotor cortex at all. In a woman born without forearms or lower legs who experiences vivid phantoms of all four limbs, functional imaging of phantom movement showed *no* activation of primary sensorimotor areas and bilateral activation of **premotor and parietal cortex**; transcranial magnetic stimulation of premotor and parietal sites evoked phantom sensations in limbs that were never built, without any motor evoked potential in the stump. Limbs that never existed are represented in the cortex that represents form.

That finding, on its own, establishes only that a body model can exist without the corresponding body. It does not establish which way causation runs. The decisive addition comes from a domain the amputation literature cannot reach, because amputation always involves a body part that once existed. In thirty transgender and thirty cisgender participants performing a body self-identification task under functional imaging, both groups activated the *same* self- and body-processing systems when viewing bodies aligned with their **gender identity rather than their birth-assigned sex** — with self-similarity ratings covaried out, so that what was measured was the representational system's response rather than a stated preference. And in a magnetoencephalography study of somatosensory response to stimulation of a body part experienced as incongruent, the measured difference fell in the **supramarginal gyrus** — inferior parietal cortex, the same territory that carried the aplasic phantom, and not primary somatosensory cortex. The form model and the morphology can disagree, the disagreement is measurable, and it is measurable exactly where the form model lives.

The evidence converges around a three-level architecture. Primary sensorimotor territory is reallocated according to whatever body part assumes the missing part's function. Fine representational structure *within* that territory can persist for decades after amputation, including where brachial plexus avulsion abolished all communication between the residual arm and the central nervous system. The form model itself is carried in premotor and posterior parietal systems: in a woman born without forearms or lower legs, phantom movement activated these regions rather than primary sensorimotor cortex, and direct stimulation evoked sensations in limbs that were never built. Own-body processing adds a further case in which the internal form and physical morphology are both present but do not coincide. Across these findings, bodily form behaves less like a representation assembled from peripheral tissue and more like a prior organisation finding expression through it.

The thesis establishes that case cumulatively. It does not ask a natural-degree instrument to isolate spiritual causation, nor does it treat genetic and epigenetic development as a rival ontology. It asks what the measured natural mechanisms look like when placed inside the framework already supported by the corpus's evidence. Alternative natural accounts remain important because they specify how the natural degree may operate; they do not by themselves reverse the direction the larger evidence establishes. Limitations are therefore reported as limits on particular measurements and inferences, not recruited to turn the thesis into a contest it was never meant to be.

The thesis proceeds as follows. **Section 2** establishes the direction from soul to body, the architecture of discrete degrees, the three levels of bodily representation, and the empirical reading protocol. **Section 3** moves from the corpus's existing evidence to the gap in contemporary body-schema research. **Sections 4–7** assemble the natural evidence: functional remapping, persistence without peripheral input, representation without morphology, and schema–morphology disagreement. **Section 8** returns the findings to the framework and develops how bodily form would be held in a soul and expressed through a natural body. **Section 9** states the cumulative case, the proper relation between spiritual cause and natural mechanism, the research programme opened by the synthesis, and its limitations. **Section 10** concludes.

## 2. Methodological Framework

### 2.1 The Ontological Direction: The Body Follows the Soul

The framework begins here with an ontological claim, not a method of correspondential reading. Correspondential reading concerns the way a text, image, dream, vision, or experienced scene expresses interior realities through natural forms. The present question is prior to interpretation: **what is the relation between the soul and the body itself?**

The corpus answers that the body is not a container occupied by the soul and not an independent organism that produces the soul. The body **is the soul expressed at the natural degree**. The celestial degree is the person's love or will; the spiritual degree is the articulation of that love in understanding and form; the natural degree is the same person brought into effects, action, tissue, and extension. These degrees are discrete because each operates in its own medium and by its own laws. They are nevertheless one causal order because the interior gives rise to the exterior. The direction is from love through form into ultimate expression: the soul first, the body following.

This is the principle that the corpus has already brought into contact with evidence. In the DOPS cases, a wound retained in the soul's history appears during the formation of another body. In spontaneous remission, an interior reorganisation precedes physical reorganisation. In terminal lucidity, the person becomes fully expressible when the damaged neural ultimate briefly ceases to obscure her. Across formation, life, and death, the natural body behaves as expression rather than source.

The body-schema question belongs directly inside this established account. If the soul is prior to its natural expression, then the body cannot first become a form only when tissue reports itself to the brain. The soul must already possess the organisation by which it is a human being: not necessarily a visual image of a body, but a living arrangement of capacities, relations, affections, and powers that can be expressed in human form. The neurological body schema would then be the natural-degree articulation of that prior organisation — the level at which the soul's form becomes available for bodily ownership, localisation, posture, and action.

This changes the question asked of neuroscience. The issue is not whether imaging can prove the existence of a soul. Natural instruments measure the natural degree and properly describe the mechanisms operating there. The issue is whether those mechanisms behave as though bodily form originates from peripheral tissue, or as though they are the natural expression of a form already interior to them. Phantom limbs, preserved digit organisation, and identity-aligned own-body processing matter because they expose that direction within the natural evidence itself.

### 2.2 Discrete Degrees and the Determinacy Gradient

The architecture of discrete degrees — celestial, spiritual, natural, each complete in itself, each operating by its own laws — has a consequence for causation that *Asking the Skin to Be the Heart* develops at length and that this thesis takes as given. Influx from an interior degree to an exterior one operates **through** the laws of the exterior degree. It does not suspend them. A spiritual cause does not produce a natural effect by exception; it produces it by way of whatever natural mechanism is available.

The implication, which that thesis draws for miracle claims and which this one applies to morphology, is that the *scope* of interior causation at the natural degree is a function of how much latitude the natural degree leaves. Where a natural process is highly determinate — where its outcome is fixed by mechanisms that admit little variation — an interior state has correspondingly little room in which to operate, and the correspondential relation will be expressed in some other, less constrained register. Where a natural process is underdetermined — where multiple outcomes are compatible with the same mechanism, and the mechanism does not by itself select among them — an interior state has room, and the correspondence will be visible there.

This yields what may be called a **determinacy gradient**. Interior form becomes most visible where the natural mechanism permits multiple outcomes and least visible where development is tightly canalised. This does not mean that influx is absent from determinate processes. It means that influx appears there as the stable natural law itself rather than as variation within it. The distinction matters for the present subject because gross morphology and body schema occupy very different positions on this gradient: embryological anatomy is strongly canalised, while neural body representation remains plastic throughout life.

### 2.3 Three Levels of Bodily Representation: Cortical Map, Representational Structure, Form Model

The empirical argument of this thesis depends entirely on a distinction that the neuroscientific literature makes but that summaries of it routinely collapse. Three different things are called "the brain's representation of the body," and they are measured by different methods, live in different anatomy, and behave differently under deafferentation.

**Level 1 — cortical territory.** The somatotopic map in primary somatosensory and primary motor cortex (S1/M1) allocates cortical area to body parts, with area proportional to receptor density and behavioural importance. What is measured is *which body part activates which patch of cortex*. Method: functional imaging or electrophysiology during stimulation or movement of body parts. This is the level at which the classical plasticity literature operates, and the level at which "cortical takeover" or "remapping" claims are made.

**Level 2 — representational structure within a territory.** Within the hand area, the relations among individual digit representations — their relative positions, their degree of overlap, the similarity structure of their multivoxel activation patterns — constitute a fine-grained organisation that is not captured by asking which patch responds to what. Method: high-field imaging with representational similarity analysis. Two territories can have identical gross somatotopy and different internal structure, or, as will be seen, a territory can be functionally reallocated at Level 1 while its Level 2 structure remains intact.

**Level 3 — the form model, or body schema proper.** The sensorimotor representation of the body as a configured whole: its parts, their extents, their relative positions, the postural and kinematic model against which movement is planned and sensation is localised. This is the sense in which Head and Holmes introduced the term and in which the phenomenological tradition after Merleau-Ponty and, more recently, Gallagher has used it. It is standardly attributed to **posterior parietal cortex** — superior parietal lobule, intraparietal sulcus, supramarginal gyrus — together with **premotor cortex**, insula, and the temporo-parietal junction. It is not attributed to S1/M1.

**Body schema must further be distinguished from body image.** Body schema is sensorimotor and largely non-conscious: it governs where a limb is taken to be, how far a reach must extend, which part a touch belongs to. Body image is perceptual, conceptual and affective: how a body is consciously represented, judged and felt about. The two dissociate — deafferented patients can lose schema while retaining image, and body dysmorphic conditions distort image while leaving schema intact. This distinction is load-bearing for Section 7.3, where a serious complication in the trans-specific literature turns on precisely which of the two is being measured.

The following table fixes the terminology used throughout.

| Level | What it is | Anatomy | How measured | Behaviour on limb loss |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Cortical territory | Somatotopic allocation of cortical area | S1 / M1 | fMRI or electrophysiology during body-part movement or stimulation | Reallocated to body parts assuming the lost part's function |
| 2. Representational structure | Fine-grained relations among sub-parts within a territory | Within S1 hand area | High-field fMRI, representational similarity analysis | Preserved for decades, even without any peripheral channel |
| 3. Form model (body schema) | The configured body as a whole, against which action and sensation are referenced | Posterior parietal (SPL, IPS, supramarginal), premotor, insula, TPJ | fMRI of phantom movement; TMS-evoked sensation; MEG evoked response; own-body identification tasks | Present for parts that were never built |

The single most consequential methodological error available in this domain is to take a Level 1 result as bearing on a Level 3 claim. Section 4 documents an instance of exactly that error, examines how it arises, and shows what the study in question actually establishes.

### 2.4 Reading Studies for What They Measured

Every empirical source in this thesis was read for four things before any use was made of it: the **sample** (who, how many, selected how), the **method** (what instrument, what task), the **measure** (what quantity was actually recorded), and the **inference the authors themselves drew**. Where an author's conclusion reaches beyond the measure, that difference is noted. Where a study supplies more than one finding, each is retained rather than selecting only the part that serves the synthesis.

This protocol matters because the body-representation literature operates at several anatomical and conceptual levels that are easy to collapse. Hahamy and colleagues measured which body parts recruit the missing-hand territory; they did not measure phantom sensation. Kikkert and colleagues measured the fine structure of individual phantom digits; they did not measure the parietal form model. Majid and colleagues measured own-body processing after covarying self-similarity ratings; Feusner and colleagues raised the possibility that adjacent findings were reflective rather than reflexive. Each result belongs at the level its method reached.

The same discipline applies to alternative interpretations. Brugger and colleagues' observational-construction account belongs in the case because it appears in their own abstract and addresses the evidence they measured. Makin and colleagues' finding of degeneration in amputees without persistent phantom experience belongs beside their finding of preservation where such experience remains. Ramachandran and McGeoch's 2007 paper is identified as a hypothesis paper because it reports no data. Accuracy here is not an adversarial exercise. It is what allows the natural findings to be placed within the framework without forcing them into roles they do not perform.

## 3. From the Established Framework to the Body-Schema Question

### 3.1 What the Corpus Established: One Law Across Three Moments

*The Surface That Withholds Nothing* argues that a single correspondential law is visible at three separated points in the arc of a human life, and that the three literatures which document those points have never been read together because each is treated within its own discipline as an isolated anomaly.

The **first witness** is the moment of formation. In the Division of Perceptual Studies archive, children who report memories of a previous life bear birthmarks or birth defects attributed to wounds on the previous personality in 309 of 895 cases, or thirty-five percent. Of the two hundred and ten such cases investigated in depth, a medical document — usually a postmortem report — was obtained in forty-nine. In forty-three of those forty-nine, or eighty-eight percent, the correspondence between wound and birthmark was judged satisfactory or better by a criterion requiring both to fall within the same small area at the same anatomical location. Eighteen cases involve two birthmarks corresponding to the entry and exit wounds of a single projectile; in fourteen of these one mark was larger than the other, and in nine of those fourteen the evidence clearly showed the smaller, usually round mark at the entry site and the larger, usually irregular mark at the exit. Stevenson calculated the chance probability of two birthmarks independently matching two wound locations at approximately one in twenty-five thousand six hundred. In thirteen cases the two families had never heard of one another before the case came to light.

The **second witness** is the course of a life. The radical remission literature documents cases in which Stage IV malignancy resolves completely, and in which the resolution follows a documented and datable reorganisation of the person's governing orientation rather than a change in treatment. *The Somatic Influx* and *The Architecture of Anomaly* develop this arm at length, and the argument they make is specifically that the magnitude and timescale exceed what expectation-mediated physiology can deliver, so that the phenomenon requires an account of a different order rather than an extension of placebo mechanisms.

The **third witness** is the end. Terminal lucidity — the return of coherent, oriented, recognisably personal mentation in patients whose cortex has been extensively destroyed by advanced dementia, occurring in the hours or days before death — is a phenomenon for which the production model of consciousness has no available mechanism, since the substrate whose destruction was held to explain the deficit is not restored.

The synthesis these three permit is stated in the predecessor thesis as a claim about the ontological status of the body. The body is not an autonomous foundation on which a mind supervenes. It is what *The Seed-State of the Concrete Spirit* names the **Fixed Edge**: the outermost, most inertial, most resistant expression of a spiritual state, relatively fixed rather than absolutely independent. Its regularity — the reason it appears autonomous, the reason natural science works — is the regularity proper to an ultimate, not the regularity of a self-standing substrate. And a relatively fixed edge moves when what it expresses moves. Birthmark, remission and terminal lucidity are three timescales of one movement.

### 3.2 The Research Gap: Mechanism Without Ontological Direction

The gap addressed here is not a gap in the framework's account of the body. Across its consciousness studies and theological syntheses, the corpus repeatedly states and supports the same relation: the soul is the interior person, the body is that person in ultimates, and change proceeds from the interior into its natural expression. The birthmark, remission, and terminal-lucidity literatures are already evidence for that relation. The present work does not need to re-establish it before asking what follows.

The gap lies instead in contemporary body-schema research. That research has become increasingly precise about *where* bodily representation occurs and *how* it changes. It distinguishes primary somatotopic maps from parietal and premotor systems; identifies multivoxel patterns for individual phantom digits; records representations that survive deafferentation; and maps own-body identification under conditions of bodily incongruence. Yet its conceptual vocabulary remains almost entirely within the natural degree. A representation is described as genetically specified, developmentally laid down, maintained by input, maintained by top-down activity, or reconstructed through observation. These are valuable accounts of mechanism. They leave unasked what the represented form is in relation to the person whose form it is.

That omission becomes visible whenever the measured representation is not accountable from the available body. A phantom of an amputated limb may be called a memory because the limb once supplied input. A phantom of a limb absent from birth cannot be handled that way. A form model that aligns with identity rather than the morphology supplying it with lifelong sensory information presents the same problem more sharply. The science can identify the circuitry, specify the task, and measure the response while still leaving the direction of formation implicit.

The framework supplies the wider relation into which those mechanisms fit. It does not displace them. If bodily form proceeds through discrete degrees, then genetic specification, embryological development, sensorimotor learning, and cortical plasticity are the natural history of that expression. The research question is therefore not whether a spiritual cause can be substituted for a neural one. It is how the neural findings look when the natural mechanism is understood as the ultimate of an already formed person rather than as the producer of that person.

### 3.3 Body Schema as the Continuous Case

The three witnesses make the body-soul relation visible at moments of unusual clarity. A prior wound appears in a forming body; an interior reorganisation is followed by physical healing; an intact person reappears through a profoundly damaged brain. Body schema brings the same relation into ordinary embodiment. Every reach, every localisation of touch, every unreflective knowledge of where one's limbs are, requires an internal organisation of the body as one's own form. The phenomenon is continuous even when the experimental evidence becomes most legible through amputation, congenital absence, or incongruence.

Four bodies of evidence develop the case.

1. **Functional remapping** shows that primary cortical territory belongs to an operation rather than permanently to an anatomical part. The natural map is plastic and use-dependent.
2. **Representational persistence** shows that the fine organisation of a missing hand can remain for decades without the hand and even without a peripheral route from the residual arm to the central nervous system.
3. **Aplasic phantoms** show that the form model can include limbs the physical body never built, and locate that model in premotor and parietal cortex rather than in the primary map.
4. **Schema–morphology disagreement** shows that own-body processing can align with identity rather than birth-assigned morphology, and that the difference appears in regions implicated in bodily ownership and form.

Taken together, these findings describe a progression from the outermost and most labile representation to the most interior and persistent one. The primary map reorganises with use. The fine structure of the map survives the loss of its input. The parietal form model can precede the morphology altogether. And the experienced body can remain organised around a form that the gross morphology does not express.

This is why body schema is properly called a fourth witness. It is not a fourth anomaly placed beside three others. It is the continuous natural mechanism through which the principle visible in the first three could operate in an embodied life. The DOPS cases show the soul's history entering bodily formation. Remission shows a changed interior state entering bodily reorganisation. Terminal lucidity shows the person exceeding the damaged neural expression. Body schema shows the intermediate architecture: how the person is organised as a bodily form before and beneath the moment-to-moment traffic arriving from the body's surface.

The sections that follow do not attempt to make neuroscience prove this ontology. They establish the natural findings accurately and then place them within an ontology already supported elsewhere in the corpus. The force of the case lies in convergence: evidence gathered in unrelated fields, at different moments of life and with different methods, repeatedly orders itself in the same direction — interior form first, natural expression following.

## 4. The Level Confusion: What Cortical Remapping Actually Measures

### 4.1 The Hahamy Finding in Its Own Terms

The most frequently cited recent result bearing on cortical organisation after limb absence is Hahamy and colleagues' 2017 study of congenital one-handers, published in *Current Biology*. It is a careful, well-designed, multi-modal study, and its findings are not in dispute here. What is at issue is what it measured.

The sample comprised seventeen individuals with congenital unilateral upper-limb deficit, sixteen of whom were scanned, recruited through Oxford and Opcare, against twenty-four controls of whom twenty-two were scanned. The design combined four measures: video-recorded compensatory behaviour across five ecological everyday tasks; task-based functional imaging during movement of the residual arm, lips, feet and intact hand; resting-state functional connectivity; and magnetic resonance spectroscopy of GABA concentration.

The central result is that in congenital one-handers the cortical territory that would ordinarily represent the missing hand is activated by **multiple** other body parts — the residual arm, the lips, and the feet — and that this activation is not restricted to the cortical neighbours of the hand area. Foot representation is not adjacent to hand representation in the somatotopic map, so a simple account in which neighbouring representations expand into vacated territory does not fit. GABA concentration in the deprived territory was reduced, consistent with disinhibition permitting the reallocation.

The authors' own interpretation is careful and is worth quoting exactly, because it is more interesting than the summary version of it. They propose that "the typical hand territory may not necessarily represent the hand per se, but rather any other body part that shares the functionality of the missing hand." The organising principle they infer is not *anatomical adjacency* but *functional role*. Cortical territory follows what a body part is used for. In congenital one-handers, lips and feet take over the tasks a second hand would perform, and cortical territory follows the task.

This is a substantial and genuinely surprising finding about Level 1 in the taxonomy of Section 2.3. It says that somatotopic allocation of cortical area is organised by function rather than by body-part identity.

### 4.2 What the Study Did Not Measure

The study is routinely invoked, in secondary discussion, as evidence that congenital one-handers have no phantom and therefore no persistent representation of the missing hand — and thence as evidence against any claim that a body model exists independently of the body.

Read directly, the paper does not support that use, for a straightforward reason. **It contains no measure of phantom experience.** The phrase "phantom sensation" does not appear in the text. Neither does "telescoping." No phantom questionnaire was administered, no structured interview about phantom experience was conducted, and phantom presence or absence is not among the variables reported in the results.

The paper's sole reference to phantom phenomena is a single clause in the discussion, which reads in full: "Because congenital one-handers show lip remapping but do not experience phantom pain, our results provide a counter-example to the maladaptive plasticity theory of phantom pain." Three things about this sentence bear noting. First, its subject is **pain**, not sensation — phantom pain and phantom sensation are distinct phenomena with different prevalences and, on the evidence of the very literature this study engages, different relations to cortical organisation. Second, the absence of phantom pain in this population is not a measurement made in this study; it is asserted from prior literature, as a background premise. Third, the clause is deployed *against* the maladaptive plasticity theory — that is, against the view that cortical reorganisation causes phantom pain — and not in support of any claim about representation.

The authors' broader positioning reinforces the point. They cite Makin and colleagues' 2013 work and Kikkert and colleagues' 2016 work as "recent evidence showing typical somatotopy in amputees," and call for the maladaptive plasticity theory to be reconsidered in light of it. Hahamy and colleagues are, in other words, aligned with rather than opposed to the persistence literature examined in Section 5. Their finding sits alongside it, at a different level of the system.

**Critical Finding.** *The most-cited recent evidence for cortical reorganisation after limb absence measures which body parts occupy a cortical territory. It does not measure whether a representation of the missing part persists, and its authors explicitly endorse the studies that find such persistence.*

### 4.3 Functional Reallocation Is Not Representational Erasure

The apparent tension between "the hand territory is taken over" and "the hand representation persists" dissolves once the levels are separated, and the dissolution is not a rescue manoeuvre but the reading the primary authors themselves adopt.

Consider what the Hahamy result actually asserts. Cortical territory is allocated according to functional role. A region of cortex is not labelled *hand*; it is labelled with the operation a hand performs, and whatever body part performs that operation recruits it. On this account, the finding that lips and feet activate the missing-hand territory in congenital one-handers is not a statement about the hand at all. It is a statement about how cortical real estate is assigned.

Territory occupancy and representational content are different quantities. A cortical patch may be driven by the lips while retaining, within its fine structure, an organisation whose geometry is that of a hand — because the two properties are measured by different means and are not logically coupled. Section 5 shows that this is not merely conceivable but is what is observed: in long-term amputees the fine representational structure of the missing hand is preserved in detail while, at the coarse level, the territory is subject to exactly the reallocation Hahamy describes.

There is a second reason the Hahamy sample cannot bear the weight often placed on it. Congenital one-handers have an intact contralateral hand. Whatever a second hand would contribute to the form model, its functional role in daily life is substantially absorbed by the remaining hand and by the compensatory strategies the study documents. The population in which the question "does a form model exist for a part that was never built?" arises in its sharpest form is not unilateral limb deficiency but **tetra-amelia** — absence of all four limb segments, with no contralateral compensation available. That case is examined in Section 6, and it gives the opposite answer.

Finally, it is worth stating plainly what has just been done and why, because the manoeuvre being corrected is common enough to have a shape. A study measuring cortical territory occupancy was recruited into service as a counter-datum against a claim about body representation, on the strength of a single background clause about phantom *pain* that the study did not measure. This is structurally identical to the dismissal of near-death experience data by appeal to hypoxia: a finding from an adjacent level of the system, stripped of what it actually measured, deployed for the authority its citation confers rather than for its content. The corrective is not to argue with it but to read it. Read, it says something true, interesting, and about something else.

## 5. Persistence Without Input: The Preserved Fine Structure

### 5.1 Digit Topography Decades After Amputation

The textbook account of what happens to a cortical representation when its input is removed is stated with unusual clarity in the plain-language digest accompanying Kikkert and colleagues' 2016 paper in *eLife*: textbooks "typically state that the brain will 'overwrite' its representation of a body part if input from that area no longer arrives."

That study tested the claim at a resolution which had not previously been available. Using seven-tesla functional imaging, the investigators scanned two upper-limb amputees — one twenty-five years and one thirty-one years post-amputation — together with eleven controls. Participants performed cued movements of individual digits: in the amputees, individual *phantom* digits. The analysis did not merely ask whether a digit activated the hand area. It computed the **representational similarity structure** of the inter-digit activation patterns: the pattern of overlap and distinctness among the five digit representations, which constitutes a signature of hand organisation far more specific than gross somatotopy.

The result is that the missing hand's individual finger topography persists in primary somatosensory cortex decades after the hand itself is gone. Not a residual smear of activity in the vicinity: a preserved, digit-specific, individually resolvable organisation with the inter-digit relational structure characteristic of a hand. The authors' own statement of the implication is in the abstract: "By demonstrating stable topography despite amputation, our finding questions the extent to which continued sensory input is necessary to maintain organisation in sensory cortex."

This is a Level 2 finding in the taxonomy of Section 2.3, and it is worth being explicit that it does not contradict the Level 1 findings of Section 4. Both can be, and are, true. The territory can be recruited by other body parts while the fine structure within it retains the geometry of the absent hand.

### 5.2 The Brachial Plexus Avulsion Case

The two long-term amputees leave one avenue open to an input-maintained account. An amputation stump retains nerve endings; neuromas form; residual peripheral traffic reaches the cortex. A sufficiently determined bottom-up account could hold that this residual traffic, however degraded, is what maintains the organisation.

The third case in the same study closes that avenue. A further participant, also thirty-one years post-amputation, had sustained a **brachial plexus avulsion** — the nerve roots torn from the spinal cord — such that communication between the residual arm and the central nervous system was abolished. There is no peripheral channel. Whatever maintains a cortical hand organisation in this individual, it is not input from the arm, because there is no route by which input from the arm could arrive.

Scanned at three tesla, this participant showed digit preference in the postcentral gyrus and in the central sulcus. The inter-digit overlap structure was typical. The correlation of the participant's inter-digit representational similarity structure with that of controls had a mean Spearman coefficient of **0.76, with a 95% confidence interval from 0.30 to 0.92**.

A hand organisation, thirty-one years after the hand was removed, in a cortex that has had no communication with the arm for the same period, whose fine structure correlates with that of intact controls at 0.76.

Both of the seven-tesla amputees were selected for "exceptionally vivid kinaesthetic phantom sensations," and this selection criterion is stated by the authors. It matters, and Section 5.3 takes it up rather than leaving it to be discovered as an objection.

### 5.3 Preservation Tracks Experience, Not Periphery

The selection criterion connects Kikkert's finding to Makin and colleagues' 2013 study in *Nature Communications*, which examined the same question with a different design and a larger sample, comparing amputees against both two-handed controls and congenital one-handed controls.

Makin's finding has two parts, and the second is the important one. The first part is the expected one: input loss is generally associated with structural and functional degeneration of the deprived region. The second is that this generalisation has a systematic exception. In amputees with **persistent phantom pain**, the former hand area showed *preserved* structure and function rather than degeneration. The correlations reported are substantial: activation with phantom pain at r(15) = 0.67, p = 0.003; grey matter volume at r(16) = 0.60, p = 0.009; inter-regional connectivity at r(16) = −0.62, p = 0.006.

The authors' own interpretation is that plasticity in this region is "driven by powerful and long-lasting subjective sensory experience, such as triggered by nociceptive or top–down inputs."

The phrase is worth dwelling on, because it states, in the authors' own voice and on their own data, the point this section is making. What maintains the cortical representation of the hand is not the hand. It is the **experience** of the hand — an experience which, in the population studied, has no peripheral source. The authors name two candidate drivers: nociceptive input and top-down input. The avulsion case removes the first. Whatever is left is, in the authors' own terminology, top-down: something arriving at the representation from a level above it rather than from the body below it.

This convergence should be registered accurately. Neither Makin nor Kikkert is proposing anything about consciousness, spiritual causation, or received form. Both are making a claim internal to sensory neuroscience: that the maintenance of cortical body representation is less dependent on peripheral input than the field assumed, and more dependent on experience. That claim is theirs, is measured, and is stated in their abstracts. What this thesis does with it is a further step, and Section 9.4 is explicit about how large that step is.

### 5.4 Degeneration and the State of Natural Expression

The first part of the Makin result is equally important. Input loss generally produces degeneration: grey matter volume in the deprived region declines, functional response declines, and connectivity patterns alter. Makin's data reproduce that pattern. Preservation is conditional, appearing in the subgroup with persistent vivid phantom experience.

This distinguishes the soul's form from its current cortical expression. A natural neural pattern is living tissue and remains subject to use, disuse, injury, and plasticity. The framework does not require every neural trace to remain intact whenever the soul retains the capacity it once expressed through that trace. Terminal lucidity already depends on this distinction: the natural structure can cease to carry a capacity continuously without the person losing that capacity absolutely.

Makin's result shows that ongoing **experience** is associated with preservation of the cortical expression. Where the missing hand remains vividly present, its former territory retains more of its structure and function; where that experience is absent, the territory degenerates and reorganises. This is not indifference of the interior form to the brain. It is influx operating through a natural organ whose state changes according to activity.

The study does not establish whether the unexpressed form remains present when conscious phantom experience is absent, because it measures cortex rather than the person beyond that cortical expression. That question requires comparison at the parietal form-model level and, in congenital cases, between people with and without reported phantoms.

Everything in Section 5 also concerns a body part that **once existed**. The amputation findings establish persistence without continuing peripheral maintenance, but prior sensory and motor development remain part of each participant's natural history. Section 6 therefore turns to the stronger case for prior bodily organisation: a person whose represented limbs were never physically built.

## 6. Representation Without Morphology: The Aplasic Phantom

### 6.1 The Case of A.Z.

In 2000, Brugger and colleagues published in the *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences* a study whose title states its subject exactly: "Beyond re-membering: phantom sensations of congenitally absent limbs." The participant, designated A.Z., was a forty-four-year-old university-educated woman born without forearms and without lower legs. The cause of the aplasia was unknown; she had two healthy sisters and no relevant family history. She had pronounced spinal scoliosis. She had never possessed the limb segments in question, and there was therefore no period of her life during which peripheral input from them could have shaped a representation.

A.Z. experienced vivid phantoms of hands and feet.

The first stage of the investigation established that these were not merely reported beliefs. Behavioural testing of visuokinesthetic integration — tasks in which the phantom's position and movement must be integrated with visual information in a manner that constrains performance measurably — demonstrated an **intact somatic representation** of hands and feet. The phantoms had the functional properties of limb representations, not the properties of imagination or of verbal report.

That result alone reverses the expected direction of dependence. On any account in which the internal model of the body is assembled from the body's sensory traffic, a representation of a forearm that never existed has no material from which to be assembled.

### 6.2 Where the Representation Lives

The imaging and stimulation results are the load-bearing part of the study, and they answer the anatomical question posed in Section 2.3 clause 3.

Functional imaging during phantom hand movements showed **no activation of primary sensorimotor areas**. The activation was in **premotor and parietal cortex, bilaterally**.

Movements of A.Z.'s existing upper arms did expand into the deprived hand territories — reproducing, in a congenital case, exactly the Level 1 reallocation that Section 4 described. The primary map behaved as the plasticity literature predicts. The phantom was not there.

Transcranial magnetic stimulation completed the dissociation. Stimulation of sensorimotor cortex evoked phantom sensations in the contralateral fingers and hand. But stimulation of **premotor and parietal cortex also evoked similar phantom sensations, and did so without motor evoked potentials in the stump** — that is, without engaging the descending motor pathway at all. The phantom could be elicited from the form-model territory directly, independently of any sensorimotor route.

**Critical Finding.** *A limb that was never built is represented in premotor and parietal cortex, is absent from primary sensorimotor cortex, can be evoked by direct stimulation of the parietal and premotor sites without peripheral engagement, and coexists with ordinary functional reallocation of the primary map to other body parts.*

This is the anatomical result on which the thesis turns, and it should be seen for what it does to the level structure. The three levels of Section 2.3 are not a taxonomy imposed for convenience. In a single participant, in a single study, they dissociate: Level 1 behaves as the plasticity literature says it should, and Level 3 carries a limb the body never had. The question "does the brain represent a body part that does not exist?" has different answers depending on which level is interrogated, and the level that answers yes is the level the neuroscientific literature independently identifies with body schema.

It also settles, for the case in hand, what Section 4 argued on general grounds. Level 1 reallocation cannot be used as evidence against a persisting body representation, because here the two are observed in the same brain at the same time.

### 6.3 Base Rates and the Question of Universality

The single case must be placed against a population estimate. A.Z. establishes that a somatic representation of never-built limbs is possible and anatomically measurable; prevalence data are needed to know how commonly that representation becomes conscious as a phantom.

Melzack, Israel, Lacroix and Schultz reported in *Brain* in 1997 on one hundred and twenty-five people with missing limbs. Forty-one experienced phantoms: fifteen of them born limb-deficient and twenty-six who had undergone amputation before the age of six. The rates the authors report are "at least 20% of congenitally limb-deficient subjects and 50% of subjects who underwent amputations before the age of 6." Phantoms were painful in twenty percent of the congenital group who had them, and in forty-two percent of the young-amputee group. The authors' conclusion is that the data provide evidence for "a distributed neural representation of the body that is in part genetically determined."

Twenty percent is the frequency of **reported conscious phantom experience**, not a measurement of body-schema representation in the remaining eighty percent. The distinction is load-bearing because body schema is ordinarily non-conscious. Brugger established A.Z.'s representation through constrained behaviour, imaging, and stimulation rather than report alone. No comparable study has examined congenitally limb-deficient participants who report no phantom. The prevalence of underlying form-model organisation in that population is therefore unknown.

Saadah and Melzack's 1994 report in *Cortex* gives a reason to separate model from phenomenal availability. In three of four congenitally limb-deficient adults, full-length phantoms emerged only *after* a minor stump injury or surgery — at ages sixteen, twenty-six, and thirty. The fourth, born with a shortened and deformed right leg whose foot was amputated at age three, developed a full-length phantom leg and foot at age six, which persisted for more than twenty years. The later bodily event supplied no new information about the structure of a full limb. It changed whether an already organised possibility became consciously available.

The evidence does not show that every congenitally limb-deficient person carries a complete latent phantom. It shows that conscious report cannot be treated as a direct census of underlying bodily form. Determining prevalence requires the study proposed in Section 9.5: body-schema-level imaging and behavioural testing in congenital participants both with and without phantom experience.

### 6.4 Observation as a Natural Mechanism

Brugger and colleagues explicitly identify a developmental contribution to aplasic phantoms: "Both genetic and epigenetic factors, such as the habitual observation of other people moving their limbs, may contribute to the conscious experience of aplasic phantoms." That wording should be preserved. The authors say *contribute to the conscious experience*, not that observation has been shown to construct the underlying body form.

The proposed mechanism is plausible. Human beings are surrounded from birth by other human bodies. Watching them act repeatedly engages premotor and parietal circuitry, the same broad system in which A.Z.'s phantom was found. Observation can therefore provide rich natural material for motor repertoire, the visual appearance of limbs, their ordinary ranges of movement, and their relation to objects and space.

Within the framework, this is exactly the kind of developmental route through which a prior capacity becomes articulated. A soul does not enter natural embodiment carrying explicit cortical movement programmes. The natural mind acquires language, gesture, skill, and culturally available forms through experience. Observation can teach the natural expression of a hand without originating the human organisation that makes the observed hand intelligible as a possible part of one's own form.

The existing data do not specify where contribution becomes construction. A.Z.'s premotor-parietal activation is compatible with observation shaping the content and phenomenal availability of her phantoms. It does not show that watching others generated the first-person ownership of never-built limbs. The distinction is especially visible in the Saadah and Melzack cases: lifelong observation was available from infancy, while full-length phantoms became conscious only after a local injury or operation decades later. Observation supplied material continuously; another process governed when that material became one's own bodily experience.

The research task is consequently to resolve the mechanism rather than choose between observation and influx. Studies can ask whether aplasic phantom geometry tracks the bodies most frequently observed, whether action-observation networks differ between congenital participants with and without phantoms, and whether an injury changes connectivity or gating in an already organised parietal–premotor model. Each outcome would clarify how the natural degree receives and develops bodily form.

## 7. The Fourth Witness: Schema–Morphology Disagreement

### 7.1 Own-Body Processing Aligned to Identity

Sections 5 and 6 concern bodily form where the corresponding part is absent. Schema–morphology incongruence adds a different configuration: a fully developed morphology and an own-body form are both present and **disagree**. This makes it possible to observe how lifelong peripheral input, gross anatomy, identity, and bodily self-processing relate when they do not ordinarily converge on one form.

The relevant study is Majid, Burke, Manzouri, Moody, Dhejne, Feusner and Savic's 2020 paper in *Cerebral Cortex*, whose title states the finding: "Neural Systems for Own-body Processing Align with Gender Identity Rather Than Birth-assigned Sex."

The design is the largest in the present evidence set: **thirty transgender and thirty cisgender participants**. Under functional imaging, participants performed a body self-identification task in which images of their own body were morphed in sex — toward and away from their birth-assigned sex — and rated for the degree to which they identified with each image.

The behavioural result is that cisgender controls identified with images of themselves, while transgender participants identified with images morphed **"opposite" to their birth-assigned sex**.

The imaging result is the one that matters, and its analytic detail is what gives it force. Reported in the authors' own words: "After covarying out the effect of self-similarity ratings, both groups activated similar self- and body-processing systems when viewing bodies that aligned with their gender identity rather than birth-assigned sex."

The covariation is the methodological crux. A sceptic of any conclusion drawn from a self-identification task would observe that ratings of identification are conscious judgments and can be produced by belief, self-concept, or the demand characteristics of the task. Covarying out the self-similarity ratings removes exactly that. What remains is the response of the own-body processing system itself, with the conscious rating statistically partialled out. And what remains shows the same systems, in both groups, engaging with the body that matches the identity rather than the body that matches the morphology.

The additional finding — that transgender participants showed greater limbic involvement for androgynous images morphed toward gender identity — is reported here for completeness. It is consistent with a salience difference and does not bear directly on the structural claim.

### 7.2 The Supramarginal Locus

The Majid result establishes a functional dissociation between own-body processing and morphology. It does not, by itself, locate that dissociation in body-schema territory rather than in higher-order self-representation. The anatomical question is addressed by Case, Brang, Landazuri, Viswanathan and Ramachandran's 2017 study in *Archives of Sexual Behavior*.

The sample is small and must be flagged as such: **eight** pre-surgical transgender men against **eight** non-transgender women. The method is magnetoencephalography, recording the somatosensory evoked response to stimulation of the chest — a body region experienced as incongruent — compared against the hand, a congruent region serving as a within-subject control. Diffusion tensor imaging was also acquired.

The result for chest stimulation is **significantly reduced activation in the supramarginal gyrus and secondary somatosensory cortex, with increased activation at the temporal pole**. The structural findings were increased white matter coherence in the supramarginal gyrus and temporal pole, and decreased diffusivity in the anterior insula and temporal pole.

The **supramarginal gyrus is inferior parietal cortex**. It is one of the regions standardly attributed to body schema, and it is within the same parietal territory in which Brugger's aplasic phantom was localised and from which phantom sensation could be evoked by direct stimulation.

The convergence is worth stating precisely. A body part that is present, innervated, and delivering ordinary peripheral input produces an altered response — not in primary somatosensory cortex, where the input arrives, but in the parietal region that models the body as a form. The signal reaches the primary map normally; it is at the level of the form model that something differs.

The anterior insula finding is of a piece with this. The insula is centrally implicated in interoception and in the sense of bodily ownership, and it is among the regions on which the body-schema literature converges.

**Critical Finding.** *Where a fully developed morphology and a form model disagree, the measurable difference appears at the form-model level — supramarginal gyrus, secondary somatosensory cortex, anterior insula — and not at the primary sensory level where the peripheral input arrives.*

### 7.3 Locating the Level: Reflective and Pre-Reflective Measures

The trans-specific studies do not all measure the same level of bodily processing. Distinguishing those levels is necessary before the findings can be placed within the three-part architecture developed here.

Feusner and colleagues published in 2016 in *Archives of Sexual Behavior* a study of sixteen pre-hormone transgender men against twenty female and twenty male controls, using an own-body morph identification task together with an inverted-faces task. The behavioural result supports the pattern: higher self-identification for bodies morphed to the sex congruent with gender identity, holding across a broad range of morph percentages and more pronounced at longer viewing durations.

The authors' own caveat, however, is explicit and must be carried rather than dropped: "This is more likely accounted for by conscious, cognitive factors than perceptual differences."

A second study from the same group, published in 2017 in *Brain Imaging and Behavior*, examined resting-state functional connectivity in twenty-seven transgender men against twenty-seven male and twenty-seven female controls. It found decreased connectivity of the anterior and posterior cingulate and the precuneus within the default mode network. The authors' interpretation is that "perception of bodies in relation to self may be reflective rather than reflexive, as a function of mesial prefrontal processes."

Taken at face value, these findings locate own-body image partly at a reflective level. Body schema is sensorimotor and largely non-conscious; body image is perceptual, conceptual, and evaluative. The Feusner studies therefore do not by themselves establish that identity-linked bodily form extends into pre-reflective sensorimotor organisation. Their samples are substantial, and this limit belongs in the account.

The Case 2017 result reaches a different level. It is not a self-identification rating but a somatosensory evoked response to physical stimulation of the chest, recorded by magnetoencephalography against a within-subject congruent control region. A reflective account of morphed-image judgments does not by itself explain reduced supramarginal response to touch. Yet the Case sample is eight participants per group, so the anatomical result requires replication.

The phrase "reflective rather than reflexive" is also an interpretation of resting-state connectivity, not a direct measurement of the temporal or pre-conscious character of body perception. The evidence presently establishes multiple levels: explicit own-body identification, identity-aligned activation after self-similarity ratings are covaried out, and an altered secondary somatosensory response to touch. Direct measures of proprioception, bodily ownership, peripersonal space, and motor organisation are still needed to determine how these levels join.

### 7.4 What Schema–Morphology Disagreement Adds

Subject to the measurement boundary in Section 7.3, this evidence contributes something the amputation and aplasia literatures cannot. Those literatures concern **absence**: a bodily form persists or appears where the corresponding tissue is missing. Here the morphology and the person's own-body organisation are both present, allowing their relation to be observed when they do not coincide.

**First, the two natural expressions develop through different mechanisms.** Gross sexual morphology follows strongly canalised chromosomal, gonadal, hormonal, and embryological pathways. Own-body processing develops through multisensory integration, bodily ownership, self-recognition, and parietal–insular networks. Their divergence is therefore intelligible within discrete degrees: the same person can reach natural expression through mechanisms with different degrees of fixity.

**Second, lifelong peripheral input does not force complete alignment.** The morphology has supplied visual, tactile, proprioceptive, and interoceptive information throughout development. Yet own-body processing aligns with identity in the Majid task, and touch to an incongruent region produces differences beyond the primary sensory map in Case's study. The natural system registers both the physical input and its relation to the person's bodily selfhood.

**Third, observation supplies available forms but does not explain personal selection.** Canonical bodies of both sexes are visible in the social environment. That can contribute imagery, movement, and conceptual content. It does not by itself explain why one form is stably organised as *my body* for one person and not another, or why that organisation can remain continuous despite lifelong contrary morphology.

The contribution is therefore precise. A complete morphology and an identity-linked own-body form can disagree; the disagreement is measurable; and at least part of it appears in parietal, secondary somatosensory, and insular systems concerned with bodily form and ownership rather than only in explicit declaration. In the framework's terms, the case shows how a soul's bodily selfhood can remain present within natural embodiment even where one of the most determinate layers of that embodiment does not express it.

## 8. From Soul to Body: Form Across Discrete Degrees

### 8.1 The Body Follows the Soul

The evidence can now be returned to the framework from which the question arose. The governing claim is not that the body symbolises the soul, nor that a soul sends instructions across a divide to a separate body. It is that the body **is the soul in ultimates**. The person exists first as an interior organisation of love and understanding and reaches bodily expression through discrete degrees. The soul does not follow the contours supplied by matter. Matter is the outermost degree at which the soul's form becomes fixed, extended, and available to the shared natural world.

The corpus has already established this direction through several independent lines. In the DOPS cases, the biography of a previous life — including the exact situation of a fatal wound — precedes and enters the formation of a new body. In spontaneous remission, an alteration of the person's interior orientation precedes physical reorganisation in 85.5 percent of cases with clear temporal ordering. In terminal lucidity, the intact person reappears through a neural structure incapable, on a production model, of rebuilding what has been lost. The point of bringing these findings together is not that each one separately proves an invisible cause. It is that they disclose the same directional order at formation, during embodied life, and at its end.

Body schema gives that order an ordinary mechanism. A person does not wait for a stream of peripheral signals to become a bodily self. Sensory traffic continuously informs and adjusts embodiment, but it enters an organisation already capable of receiving a hand *as a hand*, locating it within a whole, and using it toward an intended action. The organisation is prior in function even when its natural expression develops over time. This is the same relation the corpus describes elsewhere: the soul receives influx according to its form, the spiritual degree articulates what is received, and the natural degree brings that articulation into effect.

The Hahamy finding becomes especially clear in this light. Cortical territory follows the *office* performed rather than anatomical adjacency: lips and feet recruit the missing-hand territory when they perform the hand's work. This is not primarily a lesson in textual correspondence. It is the natural nervous system behaving according to the same functional order by which the framework understands creation. Form is organised by use. Anatomy serves the use; the use is not produced by the anatomy assigned to it.

### 8.2 Morphogenetic Determinacy Versus Representational Latitude

The empirical content of the limit is a comparison between two developmental processes, and the comparison is not close.

**Sexual morphogenesis is among the most determinate processes in vertebrate development.** The pathway is well characterised: a chromosomal signal initiates gonadal differentiation, gonadal hormones drive differentiation of internal ducts and external genitalia within defined critical windows, and the resulting morphology is canalised — buffered against perturbation, redundantly specified, and producing one of a small number of outcomes with high reliability. Where the pathway is disrupted, the disruptions themselves are characterised, discrete, and traceable to identifiable molecular causes. This is a mechanism with very little latitude in it. There is no free parameter for anything else to act through.

**Body schema development is, by contrast, among the least determinate processes in neural development.** The relevant literature is unambiguous about this. Cortical representations of the body are shaped by use over extended periods. They incorporate tools into the reach representation within minutes of tool use. They are altered by expertise, by training, by immobilisation, and by illusory manipulations of ownership in the laboratory. Peripersonal space boundaries shift with context. Multisensory integration weights are recalibrated continuously. This is a mechanism defined by its plasticity — a process whose outcome the mechanism does not by itself determine.

Across discrete degrees, both processes remain expressions of the same person, but they do not have equal latitude. The soul does not bypass chromosomal and hormonal development to sculpt gross anatomy directly. Its natural expression occurs through those mechanisms, and their canalisation constrains the form that can appear at that degree. Neural body representation remains far more plastic. It can therefore carry aspects of the person's interior form that gross morphology does not express.

The birthmark evidence displays the same gradient. Every documented DOPS correspondence is a **lesion**: a wound, puncture, burn, surgical incision, or mark. The archive does not show wholesale replacement of limb plan, facial architecture, or skeletal configuration. Pigmentation and local tissue development permit variation that gross anatomical plan does not. What the soul carries becomes natural form where the natural process has room to receive it.

### 8.3 How Bodily Form Is Held in the Soul

To say that the body follows the soul immediately raises the question the broad formula can obscure: **what is it, in a soul, that has bodily form?** The answer cannot be a miniature anatomical double stored behind the visible body. That would recreate the dualism the framework rejects — two bodies, one hidden and one visible, with an unexplained channel between them. Nor can it be a detached visual image. Body schema operates before and beneath visual reflection, and people born without a limb can experience its position and movement without ever having seen it as their own.

The corpus supplies a different account. The soul is a **formed recipient of life**. Its form is the organisation by which love and wisdom can be received as this particular person. At the broadest level, this is the human form itself: the complete organisation of capacities and uses through which divine life can become finite human life. At the individual level, the form is the person's accumulated history of states, choices, loves, relations, and recognitions. *The Seed-State of the Concrete Spirit* calls this a person's spiritual geometry: biography is the form and ruling love is its organising substance. The soul is not contained by a spiritual skin. It is held together by the coherence of what it has become.

This form is bodily without being anatomical in the natural sense. Love is not shapeless. It orders attention, nearness, action, relation, and use. Understanding differentiates that order into distinguishable capacities. In the spiritual degree, where state determines proximity and affection determines association, the person's organization is already lived as space, relation, orientation, and movement. The human form is what that ordered life looks like when all its functions are present together. This is why the corpus's account of post-mortem experience does not describe a disembodied point of awareness. The soul remains a person in human form, with a body experienced as solid and complete, because human form belonged to the person before it belonged to flesh.

Within embodied life, this prior form has to be translated into the conditions of the natural degree. An intention to reach is whole before it is a sequence of muscle contractions. At the celestial degree it belongs to will: the end sought. At the spiritual degree it is articulated as a relation among self, limb, object, distance, and possible movement. At the natural degree it appears as premotor planning, parietal integration, motor command, proprioceptive correction, and muscular action. These are not separate events connected from outside. They are one act unfolding through degrees until it reaches its ultimate.

Body schema belongs at the point where the spiritual articulation of bodily selfhood becomes usable at the natural degree. It is not the soul itself. It is not a secret second anatomy. It is the nervous system's expression of the soul as a located, extended, acting person. This is why it can be continuously revised by sensory experience without being constituted by that experience. Sensation informs the expression; it does not originate the person whose sensation it is.

The evidence can now be read as four different ways this relation becomes visible within a soul's embodied life.

**In an amputee**, the physical ultimate has lost a part while the person's formed organisation still includes it. The primary map adapts to present use, but the fine structure and experienced limb can persist because the natural loss does not erase the interior form.

**In congenital limb absence**, the natural morphology never completes one part of the ordinary human plan, yet the person's form can still include the absent hand or foot. The aplasic phantom is then not a memory of tissue but the natural availability of a form that belongs to the person at a more interior degree.

**In schema–morphology incongruence**, the morphology follows one highly canalised natural pathway while the person's bodily selfhood carries another form. The body schema is where that difference can remain present because it is the more plastic, more interiorly responsive part of natural embodiment. On this account, the incongruence is neither an intellectual opinion about the body nor a failure to perceive it accurately. It is the experience of two degrees of one's own embodiment failing to coincide.

**In restorative incarnation**, the soul carries not only a generic human organization but a biography. A violent wound belongs to that history and therefore to the soul's formed state. When another natural body forms, the retained state can enter its most receptive local processes and appear as a mark. The birthmark and the congenital phantom are different expressions of the same deeper fact: natural embodiment does not begin with a blank, formless recipient.

These are not four separate kinds of soul-body relation. They are four conditions under which the same relation becomes legible: the soul has a form, that form includes the person's bodily selfhood, and the natural body receives it according to the possibilities and constraints of its own degree.

### 8.4 Body Schema as Natural-Degree Articulation

The three-level neural architecture can now be placed within this account without turning it into a symbolic code.

| Natural organisation | Place within the soul-to-body unfolding | Observed behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| Primary sensory and motor map | The body's immediate contact with the natural environment: incoming sensation and outgoing movement | Most labile; reallocated according to use and functional substitution |
| Fine representational structure | The stable differentiation of bodily capacities within the natural mind | Persists for decades without ordinary input and survives loss of the peripheral channel |
| Parietal–premotor body schema | The natural articulation of the person as a configured, located, acting human form | Can include absent and never-built parts; can differ from gross morphology |
| Gross morphology | The fixed edge at which the person enters inherited biological order and shared extension | Highly constrained by chromosomal, hormonal, embryological, and mechanical law |

The ordering matters. Moving inward from primary map to form model, dependence on present peripheral input decreases while stability of bodily organisation increases. Moving outward toward morphology, natural fixity increases while direct flexibility to interior state decreases. Body schema occupies the hinge: natural enough to be measured and modified, interior enough to retain the person's form when tissue is absent or incongruent.

This also clarifies what an "early-laid" neural representation means within the framework. Genetic and epigenetic specification are not explanations that compete with the soul. They describe the natural degree of the laying down. The same applies to observational learning. Watching other bodies may supply natural material through which an aplasic form becomes explicit; it does not by itself explain why the observer can organise those movements as possibilities of *her own* body, nor why a particular form becomes stable as self. Natural development describes the route. The framework addresses the person whose route it is.

The broader perspective and the individual one therefore meet at the same point. Cosmologically, discrete degrees describe how every natural form comes into being from an interior cause. Within one soul, they describe how love becomes intention, intention becomes an organised bodily possibility, and that possibility becomes movement and tissue. Body schema is where this universal direction becomes intimate: the place in natural embodiment where a person continuously knows, before reflection, the form from which she acts.

## 9. Discussion

### 9.1 A Continuous Expression Made Visible

The corpus's earlier evidence makes the body-soul relation most visible where ordinary embodiment is interrupted or reorganised. Birthmarks, radical remission, and terminal lucidity are rare, but the relation they disclose is not. If the body is the soul in ultimates, then every ordinary act of bodily ownership and movement is already an instance of interior form becoming natural effect.

Body-schema research makes that continuous relation empirically available. The field does not begin with the framework's ontology. It begins with missing hands, cortical maps, ownership illusions, and the practical need to understand how a person controls a body. Yet the architecture it uncovers is ordered from outer malleability toward inner persistence. The territory receiving present input changes with use. The differentiated representation of a missing hand can remain without that input. The higher form model can contain a limb the body never supplied at all.

The fourth witness therefore differs from the preceding three. It is not another exceptional event. It is the natural organisation required whenever a soul acts through a body. Amputation, aplasia, and incongruence reveal it because they separate levels that usually coincide. In ordinary embodiment the morphology, primary maps, representational structure, experienced form, and personal identity align so completely that their distinctness is invisible. The exceptional case does not create the architecture; it lets the architecture be seen.

### 9.2 What Body-Schema Research Adds

The neuroscience adds four forms of resolution to the corpus's broader claim.

**From bodily outcome to bodily organisation.** The earlier evidence showed interior state expressed as physical mark, healing, or restored function. Body-schema research concerns the organisation by which a body is a whole with differentiated parts and possible actions. It therefore reaches the form through which somatic influx becomes embodied rather than only its visible outcomes.

**From event to continuous operation.** Body schema is active in every reach, posture, localisation of touch, and adjustment of movement. The body-soul relation is no longer visible only at formation, crisis, or death. It is operating whenever intention becomes action.

**From broad direction to anatomical articulation.** Premotor and posterior parietal systems, including the supramarginal gyrus, provide a natural locus at which bodily form, ownership, position, and possible action are integrated. Primary sensorimotor cortex provides the more exterior interface with current input and output. The neuroscience thus begins to resolve how the fixed edge is internally organised.

**From undifferentiated embodiment to a gradient of fixity.** Gross morphology is highly canalised. Primary maps are highly plastic. Fine representational geometry is more persistent. The parietal–premotor form model can survive absent morphology and, in some cases, conflict with present morphology. The natural degree is not uniformly fixed; it becomes more receptive to ongoing interior organisation as one moves from gross tissue toward integrated bodily selfhood.

**From absence to disagreement.** The corpus's evidence and the amputation literature both concern something missing. Section 7 concerns two things present and in conflict. Disagreement is the more informative configuration, because it forces the two terms apart.

### 9.3 The Cumulative Case

No single item carries the case. Its force lies in the recurrence of the same order across independent methods and domains.

1. **Previous-life memory with birthmarks** shows a soul's retained history preceding a new body's local formation. In the medically documented subset, 43 of 49 cases show satisfactory wound–mark correspondence.
2. **Spontaneous remission** shows interior reorganisation preceding bodily reorganisation in 85.5 percent of cases with clear temporal sequence.
3. **Terminal lucidity** shows the person becoming fully available while the damaged neural substrate remains damaged.
4. **Functional remapping** shows that cortical territory is organised by use rather than fixed anatomical ownership.
5. **Long-term phantom representation** shows detailed bodily organisation persisting for decades without the represented part and, in the avulsion case, without a peripheral channel capable of maintaining it.
6. **Aplasic phantom limbs** show bodily form for parts that never entered the person's morphology, localised in premotor and parietal systems rather than the repurposed primary territory.
7. **Own-body incongruence** shows that bodily self-processing can organise around identity rather than the morphology that has supplied lifelong sensory input.

These findings do not all measure the same variable, and their difference is part of their value. Case investigation, clinical chronology, end-of-life reports, high-field fMRI, representational-similarity analysis, TMS, MEG, DTI, and own-body tasks converge without sharing method, sample, or theoretical purpose. The common order is that the person's form is not exhausted by the present condition of the tissue through which it is expressed.

Within the framework, that order is expected because the natural body is an effect within a longer causal sequence. The soul brings a formed history and orientation; the spiritual degree articulates this as a human form capable of relation and use; the natural degree fixes that form within inherited biology and physical law. Body schema occupies the measurable region where the interior human form becomes a bodily point of view and field of action.

### 9.4 Natural Mechanism and Spiritual Cause

The developmental account and the framework account answer different levels of the same question. A genetic and epigenetic programme may lay down the initial neural organisation. Sensorimotor experience may calibrate it. Observation of other people may help render an aplasic limb phenomenally available. Injury, training, and tool use may reorganise it. Every one of these claims concerns the means by which body schema develops at the natural degree.

None establishes that the natural sequence begins itself. To say that a representation is genetically specified identifies a stable biological transmission mechanism; it does not explain why the inherited programme has the form of a whole human body, why it can organise an absent part as one's own, or how identity selects among forms made available by development and observation. Those are questions about the organised person for whom genes, experience, and observation become formative rather than merely causal inputs.

The framework does not insert a spiritual event into an otherwise complete neural chain. Influx is the chain considered from its interior origin. At one degree it appears as will and form; at another as gene regulation, developmental timing, synaptic weighting, and network activity. The natural account becomes more precise without becoming more ontologically self-sufficient. Conversely, the spiritual account acquires content only by respecting the natural mechanisms through which it appears.

This is why the finding that body representation is early-laid and internally maintained belongs inside the case rather than against it. A form preceding substantial sensorimotor construction is the natural pattern expected where the body follows an interior form. The finding does not by itself identify that interior, but neither does naming its genetic route turn the route into an origin. The corpus supplies the directional context through evidence gathered at other points in the soul-body relation; neuroscience supplies the developing natural structure through which that relation becomes embodied.

### 9.5 Research Programme

The synthesis opens a research programme aimed at resolving the natural articulation more precisely.

**Congenital limb absence.** Body-schema-level imaging should compare participants with and without conscious phantom experience. The central question is whether parietal and premotor representation is present in both groups, with phenomenal availability differing, or only in those who report a phantom. Longitudinal study around stump injury or surgery could test the transition reported by Saadah and Melzack.

**Early development.** Neonatal and infant imaging can distinguish initial primary somatotopy from later parietal–premotor integration and track how each changes with motor experience. The framework does not require adult-like circuitry at birth; it expects natural development. The relevant question is whether experience constructs bodily organisation from undifferentiated input or progressively actualises and calibrates an organisation already directed toward human form.

**Schema–morphology incongruence.** Research should move beyond image identification to direct body-schema measures: proprioceptive drift, multisensory ownership illusions, peripersonal-space boundaries, motor imagery, autonomic response, and tool incorporation. This would determine which aspects of bodily form align with identity and which remain anchored to morphology.

**The determinacy gradient.** Developmental processes can be ranked independently by canalisation and compared with the distribution of somatic correspondences in the DOPS archive. Pigmentation, vascular patterning, dermatoglyphics, handedness, organ situs, limb number, and skeletal plan offer differing degrees of latitude. A prospective analysis would show whether retained states enter natural formation preferentially where variation is biologically available.

**Cross-witness integration.** The strongest future design would connect domains now studied separately. DOPS cases involving congenital defects could include structured phantom and body-schema assessment. Radical-remission studies could include longitudinal interoceptive and own-body measures. Terminal-lucidity observation could distinguish restoration of propositional memory from restoration of bodily orientation and agency. Such work would test whether the same layered architecture appears at formation, reorganisation, and release.

### 9.6 Limitations and Lacunae

The evidential reach of the present synthesis is limited in specific ways. These limits determine which parts of the natural articulation are established and which remain open for research.

**Sample sizes.** Brugger's aplasic phantom study is a single case. Kikkert's high-resolution study has two participants plus one avulsion case. Case and colleagues have eight and eight. Only Majid and colleagues, at thirty and thirty, and Melzack and colleagues, at one hundred and twenty-five, are adequately powered by ordinary standards. The anatomical result on which Section 6 turns — the parietal localisation of a phantom of a never-built limb — rests on **n = 1**. It has not been replicated to the present author's knowledge. A single case can establish that something is possible; it cannot establish that it is typical, and the argument of Section 6 depends on it being more than possible.

**The base rate.** Approximately twenty percent of congenitally limb-deficient individuals report phantoms. Section 6.3 distinguishes phenomenal availability from model presence, supported by the Saadah and Melzack temporal pattern in which complete phantoms became conscious only after injury or surgery. No study has yet assessed body-schema-level representation in congenitally limb-deficient individuals who report no phantom. Until that comparison is made, the twenty-percent figure describes conscious phantom report, not the prevalence of an underlying form model.

**The role of observation.** Brugger and colleagues propose that habitual observation of other people's movement contributes to aplasic phantoms. Their study does not determine whether observation constructs the form, supplies motor content to a prior form, or makes that form phenomenally available. The Saadah and Melzack temporal pattern complicates a simple construction account, because observation preceded the phantom by decades while a local bodily event immediately preceded its appearance. Observation remains a plausible natural mechanism whose exact role is unresolved.

**The level of the trans-specific findings.** Feusner and colleagues interpret their own-body image result as more likely reflective than perceptual. Majid's covariate analysis and Case's somatosensory findings reach beyond explicit self-rating, but neither directly measures proprioceptive body schema. The present synthesis can therefore establish schema–morphology disagreement at the level of own-body processing and secondary somatosensory response; it cannot yet establish how deeply that disagreement extends into pre-reflective posture, ownership, and motor organisation.

**Ramachandran and McGeoch 2007.** This paper is frequently cited in discussions of the present topic and carries **no evidential weight whatever**. It is a hypothesis paper in *Medical Hypotheses*. It reports no data. It notes that approximately sixty percent of men undergoing penectomy for cancer report a phantom penis, and *predicts* that transgender women will be less likely to report a phantom after surgery and that some transgender men will report a phantom of an organ never physically present. A follow-up survey appeared in the *Journal of Consciousness Studies* in 2008; that journal is not MEDLINE-indexed and the follow-up has not been read by the present author. It is therefore not cited in support of anything, and the frequency with which the 2007 prediction is reported as though it were a finding is itself an instance of the reading failure Section 2.4 was written to prevent.

**Unread sources.** Two further items were identified in the literature search and not obtained: Burke and colleagues' 2019 study in *Human Brain Mapping* on own-body and other-body perception, and a 2008 case report on phantom sensation after gender-affirming surgery in *Acta Medica Okayama*. Neither is relied upon. Their absence is recorded because a search that identifies sources and does not read them should say so.

**Scope within the corpus.** The corpus did not previously use the neuroscientific category *body schema*. Its treatment here is new. The ontology into which it is placed is not: the body as soul in ultimates, the soul as formed vessel, biography as spiritual geometry, and causation through discrete degrees are independently developed throughout the framework. The new claim is therefore the identification of body schema as a natural articulation of that established relation, not the body-soul direction itself.

**Level of measurement.** The imaging and stimulation studies measure natural-degree expression. They can show that a representation survives absent input, exists without corresponding morphology, or aligns with identity; they do not directly image a spiritual degree. The case for direction consequently remains cumulative across the corpus rather than reducible to a single neural experiment. This is a boundary of the instrument, not a reason to discard what the instrument does show.

## 10. Conclusion: The Form That Precedes the Body

This thesis began from a direction already established across the corpus: the body follows the soul. Previous-life birthmarks show retained history entering bodily formation; spontaneous remission shows interior reorganisation entering bodily reorganisation; terminal lucidity shows the intact person exceeding the damaged neural expression. The open work was not to prove that direction again, but to ask how a soul already in human form becomes a bodily self capable of ownership, location, sensation, posture, and action.

Body-schema research supplies the missing natural resolution. At the level of cortical territory, allocation follows **functional role**: the hand area is recruited by whatever body part performs the missing hand's work. At the level of representational structure, individual digit organisation can persist for decades after amputation, including in a participant whose brachial plexus avulsion abolished communication between the residual arm and the central nervous system. At the level of the parietal–premotor form model, a woman born without forearms or lower legs carried vivid, behaviourally verified phantoms of hands and feet; direct stimulation evoked those limbs without motor output to the stump even while the primary hand territory had been recruited by her upper arms.

The levels disclose an ordered natural architecture. The outer interface reorganises with present use. Fine bodily differentiation can persist without present input. The integrated form model can include what gross morphology never built. Persistence increases toward the interior, while dependence on current peripheral tissue decreases.

Schema–morphology disagreement makes the same architecture visible where neither side is absent. Own-body processing can align with identity rather than birth-assigned morphology after explicit self-similarity ratings are covaried out, and somatosensory response to an incongruent body region differs in the supramarginal gyrus and anterior insula rather than only in the primary map where input arrives. The studies do not yet establish how far this difference extends into pre-reflective body schema, but they do establish that bodily self-processing is not simply a readout of the morphology supplying it with sensory data.

Within the framework, the soul's human form is not a hidden anatomical copy. It is the organisation of a receiving person: ruling love, understanding, biography, identity, capacities, relations, and uses held together as one spiritual geometry. An embodied act unfolds from that organisation through discrete degrees. Love supplies the end; understanding articulates the end as a possible relation and action; the natural degree realises it through parietal integration, premotor planning, sensorimotor maps, peripheral nerves, muscles, and tissue. Body schema is the natural articulation through which the soul's human form becomes a located and acting body.

Gross morphology and body schema need not coincide in every respect because the natural mechanisms through which they develop differ in determinacy. Chromosomal, hormonal, and embryological pathways strongly canalise anatomy. Neural body representation remains plastic, use-dependent, and open to top-down maintenance. Both are natural expressions of the person; neither is a rival to spiritual causation. Genetics and development describe how form is laid down at the natural degree. They do not convert the means of expression into the origin of the person expressed.

The cumulative case is therefore larger than the claim that peripheral input is unnecessary to maintain a cortical map, though that claim is securely supported. Across formation, healing, neural loss, congenital absence, and bodily incongruence, the same direction recurs: the person's form is not exhausted by the current state of the physical structure, and changes or absences in that structure do not consistently erase the form. The neuroscience identifies where and how this prior organisation becomes naturally effective; the wider corpus supplies the evidential context in which its direction can be understood.

The form that precedes the body is the soul as a formed human recipient of life. The body does not manufacture that form and then hand it inward. Through the lawful mechanisms of the natural degree, it gives the form an ultimate in which it can act.
