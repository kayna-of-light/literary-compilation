## Key Findings

### What Eckhart Taught
- Every soul has an uncreated, eternal "spark" or "ground" that is identical with God
- Direct, unmediated experience of the divine is available to every soul
- "Detachment" (*Gelassenheit*) — letting go of the proprium so God can flow through without obstruction
- The "birth of the Word in the soul" — God continuously generating truth within the human interior
- Apophatic theology — God is beyond all categories, all names, all concepts

### Why He Was Condemned (1329)
- 28 propositions condemned in the papal bull *In agro dominico*
- 17 declared heretical, 11 "suspect of heresy"
- The commission took his propositions "literally and out of context"
- The real threat: his teaching made the institutional church's mediating function unnecessary
- If every soul has direct access to God, why do you need priests, sacraments, hierarchy?

### The Pattern
This is EXACTLY the same dynamic as:
- **Ananus executing James (62 CE):** James had access to the interior of the tradition without going through Sadducean channels. His presence made their mediation unnecessary.
- **Domitian interrogating Jude's grandsons (95 CE):** The family described a kingdom "not of this world" — operating at a degree the institution couldn't control.
- **The NDE Being of Light:** Singular, personal, loving, teaching — reaching every soul directly, bypassing all institutional mediation. The 36.5:1 love-to-harsh ratio corrects institutional theology, doesn't confirm it.

Eckhart is James the Just in medieval costume. The institutional response is Ananus in papal robes. The pattern doesn't change because the pattern is structural, not historical.

### Scholarly Rehabilitation
- 1980: Dominican order petitioned Rome to rescind the condemnation
- Most scholars now consider Eckhart "fundamentally orthodox"
- Bernard McGinn and others have shown the commission misunderstood him
- Rome has NOT responded to the petition — the condemnation still officially stands (as of search date)

### Eckhart's Influence
- Shaped German mysticism (Tauler, Suso, Ruysbroeck)
- Influenced the Protestant Reformation (though Luther wouldn't have acknowledged it)
- Today studied by Buddhists, philosophers, and mystics worldwide
- His language of non-dual consciousness anticipates modern consciousness research

## Significance for the Research

Eckhart is evidence that the woman in the wilderness survived INSIDE the institutional church, not only in buried texts. His teaching is correspondential perception expressed in scholastic vocabulary:
- The "divine spark" = the Lord's influx into the inmost of the soul
- "Detachment" = the removal of the proprium's obstruction
- "Birth of the Word in the soul" = influx received and generating truth within
- The "ground of the soul" = the celestial degree

The institution condemned him for the same reason it condemns every direct perceiver: because direct perception makes institutional mediation unnecessary. But his teaching survived — through disciples, through manuscripts, through a tradition of German mysticism that carried the living content beneath the institutional surface.

The woman was nourished even within the dragon's territory.
