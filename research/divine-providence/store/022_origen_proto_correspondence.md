## Who Origen Was

Origen of Alexandria (c.185-253). Son of a Christian martyr (Leonidas, beheaded 202). The most prolific writer of the early church — estimated 6,000 works, most lost or destroyed. Head of the Catechetical School of Alexandria at age 18. Produced the first systematic exposition of Christian theology (*De Principiis*, c.220-230). Composed the Hexapla — a six-column comparison of Old Testament versions (Hebrew, transliteration, four Greek translations). Tortured during the Decian persecution (250); died from injuries c.253.

His actual text survives only in fragments and through two hostile filters: Rufinus's Latin translation (which Rufinus admits he "softened") and Jerome's hostile excerpts (designed to discredit him). We may not have reliable access to what Origen actually wrote. The institution controlled the transmission so thoroughly that the original voice is distorted from both sides.

## The Threefold Sense of Scripture: Origen's Own Words

From *De Principiis* IV.11 (read from the New Advent translation):

> "For as man is said to consist of body, and soul, and spirit, so also does sacred Scripture, which has been granted by the divine bounty for the salvation of man."

The three levels:
1. **Body** (σῶμα / corpus): the literal-historical sense — "the common and historical sense"
2. **Soul** (ψυχή / anima): the moral sense — "for those who have made considerable progress"
3. **Spirit** (πνεῦμα / spiritus): the spiritual sense — "for the perfect"

> "Each one, then, ought to describe in his own mind, in a threefold manner, the understanding of the divine letters — that is, in order that all the more simple individuals may be edified, so to speak, by the very body of Scripture... while, if some have commenced to make considerable progress, and are able to see something more, they may be edified by the very soul of Scripture. Those, again, who are perfect... may be edified by the spiritual law itself."

## The Deliberate Stumbling Blocks

Origen's most radical claim: the Holy Spirit DELIBERATELY placed impossibilities and contradictions in the literal text to force the reader beyond the surface.

From *De Principiis* IV.15:

> "Divine wisdom took care that certain stumbling-blocks, or interruptions, to the historical meaning should take place, by the introduction into the midst of the narrative of certain impossibilities and incongruities; that in this way the very interruption of the narrative might, as by the interposition of a bolt, present an obstacle to the reader, whereby he might refuse to acknowledge the way which conducts to the ordinary meaning."

And IV.16 — the creation narrative itself:

> "Now who is there, pray, possessed of understanding, that will regard the statement as appropriate, that the first day, and the second, and the third... existed without sun, and moon, and stars — the first day even without a sky? And who is found so ignorant as to suppose that God, as if He had been a husbandman, planted trees in paradise?"

This is EXACTLY the same insight as the library's reading of Genesis 1-11 as correspondential composition, not literal history. Origen perceived in the 3rd century what the library demonstrates through the correspondential key: the creation narrative's "impossibilities" are not errors but signals that the text operates at a level beyond the literal.

## The Scripture-Creation Parallel

Origen extended the correspondence beyond Scripture to creation itself:

From the Coptic Church scholarly summary of his cosmology:

> "Man can acknowledge the invisible heaven through the visible things of this world; God made all things in wisdom so He created all species of visible things on earth in which to place some knowledge of things invisible, whereby the human mind can mount to spiritual understanding."

And from *De Principiis* IV.13:

> "The sensible things become a ladder towards understanding greater things. Therefore, the whole Scripture speaks in sensible terms, so that we may step up on them to spiritual things."

The "firmament" of Genesis corresponds to the human being's dual nature: "The first heaven, which Origen calls spiritual, represents our mind and our inner spiritual being which looks on God, while the corporeal heaven, the firmament, represents our external being which sees physical reality."

This is the doctrine of correspondence applied to cosmology: the natural world IS a structured expression of spiritual reality. Origen perceived this without having the systematic key.

## The Field and Treasure Analogy

*De Principiis* IV.23 — Origen's most explicit statement of the relationship between letter and spirit:

> "The kingdom of heaven is like a treasure hid in a field; which when a man finds, he hides it, and for joy thereof goes away and sells all that he has, and buys that field... the very soil and surface, so to speak, of Scripture — that is, the literal meaning — is the field, filled with plants and flowers of all kinds; while that deeper and profounder spiritual meaning are the very hidden treasures."

The field (natural/literal) contains the treasure (spiritual/interior). You must possess the field to find the treasure. The outer form is not discarded — it is the necessary container. This is correspondential hermeneutics.

## Apokatastasis: What Origen Actually Taught

Origen's core axiom (*De Principiis* III.6): "the end must be like the beginning." But Pierce's recent scholarship shows this is NOT a simple circle. Origen's vision is a "spiral" — the end surpasses the beginning:

> "Whereas in the beginning creatures fell from God, God will in the end become their 'all in all.' Apokatastasis represents not mere restoration but a cosmic transformation towards deification."

Origen viewed ALL punishment as therapeutic — corrective fire, not punitive fire:

> "All punishment in this world and the next is therapeutic."

Ramelli's key linguistic finding: the Greek αἰώνιος (aiōnios) means "of a different age/era," NOT "eternal/everlasting." Augustine's Vulgate translated both ἀίδιος (genuinely eternal) and αἰώνιος (age-related) as *aeternus*, collapsing the distinction. The doctrine of eternal punishment may rest on a MISTRANSLATION — the same kind of literalization the Beast thesis documents throughout church history.

## What Was Condemned (553) vs. What Origen Actually Taught

The Fifteen Anathemas of Constantinople II target:
1. Pre-existence of souls as incorporeal intelligences
2. Fall of souls through satiety (*koros*) with divine contemplation
3. Embodiment as punishment for the fall
4. Christ's soul pre-existing and uniting with the Logos
5. Resurrection bodies becoming spherical and immaterial
6. Universal restoration ending all punishment

Ramelli's argument: these doctrines derive more from Evagrius Ponticus's *Kephalaia Gnostica* than from Origen's own *De Principiis*. The proof: Gregory of Nyssa held universal restoration WITHOUT pre-existence — and was COMMENDED by the same council. The condemnation targeted the cosmological machinery, not the hope of universal salvation.

But the REAL threat was the hermeneutical method:

If Scripture has a body, soul, and spirit — if the literal text is the "field" and the spiritual meaning is the "treasure" — then anyone who can perceive the spirit directly doesn't need the institution to tell them what the text means. Origen's threefold sense placed the authority for interpretation in the perceiver's spiritual development, not in the institution's doctrinal control.

This is why his method survived (as the fourfold sense of medieval exegesis) while his name was condemned. The institution needed the method — it was too useful to discard. But it domesticated the method by making INSTITUTIONAL authority the arbiter of which spiritual readings were authorized. The key was kept, but the lock was changed.

## The Alexandrian Chain: Therapeutae → Philo → Clement → Origen

Origen did not invent the allegorical method. He inherited it through a documented chain that reaches back before Christianity.

### The Therapeutae (1st century BCE/CE)
A Jewish contemplative community near Alexandria, described by Philo in *De Vita Contemplativa*. Key characteristics:
- They "read the Holy Scriptures, from which they sought wisdom by treating them as allegorical, believing that the words of the literal text were symbols of something hidden"
- They had **"books composed by the founders of their sect on the allegorical method of interpreting Scripture"** — lost texts preserving a tradition of reading the Torah as encoding spiritual meaning
- They were "extreme allegorizers" who pursued trance-like states in which they experienced **"a vision of God's light"**
- **Women participated as equals** — the same pattern as the Cathar perfectae, 1,200 years later
- They concentrated on **wisdom** as their primary objective
- Light and the sun played a large role — connected to "light metaphysics" and "Persian philosophical concepts"

The Therapeutae are related to but distinct from the Essenes. The Essenes were practical and more literal; the Therapeutae were contemplative and allegorical. Both were pre-Christian Jewish communities. The library traces the correspondential tradition through the Essene-Enochic matrix (`00_Master_Theses/The Carriers of Living Water.md`). If the Therapeutae were the CONTEMPLATIVE branch of the same tradition, their lost "books on the allegorical method" may preserve the same correspondential perception the Essenes preserved in practice.

The "Persian philosophical concept" of light metaphysics that scholars note in the Therapeutae connects them to the Zoroastrian/Bene Qedem tradition the library identifies as the carrier of the Ancient Word. This is not proof — but it is a lead worth following.

### Philo of Alexandria (c.25 BCE - c.50 CE)
Drew on the Therapeutae's tradition and systematized it through Platonic/Stoic categories:
- Adam = the mind; Eve = the senses. Biblical characters = aspects of the human being. Stories = episodes from universal human experience.
- "God created all species of visible things on earth in which to place some knowledge of things invisible" — nature as structured expression of spiritual reality
- The literal sense is "the shadow which the body casts" — the outer form of an interior content
- The Logos stands "between the Lord and you; I am neither uncreated like God nor created like you, but midway between the two extremes" — the mediating principle between degrees
- "Things on earth are dependent on the heavenly realm through a natural affinity" — vertical causation

Philo may have influenced Paul, the Gospel of John, and the Epistle to the Hebrews. His primary importance is as the bridge between Jewish allegorical tradition and Christian theological development.

### Clement of Alexandria (c.150-215)
Inherited from Philo and Christianized the tradition:
- Distinguished *pistis* (faith, the foundation) from *gnosis* (knowledge, the perfection) — two levels of Christian life
- "One cannot know the truth without living it, nor live the truth without knowing it" — knowledge and moral life are inseparable
- Greek philosophy was "their own Testament" given by God — a preparation for truth
- Scripture contains multiple levels of meaning: literal, symbolic, allegorical

### Origen (c.185-253)
Inherited from Clement and built the first systematic hermeneutic (see above).

**The chain:** Therapeutae (contemplative Jewish allegorizers with lost founding texts, connected to "Persian light metaphysics") → Philo (systematized into Platonic framework) → Clement (Christianized, distinguished faith/gnosis) → Origen (systematized threefold sense, extended to creation).

This is a documented transmission chain of correspondential perception running from pre-Christian Judaism through Hellenistic philosophy into the heart of the early church. And it was suppressed: Origen condemned, his texts corrupted, his method domesticated.

## The Preexistence Debate: Did Origen Even Teach It?

Recent revisionist scholarship raises a radical possibility: the famous doctrine of the preexistence and fall of souls may NOT be Origen's own teaching.

The key argument (from the "On First Principles" blog, drawing on textual analysis):
- **Fragment 15** — the central text where Origen "teaches" preexistence — is "not a text written by Origen but a pastiche from several authors who, in some cases, are not even claiming to quote from Origen but are simply repeating hearsay accusations against him"
- Koetschau's 1913 critical edition "supplemented" Rufinus's Latin with Greek fragments from HOSTILE sources, treating them as objective witnesses. Butterworth's English translation (the only one available) perpetuates this.
- In Origen's ACTUAL authenticated text (*De Principiis* via Rufinus), he asserts that "only the Trinity can exist entirely bodiless — to be a creature is to be embodied." He "quite unambiguously asserts that rational creatures have been embodied from the beginning."
- Justinian — who convened the council that condemned Origen — may never have read *De Principiis* himself.

If this is correct, then what was condemned in 553 was a straw man — a doctrine fabricated from hostile fragments and attributed to a dead man who couldn't defend himself. The condemnation served to suppress the REAL threat (the hermeneutical method that made institutional mediation unnecessary) by attaching the heretic's name to cosmological speculations he may not have held.

**This needs further investigation.** The revisionist reading is provocative but not yet mainstream. Key scholars to follow: Mark Edwards, Samuel Fernández (on the reliability of Rufinus), Ilaria Ramelli (on what was actually condemned).
