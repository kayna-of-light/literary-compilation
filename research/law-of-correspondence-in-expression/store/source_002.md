# Source 002 — Delsarte in his own words

**Citations**:
- *Delsarte System of Oratory*. 4th ed. New York: Edgar S. Werner, 1893. Read in archive.org `delsartesystemof00delauoft` and Project Gutenberg #12200, both full text. A compilation of Delaumosne's and Arnaud's records of Delsarte's teaching, with Delsarte's own *Literary Remains*.
- Delsarte, address before the Philotechnic Society of Paris, as printed in Stebbins, *Delsarte System of Expression*, 6th ed. (1901), 67 (store 006).
- The French original of the law as given by Waille 2012, n. 31 (store 003).

**Accessed**: 2026-10-24.

## The law

"To each spiritual function responds a function of the body. To each grand function of the body corresponds a spiritual act." (Philotechnic address, in Stebbins 1901, 67.) The French: « À chaque fonction spirituelle répond une fonction du corps; à chaque grande fonction du corps répond un acte spirituel » (Waille 2012, n. 31).

The surrounding sentences in the address: "Man considered from the point of view of art presents three orders of essential functions, each one depending upon a proper and determined organic apparatus. … These three orders of functions, of apparatuses and of languages constitute the natural division of the phenomena of the human personality and of its triple causality."

## His frame (from *Oratory*, 1893)

- Trinity as universal formula: "there is in the world a universal formula which may be applied to all sciences, to all things possible. This formula is the trinity." (Fragment quoted in Stebbins 1901, 111 and 384.)
- "The soul being the form of the body". This is Thomist, *Summa Theologiae* I q.76 a.1 (store 017).
- "This marvelous correspondence of the organs with the sentiment arises from the close union of soul and body."
- Three worlds, "the natural, the intellectual and the supernatural"; the light "of the senses, the reason, or the light of grace". This is a Thomist nature–grace scheme, not Swedenborg's natural–spiritual–celestial.
- The thermometers: "the eyebrow is the thermometer of the mind; the shoulder is the thermometer of the life; the thumb is the thermometer of the will" (Gutenberg text, line 2701). Also "The shoulder … is the thermometer of emotion and of love".
- Art: "Art is at once the knowledge, the possession, and the free direction of the agents by virtue of which are revealed the life, soul and mind. It is the appropriation of the sign to the thing." (Arnaud, quoted in Stebbins 1901, 111–112.) Volkonsky's epigraph: "Art is the finding of the sign corresponding to the essence" (store 009).

## Negative result

Neither text names Swedenborg (full-text search of both). The compilation is not the whole of Delsarte; his manuscripts at LSU are mostly unpublished (store 003).

## Why it matters

The law is Delsarte's own formulation, and his vocabulary is Catholic and Thomist. The Swedenborg attribution belongs to his American expositors (stores 005–007), not to him. Note the clause "to each grand function of the body corresponds a spiritual act": the law runs in both directions (synthesis theme_003).
