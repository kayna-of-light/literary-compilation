## The Scope of Transmission

Swedenborg's correspondential framework entered world literature through documented chains affecting:

### English-Language
- **William Blake** (annotated Swedenborg's works, signed New Jerusalem Church guestbook 1789)
- **S.T. Coleridge** (engaged with Swedenborg's works)
- **Ralph Waldo Emerson** (chose Swedenborg as one of his "Representative Men" 1850, alongside Plato, Shakespeare, Napoleon)
- **Henry David Thoreau** (through Emerson's circle)
- **Walt Whitman** (absorbed through Transcendentalist milieu)
- **Robert and Elizabeth Browning**
- **W.B. Yeats** (engaged with Swedenborg through Golden Dawn)
- **Arthur Conan Doyle** (spiritualist connections)
- **Robert Frost**
- **A.S. Byatt**

### French-Language
- **Honoré de Balzac** — declared "Swedenborgianism is my religion" (letter to Mme Hanska, 1837). *Séraphîta* and *Louis Lambert* are explicitly Swedenborgian novels. Also *La Peau de Chagrin*, *Ursule Mirouët*, *Cousin Pons*.
- **Charles Baudelaire** — encountered Swedenborg through Balzac. The sonnet "Correspondances" in *Fleurs du Mal* (1857) made the doctrine of correspondences a cornerstone of French symbolist aesthetics.
- **Gérard de Nerval, Théophile Gautier, Gustave Flaubert** — traces of Swedenborgian thought

### Swedish-Language
- **August Strindberg** — discovered Swedenborg through Balzac's *Séraphîta* in 1896. "I was seized with ecstatic admiration as I listened to the voice of this angelic giant." Swedenborg influenced his innovative psychic dramas.

### Russian-Language
- **Fyodor Dostoevsky** — three Swedenborg-related books in his library; Father Zosima's teachings in *Brothers Karamazov* are essentially Swedenborgian

### Spanish-Language
- **Jorge Luis Borges** — a pioneer of Magic Realism, acknowledged Swedenborg as a major philosophical influence. Essay "Testimony to the Invisible," sonnet "Emanuel Swedenborg."

### Polish-Language
- **Czeslaw Milosz** (Nobel Prize 1980) — acknowledged Swedenborg as one of his main sources of inspiration

### American Civic
- **Helen Keller** — lifelong Swedenborgian, published *My Religion* (1927)
- **Johnny Appleseed (John Chapman)** — Swedenborgian missionary distributing *Heaven and Hell* across the frontier
- **Henry James Sr.** — devoted Swedenborgian, father of William James and Henry James Jr.

### Visual Arts
- **George Inness** — landscape painter, devoted Swedenborgian
- **John Flaxman** — sculptor, Swedenborgian
- **Daniel Burnham** — architect, Swedenborgian

## Chains of Transmission

Documented chains:
1. **Swedenborg → Balzac → Baudelaire → French Symbolism** — the doctrine of correspondences entered French aesthetics
2. **Swedenborg → Balzac → Strindberg → Swedish drama** — Séraphîta was the bridge
3. **Swedenborg → Emerson → American Transcendentalism → Whitman, Thoreau** — the American spiritual imagination
4. **Swedenborg → Oetinger → Schelling → Heidelberg Romantics → Grimm** — fairy tales (documented in library)
5. **Swedenborg → Graham/Blair/Wayfarers → Disney** — animation (documented in library)
6. **Swedenborg → Aksakov → Dostoevsky → Russian literature** — Brothers Karamazov
7. **Swedenborg → Milosz → modern Polish literature** — Nobel Prize lineage

## Séraphîta: The Most Explicitly Swedenborgian Novel

Balzac's *Séraphîta* (1834) is a novel about an androgynous angel living in a Norwegian fjord, born to Swedenborgian parents. Key elements:
- The protagonist transcends gender — "Adamic androgyny" before the fall
- "Where you see bodies, I see forces...affinities which are linked to centers...God did not create different kingdoms, but one substance"
- The language of correspondences as a "language of true names" that annuls post-Babelian confusion
- The angel's ascension at the novel's climax = regeneration completed
- Melville also engaged with Séraphîta

The woman in the wilderness was nourished not through one channel but through EVERY major literary tradition in the modern world. The correspondential key entered:
- English poetry (Blake)
- American philosophy (Emerson)
- French aesthetics (Baudelaire)
- Russian fiction (Dostoevsky)
- Swedish drama (Strindberg)
- Latin American magic realism (Borges)
- Children's fairy tales (Grimm, Andersen)
- Global cinema (Disney)

Swedenborg published in Latin in Sweden. Within two centuries, the key had entered every major language and literary tradition on earth. This is not cultural diffusion. This is the river flowing through every vessel that opens.
