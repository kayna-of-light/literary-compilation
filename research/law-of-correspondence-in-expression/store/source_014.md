# Source 014 — Don Graham's Action Analysis classes, 1937 (transcripts)

**Citations** (typescript transcripts, collection of Hans Perk, published at *A. Film L.A.*):
- Graham, "Introduction to Study of Action: Primary Actions" (Lecture I), 22 February 1937. Scans `AAC-022237-01` to `-20`.
- Graham, "Animation of Forces vs. Animation of Forms …", 21 June 1937. Scans `AAC-062137-01` to `-15`; http://afilmla.blogspot.com/2009/03/animating-forms-vs-forces-action.html.
- Graham, "Tytla Speaks on Forms vs. Forces", 28 June 1937. Scans `AAC-062837-01` to `-20`.

**Accessed**: 2026-10-24. All 183 published pages (13 series, January 1936 – October 1938) were downloaded from Perk's blog feed and OCR'd (rapidocr). The quotations below were read on the scan images.

## 22 February 1937

- "every primary action must be the result of a thought process either subconscious or conscious" (p. 6).
- "it is the emotion and mood that come first, the action comes second. The action is a secondary thing - it is a result of the thought or feeling"; "it is the thought process that determines primary actions, not the action that determines the thought" (p. 11).
- "A character walks angrily across the room. Before he can walk angrily, he must be angry, - he has to have that thought, that feeling."
- **Reading list** (p. 20): "(Suggestions offered by various members of the class on helpful reading along these lines, included: THEATRE WORKSHOP, a quarterly - the first two issues, and more especially the first, with articles on acting that offered practical guidance to animators who had read them; AN ACTOR PREPARES, by Stanislawski; FIRST SIX LESSONS IN ACTING, by Boleslavski.)"

## 21 June 1937

- "In drawing lines representing a leg, a symbol for the force or thrust, is first drawn. The artist is not drawing a form at all, but indicating a force."
- On Tytla: "The actual form is something which eventually is necessary but which is really a by-product of animation. The vital element is force."
- "There is a world of difference in the mental approach - there is an entirely new philosophy of animation wrapped up in this concept of animating forces" (p. 2).
- KLEIN: "I think if you put it just as forces, it sounds too much like philosophy." GRAHAM: "It *is* a philosophy." (p. 3)
- "This is merely a new way of looking at old material … there is nothing new in it except that the whole thing is new - it is a conception. Review and study the different philosophies, the different big steps forward, the progress made in science, history, art. Rarely will one find these forward steps entirely new - merely old material given a new twist. … It is putting a new twist to an old subject - a new philosophy of approach … not really new, but adapted to Studio uses." (p. 5)
- "Everyone who has ever drawn a figure has been concerned with forces. But whether an artist starts with the conception of force is the important thing - and whether he sees the force as the by-product of the drawing, or vice versa. It is the approach that is important." (p. 5)

## 28 June 1937 (Tytla)

- "This proves to me that the ultimate thing is the mood or sensation you get into a piece of work."
- "We can't be mystical about our work … we cannot turn out the lights and have confessions. This is a business."
- He took his approach from the action analysis classes: "I fell for them like a ton of brick."

## Why it matters

- **The 1937 studio taught the law's order, one way only**: thought and feeling first, action as their result.
- **Graham called it an old philosophy adapted**, not his invention.
- **The class itself named the books it read "along these lines"**: Stanislavsky and Boleslavsky. That is the documented point where the expression tradition (stores 009–012) entered the studio.
- **It was received as craft, not mysticism** (Tytla), exactly as the author's claim states.
