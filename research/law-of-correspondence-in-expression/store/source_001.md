# Source 001 — Swedenborg's own statement of the law

**Citations**:
- Swedenborg, *Divine Love and Wisdom* (Ager translation) §§154, 218–219.
- Swedenborg, *Heaven and Hell* (Ager) §§65, 87, 89–90, 96.
- Swedenborg, *Divine Providence* (Dick and Pulsford) §103.

**Accessed**: 2026-10-24. e-swedenborg static pages: `writings/static/d9090/<n>.htm` (DLW), `d5399` (HH), `d15083` (DP). Text of the Academy of the New Church edition, read section by section.

## What it says (quoted)

**DLW §218, the order of conatus, force and motion.** "It is known that conatus does nothing of itself, but acts through forces corresponding to it, thereby producing motion; consequently that conatus is the all in forces, and through forces is the all in motion; and since motion is the outmost degree of conatus, through motion conatus exerts its power. Conatus, force, and motion are no otherwise conjoined than according to degrees of height, conjunction of which is not by continuity, for they are discrete, but by correspondences."

**DLW §219, the same order in a living person.** "Living conatus in man, who is a living subject, is his will united to his understanding; living forces in man are the interior constituents of his body, in all of which there are motor fibers interlacing in various ways; and living motion in man is action, which is produced through these forces by the will united to the understanding." Then the sentence that states the law: "These three do not act by what is continuous, but by what is discrete; and to act by what is discrete is to act by correspondences. The interiors of the mind correspond to the interiors of the body, and the interiors of the body correspond to the exteriors, through which actions come forth."

**DLW §154, threefoldness in every created thing.** "In every thing created, greatest as well as least, there are these three, end, cause and effect. A created thing in which these three are not, is impossible."

**HH §87, the lost knowledge.** "To them [the ancient people] the knowledge of correspondences was the chief of knowledges … But at this day that knowledge has been so completely lost that no one knows what correspondence is." Swedenborg's note to the section: "The knowledge of correspondences flourished among the Eastern nations and in Egypt."

**HH §89.** "The whole natural world corresponds to the spiritual world … precisely like an effect from its effecting cause."

**HH §90, the person as the case of the law.** "The interior things that belong to his mind, and that have relation to understanding and will, constitute his spiritual world; while the exterior things that belong to his body, and that have relation to its senses and activities, constitute his natural world. Consequently, everything in his natural world … that has its existence from his spiritual world … is called a correspondent."

**HH §96, correspondence through function.** "This inflow of heaven is into the functions and uses of the bodily members; and the uses, since they are from the spiritual world, take on a form by means of such things as are in the natural world, and thus present themselves in effect. From this is the correspondence."

**HH §§65, 96, the Greatest Man's body map.**
- The third heaven "forms the head down to the neck"; the second "the breast down to the loins and knees"; the first "the feet down to the soles, and also the arms down to the fingers".
- Those in the head are in "love, peace, innocence, wisdom, intelligence".
- Those in the breast are "in the good of charity and of faith".
- Those "in the arms and hands are in the power of truth from good".

**DP §103, the direction.** "The action of the body, whether in word or in deed, is only an effect from the internal and the external of man's spirit; for the body is only an obedient instrument."

## Why it matters

These are the terms against which every later link is measured.

- **The order.** Will united to understanding (conatus) → forces in the body → action (*DLW* §§218–219). This is the order of Walt's memo ("the driving force behind the action is the mood … the mind is the pilot") and of Graham's force before form.
- **Correspondence through function and use** (*HH* §96). This is the criterion the Revival art books follow (store 016) and the one Delsarte's law states in the person ("to each spiritual function responds a function of the body", store 002).
- **The direction.** One way, inside to outside (*DP* §103; *HH* §89).
- **Discrete degrees.** "Not by what is continuous, but by what is discrete". This is the feature on which Delsarte's interpenetrating trinity differs (store 017).
