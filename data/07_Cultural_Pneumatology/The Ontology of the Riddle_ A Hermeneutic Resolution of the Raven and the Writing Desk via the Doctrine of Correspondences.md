# **The Ontology of the Riddle: A Hermeneutic Resolution of the Raven and the Writing Desk via the Doctrine of Correspondences**

## **I. Introduction: The Epistemological Fracture and the Riddle of Existence**

The enigmatic query posed by the Mad Hatter in Lewis Carroll's *Alice's Adventures in Wonderland*—"Why is a raven like a writing desk?"—has long been relegated to the realm of literary nonsense. Carroll himself said that "the Riddle, as originally invented, had no answer at all", and that the answer he later offered was "merely an after-thought" (Carroll 1896; see Section 4.4). Read through the **Doctrine of Correspondences**, however, the riddle's two terms describe a single spiritual state: the **Natural Unregenerate Mind**. This study offers that as a reading of the riddle. It makes no claim about what Carroll meant by it, since his own words say he meant no answer.

This report undertakes an exhaustive analysis of this riddle using the "Science of Correspondences," a worldview recovered and systematized by Emanuel Swedenborg and traced back to the "Magian Science" of the Ancient Near East (*The Celestial Scribe* §2). Within this ontology, the physical universe is not a closed system of material interactions but, in Swedenborg's phrase, "a theater representative of the Lord's kingdom" (*Arcana Coelestia* [*AC*] §3483), where every object—whether a bird of the air or an artifact of wood—functions as a "letter" in a divine alphabet, articulating invisible spiritual realities.

The hypothesis presented here, derived from a synthesis of ancient "nature wisdom" and Swedenborgian theological physics, posits that the Raven and the Writing Desk are ontologically identical in their correspondence to the **Natural Unregenerate Mind**. Both entities, in their specific correspondential functions, exhibit the defining characteristics of this state: they are **"flat"** (operating solely on the horizontal plane of effects) and **"without life of their own"** (derivative, necrotic containers devoid of independent spiritual vitality). By mapping the "topography of the void" inherent in both the scavenger bird and the inanimate object, we unveil the "flatness" of the unregenerate state—a condition where the human intellect, severed from the "vertical" influx of the Divine, becomes a "dead" mirror reflecting only its own darkness.

To fully resolve this riddle, we must first reconstruct the cognitive architecture of the "Science of Correspondences" itself, tracing its lineage from the direct perception of the most ancient church, through the externalization that the Fall made necessary, to the "Scribal" codifications of the Iron Age (*The Bifurcated Inheritance* §II; *The Semiotics of Sanctity* §VII). We will then perform a forensic spiritual autopsy on the "Natural Mind," defining its parameters of "flatness" and "death". Finally, we will apply these definitions to the Raven (the animate symbol of dark thought) and the Writing Desk (the inanimate symbol of the dead letter), demonstrating their convergence in the "Science of the Self."

**II. The Architecture of Reality: The Science of Correspondences**

To understand why a bird might be like a piece of furniture, one must first dismantle the modern bifurcation between "subject" and "object," or "mind" and "matter." In the epistemological landscape of the Ancient Near East—and specifically within the Swedenborgian system—these categories are fluid. The "Science of Correspondences" posits a causal, immutable relationship between the spiritual realm (*causa*) and the natural world (*effectus*) (*The Celestial Scribe* §2.1). This relationship is not metaphorical but ontological; the natural object is the physical termination of a spiritual cause.

### **2.1 The Magian Paradigm: Mēnōg and Gētīg**

The intellectual bedrock of this worldview is found in the Zoroastrian (Magian) concepts of *mēnōg* and *gētīg*, which dominated the Jewish scribal interface in the Persian period (539–332 BCE) that followed the Babylonian Exile (*The Celestial Scribe* §2). The *mēnōg* represents the mental or spiritual realm—the seed, the root, and the archetype. It is invisible, intangible, yet fully real, representing the potentiality and the spiritual "cause" of all things. Conversely, the *gētīg* is the material or physical realm—the fruit, the manifestation, and the tangible. It is not a "fallen" world in the Gnostic sense but the *mēnōg* made visible and dense.

In this paradigm, a physical object does not exist independently. It is the "crystallization" of a spiritual force. A lion is not merely a biological predator; it is the *gētīg* manifestation of the *mēnōg* principle of "Power" or "Truth" (*The Semiotics of the Spirit* §1.1). Similarly, a raven or a desk exists only because a specific spiritual affection flows into the natural degree and clothes itself in that form. The "Science of Correspondences" is the operational logic connecting these two states, allowing the initiate to read the "Book of Nature" as a coherent text. This creates a "resonant universe" where the physical and spiritual are linked by vertical causality (*The Celestial Scribe* §2.1). Jewish scribes living in the Persian Empire, such as Ezra or Nehemiah, would have been immersed in this administrative and scientific ontology, viewing the world as a structured list of correspondences where specific physical signs correlated with specific spiritual outcomes.

### **2.2 Vertical Causality and the Law of Influx**

The mechanism driving correspondence is **Influx**. Swedenborg argues that the natural world is the "realm of effects" caused and sustained by the spiritual world: "nothing can subsist from itself, but only from that which is prior to itself, thus from a First, and if separated from that it would utterly perish and vanish" (*Heaven and Hell* [*HH*] §106). This establishes the primary attribute of all natural objects: **Dependency**. No natural object has "life of its own." Its "life" is entirely borrowed from the spiritual cause it containers.

When that connection is acknowledged and ordered, the object is "alive" or "significative." When that connection is denied or disordered—as in the case of the **Natural Unregenerate Mind**—the object becomes "dead" or "flat," a mere shell disconnected from its source.

### **2.3 The "Flatness" of the Natural Degree**

The "Natural Degree" of reality corresponds to the physical world and the "External Man" (*The Universal Mirror* §3.3). Unlike the "Spiritual Degree" (which involves the "Understanding") or the "Celestial Degree" (which involves the "Will"), the Natural Degree is the plane of **Action and Effect**. The library's framework history distinguishes the horizontal "Analogy of History" from the vertical "Analogy of Correspondence" (*The Divine Bricolage* §1.1); the first is what this study calls **"Flat" correspondence**.

This refers to the limitations of the natural mind when it operates solely on the "horizontal plane" of sensory data and historical analogy. The natural mind compares past natural events to present ones (Analogy of History), looking "across" time and space but cannot look "up" to causes. In contrast, the spiritual mind operates on the vertical axis (Analogy of Correspondence), perceiving the spiritual cause within the natural effect. The "Natural Unregenerate Mind" is defined by its confinement to this **flat**, horizontal plane. It is "constitutionally blind" to the vertical dimension. It sees the *writing desk* as wood and the *raven* as a bird, failing to perceive the spiritual affections they embody. Thus, the state of "unregeneracy" is literally a state of **flatness**—a two-dimensional existence in a three-dimensional spiritual cosmos.

**III. The Topography of the Void: Defining the Natural Unregenerate Mind**

To solve the riddle, we must rigorously define the *tertiary* term: the **Natural Unregenerate Mind**. The research materials provide a detailed "cartography" of this state, describing it not as a neutral vacuum but as a reactive, "dead" landscape.

### **3.1 The "Dead" Sun and the Absence of Life**

The most fundamental characteristic of the natural world in Swedenborgian physics is that it is **dead**. Swedenborg explicitly describes the **Natural Sun**—the source of all physical energy—as "pure fire, consequently dead" (*Divine Love and Wisdom* [*DLW*] §157). Its function is merely to serve as a recipient of the living force of the spiritual sun, clothing what that force produces "with the matters that give them fixedness" (*DLW* §§157, 315). Consequently, the **Natural Mind**, which is formed from the materials and images of this dead world, is also **"without life of its own"**. It possesses no independent vitality.

* **Genuine Life:** Life, in the proper sense, corresponds to **Love** and **Wisdom** flowing from the Divine.  
* **Apparent Life:** The natural mind appears alive because it is animated by "lusts" (evil loves) or "scientifics" (facts), but without the connection to Divine Love, this activity is merely the galvanization of a corpse.

The "Natural Unregenerate Mind" is the mind that has confirmed itself in this state of death. It rejects the Influx of the Spiritual Sun and lives solely by the light of the "dead" Natural Sun (self-love and nature-worship). It is a "vessel" that refuses to be filled, remaining a **"dark valley"** (*The Universal Mirror* §3.3) or a **dry wilderness**. This is the mind that lacks spiritual "knowledge" (Hosea 4:6), resulting in a "flat" existence where the individual is "without life of their own" because they are submerged in group identity or material concerns.

### **3.2 The Cartography of Flatness: Valleys and Plains**

Topographically, the Natural Mind corresponds to **Valleys** and lowlands, for "a 'valley' signifies lower, or what is the same, exterior things" (*AC* §3417). Mountains correspond to **the good of love to the Lord** (High, Vertical, Near the Sun), while Hills correspond to **the good of mutual love** (*AC* §6435). Valleys and Plains correspond to the **Natural Mind** (Low, Flat, Receptive). A "regenerate" natural mind is a "fruitful valley"—flat, yes, but irrigated by the "rivers" of truth flowing from the mountains. However, the **Unregenerate Natural Mind** is a **"dark valley"** or a rocky plateau (*The Universal Mirror* §3.3). It is **flat** not in the sense of being level, but in the sense of being **depressed**, **horizontal**, and **cut off**. It is the "Earth" without the "Heavens."

This "flatness" is further elucidated in the description of the **"modern mind"** (Historical Critical Method) as "constitutionally blind" to the soul of the text (*The Critic Transformed* §3.1), seeing only the "flat" literal sense. It is a vision capable of analyzing the surface (the desk) but incapable of reading the message.

### **3.3 The Self: The Trajectory into Nothingness**

The core of the Unregenerate Mind is the **Self** (Selfhood). The library's own reading of the self states that "falsity has no life of its own" and "borrows apparent existence from the truth it distorts". The trajectory of the Self—the self-centered will—is a trajectory toward **nothingness**. If one follows the Self to its completion, "what remains? Nothing." It is the "repetition of a void" (*Epistle — The Divine Marriage* §VI).

This void is the habitat of the Raven, the bird of the wasteland, and the nature of the Desk, the inanimate object. Thus, the "Natural Unregenerate Mind" is defined by two primary ontological deficits:

1. **Flatness:** A confinement to the horizontal/material plane.  
2. **Lifelessness:** A lack of independent being; a reliance on borrowed or distorted energy (Selfhood).

With this definition established, we can now turn to the two terms of the riddle—the Raven and the Writing Desk—and demonstrate how they are perfect correspondential embodiments of this state.

**IV. The Semiotics of the Raven: The Animate Shadow**

In the "Zoology of Affection" cataloged by Swedenborg and the Magian priests, animals are not biological accidents but affections in natural form: "the animals of the earth correspond in general to affection" (*HH* §110; *The Mirror of the Invisible* §2.1; *The Semiotics of Sanctity* §I). The bird, with its capacity for flight, generally corresponds to the **Intellect** or **Rational Thought**: "by 'birds' in general, rational and intellectual things" (*AC* §40). However, the specific species determines the *quality* of that thought.

### **4.1 The Raven as Dark Rationality**

The Raven (*Corvus corax*) acts as the correspondential inverse of the Eagle or the Dove. While the Eagle represents the rational, "because the eagle flies high, and from above has a wide view of the things that are below" (*AC* §3901), and the Dove represents "the truths and goods of faith with him who is to be regenerated" (*AC* §870), the Raven corresponds to **"falsities"**, and specifically to "gross and dense falsities" (*AC* §866).

* **Blackness:** Its color corresponds to the absence of spiritual light (Truth). Swedenborg gives ravens to gross falsities "because they are of a black color", and the raven's state is one in which "somewhat of the obscurity of night still remains" (*AC* §§865–866).  
* **Carrion Diet:** The raven is a scavenger. It feeds on the dead. This corresponds to the **Natural Unregenerate Mind** feeding on "dead" scientifics, "corporeal lusts," and the "fallacies of the senses" (the carcasses of truth). The pig, which the Shasu nomads shunned as "of the earth," bound to the mud (*The Semiotics of Sanctity* §2.3), is the raven's counterpart among the beasts: both are unclean (Lev 11:7, 15).  

### **4.2 The Raven of Noah: Falsities in the Twilight**

The correspondential function of the raven is most explicitly given in the narrative of Noah's Ark (Genesis 8), a text central to Swedenborg's analysis of the "Ancient Church." Noah first sends out a raven, "and it went forth, going and returning, until the waters were dried up from off the earth" (Gen 8:7). Swedenborg reads the verse closely. The raven signifies falsities, and its "going and returning" signifies "that falsities still made disturbance" (*AC* §864). The state described is not that of a man lost in the flood but "the second state of the man who is to be regenerated, after temptation, when the truths of faith, like the first dawning of light, begin to appear", a state that "resembles the morning twilight, while somewhat of the obscurity of night still remains, as is here signified by a 'raven'" (*AC* §865). Birds signify things of the understanding, and in the opposite sense reasonings and falsities; "gross and dense falsities are described by owls and ravens; by owls because they live in the darkness of night, and by ravens, because they are of a black color" (*AC* §866). The falsities "fly about, going and returning", because a man in that state "can be only in the knowledge of the most general things, into which flow fantasies arising from corporeal, sensuous, and worldly things" (*AC* §867).

The raven of Noah is therefore not the unregenerate mind at home in its desolation. It is the falsity of the natural mind as it still troubles one who is being regenerated: thought that has not yet risen above "corporeal, sensuous, and worldly things", going out and coming back without finding ground. The reading that follows rests on that meaning. The raven of the riddle is natural-degree thinking: black because it is without the light of truth, restless because it has nothing higher to settle on, and not yet dissipated.

### **4.3 The "Flat" Note of the Raven**

In the Doctrine of Correspondences, **sound** corresponds to **affection going forth**—what proceeds from someone reflects the quality of the affection from which it proceeds: in heaven "the tones of their speech" correspond "to their affections" (*HH* §236). When Swedenborg describes the sounds of the spiritual and celestial heavens, he notes their extraordinary beauty, richness, and refinement—incomparably finer and more detailed than anything we experience in the natural world (*HH* §241). The "flatness" of a sound, then, corresponds to the **natural degree without elevation**: what proceeds from a mind that cannot rise above the natural is itself natural, unrefined, lacking the harmonic complexity of spiritual thought.

The raven's call is a harsh, monotonic croak. It lacks the melodic richness of the songbird not because of mere biology, but because **the raven corresponds to the natural mind trapped in fallacies**. A mind that cannot elevate itself above the natural plane can only produce reasoning that "sounds flat"—natural-degree thinking mistaking itself for wisdom. The "flat notes" are not about musical pitch but about the **quality of reasoning**: what comes forth from the unregenerate mind is flat because the mind itself operates only on the flat, horizontal plane of natural causation.

This is the **"Monotony of the Self"**—the "repetition of a void" (*Epistle — The Divine Marriage* §VI). The unregenerate mind has only one song: "I, I, I." Its reasoning sounds flat because its affections are flat—self-referential, unable to receive the influx that would give elevation and depth. Thus, the Raven is **like** the writing desk because it is the **animate embodiment** of the **flat, dead, natural mind**—not geometrically flat, but flat in the quality of what it produces.
### **4.4 What Carroll Said About the Riddle**

Carroll's only recorded statement about the riddle is a preface dated Christmas 1896 for a new printing of *Alice's Adventures in Wonderland*:

> Enquiries have been so often addressed to me, as to whether any answer to the Hatter's Riddle can be imagined, that I may as well put on record here what seems to me to be a fairly appropriate answer, viz. "Because it can produce a few notes, though they are very flat; and it is nevar put with the wrong end in front!" This, however, is merely an after-thought: the Riddle, as originally invented, had no answer at all. (Carroll 1896)

That is the whole of the record. It establishes three things: that the riddle had no answer when Carroll invented it; that three decades later he offered one, which he called "a fairly appropriate answer" and "merely an after-thought"; and that the reversed spelling "nevar", which reads "raven" backwards, is his. Edward Wakeling, who prints the preface, points out the reversal (Wakeling 1995); a 1927 Macmillan reprint prints "never", so later printings lost it. The record says nothing about a hidden meaning, for or against. The readings below are readings of the riddle and of Carroll's after-thought. They do not claim that Carroll intended them.

#### **4.4.1 "No Answer" as a Reading**

Carroll's statement that the riddle "had no answer at all" can itself be read in correspondence. The natural mind sees the raven and the desk as different things, bird and furniture, animate and inanimate; on its own premises, which recognize only the natural, both are matter, and a question asking what hidden likeness joins them has nowhere to go. On this reading the riddle has no answer on the plane where it is asked. Its terms meet only above that plane, in the state of mind that both correspond to. The reading takes Carroll at his word: the riddle has no answer, and that is the point at which a correspondential reading begins.

#### **4.4.2 The Hatter as Correspondent**

The riddle is asked by the Hatter, at a tea party where it is always six o'clock, because, as the Hatter explains, he quarrelled with Time and Time "won't do a thing I ask" (Carroll 1865, ch. 7). In Swedenborg's account time is the outward measure of changes of state: "instead of years and days there are changes of state" (*HH* §163). The Hatter's party is a state that has stopped changing, going round the table "as the things get used up" without advancing. Read in correspondence, the riddle comes out of that state. It is the kind of question a mind asks that turns in a circle without rising: unanswerable where it is asked, asked again, and left. The question and the questioner belong to one state.

#### **4.4.3 The After-Thought Read in Correspondence**

Carroll's after-thought can be read the same way, as a reading of a text he wrote and not as a key he hid. "Because it can produce a few notes, though they are very flat" describes what comes from the natural mind: thought that cannot rise above the plane of effects (§4.3). "It is nevar put with the wrong end in front" spells *raven* backwards, and a mind that puts the natural first and the spiritual second has things the wrong way round, reasoning from effects to causes and from the world to God. That is a reading of Carroll's words. It is offered because the words bear it, and it does not need Carroll to have meant it.

**V. The Theology of the Writing Desk: The Inanimate Container**

If the Raven is the *active* principle of the unregenerate mind (Falsity), the Writing Desk is the *passive* principle (The Dead Letter). While "writing desk" does not appear in Swedenborg’s standard lists (*The Universal Mirror*), its components—**Wood**, **Writing**, and **Flat Surface**—are rich in correspondential meaning.

### **5.1 The Desk as the "Dead" Container**

The writing desk is an artifact of the **Mineral** and **Vegetable** kingdoms, fashioned by human hands. Wood corresponds to good "which is of affection, or of the will" in a positive sense (*AC* §2702), but in the context of the unregenerate mind it corresponds to **"wood"** and **"stone"** idols—the work of men's hands: "serving wood and stone" is worshiping graven images, by which is signified that "they served yearnings and fantasies" (*AC* §643). It is "dead" matter. Swedenborg emphasizes that the mineral kingdom (dust, sand, stone) belongs to the **"Ultimate"** degree—the lowest and most fixed level of reality, where "each and all things of the earth" are dead (*DLW* §158).

* **Sand:** "memory-knowledges specifically or in particular" (*AC* §2850)—knowledge stored in the memory and not organized into a life of use (*The Universal Mirror* §3.1).  
* **Dust:** those who "do not regard spiritual and celestial things, but only what is corporeal and earthly" (*AC* §249); earth "that has no cohesion and no life" (*The Universal Mirror* §3.1).

A desk is a surface for holding **Writing**. In the Swedenborgian view, the "Literal Sense of the Word" (the text itself) is "the basis, the container, and the support" of its spiritual and celestial senses (*Sacred Scripture* §27). It is the "body" without the "soul." Without the spiritual sense, the literal text is **dead**. "The letter killeth, but the spirit giveth life" (2 Cor 3:6). The "Writing Desk" is the altar of the Letter. It supports the "dead" correspondence of ink on paper.

### **5.2 The "Flatness" of the Literal Surface**

The desk is defined by its **Flatness**. It provides a horizontal plane for the act of writing. As established in Section 2.3, the "Natural Unregenerate Mind" operates on the **Horizontal Plane** ("Flat Correspondence"). It cannot see "up" (Vertical Causality); it can only see "across" (Horizontal Analogy). The desk presents a **surface** to the writer. The unregenerate mind is obsessed with **surfaces**—appearances, skin, literal meanings, physical phenomena. It is "flat" because it lacks the **depth** of the spiritual degree. The "Historical Critical Method" (HCM) is the clearest instance of this "flatness": its methods are "perfectly suited to study the external vessel, but they are constitutionally blind to the internal life it contains" (*The Critic Transformed* §3.1)—meticulously mapping the "surface" of the text (the desk) while unable to see the "depth" of the revelation (the spirit).

### **5.3 Writing as the "Fall" from Perception**

The very existence of the "Writing Desk" is a testament to the **Unregenerate State**. According to Swedenborg, the men of the most ancient church "had internal respiration, and only tacit external respiration" (*AC* §607), and with it direct **Perception**. That state belongs to no datable archaeological period: it is the long span of hominin evolution before the Upper Paleolithic, in which the thorax freed by bipedalism gave internal respiration its physiological ground (*A Validation Analysis* §1; *The Bifurcated Inheritance* §II). They had no need for books or desks because "the world itself was the text" (*The Bifurcated Inheritance* §II). They lived in direct correspondence. The "Fall" was the loss of this direct perception: "When internal respiration ceased, external respiration gradually succeeded … and with external respiration a language of words" (*AC* §608). It was not the breakdown of a "bicameral" automatism. The sporadic, commanding "voices" that Jaynes took for the original state of the ancient mind are the wreckage of internal respiration after it broke, not its source (*The Agency of the Ancients* §6.3). The loss necessitated **Writing** and **Doctrine**—external, "dead" codes to preserve the fading wisdom. The symbolic explosion of the Upper Paleolithic marks that necessity, not the beginning of symbolic capacity: humans began to encode in paint, then in stone, and at last in written doctrine, because perception was failing (*The Bifurcated Inheritance* §II).

The Writing Desk is the **prosthetic** of the fallen mind. It is the tool we use because we can no longer *hear* the voice of God in the wind. It is the symbol of the **mediated**, **externalized**, and **flat** mode of cognition that defines the "Ancient Church" (Silver Age) and the subsequent "Jewish Church" (*True Christian Religion* §762). Thus, the Writing Desk is **like** the Raven because it is the **inanimate embodiment** of the **flat, dead, externalized mind**.

**VI. Synthesis: The Solution to the Riddle**

We are now equipped to answer the Mad Hatter’s riddle with theological precision. The Raven and the Writing Desk are not merely "alike" in some trivial, linguistic sense (e.g., "both have a/in/e"). They are **ontologically identical** in their correspondence to the **Natural Unregenerate Mind**.

### **6.1 The Shared Attribute of "Flatness"**

* **The Raven:** Represents the **Intellect confined** to the natural degree. Its "flat notes" (Carroll 1896) are not about musical pitch but about the **quality of reasoning**—what proceeds from a mind that cannot elevate itself is flat in its affection, lacking the richness and detail of spiritual thought. The raven's reasoning "sounds flat" because the mind producing it operates only on the natural plane. It goes "going and returning" over the waters of the Flood, the falsity that still disturbs the natural mind (*AC* §§864–867).  
* **The Desk:** Represents the **Cognition confined** to Literalism. Its physical form provides a **horizontal surface** for writing—the externalized, mediated mode of cognition. The natural man sees only surfaces, effects, and containers, blind to the vertical causes. Both raven and desk are "flat" in different but convergent senses: the raven in what it produces (quality of affection), the desk in what it represents (the horizontal plane of the literal).

### **6.2 The Shared Attribute of "Lifelessness"**

* **The Raven:** As a bird of prey/carrion, it represents the mind that **feeds on death** (falsities, lusts). It has **"no life of its own"** because falsity is merely the distortion of truth; it is a parasite on reality. It is the "shadow" that mimics the eagle.  
* **The Desk:** As an object of wood and joinery, it represents the **"dead" container** of the Letter (*Sacred Scripture* §27). It holds knowledge (scientifics) which, like sand or dust, has **"no cohesion and no life"** until ordered by Love (*The Universal Mirror* §3.1). It is the "corpse" of wisdom, preserved in ink.

### **6.3 The "Natural Unregenerate Mind"**

Therefore, the Raven and the Writing Desk are the **Twin Totems of the Unregenerate State**.

* The **Raven** is the **Active Void**: The mind *thinking* falsely.  
* The **Desk** is the **Passive Void**: The mind *storing* dead facts. They are alike because they are both **fragments of the Gētīg severed from the Mēnōg**. They are the "Outer Dark" where the "Natural Sun" rules—a sun that is "pure fire" and **"dead"** (*DLW* §157).

### **6.4 The Hatter's Confession**

Read in correspondence, the riddle is a confession. As set out in Section 4.4.2, the Hatter's perpetual 6:00 PM stasis — "Time without Progress" — mirrors the Unregenerate Mind that cannot regenerate but only spins in the "flat" circles of its own madness. The Hatter asks, "Why is my mind (Raven) like my world (Desk)?" The answer is: **Because both are dead, flat, and waiting for a Life they cannot generate.**

**VII. Deepening the Analysis: Second and Third-Order Insights**

Beyond the direct solution, this investigation yields deeper insights into the nature of the Swedenborgian worldview and its application to modern epistemology.

### **7.1 The "Science of Lists" and the Necessity of the Riddle**

The "Science of Correspondences" is deeply rooted in the Mesopotamian tradition of *Listenwissenschaft* (The Science of Lists) (*The Celestial Scribe* §2.3). Scribes believed that by listing things (Raven, Desk, Lion, Stone), they could map the mind of God. The Riddle is the **inverse** of the List. The List separates; the Riddle connects.

* **Implication:** The Mad Hatter’s riddle represents the **collapse of Listenwissenschaft**. In the "Wonderland" of the unregenerate/dream mind, the orderly lists of the scribes break down. The Raven merges with the Desk. This suggests that without the "Vertical Influx" of Divine Order, the categories of the natural world dissolve into chaotic similarity. The "Unregenerate Mind" cannot distinguish between a bird and a table because it has lost the "Spiritual Light" that differentiates essences. To the man in the dark, everything is a shadow.

### **7.2 The "Pig Taboo" and the "Raven Taboo"**

*The Semiotics of Sanctity* highlights the "Pig Taboo" among the Shasu as a rejection of "sedentary chaos" and the mud (*The Semiotics of Sanctity* §§2.3, VII). The pig was "unclean" because it was a flat-lander—unable to migrate, stuck in the mud.

* **Insight:** The Raven is the "Pig of the Air." It is unclean for the same reason: it feeds on death and signifies the mind still unsettled by falsity (the "going and returning" of *AC* §§864–867).  
* **Implication:** The "Writing Desk" is the "Pig of the Study." It is the sedentary, fixed, heavy object that traps the mind in the "mud" of literalism. The "nomadic" spirituality of the Shasu rejects the Desk (fixed doctrine) just as it rejects the Pig (fixed livestock). It prefers the **Tent** (Tabernacle) and the **Oral Tradition** (Living Word)—containers that move and breathe.

### **7.3 The "Dead Sun" and the "Black Screen"**

Swedenborg’s description of the **Natural Sun** as "pure fire" and **"dead"** (*DLW* §157) offers a chilling parallel to modern technology.

* **Insight:** The "Writing Desk" of the 21st century is the **Screen** (Computer/Phone). It is a "flat" surface that glows with "dead" light (pixels/fire).  
* **Implication:** The modern "Raven" is the algorithm—the "spying thought" that flies through the digital void, feeding on the "carrion" of data. The riddle "Why is a raven like a writing desk?" is the prophetic riddle of the Information Age. Why is the Algorithm like the Screen? Because both are the **Natural Unregenerate Mind** externalized into a global machine—**flat**, **dead**, and **without life of its own**, yet simulating life through the electric galvanization of the "Natural Sun."

### **7.4 The Proto-Lukan Counter-Thesis: The Raven's Desk**

A notable counter-point is found in the analysis of the **Jamesian Protograph** (Proto-Luke). Here, the term "ravens" (*korakas*) is restored over Matthew's generic "birds" in Jesus’s teaching: "Consider the ravens" (Luke 12:24; Matt 6:26). The reconstruction links this to the **feeding of Elijah**—where ravens provided sustenance (1 Kgs 17:4–6; Proto-Luke Reconstruction n.d., ch. 10).

* **The Inversion:** In the regenerate context (Divine Providence), the "Raven" is transformed. It becomes a servant of the Prophet. The "unclean" scavenger becomes the agent of **Anti-Anxiety**.  
* **The Raven's Desk:** Read beside the riddle, the ravens of this reconstruction set a "Raven's Desk": the table of **Divine Provision**. It opposes the "Writing Desk" of the Scribe (who worries about law/letter). This suggests that even the "unregenerate" symbols can be redeemed when placed in the service of the Prophet (The Word).

**VIII. Conclusion: The Final Correspondence**

The investigation concludes that the riddle "Why is a raven like a writing desk?" is unlocked by the key of **Correspondence**.

| Attribute | The Raven (Animate) | The Writing Desk (Inanimate) | The Natural Unregenerate Mind (Spiritual) |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Geometry** | **Flat Notes** (Monotony) | **Flat Surface** (Horizontal Plane) | **Flat Vision** (Horizontal Analogy/HCM) |
| **Vitality** | **Scavenger** (Feeds on Death) | **Artifact** (Dead Wood/Container) | **Lifeless** (Dead Sun/Selfhood) |
| **Function** | **Falsity** (Dark Thought) | **Literalism** (Dead Letter) | **Void** (Absence of Influx) |
| **Motion** | **Going and Returning** (Restless) | **Fixed** (Sedentary) | **Stasis** (Time without Progress) |

In the "Magian Science" of the ancients, to name a thing was to know its essence. To solve this riddle is to name the state of the soul that is trapped in the mirror. The Raven is the **Thought** of that soul (Falsity); the Desk is the **World** of that soul (Dead Matter). They are alike because they are the two faces of the same ghost—the ghost of the man who has forgotten he is a spirit.
