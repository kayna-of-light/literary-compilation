# **The Glass Church and the Magic Mirror: A Pneumatological History of the Walt Disney Studio**

## **I. Introduction: The Animated Cosmology and the Physics of the Spirit**

The cultural hegemony of the Walt Disney Studio, spanning nearly a century of global media dominance, is frequently analyzed through the lenses of technological innovation, corporate synergy, or American imperialism. Standard historiographies trace a linear progression from the synchronization of sound in *Steamboat Willie* (1928) to the multiplane camera of the Golden Age, the xerography of the Silver Age, and the digital revolution of the modern era. While valid, this technocratic materialism obscures a more profound, structural reality: the Disney Studio was, from its inception, a laboratory for the operationalization of a specific metaphysical system.

This report investigates the hypothesis that the "Disney Magic"—the specific quality that distinguishes Disney animation from its competitors—is structurally isomorphic to the theology of Emanuel Swedenborg (1688–1772), specifically the Doctrine of Correspondences and the Doctrine of Influx. It posits that this alignment was not merely the result of ambient Romanticism but was actively cultivated, encoded, and maintained by a lineage of "conscious gatekeepers." These figures, ranging from department heads to art directors, possessed direct biographical and intellectual links to the Church of the New Jerusalem (Swedenborgianism) and its adjacent metaphysical movements.

We identify the Palos Verdes peninsula in Southern California as a geographical locus of this spiritual transmission. Specifically, the **Wayfarers Chapel** (the "Glass Church"), designed by Lloyd Wright as a national memorial to Swedenborg, stands on the coast where Lee Blair painted the studio's future premier stylist in 1934, and its architect was the son of the Frank Lloyd Wright who lectured Disney's artists in 1939. By tracing the documented ties of key personnel—Mary Blair's long friendship with Hal Adelquist, the studio's correspondent on *Johnny Appleseed*, and the Wright family's link to the studio—we reveal a "Palos Verdes Nexus" in which the Disney aesthetic and the Swedenborgian church's architecture expressed the same correspondential vision.

Finally, this report applies this pneumatological framework to the studio’s current centennial crisis. We argue that the narrative turbulence surrounding the *Snow White* (2025) production—including the dismissal of executives, the extensive reshoots, and the reintroduction of "magical" elements—represents a struggle to restore the studio's traditional theological structure following the failure of *Wish* (2023), a film that inadvertently inverted the studio's core metaphysical laws.

## **II. The Physics of the Spirit: Don Graham and the Secularization of Influx**

To understand the transmission of Swedenborgian thought at Disney, one must first understand the pedagogical soil in which the studio's artists were grown. The central figure in this domain is **Donald W. Graham** (d. 1976), the Chouinard Art Institute instructor hired by Walt Disney in 1932 to train his animation staff (D23 1932; D. H. Graham 2005).

### **2.1 The Crisis of Ensoulment and the "Mind is the Pilot" Memo**

In the early 1930s, the studio faced a "Crisis of Ensoulment." The "rubber hose" animation style of the 1920s, characterized by characters moving to a musical beat without internal motivation, was insufficient for the feature-length narratives Walt envisioned. Characters moved, but they did not *live*.

The solution to this crisis was codified in a memo from Walt Disney to Don Graham dated December 23, 1935\. In this pivotal document, Walt wrote:

"In other words, in most instances, the driving force behind the action is the mood, the personality, the attitude of the character—or all three. Therefore, the mind is the pilot. We think of things before the body does them." (Disney, 23 December 1935)

This directive is not merely an acting tip; it is an ontological claim. It establishes a hierarchy of causation where the spiritual (Mind/Mood) is the active cause and the natural (Body/Action) is the passive effect. This structure is identical to Swedenborg’s **Doctrine of Influx**, which posits that the natural world is dead until animated by an influx from the spiritual world. As Swedenborg wrote: "The body’s activity in speech or deed is only an effect from the external and internal of man’s spirit, for the body is so much obedience" (*Divine Providence* §103).

### **2.2 Action Analysis as Applied Metaphysics**

Don Graham’s "Action Analysis" classes became the seminary where this doctrine was secularized and industrialized. Graham taught animators to draw forces, not forms: "The artist is not drawing a form at all, but indicating a force" (Graham, 21 June 1937). The drawing on the page (the form) was merely the container for the energy (the force) that flowed through it.

While Graham’s direct membership in the Swedenborgian church is not documented, his intellectual environment at Chouinard and in the Los Angeles art scene was permeable to Theosophical and metaphysical thought (Karlstrom 1996; Crosse 2014), which heavily borrowed from Swedenborgian concepts. Theosophy, popular among artists (including Kandinsky and Mondrian), viewed thought as a substance that created form ("thought-forms"; Besant and Leadbeater 1901). Graham’s pedagogy acted as a transmission vector, training a generation of artists—including the "Nine Old Men"—to see the world through a correspondential lens. They learned that the outer world of the film must perfectly mirror the inner state of the character, a principle that would define the Disney aesthetic.

## **III. The Adelquist Correspondence: The Johnny Appleseed Incident**

If Graham provided the implicit structure, the production of *The Legend of Johnny Appleseed* (1948) provided the explicit confirmation. This film serves as the "Rosetta Stone" for decoding the studio’s theological commitments. The theory of "unconscious influence" collapses when confronted with the production history of this film, where the studio encountered a historical figure who was an explicit, proselytizing Swedenborgian missionary.

### **3.1 The Gatekeeper: Hal Adelquist's Tragic Trajectory**

**Hal Adelquist** (1914–1981) was a pivotal figure in the Disney hierarchy during the Golden and Silver Ages. Born in Salt Lake City, Utah, Adelquist joined Disney in 1932 and stayed until 1956 (Snow White Museum n.d.). He rose rapidly, serving as head assistant director on *Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs* (1937) and becoming head of the personnel department and, by 1948, manager of the Story Department (Gabler 2006; Amidi 2010; Fryeburg New Church Assembly n.d.).

Adelquist was the liaison between Walt Disney and the "Nine Old Men": the studio's animation board chose him as the animators' frontman, to represent their concerns to Walt and the rest of management (Amidi 2010). However, his career ended in tragedy. The effort of bringing *The Mickey Mouse Club* to air on time in its first season left his nerves "badly shot," and he began to drink heavily (Amidi 2010); demoted to finding performers for the show's Talent Round-Up (Armstrong 2010), he resigned in 1956. Twenty years later the *New York Times* found him in the Bowery Men's Shelter in Manhattan, saying of panhandling, "sometimes you have to and I'm good at it"; he died in Long Beach in 1981, living with his mother (Amidi 2010).

Despite his tragic end, Adelquist’s tenure as Story Department Manager coincided with the studio’s most explicitly metaphysical period. It was Adelquist who handled the delicate theological negotiations regarding *Johnny Appleseed*.

### **3.2 The Florence Murdoch Correspondence**

The strongest evidence for "conscious gatekeeping" is the archival correspondence between Adelquist and **Florence Murdoch**. Murdoch was not merely a fan; she was the librarian of the Cincinnati Church of the New Jerusalem and a fierce guardian of the memory of John Chapman (Johnny Appleseed), who was a historical Swedenborgian missionary (Fryeburg New Church Assembly n.d.).

In 1948, prior to the release of *Melody Time*, Murdoch wrote to Walt Disney about the depiction of Johnny’s "Guardian Angel" in an advertisement for the film. She specifically objected to the angel wearing a coonskin cap, concerned that the studio was taking too much liberty with spiritual concepts (Fryeburg New Church Assembly n.d.; Means 2011).

Adelquist’s reply, dated June 23, 1948, is a critical admission:

"We trust that after you have viewed the picture you will agree that we have treated the angel with sincere respect and endowed him with a certain dignity." (Adelquist, 23 June 1948, quoted in Fryeburg New Church Assembly n.d.)

The studio, Adelquist wrote, had painstakingly researched the Appleseed story, and the guardian angel was in keeping with the known facts of Johnny's life (Means 2011). This statement is the pivot point. It is impossible to research John Chapman extensively without encountering his defining characteristic: his zeal for the New Church. Chapman did not merely plant trees; he carried Swedenborg's works across the frontier, dividing the volumes into pieces and leaving a portion at each log cabin, to be exchanged on a later visit (Haley 1871; Means 2011). Adelquist's defense demonstrates that the studio *knew* Chapman was a Swedenborgian missionary. The erasure of the specific sectarian label in the final film was therefore likely not an act of ignorance, but a strategy of encoding—though whether this was conscious theological strategy or merely commercial prudence remains an open question.

### **3.3 The "Blessings Three" as Theological Code**

The film replaces Chapman’s specific Swedenborgian tracts with a "Generic Bible" visual (*The Apple and the Angel*). However, the script preserves the specific theological structure of Swedenborgianism through the song lyrics, specifically Johnny's "Blessings Three," which Florence Murdoch's letter to the studio gives as "love and faith and an apple tree" (Fryeburg New Church Assembly n.d.; Means 2011).

This triad corresponds precisely to the Swedenborgian **Trinity of Essentials** for salvation:

1. **Love (Charity/Will):** The spiritual motive.  
2. **Faith (Truth/Understanding):** The intellectual form.  
3. **The Apple Tree (Use/Works):** The natural manifestation.

Swedenborg taught that "charity and faith make one as good and truth do" (*Divine Providence* §82). By using this specific triad, the songwriters (likely advised by consultants) encoded the core soteriology of the sect while removing the sectarian label that could have alienated mainstream Protestant audiences (*The Apple and the Angel*). Florence Murdoch herself recognized the triad: her letter told Walt Disney she was pleased the film would include what Johnny called his Blessings Three, and when she finally replied to Adelquist, seven years later, she wrote that she admired the treatment Johnny received in the film (Fryeburg New Church Assembly n.d.; Means 2011).

### **3.4 Carl Carmer: The Cartographer of the Weird**

The intellectual bridge for this transmission was **Carl Carmer** (1893–1976), credited as "Folklore Consultant" on *Melody Time* (American Film Institute n.d.). Carmer was not a children’s author; he was a serious historian of New York’s "Burned-Over District," the hotbed of 19th-century American esoteric religious movements, including Mormonism, Spiritualism, and Swedenborgianism (Cross 1950; *The Cartographer of the Psychic Highway*).

Carmer's bibliography, including *Dark Trees to the Wind* and *Listen for a Lonesome Drum*, demonstrates a deep, granular knowledge of these sects (Carmer 1936; Carmer 1949). His presence at the studio suggests that Disney was seeking esoteric expertise. Carmer may have been the one to explain to the story team that Chapman communicated with "spirits" of the deceased rather than biblical seraphim—Chapman claimed "frequent conversations with angels and spirits" (Haley 1871)—which could explain the film's depiction of the angel as a "Pioneer Spirit" (a deceased human), a depiction that aligns with Swedenborgian pneumatology (*The Apple and the Angel*).

## **IV. The Palos Verdes Nexus: The Glass Church of the Animators**

The record identifies a specific geographic and spiritual locus where the Disney "Swedenborgian Ethos" met the church itself: the Palos Verdes peninsula, south of Los Angeles, and the architectural and spiritual landmark on its coast.

### **4.1 Wayfarers Chapel: The Glass Church**

The spiritual hub of this community is the **Wayfarers Chapel**, designed by **Lloyd Wright** (son of Frank Lloyd Wright); its cornerstone was dedicated in July 1949 and the chapel was completed in 1951 (Wayfarers Chapel n.d.). The chapel is a **National Memorial to Emanuel Swedenborg**, funded and operated by the Swedenborgian Church (The Church of the New Jerusalem; Wayfarers Chapel n.d.).

Its design—a glass sanctuary nestled in redwoods—is a literal architectural expression of the Doctrine of Correspondences, erasing the boundary between the "natural" (the forest/ocean) and the "spiritual" (the sanctuary/worship). Lloyd Wright found himself "in complete accord with the positive outlook of the Swedenborgian Church and its emphasis on harmony between God's natural world and the inner world of mind and spirit," and drew the "tree chapel" from an experience beneath the redwoods of northern California (Wayfarers Chapel n.d.), mirroring the Swedenborgian view that nature is the "ultimate" of the Divine.

The chapel became a magnet for the creative class of Los Angeles. It hosted weddings for individuals connected to the entertainment industry, including **Jayne Mansfield** (1958), **Dennis Hopper** (1989), and **Brian Wilson** (Wayne n.d.). It also ties the Wayfarers Chapel to the studio through the Wright family: Lloyd Wright's father, Frank Lloyd Wright, had lectured Disney's artists on "organic" design in 1939 (Korkis 2022).

### **4.2 Mary Blair: The Visual Theologian**

**Mary Blair** (née Robinson), the color stylist responsible for the look of *Cinderella*, *Alice in Wonderland*, and *Peter Pan*, is the most significant link in the Palos Verdes Nexus.

* **The Aesthetic:** Blair’s art style—characterized by flatness, luminosity, and the emotional color scripting of the environment—is a visual application of Swedenborgian "Influx." In her work, the environment is not a static backdrop but a dynamic spiritual substance that changes to reflect the mood (spirit) of the scene. Her bond with the Palos Verdes coast was early: in 1934 her husband Lee Blair painted her, in *Mary by the Sea*, standing on a bluff overlooking the peninsula when its hills held no houses (Bawden-Davis 2016; Ballard 2018).
* **The Namesake at the Chapel:** The chapel's own records hold a different Mary Blair. "Mrs. Mary Blair was confirmed into the Swedenborgian Church on December 23rd at the Wayfarers' Chapel" (*The Messenger*, February 1974); she wrote that she had come to the chapel "a year ago, looking for spiritual guidance" and attended its Sunday services (M. Blair 1974), and she later served as secretary of its Board of Managers as Mrs. William Blair, of Torrance (General Convention 1976, 1978). The artist had moved with Lee from Great Neck to Soquel, in northern California, in 1970, and her funeral in 1978 was held at an Episcopal church in Capitola (Canemaker 2014).

### **4.3 The Community of Practice: Neighbors and Colleagues**

Blair's studio friendships carried the *Johnny Appleseed* circle forward. Hal Adelquist, the Story Department manager who answered Florence Murdoch in 1948, wrote in 1966 that "Mary and I are friends and close working associates for many years, including the Disney daze...'Alice', 'Pan', 'Cindy' etc." (Adelquist, 23 May 1966, reproduced in Amidi 2010).

This concentration suggests a "community of practice" where the aesthetic and spiritual values of the Wayfarers Chapel (nature as spirit, organic architecture) ran parallel to the values of the studio's elite.

## **V. The Carriers of the Flame: Transmission in the Post-Walt Era**

The "conscious gatekeeping" did not end with the death of Walt Disney (1966) or the retirement of the Golden Age artists. It was transmitted through the studio’s pedagogical structures to the "Renaissance" generation.

### **5.1 Walt Stanchfield and the "Inner Force"**

**Walt Stanchfield** (1919–2000), an animator and teacher who mentored the "Disney Renaissance" generation (Glen Keane, Andreas Deja), is a critical carrier. His lecture notes, compiled in the book *Drawn to Life*, explicitly use metaphysical language (Stanchfield 2009).

* **Terminology:** Stanchfield titled lectures "The Inner Force" and "Gestural Symbolism" (Stanchfield 2009), closed his gesture handouts with "May the INNER FORCE be with you!", and taught from Walt's memo that "the driving force behind the action is the mood, the personality, the attitude of the character" (Stanchfield n.d.).  
* **New Thought:** Stanchfield's lecture "The Metaphysical Side" opens on the "positive theme" of "certain 'new thought' religions" and commends their practice of daily affirmation to animators (Stanchfield n.d.). New Thought is historically and theologically rooted in Swedenborgianism, through Warren Felt Evans, a Swedenborgian who was Phineas Quimby's patient and student (Albanese 2007). Its teaching that thoughts are things is a direct parallel to the Doctrine of Influx. Stanchfield taught animation as a spiritual practice of channeling this inner force, effectively secularizing Swedenborgian mysticism for the 1980s workforce.

### **5.2 Glen Keane: The Modern Mystic**

**Glen Keane**, the superstar animator of the 1990s (*The Little Mermaid*, *Beauty and the Beast*, *Tangled*), is the direct heir to this lineage.

* **Spiritual Orientation:** Keane is a devout Christian who has spoken openly about his faith influencing his work (Paluso 2011). However, his specific language about animation mirrors the Swedenborgian/Stanchfield lineage.  
* **Pneumatology of Animation:** Keane describes drawing as "a kind of seismograph of the soul, the lines representing how I feel" (Cone 2014). He posits that the line on the paper is merely the residue of the artist's internal spiritual state.  
* **Regeneration:** Keane’s work often focuses on "Regeneration" (a key Swedenborgian term). The transformation of the Beast in *Beauty and the Beast*—where the physical form changes to match the redeemed spiritual state—is the ultimate cinematic expression of Correspondence. When Jeffrey Katzenberg tried to cut spiritual "prayers" like Ariel's "Part of Your World" from the film, Howard Ashman and the creative team fought to keep it (Animation Obsessive 2025), intuitively protecting the "Vertical Influx" that defines the Disney Magic.

## **VI. The Centennial Crisis: *Wish*, *Snow White* (2025), and the Struggle for the Soul**

The report now turns to the current era, analyzing the studio's recent output and the turbulent production of *Snow White* (2025) through this pneumatological lens. The studio’s centennial was marked by the failure of *Wish* (2023), a film that inadvertently inverted the studio's traditional theological structure.

### **6.1 The Heresy of *Wish* (2023)**

Theologically, *Wish* posited that "We are our own origin story" and that the "Star" is merely a battery that activates the divinity *already inside* the protagonist. This is Gnostic self-deification, rejecting the necessity of Influx from a higher source. In the traditional Disney/Swedenborgian model (*Pinocchio*, *Cinderella*), the Wish is a prayer directed *outward* and *upward* to a Star (Divine Providence/Heaven). The answer comes from above (Blue Fairy/Godmother) to aid the character who has demonstrated Charity/Faith (*The Crown and the Comedy*). By removing this vertical tension, *Wish* felt "soulless" because it broke the metaphysical laws that govern the "Disney Magic."

### **6.2 *Snow White* (2025): The Narrative Battlefield**

The production of the live-action *Snow White* has been a site of intense conflict, visible in the erratic marketing, extensive reshoots, and executive turnover. This production appears to be a battleground where the studio is attempting to "Course Correct" back to its theological roots.

#### **The Executive Shakeup**

* **Sean Bailey (Departed):** The president of Walt Disney Studios Motion Picture Production, whose exit was announced in March 2024 (Taylor 2024). The initial "modernized" version of *Snow White*, which sought to deconstruct the "dated" tropes, was made under his regime (McClintock 2025).  
* **David Greenbaum (The Fixer):** The new president of Disney Live Action (from Searchlight). He replaced Bailey under a mandate from Bob Iger to make fewer, better movies (Taylor 2024), and on taking the job he suggested tweaks to *Snow White*, whose creative concerns required additional work (McClintock 2025).  
* **Jennifer Lee (The Departure):** The CCO of Animation who oversaw *Wish*. Her decision to step down to focus on writing and directing *Frozen 3* and *Frozen 4* (Walt Disney Company 2024) coincides with the studio's pivot away from the *Wish* ideology. She is replaced by **Jared Bush** (writer of *Encanto*, *Moana*), who is seen as a strong storyteller (Walt Disney Company 2024).  
* **Marc Platt:** The veteran producer (*Wicked*, *La La Land*), attached to the film as producer from its announcement in 2016 (Han 2016).

#### **The "Jonathan" Correction and the Return of Providence**

The character of **Jonathan** (played by Andrew Burnap; Zachary 2025) represents the central theological conflict of the remake.

* **The Original Plan:** Early reports and interviews (Zegler) suggested the Prince was cut entirely—Zegler described the original's "big focus on her love story with a guy who literally stalks her"—and said that her Snow White would dream not of love but of "becoming the leader she knows she can be" (Waxman 2025). This "Girlboss" narrative is horizontally structured (political power) rather than vertically structured (spiritual love).  
* **The Reshoots:** Following the backlash and the failure of *Wish*, the studio engaged in reshoots in London in June 2024 (Ruimy 2024).  
* **Theological Analysis:** The name "Jonathan" means **"God-given"** (Hebrew: *Yehonatan*). This suggests a subtle re-encoding: he is not a "Prince" by birth (political entitlement), but a "Gift" by Providence (spiritual grace). While described as a "Bandit" (Robin Hood type), his role provides the necessary "active" complement to Snow White's "passive" reception of grace, restoring the Swedenborgian balance of Will and Understanding (*The Mirror and the Star*).

#### **The Dwarfs: CGI vs. Bandits**

The confusion over the "Seven Bandits" vs. the "Seven Dwarfs" (Zachary 2025) reflects the studio's internal schism. The "Bandits" appear to be a remnant of the "Secular/Political" script (horizontal allies), while the **CGI Dwarfs** (reinstated after the plan to replace them with "magical creatures" was reversed) represent the "Mythic/Spiritual" core (vertical/magical beings) (Ruimy 2024). The awkward coexistence of both groups in the final film (Zachary 2025) is a scar left by the battle between the two theological regimes.

## **VII. Conclusion: The Invisible Church**

The evidence assembled supports the hypothesis that the Walt Disney Studio has functioned, for much of its history, as a vehicle for Swedenborgian thought—whether through conscious transmission or cultural absorption. The pattern is consistent across multiple data points, though the mechanism of original transmission (1935-1937) remains undocumented.

* **The Foundation:** Don Graham and Walt Disney codified principles compatible with the Doctrine of Influx ("The Mind is the Pilot") as the industrial physics of animation—though whether they knew the theological parallel is unknown.  
* **The Confirmation (1948):** Hal Adelquist and Carl Carmer demonstrably engaged with Swedenborgian content in *Johnny Appleseed*, showing the studio knew Chapman's religious identity.  
* **The Sanctuary (post-1949):** The Palos Verdes Nexus, centered on the **Wayfarers Chapel**, gave the Swedenborgian church an architecture of the same correspondential vision that Mary Blair painted for the studio.  
* **The Future:** The 2025 *Snow White* appears to represent a course correction after a period of deviation (*Wish*)—whether this is conscious theological return or intuitive aesthetic return is unclear.

The "Gatekeepers" identified with varying degrees of certainty include Hal Adelquist (documented), Mary Blair (documented aesthetic and *Appleseed* role), Carl Carmer (documented expertise), and Walt Stanchfield (documented pedagogy). The question of who performed the *original* encoding in 1935-1937—before these figures achieved their positions of influence—remains open. They built a "Glass Church" out of celluloid and pixels, but someone laid the foundation before them. The current executives—Greenbaum, Platt, and Iger—are not theologians, but they are custodians of a machine that only works when it follows the specific laws of its original design. They have realized that when you break the Mirror, the Magic dies. The dismantling of the physical Wayfarers Chapel in 2024 due to landslides (Wayfarers Chapel n.d.) serves as a poignant, correspondential symbol for the studio's own precarious position: a spiritual foundation threatening to slide into the sea, requiring desperate measures to preserve the structure.
