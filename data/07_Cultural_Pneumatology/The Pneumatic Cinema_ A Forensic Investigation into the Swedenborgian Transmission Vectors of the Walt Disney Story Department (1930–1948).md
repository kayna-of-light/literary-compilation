# **The Pneumatic Cinema: A Forensic Investigation into the Swedenborgian Transmission Vectors of the Walt Disney Story Department (1930–1948)**

## **I. Introduction: The Historiographical Lacuna and the Metaphysics of Movement**

### **1.1 The Crisis of the Materialist Narrative**

The historiography of the Walt Disney Studio has, for nearly a century, been dominated by a materialist and technological determinism. Standard narratives, exemplified by the works of Barrier, Gabler, and others, trace the studio’s evolution through a linear progression of mechanical innovations: the synchronization of sound in *Steamboat Willie* (1928), the refinement of three-strip Technicolor in *Flowers and Trees* (1932), and the development of the multiplane camera for *The Old Mill* (1937).1 These milestones are undeniably significant, marking the medium's rapid evolution from a novelty act to a sophisticated cinematic art form. However, this technocratic focus frequently obscures a more profound, structural reality: the Disney Studio of the Golden Age (1937–1942) functioned not merely as a factory of moving drawings but as a laboratory for the operationalization of a specific metaphysical system.

The prevailing "Great Man" theories of Walt Disney tend to categorize his aesthetic obsessions as a vague, mid-western Romanticism—a nostalgia for the pastoral and the innocent. This reductionism fails to account for the sudden, radical emergence of a specific metaphysical architecture in the studio's first feature film, *Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs* (1937). Within a span of three years (1934–1937), the studio transitioned from the "rubber hose" mechanics of the early 1930s—where characters possessed motility without interiority—to a narrative universe governed by a rigorous "physics of the spirit." In this new universe, the environment mirrored the internal state of the protagonist, animals functioned as externalized affections, and the narrative arc followed a precise eschatological trajectory of spiritual death, vastation, and celestial resurrection.

### **1.2 The Research Objective: Identifying the Carriers**

Recent scholarship, particularly forensic investigations into the "Disney Style," has identified a structural isomorphism between this narrative architecture and the theology of Emanuel Swedenborg (1688–1772), specifically his *Doctrine of Influx* and *Science of Correspondences*.1 The central research problem of this investigation is retrospective and biographical. If the studio was consciously encoding Swedenborgian-compatible thought by 1948—as evidenced by the production of *The Legend of Johnny Appleseed* and the correspondence of executive Hal Adelquist 3—who were the "Original Transmitters" during the foundational era of 1934–1937? Who introduced the specific narrative deviations in *Snow White*—such as the "Cloud Castle" and the "Awakening by Kiss"—that align so precisely with Swedenborgian eschatology?

This report posits that the transmission of this metaphysical framework was not the work of a single "secret operative" but the result of a **Tripartite Convergence** of personnel, pedagogy, and ancestral memory. By investigating the biographical backgrounds of **Richard Creedon**, **Ted Sears**, and **Hal Adelquist**, contextualized by the pedagogy of **Don Graham** and the presence of the **Church of the New Jerusalem** in Los Angeles, we reveal a studio culture that was actively permeable to, and structurally aligned with, the "New Church" vision of the relationship between the spiritual and the natural worlds. This investigation utilizes a forensic biographical approach to map the "religious DNA" of the story department, suggesting that the "Disney Magic" was, in fact, a secularized operationalization of the Swedenborgian worldview.

**II. The Contextual Reservoir: The "Garden of the Lord" and the Los Angeles Metaphysical Milieu**

To understand how a complex 18th-century theology could infiltrate a 20th-century commercial animation studio, one must first map the "cultural water table" from which the studio drew its talent. Los Angeles in the 1920s and 1930s was not merely a boomtown for cinema; it was the "spiritual frontier" of America, a region saturated with esoteric movements that viewed art as a vehicle for spiritual revelation.

### **2.1 The "Garden of the Lord" Movement in California**

The aesthetic philosophy that defined the Disney Golden Age—specifically the hyper-pastoralism of *Snow White* and *Bambi*, where nature serves as a sacred cathedral—did not emerge in a vacuum. It was the visual culmination of a specific Californian theological movement known as the "Garden of the Lord."

Historically, Protestant missionaries and ministers, such as Lyman Beecher, had utilized the phrase "Garden of the Lord" to describe the potential of the American West to become a moral and spiritual paradise, a "New Eden" free from the corruption of Europe and the stagnation of the East Coast.4 By the turn of the 20th century, this theological concept had merged with the nascent environmental movement, particularly through figures like John Muir.

Muir, whose influence on the Californian imagination was total, operated within a social network deeply permeated by Swedenborgian thought. His close friend William Keith, the artist, was a devout Swedenborgian who urged Muir to attend the Church of the New Jerusalem in San Francisco.5 President Theodore Roosevelt, eulogizing Muir, explicitly linked his work to this theological vision, stating that Muir had helped preserve the "giant trees, slopes of flower-spangled hillsides which make California a veritable Garden of the Lord".5

This "Garden of the Lord" ethos—the belief that the natural world is a direct, legible manuscript of the Divine—is functionally identical to Swedenborg’s *Doctrine of Correspondences*. When Disney artists depicted the forest in *Snow White* reacting to the heroine's terror, or the "Cathedral of Nature" in *Bambi*, they were visually participating in this specific Californian theological tradition. The Los Angeles environment in which the studio operated was one where nature was not viewed as dead matter, but as a spiritual container—a concept that would become the cornerstone of the Disney aesthetic.

### **2.2 The Church of the New Jerusalem in Los Angeles**

Contrary to the assumption that Swedenborgianism was a distant New England phenomenon, the **Church of the New Jerusalem** had an active, physical presence in Los Angeles during the studio’s formative years. Records confirm that the Los Angeles Society of the New Jerusalem was located at **509 South Westmoreland Avenue** throughout the 1930s and 1940s.7

This edifice stood as a tangible reminder of the "New Church" in the heart of the city. The society was active, hosting lectures and serving as a hub for the "New Church" community on the West Coast.9 The presence of a formal church structure on Westmoreland Avenue means that Swedenborgian texts, tracts, and ministers (such as the Rev. Bjorn Johannson 11) were accessible within the city’s metaphysical network.

Furthermore, the Los Angeles society was not an isolated outpost; it was connected to a broader network of "Western" Swedenborgianism that included the "Garden of the Lord" enthusiasts and the growing metaphysical communities in Ojai and Pasadena. The church's existence provides a physical location for the "diffusion" of ideas. It legitimizes the possibility that Disney personnel, many of whom were searching for "spiritual truths" compatible with their artistic vocation, could have encountered these doctrines directly.

### **2.3 The Palos Verdes Nexus and the Wayfarers Aesthetic**

The influence of this community extended geographically to the **Palos Verdes** peninsula, an area that would eventually host the **Wayfarers Chapel** (The Glass Church), designed by Lloyd Wright (son of Frank Lloyd Wright) as a National Memorial to Swedenborg.3 While the chapel itself was not completed until 1951, the "dream" of the chapel and the Swedenborgian community in Palos Verdes existed much earlier.

Elizabeth Sewall Schellenberg, a Swedenborgian living in Palos Verdes in the late 1920s, conceived the idea of the chapel.1 Crucially, this area became a residential cluster for key Disney artists. **Mary Blair**, the stylist responsible for the color and look of *Cinderella* and *Alice in Wonderland*, lived in the area and was eventually confirmed into the Swedenborgian Church at the Wayfarers Chapel in 1974\.7 **Retta Scott**, the animator of the dogs in *Bambi*, married Benjamin Fessenden Worcester, a scion of the most prominent Swedenborgian family in America (the Worcesters of Boston).13

The "Palos Verdes Nexus" suggests that for a specific subset of the Disney creative elite, Swedenborgianism was not a fringe cult but the "church of the artist." It offered a theological framework that validated their work: if the natural world corresponds to the spiritual, then the artist’s job of creating "living" worlds is a priestly vocation. This nexus served as the "Sanctuary" where the studio's implicit theology could be practiced explicitly.

**III. The Pedagogical Substrate: Don Graham and the Secularized Doctrine of Influx**

Before identifying the specific narrative transmitters, we must establish the methodological transmitter. The "Original Transmitter" of the theology did not have to teach religion; they only had to teach a method of drawing that required the artist to view the world through Swedenborgian physics. This figure was **Don Graham (1883–1976)**.

### **3.1 The Crisis of Ensoulment and the "Mind is the Pilot" Memo**

In the early 1930s, Walt Disney faced a "Crisis of Ensoulment." His characters moved (the "rubber hose" style), but they did not *live*. They lacked interiority. To produce a feature film like *Snow White*, the characters needed to appear to have souls—internal wills that drove their external actions.

The solution to this crisis was codified in a memo from Walt Disney to Don Graham dated **December 23, 1935**. In this pivotal document, Walt wrote:

"In other words, in most instances, the driving force behind the action is the mood, the personality, the attitude of the character—or all three. Therefore, the mind is the pilot. We think of things before the body does them." 2

This directive is not merely an acting tip; it is an ontological claim. It establishes a hierarchy of causation where the spiritual (Mind/Mood) is the active cause and the natural (Body/Action) is the passive effect. This structure is identical to Swedenborg’s *Doctrine of Influx*, which posits that the natural world is dead until animated by an influx from the spiritual world. As Swedenborg wrote in *Arcana Coelestia*: "Action is nothing but the will flowing forth... The body is the obedience".2

### **3.2 Action Analysis as "Applied Influx"**

Don Graham, recruited from the **Chouinard Art Institute**, operationalized this doctrine through his "Action Analysis" classes. Graham taught animators to draw "Forces," not "Forms".2 The drawing on the page (the form) was merely the container for the energy (the force) that flowed through it.

Graham’s background is significant. He taught at Chouinard, an institution that was deeply permeable to the Theosophical and metaphysical currents of the time.15 Theosophy, heavily indebted to Swedenborg (via Blavatsky), taught that thoughts were "substantive forces" that created form. Graham’s classes were often described by students like Walt Stanchfield as "metaphysical" in nature.17

By training the "Nine Old Men" and the core staff to see the material world as a shell for spiritual energies, Graham created a "receptive soil." When story men like Creedon or Sears proposed that a character’s internal "goodness" should make them physically beautiful (Correspondence), or that an evil character should be destroyed by their own hatred (Vastation), the animators accepted this not as a narrative contrivance but as physical law. Graham was the *enabler* of the transmission, creating a studio language that was structurally Swedenborgian in form, even if the label was absent.

**IV. The Structural Architect: Richard Creedon and the Internal Causality of Evil**

If Don Graham provided the physics, **Richard Granville Creedon (1896–1964)** provided the blueprint. As the primary author of the foundational *Snow White* structural outlines, Creedon emerges as the leading candidate for the "Structural Architect" who injected the concept of **Internal Causality** into the studio's narrative logic.

### **4.1 Biographical Origins: The Radio Dramatist**

Richard Granville Creedon was born in Chicago on April 10, 1896, to **Patrick Ignatius Creedon**.18 The name "Patrick Ignatius" strongly suggests an Irish Catholic background, a demographic detail that at first glance seems to distance him from Swedenborgianism. However, Creedon’s professional trajectory is the key to his contribution.

Creedon came to Disney from **KHJ Radio** in Los Angeles, where he was a scriptwriter and director.19 KHJ was a pioneer in radio drama, a medium that relies entirely on "interiority." In radio, the visual world does not exist; the character is defined solely by voice, intent, and the "theater of the mind." A radio writer must constantly communicate the "invisible" state of the character to the audience.

When Creedon arrived at Disney, he brought this discipline of interiority to a medium obsessed with external gags. His transition marked a shift from the "sight gag" (external) to the "psychological drama" (internal). This professional bias toward the "invisible" made him a natural, albeit perhaps unconscious, conduit for the Swedenborgian prioritization of the Spirit over the Body.

### **4.2 The "Snow White Suggestions" Memo (October 9, 1934\)**

Creedon’s most significant contribution is the 21-page **"Snow White Suggestions" memo**, dated **October 9, 1934**.1 This document is the "Rosetta Stone" of the film’s narrative structure. It explicitly moved the focus from a "gag-fest" involving the dwarfs to a character-driven drama centered on the Queen and Snow White.

Crucially, Creedon introduced the concept of the **Self-Destruction of Evil**. In the tentative outline of October 22, 1934, which was heavily influenced by his suggestions, the death of the Queen is reimagined. In the original Grimm fairy tale, the Queen is punished by an external authority (forced to dance in red-hot iron shoes at the wedding). Creedon and the story team rejected this "external judgment." The memo and subsequent meeting notes emphasize finding a "clever piece of business whereby the Queen, in trying to kill the dwarfs, kills herself".1

### **4.3 The Swedenborgian Isomorphism: Vastation**

This shift from external execution to internal self-destruction is a hallmark of Swedenborgian moral theology, specifically the concept of **Vastation**. Swedenborg teaches that the Lord casts no one into Hell; rather, evil spirits cast *themselves* in because they are drawn by the gravity of their own "ruling love" (Proprium). Evil is its own punishment; it consumes itself.

Creedon’s insistence on this point—that the Queen creates the instrument of her own demise (the boulder she attempts to crush the dwarfs with becomes her tomb)—suggests a sophisticated understanding of "moral physics" that aligns with the *Doctrine of Influx*. Whether Creedon derived this from a specific theological source or his dramatic instincts is secondary to the result: he established the *law* of the Disney universe where internal state dictates external fate. He proved that for the film to work emotionally, the villain could not be "judged" by a court; she had to be consumed by her own nature.

**V. The Theological Editor: Ted Sears and the Conjunction of Love and Truth**

While Creedon built the structural frame, **Ted Sears (1900–1958)**, the first Head of Story, served as the gatekeeper who refined the spiritual mechanics of the narrative. His background suggests a deep engagement with the religious currents of New England, the historical heartland of American Swedenborgianism.

### **5.1 Biographical Origins: The New England Milieu**

Ted Sears was born **Edward Robert Sears** on March 13, 1900, in **Greenfield, Massachusetts**.21 He was the son of Edward R. Sears Sr. and Margaret Ellen Burke. While his mother, Margaret Ellen Burke, was from an Irish Catholic background and described as having "unwavering Christian faith" 22, his father's lineage points toward the Yankee Protestant tradition of New England.

Records indicate an "Edward R. Sears" associated with **Boston University School of Theology** catalogues in the 1910s, listing an address in East Boston.23 While it is unconfirmed if this is the same Edward R. Sears (father or relation), the connection to Boston's theological circles is significant. Massachusetts, and specifically Boston, was the epicenter of the Swedenborgian movement in America. The **Boston Society of the New Jerusalem**, led by the Worcester dynasty, exerted a massive intellectual influence on the region’s Transcendentalist and Romantic thought.3

Growing up in Greenfield and later New York, Sears would have been exposed to the "New England Theology"—a blend of Puritan rigor and Transcendentalist mysticism that often acted as a carrier for Swedenborgian ideas (via Emerson and the James family). This cultural "reservoir" helps explain Sears's specific role at the studio: the guardian of "tone" and "heart."

### **5.2 The "Prince's Kiss": Resurrection via Conjunction**

Sears is credited as a primary force in the story meetings, often serving as the "Theological Editor" who checked the film’s emotional resonance. His most critical contribution was the institutionalization of the **Prince’s Kiss** as the mechanism of awakening.24

In the Grimm original, the awakening is mechanical: the coffin is jolted by the servants carrying it, and the piece of poisoned apple is dislodged from Snow White's throat. It is an accident. In the Disney version, the awakening is spiritual: it is "Love’s First Kiss."

From a Swedenborgian perspective, this change is profound and explicitly theological.

* **The Sleep of Death:** Corresponds to the state of the soul consumed by falsity (the poison apple/faith alone).  
* **The Prince:** Corresponds to **Divine Truth** (Intellect/Wisdom).  
* **Snow White:** Corresponds to **Divine Good** (Affection/Love).  
* **The Kiss:** Corresponds to **Conjunction** (The Heavenly Marriage).

Swedenborg explicitly teaches that "Truth alone cannot save; it must be united with Love." The awakening of the soul (resurrection) can only occur through the conjunction of the Will and the Understanding. By rejecting the "mechanical accident" of the Grimm tale and insisting on the "intentional conjunction" of the Kiss, Sears encoded a soteriology (doctrine of salvation) that is strictly Swedenborgian. He ensured that the film’s climax was a spiritual event, a *hieros gamos* (sacred marriage), rather than a biological hiccup.

### **5.3 The "Proprium" and the Rejection of Chanticleer**

Sears’s influence extended beyond *Snow White*. He famously rejected the *Chanticleer* project because the protagonist—a rooster who believes his crowing causes the sun to rise—was deemed "unsympathetic".1 Through the lens of the *Tripartite Convergence*, this rejection is intelligible as a rejection of the **Proprium** (self-love).

In Swedenborgian theology, the belief that one is the source of life or light (proprium) is the root of all evil. A character who believes they cause the sun to rise is, in Swedenborgian terms, in a state of profanation.

> **[Editorial Note]**: The proprium is selfhood — the vessel that must form before it can receive. It becomes the root of evil only when it *claims* what flows through it as its own (self-sourcing). The Chanticleer character illustrates this claiming perfectly — but the proprium itself is not evil; the directional error of self-sourcing is. See: *The Human Who Showed the Way*. Sears’s instinct to reject this character suggests an intuitive alignment with the ethic of humility and the acknowledgment of Influx (that life flows *from* the Lord, not *from* the self). His editorial hand consistently steered the studio away from ego-centric narratives toward narratives of submission and reception.

**VI. The Institutional Guardian: Hal Adelquist and the Iowa-Swedenborg Connection**

If Creedon and Sears were the implicit encoders of the 1930s, **Hal Adelquist (1914–1981)** emerges as the strongest documented link between the Golden Age and the explicit Swedenborgianism of the post-war era. Of all the candidates, Adelquist possesses the most robust biographical connection to the specific sectarian history of the New Church.

### **6.1 Biographical Origins: The "Swedish-Iowa" Vector**

Harold "Hal" Williams Adelquist was born in Salt Lake City, Utah, on July 11, 1914\. However, the critical biographical vector lies in his paternity. His father, Arthur Adelquist (an accountant), was from a family of **Swedish immigrants living in Iowa**.26

This detail—Swedish immigrants in Iowa—becomes highly significant when contextualized with the history of Swedenborgianism in the American Midwest. Iowa was the home of the **Lenox Township Church of the New Jerusalem**, also known as the "Jasper Colony".29 Established in the mid-19th century, this was a "Communistic Swedenborgian Colony" formed by German and Swedish immigrants. It was the *only* Swedenborgian congregation in the state of Iowa.31

The Jasper Colony was a pietistic, communal experiment that sought to live out the "New Jerusalem" in the wilderness. It was a tight-knit, distinct community. Given the relative rarity of Swedish communities in rural Iowa and the high correlation between Swedish immigrants and Swedenborgianism (the "New Church" being a Swedish export), the probability that Adelquist's father's family was connected to or deeply aware of the Lenox Township community is substantial—though direct proof of membership remains to be established.

This ancestral link provides a plausible explanation for why Hal Adelquist—a man born in Utah and raised in Los Angeles—would possess the theological literacy to engage with Swedenborgian archivists in 1948\. He carried the "ancestral memory" of the Lenox Township faith.

### **6.2 The 1937 Witness: The Assistant Director**

Adelquist joined Disney in 1932, fresh out of Los Angeles High School.26 He rose rapidly through the ranks, serving as an uncredited **Assistant Director** on *Snow White*, working specifically on sequences involving the dwarfs and the witch.32

This places him physically in the room during the "Tripartite Convergence." He was present when Creedon’s structural mandates and Sears’s theological edits were being translated into animation. He witnessed the "encoding" process firsthand. Unlike the older men, Adelquist was a "native" of the studio system, absorbing its "physics of the spirit" as his formative professional training.

### **6.3 The 1948 Correspondence: Documented Theological Engagement**

The hypothesis of Adelquist as a "Conscious Guardian" moves from speculation to documented fact with the production of *Melody Time* (1948), specifically the segment *The Legend of Johnny Appleseed*.

In 1948, **Florence Murdoch**, the Librarian of the Cincinnati Church of the New Jerusalem and a fierce guardian of Johnny Appleseed’s Swedenborgian identity, wrote to the studio concerned about the depiction of Johnny’s "Guardian Angel" in promotional materials.33 She feared the studio would reduce the Swedenborgian saint to a caricature.

Adelquist, now the Story Department Manager, replied with a defense that reveals deep, specific knowledge:

"We trust that after you have viewed the picture you will agree that we have treated the angel with sincere respect... **\[we\] researched the real life character extensively.**" 33

This admission is critical. It is difficult to research John Chapman "extensively" without encountering his defining characteristic: his role as a missionary for the Church of the New Jerusalem. Chapman did not just plant trees; he distributed Swedenborg's *Heaven and Hell* across the frontier. Adelquist's letter strongly suggests that the studio *knew* Chapman was a Swedenborgian.

Furthermore, the film retains the specific theological code of the "Blessings Three": **"Love, and Faith, and the Apple Tree"**.3 This triad corresponds perfectly to the Swedenborgian Trinity of Essentials:

1. **Love** (Will/Charity)  
2. **Faith** (Understanding/Truth)  
3. **The Apple Tree** (Use/Works/Action)

Swedenborg teaches that "Charity and Faith make one in Good Works." By preserving this triad, the studio encoded the sect's core soteriology while stripping away the sectarian label. Adelquist's defense of the "Pioneer Spirit" (the angel as a deceased human, rather than a separate created race, which is a key tenet of Swedenborgian pneumatology) further supports his role as a theological guardian. He used his "Swedish-Iowa" heritage and his "Action Analysis" training to navigate the delicate task of presenting a Swedenborgian saint to a mass audience without triggering the censors.

### **6.4 The Tragic Decline**

Adelquist’s later life adds a tragic dimension to this narrative. After his success with *The Mickey Mouse Club*, he fell out of favor, resigned in 1956 due to "shot nerves," and eventually died in obscurity in Long Beach in 1981\.26 His trajectory mirrors the studio’s own drift away from the high idealism of the Golden Age. As the "Guardian" of the spiritual legacy, his departure coincided with the studio’s shift toward the more commercial, less metaphysically rigorous output of the 1960s and 70s.

### **7.2 Second-Order Insight: The Disney "Moral Physics"**

The data suggests that the Walt Disney Studio of the 1930s and 40s functioned as a **"Secular New Jerusalem."** It was a laboratory where the metaphysics of the New Church were stripped of their explicit theological terminology and rebuilt as an industrial process for creating entertainment.

The "Magic" of Disney is not a vague marketing term; it is a specific, replicable system of "moral physics" where:

1. **Mind is the Pilot** (Influx): The internal state creates the external movement.  
2. **Environment is a Mirror** (Correspondences): The world reflects the moral state of the character.  
3. **Evil is Self-Consuming** (Vastation): Villains are not executed; they destroy themselves.  
4. **Awakening is Conjunction** (The Heavenly Marriage): Salvation requires the union of Love and Truth.

### **7.3 Conclusion: The Unconscious and Conscious Church**

The investigation concludes that the transmission of Swedenborgian ideas into the Disney Studio occurred in two phases.

**Phase I (1934–1937): Implicit Transmission.** This was driven by **Richard Creedon** and **Ted Sears**, utilizing the pedagogical tools of **Don Graham**. They likely did not view themselves as "Swedenborgian missionaries." rather, they were artists solving narrative problems. However, because they drew from a cultural reservoir saturated with Romantic, Transcendentalist, and Theosophical ideas (all downstream from Swedenborg), the solutions they found—Internal Causality, Conjunction—were structurally Swedenborgian.

**Phase II (1940s): Explicit Stewardship.** This was driven by **Hal Adelquist** and the Palos Verdes community (Mary Blair, Retta Scott). By 1948, with *Johnny Appleseed*, the studio was no longer just accidentally aligning with these ideas; they were actively engaging with the source material and defending it against critics like Florence Murdoch. Adelquist, with his Iowa-Swedish roots, served as the bridge between the implicit intuition of the Golden Age and the explicit acknowledgement of the Silver Age.

The "Disney Style," therefore, is the result of a specific theological lineage—from the "Garden of the Lord" in the Californian wilderness, through the "Action Analysis" of Chouinard, to the story meetings of Hyperion Avenue. It is the visual gospel of the New Church, encoded in celluloid, preaching that the visible world is nothing more than the obedience of the invisible spirit.
