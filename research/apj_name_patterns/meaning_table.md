# Meaning table: the members and forces of the Apocryphon's anthropogony, coded from Swedenborg

**Status**: Second draft for the author's review (2026-11-09), revised after the author's correction that the first draft flattened the discrete degrees. Nothing is computed until this table is approved. It is the only place where assumptions enter the study.

**Rule of construction.** Every member and force named in the angel lists of the Apocryphon of John (NHC II 15,13–19,2; IV 24–29) receives its spiritual counterpart in one of two ways:

- **S (stated)**: Swedenborg states the correspondence of this member or of its function. The section is cited and quoted.
- **D (derived)**: Swedenborg does not treat the member. Its counterpart is derived from the member's natural function, as he derives his own: "the heavens … correspond chiefly to the functions of these viscera or organs … the use existed before the organic forms of the body came forth; and … the use produced and adapted them to itself" (*AC* §4223). The derivation is stated in the row.

**The table contains no angel names.** The meanings are coded from the members alone. The names are attached only when the analysis is run, so that the coding cannot be shaped, even unawares, by how a name sounds.

---

## 1. The principle: the body is one union of good and truth, in discrete degrees

No member is coded as "good" or as "truth". The body as a whole is the union of good and truth, in which good is to rule. Every member is an aspect of that union, at its own degree and in its own use:

- "by means of the blood vessels the heart rules in the whole of the body and in all its parts; and the lungs in all its parts by the respiration. Hence there is everywhere in the body as it were an influx of the heart into the lungs; but according to the forms there; and according to the states" (*AC* §3887).
- "These two kingdoms are wonderfully conjoined; and this conjunction is represented in the conjunction of the heart and lungs in man, and in the conjunction of their operations in the several members and viscera." In the one who is born again, "the kingdom of the heart in him was under the rule of the lungs, that is, the truth of faith previously ruled in him; but afterward the good of charity rules" (*AC* §4931).
- Every degree has its own good and truth: "there are in every man a natural, a spiritual, and a celestial will and understanding" (*DLW* §239). The degrees are "like end, cause, and effect … the prior is by itself; the subsequent by itself; and the final by itself; and yet taken together they make one" (*DLW* §184). Between them there is "no common ground by means of anything continuous" (*DLW* §238). The good of one degree is therefore not the good of another, and cannot be coded as the same component.

The good–truth relation therefore enters the coding in two places only: through the **degree**, which gives the quality that good and truth have there, and through **polarity**, the pair in which good leads.

## 5. Points for the author's decision

1. **Degree and polarity in place of good and truth.** Is this how you mean the correction? Every member now has a degree (celestial, spiritual, natural, or all degrees for the whole-body systems), a layer where it is clearly inmost or outermost, and a polarity (right/left; heart-side/lung-side). No member is "good" or "truth".
2. **The arms.** *HH* §65 places "the arms down to the fingers" with the feet, in the lowest heaven. The shoulders are coded with the arms (N). Should the shoulders go with the breast (S)?
3. **The neck and the knees** stand at the limits of their degrees (the third heaven "down to the neck", the second "down to the loins and knees"). They are coded in the degree that ends there.
4. **End, cause and effect.** *DLW* §208 gives a third axis: "end makes the inmost, cause the middle, and effect the outmost". It could be added (for example: brain and heart as ends; vessels, nerves, neck and joints as causes; senses, hands and feet as effects), but most of that coding would be derived. Should it be added?
5. **The derived rows** (D): each derivation is written out; any you would derive differently should be changed now.
6. **Rows resting on sources not rechecked**: the nails and sinews (*Spiritual Diary*, as cited in *The Unnamed Cartography*).
