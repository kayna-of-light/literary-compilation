# Source 002 — Swedenborg on stars: signs of knowledges, not places

**Citations**:
- Swedenborg, *Arcana Coelestia*, trans. Clowes, rev. Potts, Standard Edition (West Chester, PA: Swedenborg Foundation, 2009): §1128 (vol. 2), §§2495, 2849 (vol. 3), §4697 (vol. 6).
- Swedenborg, *Earths in the Universe*, Standard Edition (2009), §50 and its note 24.

**Accessed**: 2026-11-03. SE PDFs from swedenborg.com (`swedenborg_foundation_arcana_coelestia_02/03/06.pdf`; *Earths in the Universe* as before), text extracted and read section by section.

## What it says (quoted)

***AC* §2495**, on Matthew 24:29 ("the stars shall fall from heaven"): "That in this passage the 'sun' does not mean the sun, nor the 'moon' the moon, nor the 'stars' the stars; but that the 'sun' signifies love to the Lord and charity toward the neighbor; the 'moon' the faith of love and charity; and the 'stars' the knowledges of good and truth."

***AC* §2849**, on "as the stars of the heavens" (Gen 22:17): stars signify "the multitude of the knowledges of good and truth … The spiritual are they who in the Word are in various places compared to the stars, and this owing to the knowledges of good and truth which they have; but the celestial are not so compared, because they have not knowledges but perceptions; moreover the stars illumine the night, and the spiritual have a light of night (as from the moon and stars) in comparison with the light of day in which the celestial are."

***AC* §4697**, on Joseph's dream ("eleven stars"): "'stars' have this signification in the Word … because they are small luminaries which shine at night, when they give forth into our atmosphere gleams of light, just as knowledges give forth gleams of good and truth."

***AC* §1128**, a scene in the other life: "There appeared one veiled over as with a cloud, about whose face were many wandering stars, which signify falsities. I was told that such were the posterity of the ancient church when it began to perish, especially among those who instituted worship by sacrifices, and by images."

***EU* §50**, in the chapter on the spirits of Jupiter: when the spirits of Jupiter disagree, "there appears a sort of slender bright irradiation, like that of lightning, or like the little swath encompassing glittering and wandering stars … Glittering stars, which are at the same time wandering, signify what is false; but glittering and fixed stars signify what is true; thus the former signify disagreement." Note 24: "Stars in the Word signify the knowledges of good and truth, consequently truths (n. 2495, 2849, 4697). And in the other life truths are represented by fixed stars, but falsities by wandering stars (n. 1128)."

## Why it matters

1. **In Swedenborg's own reading, a star in the Word and in the spiritual world is a sign of a state of knowing, not an address.** "The 'stars' [do not mean] the stars" (§2495). The function grounds the correspondence: a star is a small light in the night, as a knowledge is a small light in a mind that does not yet see by day (§§2849, 4697).
2. **The literal-astronomical reading of the stars is the reading Swedenborg's own key sets aside.** When later literature makes a star the home of a people, it reads the sign at the level of the letter. That is the literalization *Reversing the Arrow* (`data/08_Correspondential_Texts/`) describes: "the interior becomes exterior … the quality becomes a person," and in *Zostrianos* "the heavens have been spatialized into locations."
3. **The function of stars sits at the "spiritual," not the "celestial," level (§2849):** knowledges seen by night, not perception by day. That fits a pattern in which experiencers see stars as signs and guides, partial lights, not as the full presence itself. This is an interpretation, to be stated as such.

## Cautions

- The "wandering stars" of §1128 and *EU* §50 are appearances in the spiritual world. Swedenborg does not say that the planets, which were called "wandering stars" in older usage, signify falsities. It is tempting to read the move "from planets to constellations" as a move "from wandering to fixed stars." That would be wordplay, not evidence, and must not be used as an argument.
- Swedenborg himself, in the same book, also treats the stars as physical suns with inhabited earths (*EU* §§123–126, the "earths in the starry heaven"). His key reads the stars of the Word as knowledges; his natural philosophy placed peoples around the stars of the sky. Both are in his work. The library's standing principle (right in his observations, not always in his explanations of the physical world) applies to the second, not the first.

## Connections

- Source 001: the address of one group of spirits moves from Jupiter to "a third earth in the starry heaven." The key in this source says what a "starry heaven" signifies in the Word; his natural philosophy made it a place.
- *A Library of Biblical Correspondences* (`data/02_Swedenborgian_Theology/`) already lists stars with *AC* §§2495, 2849, 3448 and *AR* §51. Both further references are verified:
  - *AC* §3448 (vol. 4), on "the army of the heavens": "it is principally the stars that are called the 'army of the heavens'; and that by the 'stars' are signified truths, and in the opposite sense falsities."
  - *Apocalypse Revealed* §51 (SE vol. 1), on the seven stars of Rev 1:16: "Around the angels, when they are below the heavens, there appear, as it were, little stars in great abundance; and in like manner, around spirits, who, when they lived in the world, had acquired to themselves knowledges of good and truth … These stars appear fixed with those who are in genuine truths from the Word; but wandering with those who are in falsified truths."
