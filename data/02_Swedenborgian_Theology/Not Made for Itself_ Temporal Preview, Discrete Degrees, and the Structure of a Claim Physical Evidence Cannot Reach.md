# Not Made for Itself

## Temporal Preview, Discrete Degrees, and the Structure of a Claim Physical Evidence Cannot Reach

> **Abstract.** This thesis asks what a framework of discrete degrees is entitled to claim about temporal preview — the experience, reported in near-death accounts, of being shown one's own future and meeting it later as recognition — once it is granted that a representational format sufficient to produce every reportable property of that experience has already been identified in the memory sciences. Its governing premise is that no physical result can establish a claim about a higher degree, and that this is not a temporary evidential shortfall but a structural feature of the framework itself: on the doctrine of discrete degrees the natural plane has its own complete expression of whatever manifests at a higher one, so an account with a hole in it would not be better evidence of a higher degree but a worse account. The framework therefore forfeits, permanently, the entire class of argument that runs *the physical account cannot reach X, therefore something else must*. What remains available is an argument from form rather than from gaps. Dual representation theory, developed in 1996 for intrusive imagery in post-traumatic stress, places the temporal index outside the content and locates its supply in a separate contextualising system; Swedenborg, publishing in 1758 on the basis of claimed spiritual experience, holds that extension and succession are properties of the natural degree rather than of consciousness as such. Two descriptions, arrived at independently and 238 years apart, put time in the same relation to what is received: outside it, added by whatever does the filing. From this the thesis develops a temporal gradient — expression at the celestial degree instantaneous, at the spiritual rapid, in ultimates necessarily successive — which accounts for why a condition apprehended without succession is met later as events in sequence, and why arrival produces identification rather than retrieval. The near-death corpus is then read against this account, with attention to the finding that carries the most weight and is least often noticed: personal future knowledge co-occurs with a commissioned mission at 50.9% against a base rate of 16.7% (χ² = 598.2, φ = 0.298), a concentration of content that no content-neutral representational format predicts, while the physiological comparison returns results that disagree across coding methods and are reported in full. The past-life memory corpus is examined as a second instance of the same format question, and the strongest-looking empirical argument available in this library — that no known mechanism accounts for birthmark-wound correspondence — is declined, because it is the argument from ignorance the framework has already forfeited. The demand behind it is also refused, and on separate grounds: influx runs in one direction only, so a framework positing no route from the natural degree upward posits no route across it either, and a mechanism of transfer between two natural particulars belongs to no part of its account. The absence of such a mechanism is not a gap in what the framework claims but a gap in a claim it never made. What follows is a point about method rather than about findings — premises which exclude determination from a prior degree permit only the transport question to be formed, and a null result on that question is a finding about the question. A problem that has stood open in this framework is then taken up: how conscious experience moves between degrees, when the framework appears to require that a consciousness exists on all of them at once while its attention is held by the degree on which it is prominently expressed. The thesis argues that this question is malformed rather than unanswered. It imports locomotion into a domain where movement from place to place is change of state, and the doctrine of the Grand Man supplies the positive account: the whole expresses at every degree and does not move in itself, as a body acts as one without its members changing places, while which degree the expression is prominent at is determined by ruling love — including the resistance and longing Swedenborg describes for a spirit away from its own place, which is what the reports render as the weight of a degree. Three matters are left genuinely open. The thesis claims organising power and nothing beyond it. It does not claim proof, and argues that proof of this kind is unavailable in principle. A vessel is entirely explicable — its material, its shape, why the wall is thick where it is — and none of that explicability bears on whether it is made to hold anything. What can be said is that its shape is a holding shape, and that a shape is visible whether or not anything is in it.

> **Keywords.** discrete degrees, correspondence, influx, temporal preview, state-perception, dual representation theory, near-death experience, past-life memory research, ultimates, evidential asymmetry, organising power, the Grand Man, ruling love

## 1. Introduction

### 1.1 The Problem

A recurring class of near-death report describes being shown something of one's own future. The content is typically said to arrive all at once rather than in sequence; it is given as conditional on returning to life; it cannot afterwards be called to mind on demand; and when the corresponding circumstance eventually arrives it is met with the sense of knowing it again rather than of remembering having been told. Reports of this kind occur at appreciable rates in the structured near-death archives, and their properties have been treated as anomalous — as behaviour that no ordinary account of memory would produce.

That treatment is no longer available. *[The Future That Cannot Be Remembered Until It Arrives](../01_Consciousness_Studies/The%20Future%20That%20Cannot%20Be%20Remembered%20Until%20It%20Arrives_%20Temporal%20Preview%20in%20Near-Death%20Experience%20as%20State-Perception.md)* examined 6,753 structured accounts from the NDERF and IANDS archives and argued that what is received in such episodes is better described as a state than as a recording. *[The Format That Cannot Say When](../01_Consciousness_Studies/The%20Format%20That%20Cannot%20Say%20When_%20Recognition%20Without%20Tense%20in%20the%20Memory%20Sciences.md)* then identified the carrier of such content with a representational format that the memory sciences had described independently and for unrelated reasons: a sensory representation that fires without its contextual counterpart holds content with no position in sequence, remains outside the reach of the system that would go looking for it, and is triggered by situational match rather than by voluntary search. Eleven of fourteen properties matched, two of them convergences that could not have been arranged after the fact. The consequence is that the features which had made temporal preview look anomalous turn out to be the ordinary behaviour of a format already documented in the study of post-traumatic intrusive imagery, and the question of what preview *is* collapses into the question of what laid a particular representation down.

This is where the present investigation begins, and the position it begins from is more constrained than it may appear. A sufficient natural account now exists. Any framework proposing that something further is involved must therefore say what it is proposing, given that the phenomenon is no longer unexplained — and must do so without appealing to the explanatory shortfall that no longer exists.

The question this thesis addresses is accordingly not whether temporal preview is anomalous. It is what a framework of discrete degrees is entitled to claim about a phenomenon for which a complete natural account is available, and whether anything it claims can be assessed at all.


### 1.2 What This Thesis Claims

The claim is narrower than the subject matter might suggest, and stating its limits first is not a defensive gesture but the substance of the method.

No physical result can establish a claim about a higher degree. This is not an admission that the evidence is currently insufficient, nor a promise that better instruments would close the gap. It follows from the framework's own architecture. On the doctrine of discrete degrees, set out in *[Asking the Skin to Be the Heart](Asking%20the%20Skin%20to%20Be%20the%20Heart_%20The%20Category%20Error%20of%20Physical%20Miracle%20Theology%20and%20the%20Architecture%20of%20Discrete%20Degrees.md)* §§3.1–3.3, the natural plane possesses its own complete expression of whatever manifests at a higher one, and causation runs from inside to outside rather than the reverse. A natural account with a hole in it is therefore not better evidence of a higher degree. It is a worse account. The framework predicts that the physical description will close, and it has no stake in any particular failure of physical explanation.

What follows is severe and should be said before anything is built on it. The framework forfeits the entire class of argument that runs *the physical account cannot reach X, therefore something else must.* It forfeits it permanently and by construction, not pending better evidence. It cannot appeal to an unexplained residue, cannot treat a difficulty in a competing explanation as a gain, and cannot occupy ground vacated by a failed materialist account, because it was never a competitor for that ground. Every rhetorical move that anomalous-experience literature most commonly makes is unavailable here, and unavailable from the inside rather than by external objection.

What remains is an argument from form. A vessel is entirely explicable. Its material, its shape, the manner of its making, the reason the wall is thick where it is — all of this can be given in full, and none of it bears on whether the vessel is made to hold anything. What can be said of it is that its shape is a holding shape. That observation is not a proof of contents and cannot be converted into one; a person who does not perceive the water will not be brought to it by an inventory of the bucket. But the shape is a real feature of the object, it is visible whether or not anything occupies it, and it is not explained by saying that the vessel exists for its own sake.

This thesis argues that the account of temporal preview which emerges from three independent bodies of work — a theological system published in 1758, a clinical memory model developed in 1996, and a corpus of 6,753 contemporary reports — has that shape. It claims that the framework organises this material better than the alternatives do, which is the standard *[The Epistemic Architecture of Post-Materialist Inquiry](The%20Epistemic%20Architecture%20of%20Post-Materialist%20Inquiry_%20A%20Methodological%20Thesis%20on%20Hypothesis-Testing%20with%20the%20Swedenborgian%20Framework.md)* §1.3 sets for a hypothesis worth following. It claims nothing more, and §2.3 explains why nothing more is available.

### 1.3 Plan of the Argument

Section 2 sets out the method: correspondence as functional identity across discrete degrees rather than as resemblance or symbol (§2.1); the evidential asymmetry and its consequences (§2.2); organising power as the standard against which the argument should be judged (§2.3); the two inferences the framework denies itself, one of which costs it a great deal and one of which costs it nothing, which is why the second is the one more likely to go unsaid (§2.4); and the boundary of that prohibition, since influx runs in one direction only and a framework positing no upward route posits no lateral one either, so that a demand for transfer between natural particulars is a demand the framework never incurred (§2.5).

Section 3 establishes the space in which the rest of the thesis operates. It restates what the format result removed, sets out why the framework has no entry in the column of candidate physical origins, and argues that this absence is a structural commitment rather than a retreat from a contest it was losing.

Section 4 presents the Swedenborgian position on extension and succession, and develops from it a temporal gradient across the degrees: expression at the celestial degree instantaneous, at the spiritual rapid, in ultimates necessarily successive. Section 5 sets this beside the memory-science result and states the convergence precisely, including what kind of claim a convergence of this sort can and cannot be.

Sections 6 and 7 bring the empirical corpora to bear — the near-death material first, the past-life memory material second — and in each case separate the findings that carry weight from those that do not. Section 7 also declines, explicitly and at cost, the strongest-looking empirical argument available in this library, while refusing the demand that generated it. Section 8 takes up what has stood as an unsolved problem in the framework's account of how conscious experience moves between degrees, and argues that the question is malformed rather than unanswered, while recording what genuinely remains open. Section 9 discusses and records limitations. Section 10 concludes.

## 2. Methodological Framework

### 2.1 Correspondence as Functional Identity Across Discrete Degrees

Correspondence in this framework is functional identity across discrete degrees. It is not resemblance, allegory, symbol, or metaphor, and the difference is not a matter of emphasis. A natural object corresponds to a spiritual reality when it performs, at the natural degree, the office that the spiritual reality performs at its own. The relation is one of *use*, and it runs in one direction only, from inner to outer. The outer is the inner expressed in ultimates. It does not represent the inner to an observer, because there is no observer for whom the representation is staged and nothing is being encoded.

This distinction does real work throughout what follows, and losing it would turn the entire argument into a set of loose analogies. *The Epistemic Architecture of Post-Materialist Inquiry* §2.3 sets out the contrast at length: an allegorical reading assigns meanings to objects by convention, and a different convention would assign different meanings, whereas a correspondential reading claims that the relation is fixed by function and is therefore discoverable rather than stipulated. When the framework says that the lungs correspond to the understanding, it is not observing that breathing resembles thinking. It is claiming that respiration performs at its degree the office that reception of truth performs at another, and that the two are one operation described at two levels.

The architecture within which this operates is set out in *Asking the Skin to Be the Heart* §§3.1–3.3. Reality is stratified into celestial, spiritual, and natural degrees, each of which is a complete world operating by its own laws rather than a thinner version of the one above it. The degrees are discrete rather than continuous: one does not shade into the next by gradual diminution, and no quantity of natural process accumulates into a spiritual one. Causation runs from inside to outside. The natural degree is where the whole series terminates and is held — the plane of ultimates, in which what exists interiorly is given its final and fixed expression.

Two consequences of this architecture govern the present thesis and are stated here so that they cannot later be quietly set aside. The first is that each degree has its own complete account of whatever manifests within it. There is no phenomenon that is half-natural, no event at which a spiritual cause reaches in and supplies what natural causation failed to provide. *Asking the Skin to Be the Heart* §4.4 develops this as the doctrine that natural causation is the vehicle of divine action rather than its competitor, and §3.4 gives the corresponding error its name: demanding that the skin pump blood is not piety but a category mistake, an insistence that one organ perform the office of another. The second consequence is that the direction of causation forbids inference upward from insufficiency. If the natural account of some event were incomplete, the framework would have no explanation for the incompleteness, because on its own terms the natural degree is where expression is completed.

### 2.2 The Evidential Asymmetry

From these two consequences follows the constraint that shapes everything in this thesis, and it is worth stating in its strongest form rather than in a form that leaves an escape.

Physical evidence cannot establish a spiritual claim. Not *has not yet*; not *has not with the instruments available*. The framework itself predicts that the natural account will close, and a closed natural account is compatible with the spiritual claim being true and equally compatible with its being false. Nothing that could be measured at the natural degree discriminates between those two states of affairs, because the framework does not locate the difference between them at that degree.

The image that captures this most exactly is a vessel. A bucket cannot demonstrate that there is water in it. Everything about the bucket can be given completely — the material it is made from, the thickness of its wall, the method of its manufacture, the reason its base is shaped as it is — and no item in that inventory settles the question of contents. Only perception of the water settles it, and perception of the water is available only to whoever perceives it. Someone who does not see the water cannot be argued into seeing it by a more detailed account of the bucket, and any attempt to do so mistakes the kind of thing that is in question.

What the inventory can establish is something less than proof and more than nothing: that the bucket is not made for itself. Its form is the form of a thing made to hold. That observation does not smuggle in the contents. It is a claim about shape, it is available to anyone examining the object, and it survives whether the bucket is full or empty.

This is the exact epistemic position of the present thesis, and it explains a feature of the argument that would otherwise look like weakness. Section 5 does not argue that the memory sciences have discovered a gap through which something spiritual enters. It argues that the representational system they describe has a holding shape. A reader who does not already stand within the framework will find that observation interesting at most, and this thesis does not pretend otherwise. An argument capable of compelling such a reader would have to be an argument from insufficiency, and §2.1 has just explained why no such argument is available.

The asymmetry cuts in both directions, and the second direction is the one this project has more often had to guard against in others' work than in its own. If physical evidence cannot establish the spiritual claim, neither can it refute it. That is not a convenience. It is the reason the claim must be assessed on grounds other than evidential ones, and §2.3 sets out what those grounds are.

### 2.3 The Standard of Success: Organising Power

If proof and refutation are both unavailable, the question becomes what would make such a framework worth holding at all, and the answer this project has consistently given is organising power.

The standard is set out in *The Epistemic Architecture of Post-Materialist Inquiry* §1.3 and §7.2. A hypothesis is worth following when it organises data better than the alternatives — when patterns that were noise under other descriptions become structure under this one, when findings that had to be treated as unrelated turn out to stand in a stated relation, and when the framework's commitments were fixed before the data arrived rather than adjusted to accommodate it. This is not a weaker substitute for proof offered because proof is unavailable. It is the ordinary standard by which frameworks are assessed in any field, since frameworks are not the kind of thing that individual observations confirm or disconfirm.

Three conditions have to hold for an appeal to organising power to carry weight, and each of them can fail.

The first is that the commitment must be prior. A framework that produces its account after seeing the data has explained nothing, however well the account fits. This condition is satisfiable here in an unusually clean way, and §5.2 turns on it: Swedenborg's position on extension and succession was published in 1758, and the memory-science result it is set beside was arrived at in 1996 by researchers working on post-traumatic intrusive imagery who had no interest in any of this. Neither could have been shaped to meet the other.

The second is that the organisation must be non-trivial. Any sufficiently flexible framework can be draped over any data set, and a framework that accommodates every possible finding equally well has organised nothing. The relevant test is whether the framework's commitments exclude outcomes. They do: §6.3 reports a finding that would have embarrassed the framework had it come out differently, and *The Format That Cannot Say When* §7 records two claims withdrawn because the analysis went against them.

The third is that the reporting must be symmetrical. A framework that publishes the corrections favouring it and files the others is not following data, and its organising power cannot be assessed because the sample of findings has been curated. This thesis inherits a live instance of that discipline: the format analysis removed two of the five structural signatures on which *The Future That Cannot Be Remembered Until It Arrives* rested and undermined its veridical corpus, and those results are restated in §3.1 below rather than relied upon selectively.

### 2.4 The Two Inferences the Framework Denies Itself

Two inferences are ruled out for the remainder of this thesis. They have the same logical form, they fail for the same reason, and only one of them costs the framework anything — which is why the other is the one more likely to be passed over in silence.

The first is the argument from ignorance: *the physical account cannot reach X, therefore something else must.* This is unavailable permanently and by construction rather than pending better evidence, for the reason given in §2.1. It does not improve by being made about consciousness rather than about anything else, and the tendency to think it does is itself worth noticing, since the subject matter is unusually good at making a familiar fallacy feel like a special case. An account with a hole in it is not better evidence of something beyond it. It is a worse account. Every appeal to the mysterious, the not-yet-explained, and the residue left over after the known routes have been eliminated falls under this prohibition, and §3.2 shows what it costs.

The second is the inverse: *the physical account reaches X, therefore there is nothing else.* The form is identical and the failure is identical, but because this half costs the framework nothing it tends to go unstated, and stating it is therefore the more important of the two. An account that is complete at the level on which it operates settles nothing whatever about whether that level is exhaustive. A full description of a machine in terms of voltages is a full description, and says nothing at all about what the machine is computing. Completeness at one level of description is compatible with a great many positions about other levels, which is precisely why it discriminates among none of them.

The material assembled in this thesis invites the second inference more strongly than the first, and §5 invites it most of all. A reader who follows §5.1 to its conclusion — that the format supplies every reportable property of the experience, and that nothing in the report can distinguish an ordinary instance from an extraordinary one — is a short step from concluding that the experience has been explained away in full and that no further question remains. That step is a non sequitur, and it is the same non sequitur as the first with the sign reversed. Sufficiency removes a necessity; it does not remove a possibility. Section 9.3 returns to both errors, because the argument developed here is precisely the kind that invites them.

### 2.5 The Demand the Framework Does Not Owe

The prohibition just stated has a boundary, and mistaking where it falls produces a further error which resembles rigour closely enough to pass for it.

The boundary is fixed by a commitment already made in §2.1 and adds nothing to it. Influx runs in one direction only. This is not a peripheral feature of the system but the rule of order on which the whole doctrine of degrees stands, stated as such and restated across four decades: "influx by no means takes place from externals into internals, but always from internals into externals; and that this is according to order, contrary to which there can be no influx" (*Arcana Coelestia* §6322). Again: "posterior things cannot flow into prior; or what is the same, lower things into higher; or what is the same, exterior things into interior" (§3721). And in summary, with a chain of six earlier references appended in support: "there is spiritual influx, that is, influx from the spiritual world into the natural, but not the reverse," the converse being "quite contrary to order, and contrary to nature, thus is impossible" (§9110). What is excluded under the name of physical influx is the passage of anything from a posterior degree into a prior one.

The consequence for the present argument is easily missed, though it is a matter of reading the commitment rather than of extending it. A framework positing no route from the natural degree upward equally posits no route *across* it — no channel by which something is lifted out of one natural particular, carried by some spiritual conveyance, and set down in another. No such transit appears anywhere in the architecture, and nothing in the doctrine requires or permits it. What the architecture does posit is determination of form from a prior degree into its own ultimate, which is a different relation and involves no passage between natural things whatever. The relation is at its most compact where the body is treated directly: "the bodily form is added to the spirit in accordance with the spirit's form, and not the reverse, for it is in accordance with its own form that the spirit is clothed with a body" (*Heaven and Hell* §453).

This matters wherever a phenomenon presents as a correspondence between two natural particulars with no natural channel joining them. A demand that the framework produce the channel is a demand for something it has never claimed, and there are two ways of mishandling such a demand, related as mirror images. The first is the inflation §2.4 forbids: treating the missing channel as evidence that a higher degree supplied one. The second is subtler and is by some distance the more likely to survive an author's own scrutiny, because it wears the costume of restraint — accepting that the framework owes a channel, and then declining the argument on the ground that no channel can be produced. That has the appearance of discipline. It is in fact the first concession with its conclusion reversed, since both proceed from the assumption that the framework and the physical account are competing to fill a single slot, which is the assumption §3.2 exists to deny.

A point about method follows, and it needs stating carefully, because it is readily heard as a complaint against scientific findings and is not one. No result reported anywhere in this thesis is in dispute, and none of what follows suggests that any investigation was conducted poorly. The observation concerns premises rather than execution. A method which does not admit determination from a prior degree among its premises is not thereby prevented from studying phenomena of this kind; it is prevented from forming one particular question about them. The only question such a method can pose about a formal correspondence between two natural particulars is the transport question — by what physical route did the information travel — and when investigation returns no route, that null result is a finding about the question rather than about the data. It is commonly read the other way, as grounds for concluding that the reported correspondence must be artefactual. That reading is not compelled. Premises fix what can be asked; they do not thereby settle what is the case. This is an observation about the scope of a method and it entitles the framework to nothing at all. It removes a demand. It supplies no evidence.

## 3. The Space the Format Result Opens

### 3.1 What Was Removed

*The Future That Cannot Be Remembered Until It Arrives* rests on five structural signatures, each presented as incompatible with the preview being a recording of a fixed future and each following from its being the perception of a state. They were **simultaneity**, the whole content arriving at once rather than in sequence; **conditionality**, the content given as contingent on return in the experiencers' own grammar; **non-retrievability**, the content resisting ordinary recall and becoming available only when the corresponding moment arrived; **purposive selection**, what is shown being chosen by function rather than by chronology; and **recognition rather than recall**, the later event met with the phenomenology of knowing-again.

The format analysis removes the third and the fifth. Both were formal signatures — claims about how the material behaves rather than about what it contains or why it was given — and both turned out to be ordinary properties of a representational format described in the memory sciences for unrelated reasons. A sensory representation lacking its contextual counterpart cannot be retrieved by voluntary search, because voluntary search operates on the contextual system it does not possess; and its arrival produces the phenomenology of knowing-again rather than of remembering, because familiarity without recollection is what that format delivers by definition. Neither property required a special mechanism. Both had been paid for in special pleading, and the identification pays for them out of published research instead.

The format result reaches beyond the two signatures in one further respect, and the extent of the reach has to be stated exactly. What a contextual index carries is placement, not direction. The clinical case the format was theorised from settles the point: the flashback arrives with an overwhelming sense of present threat, which is maximal felt direction delivered by a representation that has no index at all. What the format determines is that the material cannot be located. What fixes its direction is the relation between the delivered content and the scene into which it is delivered.

A report of felt direction is accordingly defeasible evidence: produced by determinate conditions, which is what makes it evidence, and producible by more than one configuration of those conditions, which is what makes it defeasible. It requires a corroboration it cannot supply itself, and it does not establish what laid the representation down, because the format carries no provenance whatever — which is the point §3.2 turns on. The veridical corpus assembled in *The Future That Cannot Be Remembered Until It Arrives* therefore fails at its own stated requirement, and it fails for the reason that thesis itself records.

What survives is the first, second, and fourth signatures, and they are of a different kind from the two that fell. Simultaneity, conditionality, and purposive selection are not claims about the format of the experience. They are claims about its content, its grammar, and its distribution across a population, and the format result does not touch them. Sections 6.2 and 6.4 take them up.

Stating all of this at the head of a thesis whose sympathies lie with the parent argument is not a display of even-handedness. It is required by §2.3. A framework that reports only the corrections favouring it has forfeited the claim to be following data, and the corrections in question were not optional once the format identification was granted.

### 3.2 Why the Framework Has No Entry in the Origin Column

*The Format That Cannot Say When*, having relocated the burden from form to origin, sets out three candidate origins for which published mechanisms exist: nightly dreaming, unretrieved waking exposure, and constructive simulation of anticipated scenes. Nothing currently discriminates among them, and the three are not a closed field but the routes that happen to have been studied.

There is an obvious fourth entry, and it must not be added. The candidate would be that in some cases the representation was laid down during a boundary state — the near-death episode itself, or a comparable condition — and that its content originates neither in prior perception nor in constructive assembly. The reason for withholding it has nothing to do with how it would score if it were entered.

The column asks a single question: which physical process laid this representation down? Every entry names a process — a night's REM cycle, an unattended glimpse, an act of constructive assembly — and the dispute among them is a dispute about which was operating. The framework's claim is not a rival answer, because it is not a claim about processes at all. The state-perception account holds that what is received in a boundary state is the apprehension of a condition rather than a preview of events in sequence, met later as the events themselves. That says nothing about which encoding event occurred, when, or in which structure. It neither predicts nor forbids any answer to the question the column asks. A claim of that kind cannot be entered because it is not the kind of thing the column holds.

Two consequences follow, and they run in opposite directions.

The first is that the framework gains nothing from any absence in that column. If prospective work establishes that constructive simulation accounts for most or all of ordinary déjà vu, that is not a defeat, because there is nothing there to be defeated. Equally — and this is the half that matters — if a residue remains after every searchable source has been eliminated, that residue is not an opening. A column the framework does not occupy cannot be won by the difficulties of its occupants. A residue is a description of what a search did not find. It is not a fourth candidate arriving by elimination.

The second is that this forbids an entire class of argument, including arguments this thesis would otherwise be in a position to make. Every inference of the form *no ordinary source was found, therefore an extraordinary one operated* is unavailable, and its unavailability is permanent. This is the prohibition of §2.4 applied to the specific case, and the specific case is where prohibitions of this kind are usually abandoned.

### 3.3 A Commitment, Not a Retreat

It would be possible to read §3.2 as a framework declining a contest it was losing, and the reading should be addressed rather than left to stand.

The absence from the origin column is not a concession extracted by the memory literature. It follows from the framework's own account of how degrees relate, applied consistently rather than selectively, and it was derivable before any of this evidence existed. *Asking the Skin to Be the Heart* §§3.3–3.4 states the position in a context that has nothing to do with near-death experience: a spiritual cause does not supply what natural causation failed to provide, and the expectation that it should is the category error the whole document is named for. A framework that held this in the case of resurrection narratives and quietly abandoned it when an evidential opportunity appeared in consciousness research would be demonstrating that the principle was never load-bearing.

There is also a positive point that the defensive framing obscures. The framework expects the physical account to close. That expectation is a commitment with content, and it can fail: were the memory sciences to converge on the conclusion that some class of experience is physically unaccountable in principle, the framework would be in difficulty, because it holds that the natural degree completes its own expression. The framework is therefore not indifferent to the progress of physical explanation. It is committed to that progress, and the supply of a mundane mechanism for two of the five signatures is a result in the framework's favour under its own account, however unwelcome it was to the argument those signatures supported.

What the framework does claim in this vicinity is set out in §5, and it is a claim about form rather than about origin. That distinction carries the weight of the remainder of the thesis, and §5.4 states what it does not license.

## 4. Extension and Succession as Properties of the Natural Degree

### 4.1 The Swedenborgian Position

The commitment on which the rest of this thesis turns was published in 1758 and has nothing to do with memory research.

Swedenborg holds that in the spiritual world there is neither space nor time, but state. What appears there as distance is produced by likeness and unlikeness of state, so that those in similar states appear near one another and those in dissimilar states appear remote, and movement from one place to another is a change of state rather than a traversal (*Heaven and Hell* §§191–199). The same principle governs orientation: the four quarters of heaven are determined by the direction in which a given state faces rather than by any fixed position, so that a change of quarter is a change of state (§§141–153).

The extension to duration is not an inference from the treatment of space but a separate and equally explicit doctrine, set out in the chapter on time in heaven (§§162–169). Its statement is unambiguous:

> Angels do not know what time is, although with them there is a successive progression of all things, as there is in the world, and this so completely that there is no difference whatever; and the reason is that in heaven instead of years and days there are changes of state; and where there are years and days there are times, but where there are changes of state there are states. (*Heaven and Hell* §163)

The same position appears in the *Arcana Coelestia*: "It is indeed true that in the other life all things appear as in space, and follow one another as if in time; but in themselves the space and time are changes of state, for they come from this source" (§3356), and again at §4901, where the progression of the life of spirits is said to appear as in time while their thought proceeds from states of life rather than from times.

The position is easily misread as a claim that spiritual existence is static, or that it is a timeless block in which everything is simultaneously present. It is neither, and the §163 passage forecloses both readings in its opening clause: there *is* a successive progression of all things, "so completely that there is no difference whatever" from the world in this respect. States change, and their changing is real and ordered. What is denied is that the changing is measured against an independent temporal dimension through which it passes. Duration on this account is not the container of change but the appearance change presents. Extension is treated in exactly the same way, which is why the two are handled by the same reasoning rather than as unrelated topics.

Two features of this position matter for what follows, and both are consequences of its being a claim about degrees rather than about the spiritual world considered in isolation.

The first is that succession is a property of the natural degree. It is not a feature of reality as such that the natural degree happens to share, nor a limitation of human cognition that a wider view would correct. It is what expression looks like when it occurs in the plane of ultimates. The natural mind therefore does not perceive time because it is limited; it perceives time because time is the form its own degree takes, and its degree is real.

The second is that what is received from above has no rate. This follows directly: if succession belongs to the natural degree, then content apprehended at an interior degree does not arrive quickly. It does not arrive at any speed at all, quickly or slowly being categories that apply where there is an interval to divide. When such content is subsequently reported by a mind operating in succession, the absence of rate is the one property that cannot be reproduced in the report, and it is registered instead as instantaneity — as everything at once, in a flash, all of it together.

This last point has already been put to empirical work. *The Future That Cannot Be Remembered Until It Arrives* observes that near-death accounts of the life review routinely combine instantaneity with exhaustive detail, and that the combination is contradictory at the natural degree, where detail takes time to enumerate. The resolution offered there is that the instantaneity belongs to the reception and the exhaustive detail to the explication — one event reported in two registers because it crossed a degree boundary between being received and being told.

### 4.2 The Temporal Gradient

The preceding section states Swedenborg's position. This section extends it, and the extension is this thesis's own rather than his; it is offered as a derivation from his premises and should be assessed as such.

If succession is the form expression takes in ultimates, and if the degrees are discrete rather than continuous, then the relation of expression to succession is not uniform across the degrees but graded across them.

At the celestial degree, expression is instantaneous — which is to say, not fast, but without succession altogether. There is no interval in which it could occur. What exists there exists whole, and the framework's language for celestial perception throughout the corpus reflects this: the Most Ancient Church is described as perceiving spiritual meaning in natural objects immediately, without inference, without the intervening steps that reasoning requires.

At the spiritual degree, expression takes form rapidly but not instantaneously. Changes of state occur, one state gives way to another, and this succession of states is what appears to those undergoing it as duration. The appearance is not illusory in the sense of being false; it is the genuine presentation of a real ordering. What it is not is a measured interval, and this is why the spiritual degree can present what looks like a sequence of events while remaining, on Swedenborg's account, outside time.

In ultimates, expression is necessarily successive and cannot be otherwise. A state that is to be fully expressed at the natural degree must be laid out in order, one part after another, because that is the only way the natural degree expresses anything. What was whole above becomes extended below — not by being diluted or partially lost, but by being given the form its terminal degree requires.

The gradient accounts for something that is otherwise puzzling in the material, and this is the reason for proposing it. A condition apprehended without succession, if it is to be expressed in ultimates at all, must be met as a series of events. There is no other way for it to arrive. What was received whole is therefore encountered piece by piece, and the encounter takes the time that the events themselves take. This is not a preview being checked against a later reality. It is one state, apprehended above and expressed below, and the difference between the two encounters is a difference of degree rather than of content.

### 4.3 What Rooting in Ultimates Requires

A consequence follows that bears directly on §5, and it is the point at which the framework becomes unusually specific.

If a state apprehended at an interior degree is to be expressed at the natural degree, something at the natural degree must carry it. The framework does not permit an alternative here. It holds that the natural plane is where the whole series terminates and is fixed, and *Asking the Skin to Be the Heart* §4.3 states the general principle in its strongest form: the body is the soul in ultimates, not a vehicle the soul occupies. Whatever exists interiorly has a natural expression, and that expression is not optional, not partial, and not supplementary to some other mode of existence the interior content might have had instead.

Applied here, this produces a requirement rather than a permission. If something is apprehended in a boundary state and is later met in the course of an ordinary life, then between the apprehension and the meeting it must have been held in some natural form. The framework cannot say that it was held spiritually and then materialised on demand, because that would make the natural degree a display surface for a process occurring elsewhere — which is the category error *Asking the Skin to Be the Heart* is written against, in a new location.

The requirement is therefore quite demanding, and it is worth stating exactly what it demands. Whatever carries such content must hold it without a position in sequence, since what was apprehended had no rate and no date. It must be unavailable to ordinary recall, since content with no temporal index cannot be found by a system that searches by temporal and contextual cues. And it must deliver its content when the corresponding situation arrives, since that arrival is the point at which the state is expressed in ultimates and therefore the point at which identification becomes possible.

The framework arrives at these three specifications from its own commitments, without reference to any empirical literature. Section 5 sets them beside a representational format that the memory sciences describe.

## 5. The Convergence

### 5.1 A Representation Without a Contextual Index Cannot Be Placed

Dual representation theory was developed by Brewin, Dalgleish, and Joseph in 1996 to account for a clinical puzzle in post-traumatic stress: why traumatic material intrudes involuntarily, in vivid sensory form, while resisting the ordinary narrative recall that other autobiographical material permits. Their answer was that two memory systems operate in parallel. One produces representations that are verbally accessible and can be deliberately retrieved and narrated; the other produces representations that are, in their formulation, "automatically accessible through appropriate situational cues." The 2010 revision by Brewin and colleagues renamed these sensory-based representations (S-reps) and contextual representations (C-reps), and located the pathology of flashback in the activation of an S-rep whose corresponding C-rep is absent or unbound.

The contextual representation is what supplies position. It situates content in a time, a place, and a relation to the rest of a life, and it is the structure through which deliberate search operates. An S-rep firing without its C-rep therefore has three properties, and they are consequences of the definition rather than additional empirical findings.

It cannot be placed. Position in a sequence is a contribution of the contextual system, and a representation lacking that contribution does not arrive dated. It is not that the content is dated wrongly; it is that it is not dated at all, cannot be set among the events around it, and cannot be reached by a search that proceeds by date and context. What such a representation does not lack is direction. The flashback, which is the clinical case dual representation theory was written to explain, arrives with an overwhelming sense of present threat; déjà vu, on the identification argued in *The Format That Cannot Say When*, arrives with an equally unambiguous pastness. One format, opposite directions, both at full strength — so direction is neither supplied nor removed by the format, and is fixed instead by the relation between the delivered content and the situation into which it is delivered. What the missing index removes is placement, and placement only.

It cannot be retrieved voluntarily. Deliberate recall operates through the contextual system, and a representation outside that system is outside the reach of search. It is not hidden, suppressed, or defended against. It is simply not the kind of thing the searching mechanism can address.

It is delivered on situational match. Since the representation is bound to sensory and configural features rather than to a narrative position, what activates it is the arrival of a situation resembling the one that laid it down.

These are precisely the three specifications §4.3 derived from the framework's own commitments: content held without position in sequence, unavailable to ordinary recall, delivered when the corresponding situation arrives.

### 5.2 Two Descriptions, 1758 and 1996

The convergence can now be stated exactly, and stating it exactly matters more than stating it forcefully.

Dual representation theory arrives at a representational form that carries content without carrying a position in time, and locates the supply of that position in a separate contextualising system. Swedenborg holds that time is not a property of what is received but of the degree at which the receiver operates. Two descriptions — one from clinical psychology in 1996, one from a theological corpus in 1758 — place the temporal index in the same relation to the content: outside it, added by whatever does the filing.

Three features of this convergence give it whatever weight it has.

The derivations are independent. Brewin and colleagues were working on intrusive imagery in psychopathology and had no interest in the metaphysics of time, in near-death experience, or in anything Swedenborg wrote. Swedenborg, publishing 238 years earlier, had no access to memory research, to the clinical category of post-traumatic stress, or to any experimental method capable of distinguishing memory systems. Neither account could have been shaped to meet the other, which satisfies the first of the three conditions §2.3 requires of an appeal to organising power.

The agreement is structural rather than verbal. It would be worth little if it amounted to two traditions both using the word "time" in a loose sense. What agrees is the placement of a specific functional element: both accounts remove the temporal index from the content and assign its supply to a separate system, and both make the resulting content unavailable to whatever operates through that system. This is a claim about architecture, and it is the same architecture in both cases.

The specifications were fixed in advance. Section 4.3 derived three requirements from the framework's commitments about ultimates without consulting any empirical literature, and §5.1 reported a format satisfying all three from a literature that developed for unrelated reasons. Had the memory sciences produced a format that failed any of the three — one that carried a temporal index, or that was voluntarily retrievable, or that fired without situational cueing — the framework would have been in difficulty, since it holds that content apprehended non-successively must nonetheless be held in ultimates in a form that meets those conditions.

### 5.3 The Holding Shape

What is available from this is a claim about form, and the form of the claim matters more than its content.

It is emphatically not that the natural account leaves something out. Section 3.2 makes the opposite a requirement: the natural degree has its own complete expression of whatever manifests at a higher one, and an account with a hole in it would not be better evidence of a higher degree but a worse account. The observation being offered here is compatible with the natural account of recognition being complete, and would be undamaged if it were completed further tomorrow.

The observation is that the natural-degree account of recognition has a particular structure. It stores content in a form carrying no position in sequence; it keeps that content outside the reach of the system that would go looking for it; and it delivers it when a situation arrives that matches it. Held without an index, delivered at the point where it belongs. That is what a representational system would have to look like in order to carry something apprehended non-successively through a process that runs successively — and it is also, entirely and without remainder, what the trauma-memory literature describes for reasons of its own that have nothing to do with any of this.

Both halves of that sentence have to be kept together, and the second half is the one more often dropped. There are good functional reasons for a nervous system to encode sensory material quickly and bind it slowly, and the literature supplies several: the encoding advantage under high arousal, the differential vulnerability of hippocampal contextual binding to stress hormones, the survival value of rapid situational triggering. The observation is not that the structure is unexplained. The moment it becomes an argument from inexplicability it has turned into the argument from ignorance that §2.4 forbids.

The observation is about shape. A vessel is entirely explicable, and its explicability is not in tension with its being a vessel. One can give a complete account of how a bucket was made, what it is made from, and why its walls are shaped as they are, without any part of that account bearing on whether it holds anything. What can be said is that the shape is a holding shape — and that observation is not weakened by the completeness of the manufacturing account, because it was never in competition with it.

### 5.4 What This Argument Is Not

Three things must be said about the preceding subsection, and they are not concessions offered in advance of objections but statements of what the argument is.

It is not a prediction and not a confirmation. The framework did not predict dual representation theory, and dual representation theory does not support the framework. Section 5.2 sets out a coherence relation, and coherence is weaker here than in an ordinary empirical comparison, because the two descriptions are not describing the same object at the same level. One concerns a representational format in a brain; the other concerns the structure of a degree of reality. Reading the first as an instance of the second is a framework move, available only to someone already standing within the framework.

It cannot compel a reader who does not stand there, and no reading of natural-degree evidence could. Section 2.2 gave the reason: a claim that never competes at the natural degree cannot be carried by natural-degree evidence in either direction. A reader who declines the framework loses nothing from §5.1, which is a report of published findings, and gains nothing from §5.3, which is an interpretation of them.

It licenses no conclusion about origin. Establishing that two descriptions pick out one structure says nothing whatever about what produced any particular instance of it. The claim here concerns format; it is silent on origin, and §3.2 holds origin fully open. An argument sliding from *this is the format* to *therefore this is where it came from* would be a non sequitur, and the fact that one of the candidate origins is more congenial than the others to the position argued here makes the slide more tempting rather than less.

## 6. What the Near-Death Corpus Supports

### 6.1 What the Corpus Measures

The empirical material available for this question consists of 6,753 structured accounts drawn from the NDERF and IANDS archives, analysed in *The Future That Cannot Be Remembered Until It Arrives*. Personal future knowledge appears in 15.3% of them (1,031 accounts: 596 explicit, 435 implied), and knowledge concerning the future of the world in 3.8%.

The meaning of those percentages has to be fixed before anything is built on them, and the source analysis is explicit that the obvious reading is unavailable. The archives are self-selected: people submit accounts because they consider them significant, using instruments that ask particular questions and not others, at varying intervals after the events described. A statement of the form *personal future knowledge occurs in 15.3% of near-death experiences* is not licensed. What is licensed is a statement about the corpus — that in this body of accounts, this proportion reports it — and every use made of the figures below is a use of that weaker statement.

This is not a formality. It removes at a stroke any argument depending on the phenomenon being common, rare, or of any particular incidence in the population, and it means the corpus can support claims about how features distribute *relative to one another within it* while supporting nothing about how often anything happens outside it. The results that follow are all of the relative kind.

Inter-rater agreement on the coding of temporal preview was κ = 0.672, with perfect sensitivity — the coding scheme caught every account a human rater identified, at the cost of some over-inclusion. Errors therefore run toward false positives, which matters for interpreting the base rates below.

### 6.2 Function Discriminates

Of the five structural signatures set out in *The Future That Cannot Be Remembered Until It Arrives*, three survive the format analysis: simultaneity, conditionality, and purposive selection. These are the signatures concerning what the content contains and why it was given, rather than how the material behaves, and it is not accidental that these are the ones a representational format cannot supply. A format determines the shape in which content is held. It does not determine what the content is about, and it does not determine the distribution of subject matter across a population of accounts.

The strongest result in the corpus concerns purposive selection. Accounts reporting personal future knowledge co-occur with a stated mission or purpose for returning at 50.9%, against a corpus base rate of 16.7% — χ² = 598.2, *p* = 4.2 × 10⁻¹³², φ = 0.298. The effect size is moderate rather than large, and the extraordinary *p*-value is a function of sample size rather than of effect magnitude; the φ coefficient is the figure to attend to. What it indicates is a real and substantial association between reporting future knowledge and reporting a reason for return.

This bears on the format question directly. A representational format is content-neutral. Whatever laid down a sensory representation without its contextual counterpart, the mechanism has no view about whether the content concerns a purpose for living. If temporal preview were simply the format operating, there is no reason for its content to concentrate around mission at three times the base rate, and the concentration is not explained by anything in dual representation theory.

The déjà vu aftereffect shows a comparable pattern: 20.6% of accounts reporting temporal preview also report a subsequent persisting déjà-vu-like recognition, against 7.4% of the remainder (χ² = 179.3, *p* = 6.8 × 10⁻⁴¹). This is the format's predicted behaviour appearing as a lasting feature of a life rather than as a single episode, and it is consistent with content held in a form that recall cannot address being met repeatedly as situations match it.

### 6.3 Physiology Does Not Discriminate Cleanly

The corpus was also examined for a physiological gradient, and the result is mixed in a way that must be reported in full rather than summarised in whichever direction is convenient.

Comparing accounts of self-declared non-clinical crisis (n = 556) against accounts declaring clinical death (n = 193), the experiencers' own questionnaire responses give personal future scenes at 9.7% and 13.5% — a difference that does not reach significance (χ² = 2.08, *p* = 0.150). The timeless or everything-at-once marker is effectively identical across the two groups, at 55.7% and 52.8%. On these measures there is no gradient.

By extraction coding, however, the same comparison gives 14.1% and 23.3%, and this difference is significant (χ² = 8.94, *p* = 0.003). The two codings disagree, and the disagreement runs in the direction less favourable to a reading on which the phenomenon is indifferent to the depth of physiological crisis.

The conclusion drawn in the source analysis is the correct one and is stated there without softening: clinical death is not necessary for temporal preview, and may be facilitating. Cardiac arrest accounts for only 3.6% of the corpus, so the phenomenon is overwhelmingly reported by people who were not in arrest — which establishes that arrest is not required. But the extraction-coded gradient is a real result, and it indicates that depth of crisis is associated with reporting.

Nothing in the framework's position is damaged by this, and it is worth being precise about why, because the reasoning is the same reasoning that made §3.2 costly. The framework holds that whatever manifests at an interior degree has a complete natural expression, and that natural causation is the vehicle rather than the competitor of what occurs above. A physiological facilitation of temporal preview is therefore exactly what the framework should expect to find. A result showing that the phenomenon floated entirely free of bodily condition would be the awkward one, since it would suggest a manifestation with no natural expression, and the framework has no room for that.

### 6.4 What the Corpus Supports

The corpus supports a distributional claim and nothing stronger.

Within this body of accounts, reports of temporal preview are associated with reports of purpose for return at roughly three times the base rate, are associated with a persisting recognition aftereffect at roughly three times the base rate, and are associated with depth of physiological crisis inconsistently across coding methods. The first two associations concern content and function; the third concerns physical circumstance.

What the corpus does not support should be stated with equal clarity, since three separate temptations are available here and all three are refused.

It does not show that anything was foreseen. Every account is retrospective, and the association between preview and mission is an association between two things reported afterwards by the same person in the same document. The most parsimonious reading of that association is that people who came out of a near-death experience with a strong sense of purpose narrate the experience in terms that include having been shown what the purpose was for. This reading is not refuted by anything in the corpus, and the analysis in *The Future That Cannot Be Remembered Until It Arrives* does not claim to refute it.

It does not identify an origin. Section 3.2 holds the origin column open, and no distributional result closes it. That preview content concentrates around mission is a fact about what the reports are about; a process operating during physiological crisis in a person whose life has just been threatened might well produce content concentrated around purpose, and the association is therefore consistent with more than one origin.

It does not establish anything about degrees. A degree-claim cannot be reached from a distributional result, for the reason given in §2.2. What the corpus does is describe the phenomenon accurately enough that the framework's reading of it has something determinate to be a reading of. That is a real service and it is the whole of the service.

## 7. A Second Body of Material, and an Argument This Thesis Declines

### 7.1 The Past-Life Memory Corpus

A second body of empirical material bears on the question of what is carried and in what form, and it is worth examining precisely because the temptation it presents is the one §2.4 forbids.

The Division of Perceptual Studies at the University of Virginia has documented more than 2,500 cases of young children spontaneously reporting memories of a previous life, investigated over six decades (Stevenson, 1997; Tucker, 2021). Two features of the corpus are relevant here, and both are reported in *[The Epistemic Architecture of Post-Materialist Inquiry](The%20Epistemic%20Architecture%20of%20Post-Materialist%20Inquiry_%20A%20Methodological%20Thesis%20on%20Hypothesis-Testing%20with%20the%20Swedenborgian%20Framework.md)* §3.1.

The first is a distributional finding: cases cluster heavily around violent or premature death, at rates above 70%. The second concerns physical correlates. In the subset of cases where the previous personality died by violent trauma and where autopsy records exist for both bodies, birthmarks and birth defects in the child correspond to the fatal wounds of the previous personality at 88% accuracy — bullet entry and exit sites, stab wounds, blunt-force trauma locations.

The 88% figure requires immediate qualification that it does not always receive. It describes a small subset selected on two conditions — violent death and the availability of autopsy documentation for both individuals — not 88% of the 2,500 cases. Selection into that subset is not random with respect to the outcome, since a case is more likely to be pursued to the point of autopsy comparison when a striking correspondence has already been noticed. The figure is therefore a statement about a corpus assembled under a particular selection regime, in exactly the sense §6.1 required of the near-death percentages, and it cannot be read as an incidence rate.

### 7.2 The Argument This Thesis Declines, and the Demand It Refuses

The use most commonly made of this material, including within this library, is that no materialist mechanism exists by which trauma to one body could produce marks on another, and that the framework should therefore be preferred.

This thesis cannot make that argument, and the reason is the position established in §2.4. An appeal to the absence of a physical mechanism is an argument from ignorance whichever phenomenon it is made about, and impressiveness of the data does not convert it into something else. The 88% figure is arresting, the inference is available and easy, and retaining it here while refusing it elsewhere would make §2.3 a formality rather than a method. The refusal costs this thesis the whole of the positive weight the figure appears to carry, and that cost is real and is not recovered later.

But the argument must be declined for the right reason, and the reason usually given is the wrong one. It is not that the framework has a candidate mechanism which cannot presently be exhibited, nor that the framework is waiting for the physical account to fail. Neither describes its position. Two questions are run together in the standard treatment, and separating them dissolves most of the dispute.

The first question is how the mark came to be in this body. That is a question about a natural process operating on natural material — melanocyte migration, tissue formation, whatever the developmental account turns out to be — and the framework expects it to close, for the reason given in §3.3. A birthmark with no embryology would not be a better result for the framework. It would be an incomplete natural account, and §3.2 commits the framework to expecting completion.

The second question is why this configuration rather than another, and it is not a question about a process at all. Put as a transport question — by what route did the shape of a wound in one body reach a second and unrelated one — it has no answer the framework wishes to give, because on the architecture set out in §2.5 there is no such route, and none is posited. Nothing crosses. Correspondence of form between a prior degree and its ultimate is not the conveyance of anything from one natural particular to another; it is what *Heaven and Hell* §453 describes when it holds that the bodily form is added in accordance with the form of the spirit and not the reverse. A demand for the crossing is therefore a demand for a mechanism belonging to no part of the framework's account, and the absence of such a mechanism is not a gap in what the framework claims. It is a gap in a claim the framework never made.

This is why declining the argument on the ground that the mechanism is missing would be the wrong move rather than a scrupulous one. That formulation concedes the demand in the act of refusing its conclusion, and §2.5 gives the reason the concession is not owed. What is declined here is the inference from absence. What is refused is the demand that generated it.

### 7.3 What the Material Does Contribute

Something narrower survives, and it concerns form rather than origin.

The reported phenomenology of these cases has the same three properties §4.3 derived and §5.1 located in the memory literature. The children's reports are not presented as recollections of a past. They are given in the present tense, as knowing rather than as remembering, without the sense of retrieving something from an earlier position in one's own life — which is what content lacking a contextual index would produce. They are not produced on demand; the reports are characteristically spontaneous and resist elicitation, and they fade at the age at which contextualised autobiographical memory consolidates. And they are frequently triggered by situational match — the sight of a place, an object, a person resembling something in the reported prior life.

This is the same profile, in a second and independent body of material, and it is the strongest thing this section says.

Two further features are worth setting out as observations about form, on the understanding fixed in §2.5 that observations of this kind remove demands rather than supply evidence.

The first is that what the birthmark material reports is the reproduction of a configuration and not the transfer of a substance. Nothing material is claimed to have moved; what recurs is a shape, a location, a pairing of entry and exit. A framework in which prior degrees determine the form of their ultimates and convey nothing across the natural plane predicts correspondences with exactly that character — formal, not substantial — and it is worth noticing that this is the character the reports actually have. The observation is weak. It is compatible with the reports being artefacts of coding, of selective attention, or of investigation shaped by what investigators expected to find. What it is not compatible with is the framing on which the framework needs a transport mechanism, since a transport model is the one thing the formal character of the correspondence does not suggest.

The second concerns distribution. Cases cluster around violent and premature death at rates above 70%, and it is not obvious what a transport account would make of this. Information does not travel more readily because a death was violent; on any model in which something is conveyed, manner of death is an odd variable to govern the rate. On the framework, what roots in ultimates is what is held with the greatest intensity as state, and severity of state is precisely the variable expected to govern which conditions express and how strongly. The clustering is therefore what the framework would look for.

It is also, however, exactly what an investigative selection regime would produce. Violent deaths are documented, dated, locally remembered, and verifiable in ways that ordinary deaths are not; a child's statement about a violent death can be checked, and a case that can be checked is a case that gets pursued and published. The 70% figure describes a corpus assembled under that regime, in the same sense §6.1 required of the near-death percentages and §7.1 required of the 88%. Nothing available here discriminates between the two readings, and this thesis does not claim that the framework's reading is established. It claims that the framework organises the distribution rather than being embarrassed by it, which is the standard §2.3 sets and the whole of what is claimed.

The birthmark correspondence bears on §4.3 in one further way. Section 4.3 held that whatever exists interiorly must have a natural expression, and that the expression is not optional or supplementary. A physical correspondence to an interior condition is therefore what the framework requires rather than a surprise it capitalises on — and the requirement is symmetrical, applying to states one would prefer not to see expressed as much as to states one would. The framework would be in difficulty if interior conditions of this severity left no natural trace at all. That they leave one is consistent with the position; it does not establish it, and no distributional finding could.

## 8. The Question of Movement Between Degrees

### 8.1 The Tension as It Presents Itself

A difficulty sits underneath everything argued so far, and it has not been addressed because it required the material of §4 to state properly.

The experiential accounts describe something that looks unmistakably like transit. Consciousness is reported as elevated, and then as dropping, and at each level the character of experience changes completely — not in degree of vividness but in kind, taking the form that seems to be permitted there and no other. The reports are consistent about this across very different sources. The past-life material presents the same shape from another direction: states that are not imprinted on a brain nonetheless expressed on a body, and expressed anatomically rather than representationally.

Assembled, these suggest a picture in which a consciousness exists at all degrees simultaneously while its attention is held by whichever degree it is prominently expressed at — held, moreover, with something that behaves like weight. The prominence is difficult to leave. It shifts under extreme pressure and not otherwise.

The obvious question is what moves, and by what mechanism. Framed that way the question is unanswerable, and it has been left standing as an open problem in this framework for that reason.

### 8.2 Why the Question Is Malformed

The question is unanswerable because it is the wrong question, and the framework already contains the material to show why.

Section 4.1 established that in the spiritual world movement from one place to another is a change of state rather than a traversal, and that what appears as distance is likeness and unlikeness of state. Asking how consciousness *moves between* degrees imports precisely the notion of locomotion that this doctrine removes. It is the same error one degree further up: treating a difference of state as a gap to be crossed, and then demanding an account of the crossing.

There is no transit because there is nothing to traverse. The apparent movement is the appearance a change of state presents — which is, on Swedenborg's account, what all apparent movement is.

This also explains why the reports take the form they do. A natural mind reporting a change of state has only the vocabulary of its own degree available, and that vocabulary is spatial and locomotive. It reports elevation and descent because rising and falling is how succession-bound, extension-bound description renders a difference that is neither. Section 4.1 observed the same mechanism producing reports of instantaneity: the natural mind registers the absence of rate as infinite speed because it has no category for the absence of rate. Here it registers a difference of state as displacement because it has no category for a difference that is not a distance.

### 8.3 Place as Function, Determined by State

The dissolution of the wrong question leaves room for a positive account, and the framework supplies one in the doctrine of the Grand Man.

Heaven is in the human form, in the aggregate, in each society, and in each angel (*Heaven and Hell* §§59–86), and all things of heaven correspond to all things of the human (§§87–102). What matters here is how such a form behaves. Swedenborg is explicit that it acts as a unity:

> Heaven being such, it is ruled by the Lord as a single man is ruled, thus as a one. For although man, as we know, consists of an innumerable variety of parts... nevertheless, when he acts he acts as a single man. (*Heaven and Hell* §63)

And that the unity is constituted by each part performing its own use rather than by any part relocating:

> So many different things in man act as a one, because there is no least thing in him that does not do something for the general welfare and perform some use. The general performs a use for its parts, and the parts for the general... thus it is that they act as one. (*Heaven and Hell* §64)

A body works as a whole. Its parts do not travel through it. A hand does not become an eye by moving, and nothing in the organism's functioning consists in members changing places. Place in such a form is not a position but a function, and this is the point at which the apparent problem of §8.1 dissolves rather than being solved.

The whole spirit expresses at all three degrees. That is what being in the human form means, and it is continuous rather than intermittent. But expressing at all degrees does not entail moving between them, any more than a body's acting as a one entails its organs circulating. What varies is not location but which degree the expression is prominent at, and prominence is determined by internal state:

> The entire heaven is divided into societies according to differences of good of love; and every spirit who is taken up into heaven and becomes an angel is taken to the society where his love is; and when he arrives there he is, as it were, at home, and in the house where he was born... When he goes away to another place he feels constantly a kind of resistance, and a longing to return to his like, thus to his ruling love. (*Heaven and Hell* §479)

The same principle is stated in the *Arcana Coelestia*, where good itself, or love and charity, is said to determine and assign everyone to his own society (§3814), and where all are said to be in heaven in accordance with their good of love (§3644). Place in the Grand Man is a function of ruling love. It is not arrived at by travelling and it is not held by being occupied.

This accounts for the weight described in §8.1, and it accounts for it without introducing anything new. The closing clause of §479 describes precisely that phenomenon: away from one's own place there is "constantly a kind of resistance, and a longing to return to his like, thus to his ruling love." What presents itself as gravitational is the stability of ruling love, which is not easily displaced because it is the thing by which a person is what they are. That prominence shifts under extreme pressure and not otherwise is then unsurprising, since what has to change for prominence to change is the state itself, and states of that order do not change lightly. Section 149 adds that everyone knows his own place by virtue of the form of heaven — a knowing that is a matter of what one is rather than of where one has gone.

Internal state determines the most prominent place in the Grand Man. The internal workings of a spirit have nothing to do with movement, and the whole apparatus of transit that §8.1 seemed to require was an artefact of describing a state-difference in locomotive terms.

### 8.4 What Remains Open

The preceding subsection removes a question rather than answering one, and the distinction matters enough to state plainly rather than to let the resolution look larger than it is.

Dissolving a malformed question is genuine progress, since a framework carrying an unanswerable question at its centre is in worse condition than one that has shown the question to be ill-formed. But no mechanism has been supplied, nothing here is measurable, and three matters remain untouched.

The first is the form constraint. Section 8.1 observed that experience at each degree takes the form permitted there and no other, and the account above says where prominence comes from without saying why the mode of expression at a given degree is what it is. That each degree has its own laws is asserted throughout the framework and argued in *[Asking the Skin to Be the Heart](Asking%20the%20Skin%20to%20Be%20the%20Heart_%20The%20Category%20Error%20of%20Physical%20Miracle%20Theology%20and%20the%20Architecture%20of%20Discrete%20Degrees.md)* §3.2; why they are these laws is not derived from anything more basic.

The second concerns the past-life material specifically. The account handles cross-body continuity only through the general principle that interior states have natural expression, which does not address why a particular state is expressed in a particular body. The framework has no account of that assignment, and §7.2 has already declined the argument that the absence of a competing account favours it.

The third is that none of this is testable at the natural degree, for the reason given in §2.2. Section 8 is a clarification internal to the framework — a demonstration that its commitments are consistent with what the reports describe, which is a weaker achievement than it can appear to be when the consistency is close. Coherence is not confirmation, and §5.4 applies here with equal force.

## 9. Discussion

### 9.1 What Is Established

Four things have been established, and each is narrower than the material it rests on.

The first is architectural. A framework of discrete degrees, properly stated, cannot argue from gaps in physical explanation, and this follows from its own commitments rather than from external criticism (§§2.2, 2.4, 3.2). The framework predicts that natural accounts will close. An incomplete natural account is a worse account, not better evidence, and the entire class of argument running *the physical cannot reach X, therefore something else must* is forfeit permanently and by construction.

The second is that this forfeiture has been carried out in practice rather than merely announced. Section 7.2 declines the strongest-looking empirical argument available in this library — the absence of any known mechanism by which trauma to one body could mark another — on the grounds that using it would violate §2.4. A methodological constraint that is never costly is not a constraint.

The third concerns the specifications derived in §4.3. From Swedenborg's treatment of time and space as appearances of state, together with the doctrine that whatever exists interiorly has a natural expression in ultimates, three requirements follow for anything carrying content apprehended non-successively: it must hold that content without a position in sequence, keep it outside the reach of ordinary retrieval, and deliver it on situational match. These were derived without reference to any empirical literature.

The fourth is that a representational format satisfying all three is described in the memory sciences, independently and for unrelated reasons (§5.1). This is a coherence relation and not a confirmation (§5.4), but it is a coherence relation with the specifications fixed beforehand rather than fitted afterward, which is the only feature that distinguishes it from the pattern-matching §2.3 warns against.

### 9.2 What Is Not Established

More has been left undecided than decided, and the undecided list is the more important of the two.

Nothing here establishes that anything was foreseen. The corpus material is retrospective throughout, and §6.4 gives the deflationary reading of the mission association without refuting it.

Nothing here identifies an origin. The origin column stands exactly as §3.2 left it — open, with the framework holding no entry in it, and with three natural candidates for which published mechanisms exist and among which nothing currently discriminates.

Nothing here confirms the framework. The convergence of §5.2 is available only from inside it, and a reader who declines the framework loses nothing from the empirical sections and gains nothing from the interpretive ones. This is not a shortcoming of the argument but a consequence of §2.2, and it would be true of any argument this framework could construct.

Nothing here supplies a mechanism for anything. Section 8 removes a malformed question and offers a positive account of prominence in terms of state, which is a clarification internal to the framework rather than a mechanism, and §8.4 lists what it leaves untouched.

### 9.3 The Inflation Hazard

Four points in this thesis presented a temptation to claim more than the material supports, and naming them is more useful than asserting that the temptation was resisted.

The birthmark correspondence is the most acute. An 88% figure with no known physical mechanism is exactly the kind of result that invites the inference §2.4 forbids, and the invitation is strongest because the number is genuinely striking. Section 7.2 declines it, and §7.1 records the selection structure that the figure's usual presentation omits.

The convergence of §5.2 is the second. Two descriptions from 1758 and 1996 placing the temporal index in the same relation to the content is a close fit, and close fits invite the language of prediction and confirmation. Neither applies. Swedenborg predicted nothing about memory systems, and dual representation theory supports no theological claim.

The account in §8.3 is the third, and the hazard there is of a different kind. The fit between the resistance described at *Heaven and Hell* §479 and the weight reported in the experiential accounts is close enough to feel like discovery. It is better described as the framework proving internally consistent on a point where it might not have been, which is worth having and is not the same thing.

The fourth is the temporal gradient of §4.2. It is a derivation from Swedenborg's premises rather than a doctrine he states, and presenting it as though he had stated it would root the argument in something no source contains. It is offered as the thesis's own and marked as such at the point of use.

A fifth hazard runs the other way and deserves recording beside them, because a thesis organised around what it refuses to claim is more exposed to it than to any of the four above. Restraint can be overpaid. Declining an argument on a ground that concedes a premise the framework never held is not caution but a substantive error wearing caution's clothes, and it is harder to detect precisely because the surface behaviour — refusing an inference that would have helped — looks like the discipline §2.3 demands. The birthmark material is where the exposure is greatest: the demand for a transfer mechanism is easily granted as legitimate and the argument then declined for want of one, which concedes in the framework's name a commitment nothing in the architecture supports and which the framework denies explicitly where nothing evidential is at stake. The general form is worth stating, since it will recur wherever a framework defines itself by its forfeitures. An inference may be unavailable for a bad reason, and refusing it for that reason is not made harmless by the refusal being correct.

### 9.4 Limitations and Lacunae

**Source verification.** The Swedenborg citations were verified against a curated collection of his works rather than against a critical Latin edition. Section numbering is stable across editions, but chapter titles and translation wording vary, and the passages quoted follow standard English translations. This library contains at least two documents citing *Heaven and Hell* §§141–153 for space and duration; those sections are the chapter on the four quarters, space is §§191–199, and time is §§162–169. The present thesis uses the corrected ranges.

**Corpus limitations.** Both bodies of empirical material are self-selected and retrospective. No incidence claim is made from either, and none is available (§§6.1, 7.1). The 88% birthmark figure describes a subset selected on two conditions and cannot be read as a rate.

**Coding.** Inter-rater agreement on temporal preview was κ = 0.672 with perfect sensitivity, so classification errors run toward over-inclusion, and the base rates in §6.2 should be read with that asymmetry in mind.

**Divergent codings.** The two codings of the physiological comparison in §6.3 disagree, and the disagreement is not resolved here. Both are reported.

**Dependence on a contested model.** Dual representation theory is a live model in clinical psychology rather than a settled result, and this thesis reports it without adjudicating it. What §5 requires, however, is a representational format with three specified properties, not dual representation theory in particular; should the model be superseded by another that preserves the dissociation between sensory representation and contextual binding, §5 would need restatement rather than retraction.

**The unclosed problems of §8.4.** The form constraint at each degree, the assignment of a state to a particular body, and the absence of any natural-degree test all remain open.

**A structural limitation, not a temporary one.** No claim made here is testable at the natural degree, and this will not change with better instruments or larger corpora. It is the direct consequence of §2.2, and any future work in this line inherits it.

## 10. Conclusion: Not Made for Itself

The argument of this thesis has been mostly subtractive, and it is worth saying at the end what the subtraction was for.

A sufficient natural account of temporal preview now exists. The properties that made the phenomenon look anomalous — content arriving without a date, resisting recall, returning on recognition — are the ordinary behaviour of a representational format described for reasons that have nothing to do with near-death experience. The framework does not contest this, is not damaged by it, and under its own commitments should have expected it. Whatever else is true, the natural degree has its own complete expression of whatever occurs above it, and demanding that the natural account fail in order to make room for something else is asking the skin to be the heart.

What remains, when the anomaly is gone, is a question about shape. The natural-degree account of recognition holds content in a form that carries no position in sequence, keeps it out of reach of the system that would search for it, and delivers it when a matching situation arrives. That is what a representational system would have to look like in order to carry something apprehended without succession through a process that runs successively. It is also, entirely, what the memory sciences describe. Both halves are true, and the second does not cancel the first.

But this is where the argument stops, and the stopping is the point rather than a failure to press on. Physical evidence cannot establish a claim about a higher degree. This has been stated four times in four contexts and it is the thesis's only real conclusion. A bucket cannot demonstrate that water is in it. Only the perception of the water does that, and perception is not transferable — no one who sees it can hand the seeing to someone who does not, and no measurement of the vessel will substitute. The evidential asymmetry of §2.2 is not a difficulty peculiar to this subject. It is what the framework's own architecture entails, and a framework that claimed otherwise would have abandoned the architecture in order to win an argument.

What can be said about the vessel is what this thesis has said. Its shape is a holding shape. Every detail of its manufacture may be given, and the account may close completely, without any part of it bearing on whether anything is held — because the manufacturing account was never in competition with the observation, and the completeness of the one has no purchase on the other.

A bucket is not made for itself.
