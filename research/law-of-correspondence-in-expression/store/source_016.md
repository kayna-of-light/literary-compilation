## Passages

**Function to meaning.** Del Vecho (*Frozen 2*): "As we looked into the science of water, we learned about the concept that water has memory. Water dissolves other substances and absorbs part of everything that passes through it, so in a sense it really holds our history."

**Inner state, not arbitrary.**
- Jared Bush (*Encanto*): "the powers assigned to each member didn't feel arbitrary, but were dictated by emotions and personality."
- The house: "a literal representation of the family and their emotional connections. If the family is happy, the house is healthy, but if the family is going through struggles, the house too will crack."

**Force known by its effects.** Gale (*Frozen 2*): "It's not anthropomorphic; it's nature, it's wind. We only 'see' it by how it causes other things to react, like hair or fabric rippling."

**The inner state governs the event.** Dale Mayeda (*Frozen 2*): "What is the emotional tone of this sequence, and how can effects support that? It's not just about what would naturally happen in the real world."

**Truth to the thing.**
- Sean Jenkins (*Frozen 2*): "There has to be a truth to materials."
- Gooding (*Moana*): "we didn't want to mess with the physics."
- Harkness (*Moana*): "evolved from what we noticed in nature … we stylized what was already there."
- Mayeda (*Moana*): lasers "to measure the amount of light diffusing through the water".

**A universal figure from the cultural witnesses.** Clements (*Moana*): consultants from Mo'orea "envisioned Maui with a mane of Sampson-like hair. His hair is his mana, they explained."

## Why it matters

- The artists of 2016–2022 reason from function and force to inner quality, and reject the arbitrary in the very words of Madeley's definition ("no arbitrary relationship", store 005).
- None names Swedenborg, Delsarte or Graham.
- The continuity in between is documented: the 1995 reissue of the memo with Stanchfield's foreword, and Stanchfield's classes (1970s–2000), whose students included Clements and Musker (*Drawn to Life*, Hahn's foreword). See *The Diffracted Light* §§IV–V.
