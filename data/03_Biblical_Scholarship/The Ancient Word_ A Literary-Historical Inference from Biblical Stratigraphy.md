# **The Ancient Word: A Literary-Historical Inference from Biblical Stratigraphy**

## **Abstract**

**Background**: Emanuel Swedenborg (1688–1772) proposed that specific biblical texts—particularly Genesis 1–11 and portions of Job—derived from an "Ancient Word," a pre-Israelite literary tradition preserving a correspondential worldview where natural phenomena systematically represented spiritual realities. This claim, made without access to modern archaeological or philological tools, has been dismissed as theological speculation.

**Methods**: We examine Swedenborg's textual identifications against independent philological and archaeological evidence accumulated since the discovery of the Ebla archives (1974). We analyze lexical fossils, structural parallels, geographic constraints, and legal/economic realia to establish the stratigraphic depth of the texts Swedenborg identified. We then apply the principle of literary probability: fragments of sophisticated literary production imply the existence of a larger body of work.

**Results**: The texts Swedenborg identified as Ancient Word carriers contain genuinely archaic material spanning multiple strata: the Uruk-Ebla horizon (c. 3200–2400 BCE), the Pishon geographic cutoff (pre-2000 BCE), and the Middle Bronze Age legal/economic horizon (c. 1800 BCE). These strata predate standard scholarly dating by 500–2,000 years. The archaic material shows a correspondential structure (nature as revelation of divine order) that was already being contested in later editorial layers (Job's dialogues). The convergence of lexical, structural, geographic, and legal evidence from independent disciplines constitutes cumulative support.

**Conclusions**: While the specific content and transmission history of an "Ancient Word" cannot be directly verified, the textual evidence demonstrates: (1) Swedenborg correctly identified which texts carry archaic material; (2) a substantial pre-Israelite literary tradition must have existed given the sophistication of the surviving fragments; (3) that tradition exhibited a correspondential worldview now partially recoverable from the biblical strata. The Ancient Word hypothesis has moved from theological speculation to literary-historical inference supported by converging independent evidence.

**Keywords**: Ancient Word, Swedenborg, biblical stratigraphy, Ebla, lexical fossils, correspondences, Bronze Age, literary transmission

## **I. Introduction: The Claim and the Problem**

### **1.1 Swedenborg's Assertion**

In *Arcana Coelestia* (1749–1756) and subsequent works, Emanuel Swedenborg made a specific claim: certain biblical texts did not originate with the Israelite nation but were inherited from an earlier tradition he called the "Ancient Word" (*Verbum Antiquum*). He identified Genesis 1–11 (the Primeval History), portions of Job, and elements scattered through the Prophets as carriers of this tradition. According to Swedenborg, the Ancient Word was composed in a correspondential mode—where every natural image systematically represented a spiritual reality—and originated with peoples predating the Hebrew nation, preserved in regions he identified as "Great Tartary" (Central Asia).[^swedenborg-aw]

Swedenborg made these claims based on his theological method, not archaeological evidence. In 1758, no one could verify or falsify such assertions. The Ebla archives were undiscovered. Akkadian was undeciphered. The Pishon River was invisible under Arabian sands. Linguistic dating of biblical texts was in its infancy.

### **1.2 The Methodological Question**

This study does not ask: "Did Swedenborg receive accurate supernatural revelation?" That question lies outside empirical investigation.

We ask instead: **Do the texts Swedenborg identified as Ancient Word carriers show evidence of genuine antiquity?** And if so, **what does that antiquity imply about the existence of a pre-Israelite literary tradition?**

This reframing transforms a theological claim into a testable hypothesis:

| Swedenborg's Claim | Testable Prediction | Method of Testing |
|-------------------|---------------------|-------------------|
| Genesis 1–11 derives from Ancient Word | These texts should show archaic linguistic/structural features | Comparative Semitic philology |
| The tradition predates Israel | Features should predate Israelite literary conventions | Archaeological correlation |
| It was composed in correspondential mode | A nature-as-revelation worldview should be detectable | Literary/theological analysis |
| It was contested/lost over time | Later strata should show theological debate over this worldview | Redaction criticism |

### **1.3 Aims**

1. Establish the stratigraphic depth of the texts Swedenborg identified as Ancient Word carriers
2. Determine whether these texts predate standard scholarly dating
3. Identify whether a correspondential worldview is detectable in the archaic strata
4. Assess whether the fragment pattern implies a larger lost literary tradition
5. Evaluate the cumulative weight of converging independent evidence

## **II. Methods: Stratified Evidence Analysis**

### **2.1 The Principle of Lexical Stratigraphy**

In geological stratigraphy, the age of a rock layer is determined by the fossils it contains. Similarly, the age of a textual tradition can be assessed by "lexical fossils"—vocabulary items that:

1. Possess clear cognates in third or second millennium Semitic languages (Eblaite, Ugaritic, Old Akkadian)
2. Were functionally extinct or semantically shifted by the Iron Age (c. 1000 BCE onward)
3. Appear in fixed literary contexts resistant to scribal modernization

A Persian-period scribe (c. 500 BCE) could not "archaize" using vocabulary that had been extinct for 1,500 years—because the knowledge of that vocabulary had vanished. Its presence implies transmission of a received text from the period when the term was productive.

### **2.2 The Principle of Geographic Constraint**

Certain geographic features provide hard chronological cutoffs:

- A river that dried up in 2000 BCE cannot be described by someone who never saw it
- A coastline that submerged in 3000 BCE cannot be accurately mapped by Iron Age writers
- A trade route that ceased in 1500 BCE cannot be casually referenced in 500 BCE

When texts accurately describe geographic features that no longer existed at the proposed composition date, one of two explanations applies: (1) the tradition originates from when the feature existed, or (2) the writer had access to archival sources preserving that knowledge.

### **2.3 The Principle of Literary Probability**

**People who write fragments don't write only fragments.**

If a culture demonstrates:
- Literacy sufficient to produce the attested texts
- Conceptual sophistication (legal systematization, cosmological thinking, correspondential structure)
- Institutional continuity to transmit fragments across centuries

Then that culture *certainly* produced a larger body of work. The biblical fragments are **survivors**—pieces embedded in later editorial frameworks that thereby escaped oblivion. The complete corpus was lost.

### **2.4 The Cumulative Evidence Principle**

Any single finding could be challenged in isolation. A cognate could be coincidental; a geographic reference could be borrowed; a legal parallel could be "pan-ANE." Skeptics can always propose alternative explanations for individual data points.

But findings are not isolated. When multiple independent disciplines—philology, archaeology, geology, legal history—converge on the same conclusion, the cumulative weight becomes the argument. The question shifts from "Can we explain away each finding?" to "Which framework best accounts for ALL the documented findings?"

## **III. Results: The Stratigraphic Evidence**

### **3.1 The Uruk-Ebla Horizon (c. 3200–2400 BCE)**

The deepest linguistic stratum corresponds to the intellectual world of Early Bronze Age Mesopotamia, as revealed by the Ebla archives (discovered 1974).

#### **Lexical Fossils**

| Term | Location | Cognate | Significance |
|------|----------|---------|--------------|
| *'ed* (אֵד) | Gen 2:6, Job 36:27 | Sumerian *a.dé*, Eblaite *i-du* | Irrigation term from riverine agriculture; alien to rain-fed Judean highlands |
| *tehom* (תְּהוֹם) | Gen 1:2 | Eblaite *ti-a-ma-tum* | Common noun "ocean/abyss" at Ebla (2400 BCE), predating Babylonian mythological usage |
| *mabbul* (מַבּוּל) | Gen 6–9, Ps 29:10 | Eblaite *ma-ba-lum*, Akkadian *bubbulu* | Technical term for catastrophic celestial flooding; extinct in post-exilic Hebrew |
| *min'lam* | Job 15:29 | Eblaite *ma-ni-lum* | Economic term for "accumulated capital/herds"; unknown to Iron Age scribes |
| *mw* | Job 9:30 | Eblaite *mû*, Ugaritic *mw* | Archaic form of "water"; replaced by *mayim* in Standard Biblical Hebrew |

**Finding**: These terms do not represent "Aramaisms" or late innovations. They are retentions from the Early Bronze Age Mesopotamian lexicon—vocabulary that was already archaic by the time of David and Solomon, and completely extinct by the Persian period.

#### **Structural Evidence: *Listenwissenschaft***

Genesis 1 employs *Listenwissenschaft* ("list science"), an intellectual tradition originating in Uruk IV (c. 3200 BCE). In this tradition, knowledge was demonstrated by categorizing the cosmos into exhaustive taxonomic lists (trees, birds, professions, cities). Genesis 1 functions as a "taxonomic liturgy," categorizing creation into realms and inhabitants using the classifier *min* ("kind")—mirroring the determinatives of cuneiform lists.

This cognitive structure is alien to the narrative prose of the Persian period. Its preservation in Genesis 1 argues for transmission of a "scientific" text from the third millennium, adapted for liturgical use.

**Critical Finding**: The Uruk-Ebla horizon provides the **deepest recoverable stratum** of the biblical text. The texts Swedenborg identified as Ancient Word carriers are precisely the texts showing this third-millennium substrate.

---

### **3.2 The Pishon Geographic Cutoff (Pre-2000 BCE)**

Genesis 2:11–12 describes the Pishon River flowing around Havilah (Arabia) where gold is found. Satellite Imaging Radar (SIR-C/X-SAR) revealed a massive paleo-river channel—the "Kuwait River"—crossing the Arabian Peninsula from the Hijaz mountains to the Persian Gulf.[^el-baz]

| Fact | Implication |
|------|-------------|
| The Kuwait River was 3+ miles wide, 500+ miles long | Major hydrological feature |
| Active during Holocene Wet Phase (c. 8000–3500 BCE) | Real river system |
| Completely desiccated by c. 2000 BCE | Hard chronological cutoff |
| Headwaters near Mahd adh Dhahab gold deposits | Matches "land of Havilah where there is gold" |
| Invisible by Iron Age (buried under sand dunes) | No surface trace for Iron Age writer to observe |

**Finding**: The accurate description of a flowing Pishon River, correctly located and associated with Arabian gold, constitutes "fossil knowledge" that **must** derive from a tradition originating before 2000 BCE. An Iron Age scribe looking at Arabia would see only hyper-arid desert. The river had been invisible for a millennium.

This provides a **hard terminus ante quem**: the Eden pericope preserves tradition from the third millennium BCE, regardless of when it was finally compiled.

### **3.3 The Middle Bronze Age Horizon (c. 2000–1550 BCE)**

The patriarchal narratives and legal codes show a distinct stratum dating to the MBA.

#### **Economic Evidence: Slave Prices**

| Period | Slave Price (Shekels) | Source |
|--------|----------------------|--------|
| Old Babylonian / MBA (c. 1800 BCE) | **20** | Code of Hammurabi, Mari archives |
| Nuzi / LBA (c. 1500 BCE) | 30 | Nuzi tablets |
| Neo-Assyrian (c. 8th century BCE) | 50–60 | Assyrian records |
| Persian period (c. 5th century BCE) | 90–120 | Contemporary documents |

Genesis 37:28 records Joseph sold for **20 shekels**—the exact MBA price. A Persian-period scribe inventing the story would use contemporary values (90+ shekels) or symbolic numbers. The preservation of the historically accurate 20-shekel figure implies transmission from an MBA-era source.

#### **Structural Evidence: Treaty Forms**

The Deuteronomic covenant follows the structure of second-millennium Hittite suzerainty treaties:

| Element | Hittite Treaties (c. 1400–1200 BCE) | Deuteronomy | Neo-Assyrian Treaties (c. 700 BCE) |
|---------|-------------------------------------|-------------|-------------------------------------|
| Historical Prologue | ✓ Present | ✓ Present | ✗ Absent |
| Stipulations | ✓ Present | ✓ Present | ✓ Present |
| Blessings | ✓ Present | ✓ Present | ✗ Absent |
| Curses | ✓ Present | ✓ Present | ✓ Present (only curses) |

If Deuteronomy were a "pious fraud" composed in the 7th century BCE (the standard Josianic dating), the scribe would have followed contemporary Neo-Assyrian treaty forms. The presence of the Historical Prologue and balanced blessings/curses structurally anchors the document to the second millennium.

#### **Onomastic Evidence: Naming Patterns**

The patriarchal names (Jacob, Isaac, Ishmael) follow the *Amorite Imperfective* pattern (*yqtl*-names), dominant in the Mari archives (c. 1800 BCE) but statistically rare in the Iron Age. This naming pattern places the traditions in the MBA milieu.

### **3.4 The Correspondential Worldview Under Contest**

The book of Job provides crucial evidence for a **theological debate** within the archaic material—a debate between worldviews.

#### **The Structure of the Contest**

| Element | Theological Position | Linguistic Register |
|---------|---------------------|---------------------|
| Prose frame (1–2, 42:7–17) | Retribution theology ("the righteous prosper") | Standard Biblical Hebrew |
| The friends' speeches | Retribution theology | Standard poetic Hebrew |
| Job's complaints | Challenges retribution | Mixed register |
| Divine speeches (38–41) | **Nature as revelation of order** | Archaic vocabulary, Ugaritic parallels |
| Elihu speeches (32–37) | Late theological addition | Late Biblical Hebrew features |

**The divine speeches are the key.** YHWH responds to Job not with propositional theology but with a tour through creation: the foundations of earth, the storehouses of snow, the behavior of wild animals, the mystery of Behemoth and Leviathan. This is **correspondential revelation**—understanding the divine through observation of natural phenomena.

The book preserves a **contest** between:
- An older wisdom tradition (correspondential, nature-revelatory)
- A newer theological framework (retribution, propositional)

The later editorial layers (prose frame, Elihu speeches) attempt to contain the archaic material within an acceptable theological framework. But the archaic core—the divine speeches—preserves a worldview that matches Swedenborg's description of Ancient Word theology.

**Finding**: The correspondential worldview Swedenborg attributed to the Ancient Word is **observable** in the archaic strata of the biblical text, particularly in the divine speeches of Job and the cosmological structure of Genesis 1.

## **IV. Discussion: The Literary-Historical Inference**

### **4.1 What the Evidence Shows**

The texts Swedenborg identified as Ancient Word carriers contain genuinely archaic material at multiple stratigraphic levels:

| Stratum | Date Range | Evidence Type | Key Findings |
|---------|------------|---------------|--------------|
| Uruk-Ebla | c. 3200–2400 BCE | Lexical, structural | *'ed*, *tehom*, *mabbul*, Listenwissenschaft |
| Pishon Cutoff | Pre-2000 BCE | Geographic | Kuwait River knowledge (hard constraint) |
| MBA | c. 2000–1550 BCE | Legal, economic, onomastic | 20-shekel price, Hittite treaty form, Amorite names |
| Contested Worldview | Visible in redaction | Theological | Divine speeches vs. retribution theology |

**Swedenborg correctly identified the carrier texts.** This is empirically verifiable: the texts he pointed to are the texts showing Bronze Age substrate. He could not have known this in 1758.

### **4.2 What the Evidence Implies**

**The Argument from Literary Probability:**

We observe in the biblical text:
- Fragments of archaic legal material (Covenant Code)
- Fragments of archaic cosmology (Genesis 1–11)
- Fragments of archaic wisdom (Job's divine speeches)
- Fragments of archaic poetry (Song of the Sea, Balaam oracles)
- All converging on the third/second millennium BCE

These fragments demonstrate:
- Literacy capable of producing sophisticated texts
- Conceptual frameworks (legal systematization, taxonomic cosmology, correspondential theology)
- Institutional continuity sufficient for multi-century transmission

**A culture that produces fragments of this sophistication does not produce ONLY fragments.**

The biblical material represents **survivors**—pieces that got embedded in later editorial compilations and thereby escaped the oblivion that claimed the rest. The complete body of work was lost, but its existence is **implied by the fragments**.

### **4.3 What the Evidence Does Not Determine**

The evidence does **not** establish:

1. **The specific content of the Ancient Word** as Swedenborg describes it (the detailed correspondence system)
2. **The transmission path** through "Great Tartary" or specific ethnic groups
3. **The supernatural origin** of Swedenborg's knowledge about the Ancient Word
4. **The historicity** of the narratives (Abraham, Noah, etc.) as opposed to the antiquity of the traditions

The evidence establishes the existence of archaic literary material with a correspondential worldview. It does not verify Swedenborg's account of how he came to know about it.

### **4.4 The Cumulative Weight**

| Evidence Domain | Finding | Independent of Other Domains? |
|-----------------|---------|------------------------------|
| Comparative Semitic Philology | Lexical fossils from 3rd millennium | ✓ Yes |
| Satellite Geology | Pishon River pre-2000 BCE | ✓ Yes |
| ANE Legal History | MBA slave prices, treaty forms | ✓ Yes |
| Redaction Criticism | Contested worldviews in editorial layers | ✓ Yes |
| Literary Structure | Listenwissenschaft cognitive form | ✓ Yes |

When **five independent disciplines** converge on the same conclusion—that the texts Swedenborg identified preserve genuine Bronze Age material—the cumulative probability exceeds what any single finding could establish.

This is how historical inference works. No single clue is decisive; a pattern of clues across independent domains is signal.

## **V. Implications**

### **5.1 For Biblical Scholarship**

The standard dating paradigm (Persian-period composition of most biblical literature) requires revision—not wholesale abandonment, but stratified refinement. The texts show:

- **Final redaction** in the Persian/Hellenistic period (5th–2nd century BCE)
- **Editorial layering** through the monarchic and exilic periods
- **Core traditions** originating in the third/second millennium BCE

All three dates are "correct"—they refer to different strata of the same texts.

### **5.2 For Swedenborgian Studies**

Swedenborg's claim about the Ancient Word has moved from theological assertion to testable hypothesis to **partially confirmed prediction**. He correctly identified:

- Which texts carry archaic material (Genesis 1–11, Job)
- That this material predates the Israelite nation
- That it exhibits a correspondential worldview

He could not have known this through the scholarly tools available in 1758. This does not prove supernatural revelation—but it eliminates "lucky guess" as a plausible explanation. The specificity of his correct identifications requires accounting for.

### **5.3 For the Correspondence Hypothesis**

If the correspondential worldview is detectable in the oldest recoverable biblical strata—and was already being contested in later editorial layers—then correspondence theology is not a late medieval invention or Swedenborg's personal system. It represents the **recovery** of something ancient, something that was known and then lost.

Combined with the empirical evidence for correspondence as ontological feature (NDE phenomenology, DOPS birthmarks, myth formation patterns), this creates a convergence:

- Ancient texts show correspondential structure
- Modern empirical data show correspondential patterns
- The framework that connects them (Swedenborg's) was articulated before either evidence base existed

---

## **VI. Conclusion**

The Ancient Word hypothesis, as a literary-historical inference, rests on three pillars:

1. **Textual Evidence**: The texts Swedenborg identified contain genuinely archaic material from the third and second millennia BCE—predating standard scholarly dating by 500–2,000 years.

2. **Literary Probability**: Fragments of sophisticated literary production imply a larger body of work. The biblical fragments are survivors; the complete corpus was lost.

3. **Worldview Recovery**: The correspondential structure (nature as systematic revelation of spiritual reality) is observable in the archaic strata and was contested in later editorial layers.

The evidence does not prove that an "Ancient Word" existed exactly as Swedenborg described. But it demonstrates that:

- A substantial pre-Israelite literary tradition **must have existed**
- We have **fragments** of that tradition embedded in the Hebrew Bible
- Those fragments exhibit the **correspondential worldview** Swedenborg attributed to the Ancient Word
- Swedenborg correctly identified **which texts** carry this material, 250 years before the philological evidence existed to verify his identification

The Ancient Word is no longer theological speculation. It is a literary-historical inference supported by converging independent evidence from philology, archaeology, geology, and redaction criticism.

The question is no longer "Did an Ancient Word exist?" but "How much of it can we recover from the fragments that survived?"
