# Source 003 — Newbold 1896: Hilprecht's dream of the Kurigalzu cylinder

**Citation**: W. Romaine Newbold, "Sub-conscious Reasoning," *Proceedings of the Society for Psychical Research* 12 (1896–97): 11–20. Public-domain scan: archive.org `proceedingsofsoc12soci`.

**Accessed**: 2026-11-03. Full text read myself (djvu text, pp. 13–18).

## What it says

- **The setting.** Hilprecht's statement is dated 8 August 1893 and translated by Newbold. In March 1893 he had worked for weeks "in the vain attempt to decipher two small fragments of agate which were supposed to belong to the finger rings of some Babylonian." He worked from "a hasty sketch," never from the originals, and had published the two fragments separately. He had tentatively ascribed one fragment to Kurigalzu (p. 14).
- **The dream.** "A tall, thin priest of the old pre-christian Nippur … led me to the treasure chamber of the temple, on its south-east side." The priest said the two fragments "belong together, are not finger rings." They had been cut from a votive cylinder of Kurigalzu to make earrings for the statue of Ninib, and "the third ring you have not yet found in the course of your excavations and you never will find it" (pp. 14–15).
- **Corroboration.** Mrs. Hilprecht's statement: "I … saw Professor Hilprecht hurrying into his study. Thence came the cry 'It is so, it is so'" (p. 16). In August 1893 Hilprecht found the originals in Constantinople "in one case, and the other in a case far away from it." Put together, the stone's veining explained why the two pieces had been described in different colours: "the whitish vein of the stone appeared only upon the one fragment and the larger grey surface upon the other" (p. 17).
- **Newbold's own analysis** (pp. 17–18). He lists six apparently new items and concludes: "A careful analysis reveals the fact that not one of these items was beyond the reach of the processes of associative reasoning which Professor Hilprecht daily employs."
  - Once the fragments were mentally joined, "enough of the inscription became legible to suggest the original character of the object."
  - On the earrings: "in view of the lack of positive confirmation, it would be rash to ascribe it to any supernormal power."
  - On the treasure-room location: Peters "told Professor Hilprecht all these facts as long ago as 1891."

## Why it matters

This is the best-documented case of a spontaneous experience that pointed to a checkable fact. The account was written down within months, corroborated by a witness, and verified against the objects. Its own investigator reads it as unconscious reasoning on material the dreamer already had. The dream did not bring in new information from outside. It brought into one form what the dreamer could not yet join while awake. That limits what this case can show: it shows a spontaneous experience leading to a true, checkable result, not a source outside the dreamer.

## Open questions

- Peters later brought charges of inaccuracy against Hilprecht's Nippur publications (the Peters–Hilprecht controversy; Hilprecht was cleared by his university). This is reported by the research agent from Wikipedia and has not been checked against a scholarly source.
- No later Assyriological re-examination of the fragments was found.
