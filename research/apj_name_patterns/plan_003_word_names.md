# Part 3 — Are the readable word-names apt to their members beyond chance? Research plan

**Started**: 2026-11-10, after Parts 1 and 2 (`synthesis/001_first_run.md`, `synthesis/002_origin.md`) found no relation between members and either the letters or the origin of their names. The one level left is the meaning of the individual word. That is the level at which *The Body That Waited for Breath* reports its hit: "Where a name is a word, it is apt to its member" (§5.7); "of the fourteen word-names in the first list, thirteen are apt to their members" (§12.3). That judgment was made by the thesis's author with the pairs in view. Part 3 asks whether people who do not know the pairs recover them.

## Design: blind one-to-one matching

- **Items.** The fourteen word-names of the first list, as the thesis glosses them (§5.7): meninges, star, of sacred name, firm/true, royal, break, ruler, well-flowering, judgment, star, book, fool, body, rough. Their members are brain, right eye, right ear, teeth, tonsils, right shoulder, right elbow, left forearm, nails of the hands, right breast, kidneys, left kidney, genitals and left foot. Secondary: the four word-names of the second list (Jacob, holding together, earth, pattern/form; right shoulder, left ribs, left thigh, all the genitals).
- **Matchers.** Four fresh agents. Each sees only the glosses and the members, both in an order shuffled for it, with no names, no text, no tools and no knowledge of the true pairs. Each pairs them one to one.
  - **Version A** (two matchers): the members are named only. This tests the plain claim that the words are apt to the members.
  - **Version B** (two matchers): each member also carries its natural function and its spiritual counterpart from the frozen `meaning_table.md`, with source notes removed. This tests the claim that they are apt "in his terms" (§5.7).
- **Prompts.** Generated by `analysis/word_match.py prompts` (seed 20261110) and committed before any matcher runs: `analysis/word_match/prompt_*.txt`, keys `key_*.json`.

## Scoring

The score is the number of glosses placed on their own member. Two pairs of members cannot be told apart by their descriptions, so a gloss counts when it lands on either member of its pair: the two "star" glosses on the right eye and the right breast, and "book" and "fool" on the kidneys and the left kidney. The statistic for each version is the sum over its two matchers. Under the null, each matcher's assignment is a random one-to-one pairing, scored the same way (200,000 simulations). A random pairing gets about one right.

## What counts

- **Hit**: first list, Holm-adjusted p < 0.05 in both versions.
- **Partial**: in one version only.
- **Miss**: in neither.
- Reported in addition: the result without the meninges, a gloss that names part of the brain itself; agreement between the two matchers of each version; the second list (four items, descriptive only, since a random pairing gets all four right one time in 24).

## Stated in advance

- **The thesis's own judgment is not used.** The test does not score whether the words are apt; it asks whether blind matchers recover the pairs.
- **The glosses are the thesis's.** Where a reading is uncertain (Tebar, Euanthen, Kriman and Biblo also have decan readings, §5.7), the test is of the word reading. A miss would leave open whether the names are decans instead.
- **The version-B descriptions were written by a coder who knew the names** (`meaning_table.md` §5). They are short and functional, and they are the frozen text.
- **The matchers are language models**, not people, and judge aptness as a broadly educated reader would. That is the standard the claim "apt to its member" sets.
