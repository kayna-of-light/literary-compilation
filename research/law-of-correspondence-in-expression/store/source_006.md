# Source 006 — Stebbins, *Delsarte System of Expression* (6th ed., 1901)

**Citation**: Stebbins, Genevieve. *Delsarte System of Expression.* 6th ed., revised and enlarged. New York: Edgar S. Werner, 1901. First edition 1885; Volkonsky lists an 1892 edition.

**Accessed**: 2026-10-24, archive.org `delsartesysteme00stebgoog` (djvu full text, read in the relevant chapters; page numbers are the book's).

## Who Stebbins was

The leading American Delsartean teacher, a pupil of Steele MacKaye (who studied with Delsarte in Paris). The book went through many editions and became the standard English textbook.

## The Swedenborgian layer (her own chapters)

**Trinity chapter, pp. 108–111.** She surveys the threefold principle through the ancients, in this order:
- "The Druidical triad";
- the Egyptians, Greeks and Persians;
- "the oracle of Serapis";
- "Pythagoras, the Samian philosopher";
- "the ancient trinities of the Hindoos" and Chinese philosophy;
- the Platonic hypothesis.

Then: "The above ideas agree with Swedenborg. He defines trinity as consisting of 'love, wisdom and power. Love, being the origin and parent of all existence, is called Father; wisdom, which is the form of love, is named the Son, and the only begotten; while the power, consisting in the perfect union of love and wisdom, going forth in creative energy and life-imparting influence, is the Holy Spirit.'" Then the principle: "Every created thing is composed of two parts: life-power or energy, and a form to show this power in effect … the spiritual form being the life-power or energy, and the material form being the one which appears to the senses."

**Then *DLW* §219, introduced "Swedenborg writes"** (p. 109–110): "Three degrees: living endeavor, living power, and living motion. The endeavor in a man who is a living subject is his will united to his understanding; the living powers in him are what constitute the interior of his body, in all of which there are moving fibres variously interwoven; and living motion in him is action which is produced through those powers by the will united to the understanding. Powers have no potency but by action of the body."

**Then the body** (p. 110): "matter has no form of itself. It is being that forms the matter. That head, torso and limbs … are but the covering of a spiritual head, torso and limbs." A second Swedenborg quotation: "Man is a microcosm …". Summary: "The triune in deific essence is Love, Wisdom, Power."

**Monroe** (pp. 113–114): "The late Prof. Monroe called it [the chart of the ninefold accord] 'the key of the universe.' He also called Delsarte 'Swedenborg geometrized'." Lewis B. Monroe was the Boston teacher of expression who taught Henrietta Hovey (store 008).

**Correspondence defined through Madeley** (p. 139): "The science of semeiotics is the science of signs, of correspondences. … The material form should correspond to the inner form … 'Correspondence is no arbitrary relationship like metaphor or figure, but one founded alike on the inward and outward nature of the things by which we are surrounded.'" Footnote naming "Rev. Edward Madeley".

**Delsarte's law, in his address** (p. 67): "To each spiritual function responds a function of the body. To each grand function of the body corresponds a spiritual act."

## Her own account of Delsarte's sources (later chapters)

- **pp. 383–386.** Delsarte's statements "might have been written by any Christian student of the cabala". His definition of the Trinity "is purely neo-platonic, and is taken bodily from the cabalism of the hermetic writers". "He borrowed both his religion and his philosophy." His philosophy is "an esoteric Christianity; a compound of cabalistic lore and Platonism". His *system* of expression is "true, so far as it goes", "based on self-evident principles in nature … absolutely independent" of the philosophy.
- **p. 387 n.** "I myself am firm believer in the esoteric doctrine of the hermetic writers, that man is the microcosm and symbol of Deity."
- **pp. 389–390** (ch. IV, "Modern or Practical Delsartism"): "This fixed principle is the great Law of Correspondence, a law old almost as man. … although he may have borrowed ideas from Swedenborg, Delsarte was the first among modern students to unearth this lost principle of art, which gave to antiquity its artistic greatness and which furnishes the cornerstone of all the important systems of expression of to-day."
- **p. 391.** "Delsarte welded the whole of his system together with the universal Law of Correspondence, which in its original form was a religious conception."
- **pp. 369, 374.** "the great law of correspondence was God's law from the first"; "the basic truth of art rests on the law of correspondence between mental states and physical expression".
- **p. 420.** Delsarte "reinvented a system of expression as old almost as time, based on the so-called hermetic doctrine of correspondences".
- **p. 451.** The Greeks "seized on the hermetic formula of correspondence: 'As it is below, so it is above'".

## Reading

Stebbins holds both lines at once, which is exactly the situation the author describes:
- **Historically**, she traces Delsarte's philosophy to the cabala and the hermetic writers.
- **Doctrinally**, she reads it through Swedenborg. His trinity "agrees with Swedenborg"; *DLW* §219 supplies the order of will, forces and action; Madeley's New Church definition supplies what correspondence *is*; Swedenborg's "love, wisdom and power" names Delsarte's trinity.

So the structure came down the hermetic path, and the key she applies to it is Swedenborg's. That is the Swedenborgian layer (synthesis theme_002).
