# Why Mainstream Scholarship Insists on Late Dating Despite Philological Evidence

**Date**: 2026-02-07  
**Context**: Discussion following Job vs. Proverbs research; Ancient Word hypothesis validation

---

## The Puzzle

Basic philological facts point to 3rd-millennium origins for Genesis 1-11 and Job:
- Eblaite cognates (*mw*, *min'lam*, *ed*) that were extinct by 2000 BCE
- *Listenwissenschaft* structure matching Uruk-period lists
- Geographic knowledge (Pishon/Kuwait River) that didn't exist after 2000 BCE
- *Toledot* colophons matching Eblaite archival practice

Yet mainstream biblical scholarship dates these texts to the Persian period (5th-4th c. BCE). Why?

---

## Reasons for the Late-Dating Consensus

### 1. The Documentary Hypothesis Lock-In

19th-century German scholarship (Wellhausen et al.) built an entire paradigm around **linear development**: primitive → complex, oral → written, polytheism → monotheism. This paradigm *requires* late dating:

- Complex texts (like Job) must be "late" because complexity = development
- "Aramaisms" must indicate Persian-period composition
- Monotheism is a late achievement, so sophisticated theology = post-exilic

**The problem**: Ebla (discovered 1974) and Ugarit destroyed this model. We now have complex literature from 2500 BCE. But the paradigm persists because careers, institutions, and entire subdisciplines are built on it.

### 2. The Aramaism Fallacy

The core "evidence" for late dating is Aramaisms—words that look Aramaic and therefore (the argument goes) must be late borrowings from the Persian period when Aramaic was the lingua franca.

**What Eblaite revealed**: Many "Aramaisms" are actually **archaic Northwest Semitic forms** that predate both Hebrew AND Aramaic. They look "late" only because they dropped out of Hebrew by the monarchic period.

| "Late" Feature | Actual Origin |
|----------------|---------------|
| *mw* for "water" (Job 9:30) | Eblaite 3rd millennium—predates *mayim* |
| Certain verb forms | Attested in Ugaritic (1400 BCE) and Eblaite |
| "Aramaisms" | Common NW Semitic features preserved in Job, lost in Standard BH |

Young, Rezetko, and Ehrensvärd's work (*Linguistic Dating of Biblical Texts*, 2008) has systematically demolished the linguistic dating method, showing that "late" features appear in early inscriptions and "early" features appear in late texts. **Linguistic dating doesn't work the way the field assumed.**

### 3. The Theological Third Rail

Here's what nobody says out loud: **late dating protects theological commitments**.

If Job is genuinely 3rd-millennium or preserves 3rd-millennium material:
- The "unique" theology of Israel isn't unique—it's inherited
- The "development" from primitive to sophisticated collapses
- Swedenborg's "Ancient Word" hypothesis becomes plausible
- The entire paradigm of Israelite religious evolution is threatened

Late dating keeps everything safely within the period scholars can control. Push the origin back 2,000 years, and suddenly you're dealing with traditions that predate the categories scholarship relies on.

### 4. Career Incentives

Academics don't get tenure for confirming "fringe" hypotheses. They get tenure for working within established paradigms and making incremental contributions. Proposing that Job is 3rd-millennium would require:

- Challenging Wellhausen (sacred cow)
- Taking Swedenborg seriously (career suicide in secular academy)
- Admitting the linguistic dating method is broken (invalidates decades of work)

The incentives all point toward **maintaining the consensus**, even when the evidence doesn't support it.

---

## The Bottom Line

The "hard linguistic facts" are real:
- The Eblaite cognates are real
- The archaic morphology is real
- The fact that Job's vocabulary was extinct by 2000 BCE is real

Mainstream scholarship ignores this because:
1. The paradigm can't accommodate it
2. The linguistic dating method is assumed valid despite being falsified
3. Career incentives punish deviation
4. Late dating protects theological commitments (both secular-progressive and traditional-religious)

**This is why the framework matters.** We're not bound by academic career incentives. We can follow the evidence: if *mw* is Eblaite, if *min'lam* is Eblaite, if the *Listenwissenschaft* structure matches Uruk-period lists—then the text preserves 3rd-millennium material, regardless of what the consensus says.

The truth shows itself because it is the truth, not because scholars approve.
