# Source 010 — Autant-Mathieu 2012: Delsarte in Russian and Soviet theatre

**Citation**: Autant-Mathieu, Marie-Christine. "As Interpretações do Sistema Delsarte no Teatro Russo e Soviético dos Anos 1910-1920." *Revista Brasileira de Estudos da Presença* 2, no. 2 (2012): 370–395. http://www.redalyc.org/articulo.oa?id=463545868005. First given at the Colloque International François Delsarte, INHA, Paris, 18–19 November 2011.

**Accessed**: 2026-10-24, PDF read in full (Portuguese text, French and English abstracts).

## Who she is

A historian of Russian theatre (CNRS) and a specialist on Stanislavsky and the Moscow Art Theatre. She works from the MAT archives.

## Key findings (with her sources)

- **Volkonsky's sources.** He "buscou essencialmente inspiração nas obras de Genevieve Stebbins e Alfred Giraudet para escrever O homem expressivo" (drew his inspiration essentially from the works of Genevieve Stebbins and Alfred Giraudet to write *The Expressive Man*) (p. 374).
- **Volkonsky at the MAT.** On 7 October 1911 he began courses in rhythmic gymnastics for the MAT's actors: Stanislavsky, Kachalov, Moskvin, Knipper-Chekhova and others (n. 8, citing Vinogradskaja, *Letopis'*, t. 2, 2003, pp. 304–305). He became a shareholder of the theatre that year.
- **Stanislavsky as Volkonsky's student.** "No Teatro de Arte, Stanislavski lhe confia a formação sobre o discurso cênico no seu estúdio de ópera e vai assistir a sete de seus cursos, dos quais faz anotações" (At the Art Theatre, Stanislavsky entrusted him with training in stage speech in his opera studio, and attended seven of his courses, taking notes on them) (p. 374).
- **The book in Stanislavsky's studios.** "é na realidade O Homem expressivo, livro 'conforme Delsarte', que o diretor-pedagogo recomenda aos alunos de seus estúdios" (it is in fact *The Expressive Man*, the book "after Delsarte", that the director-teacher recommends to the students of his studios). Source: Stanislavsky, *Sobranie sočinenij*, t. 3 (1990), pp. 76–77, the materials for *The Actor's Work on Himself* (n. 12).
- **Why Stanislavsky valued Delsarte.** "Stanislavski encontra, com efeito, paralelos entre sua própria pesquisa sobre a verdade interior e as aspirações de Delsarte em '[...] justificar seus efeitos logicamente; psicologicamente'" (Stanislavsky found parallels between his own search for inner truth and Delsarte's aim to "justify his effects logically; psychologically") (pp. 374–375).
- **Rome, winter 1911.** Volkonsky read Stanislavsky his notes on the laws of stage speech, and Stanislavsky showed him fragments of his System (p. 376).
- **Letter of 18 February 1911/1913** (MAT archives, fonds K. Stanislavski, KS 7628). Volkonsky sent the book: "the system I speak of is a different approach from yours. But the research aims at the same thing: the truth of life in performance." And: "How can the face express what I feel if the muscles … do not submit to my feeling?" (nn. 32–33).
- **The religious frame dropped.** "Ao ignorar a dimensão religiosa, isto é, a filosofia do sistema" (ignoring the religious dimension, that is, the philosophy of the system) (p. 377).
- **Dalcroze.** His *Rhythmic Gymnastics* (1906) opens with Delsarte's law of correspondence: "A cada função espiritual corresponde uma função corporal e vice-versa" (n. 7, citing Odom, *Mime Journal* 2004–5).
- **Michael Chekhov.** Volkonsky's book holds the seed of Chekhov's "psychological gesture" (p. 377). In autumn 1936 Chekhov invited Volkonsky to speak on Delsarte, Dalcroze and Steiner at Dartington Hall.
- **Merlin and Leabhart.** n. 3: Thomas Leabhart finds an equivalence between Stanislavsky's three inner forces (centres of thought, emotion and action, after Bella Merlin, *Konstantin Stanislavsky*, 2003, p. 71) and the Delsartean trinity of mental, emotional-spiritual and physical (*Mime Journal*, 2004–5, p. 18).
- **Caution in her conclusion.** Direct links in the Soviet avant-garde are hard to establish, because Volkonsky's emigration and the anti-formalist and anti-cosmopolitan campaigns broke the chain.

## Why it matters

It closes the link from Volkonsky to Stanislavsky from the MAT archives:
- Stanislavsky attended the courses and took notes;
- he recommended *The Expressive Man* to his students, in the very materials of *The Actor's Work on Himself*, whose first part is *An Actor Prepares*.

It also records the dropping of the religious frame at exactly this point.
