# Source 007 — Vonk & Visser 2021: spiritual superiority is highest where spiritual skills are trained

**Citation**: Roos Vonk and Anouk Visser, "An Exploration of Spiritual Superiority: The Paradox of Self-Enhancement," *European Journal of Social Psychology* 51, no. 1 (2021): 152–165. https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.2721. Preprint: https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/qh457.

**Accessed**: 2026-11-03. Abstract read myself via Crossref. Recruitment details are from the research agent's reading of the preprint (secondary).

## What it says

- "Spiritual training is assumed to reduce self-enhancement, but may have the paradoxical effect of boosting superiority feelings."
- Three studies (N = 533, 2,223, 965). A brief spiritual-superiority measure "showed good internal consistency and discriminant validity."
- Superiority "was correlated with self-esteem and, more strongly, with communal narcissism, corroborating the notion of spiritual narcissism."
- "Spiritual Superiority scores were consistently higher among energetically trained participants than mindfulness trainees and were associated with supernatural overconfidence and self-ascribed spiritual guidance."
- "The self-enhancement motive is powerful and deeply ingrained so that it can hijack methods intended to transcend the ego and, instead, adopt them to its own service."
- Secondary (preprint, via the agent): Study 1 recruited through Dutch mindfulness schools and "energetic" training centres. The latter teach skills "such as reading auras and regressing to previous lives."

## Why it matters

The library's direction criterion (reception versus claiming, *The Empty Room* §2.3) predicts that a practice which teaches the self to produce spiritual contact will tend to inflate the self. This study measures that tendency where such skills are trained: superiority is higher there and goes with "self-ascribed spiritual guidance." It is the nearest published measurement of the claiming posture.

## Limits

- It does not sample NDE experiencers or channelers as such.
- Correlational and cross-sectional.
- "Energetic" training is a broad category.
