# **The Animating Spirit: An Exhaustive Analysis of Swedenborgian Correspondences in the Cinematic Universe of Walt Disney**

## **I. Introduction: The Cartography of the Invisible**

The visual language of animation operates upon a fundamental, almost metaphysical premise: that the internal world of emotion, intent, and character can be—and indeed must be—externalized into the visible world of form, movement, and environment. In the medium of the animated feature film, a storm is rarely a mere meteorological event; it is the externalized fury of a protagonist or the chaotic upheaval of a social order. A landscape is not simply a geographical setting; it is the moral state of the kingdom made manifest in soil and flora. Animals are not distinct biological entities operating solely on Darwinian instinct; they are anthropomorphized extensions of human affection, acting as mirrors to the soul of the hero or the villain. This "language of nature," which the studio taught its animators as a craft for communicating meaning to a universal audience, bears a striking, structural isomorphism to the theological and epistemological system proposed two centuries earlier by the Swedish scientist, philosopher, and mystic, Emanuel Swedenborg (1688–1772).  
This report undertakes a rigorous, comparative investigation into the narrative and visual structures of Disney animated feature films to determine the extent to which they align with Swedenborg’s "Doctrine of Correspondences." This doctrine posits that the natural world exists as a "theater representative" of the spiritual world (*Arcana Coelestia* §3483), where every physical object, animal, and phenomenon springs from a specific spiritual reality "precisely like an effect from its effecting cause" (*Heaven and Hell* §89). The inquiry seeks not only to identify these parallels but to trace their complex genealogy. Is the resemblance between Disney’s "Pridelands" in *The Lion King* and Swedenborg’s description of the "State of the Church" coincidental, or does it stem from a shared lineage of influence flowing through the Romantic fairy tales of Hans Christian Andersen, the method of expression the studio taught its artists, and the artistic sensibilities of the 19th-century Symbolists?  
The analysis proceeds from the hypothesis that Disney animation, through its reliance on literary devices such as the "Pathetic Fallacy" (Ruskin 1856) and mythic structures like the "Fisher King" archetype, and through a method of expression whose route from Swedenborg is documented, reconstructs a Swedenborgian cosmos, without naming him but with precision. In this cosmos, the "King" corresponds to Divine Truth (*True Christian Religion* §114), the "Land" corresponds to the collective spiritual state of the people or the church (*Apocalypse Revealed* §285), and "Animals" correspond to the specific volitional affections—the loves, lusts, and intellectual appetites—of the human spirit. By examining a corpus of films including *The Lion King*, *Beauty and the Beast*, *The Little Mermaid*, *Frozen*, *Tangled*, *Pocahontas*, *Aladdin*, *Sleeping Beauty*, *Brave*, and *Mulan*, this report will demonstrate that the "Disney Formula" is, functionally and semiotically, a "Science of Correspondences" applied to the medium of moving pictures.

### **1.1 The Epistemological Framework: Swedenborg’s Semiotics**

To assess the validity of the comparison between Disney’s cinematic language and Swedenborg’s theology, one must first define the precise mechanism of Swedenborgian correspondence. Unlike simple allegory, which is arbitrary invention or poetic simile (*The Hermeneutics of the Mundus Imaginalis* §2), correspondence is defined as a causal, ontological relationship. It is not that a lion *reminds* the viewer of courage in a metaphorical sense; it is that the spiritual affection of the "power of truth" (*Apocalypse Revealed* §241) *causes* the form of a lion to exist in the natural world. The natural form is the "ultimate" container for the spiritual cause.  
Swedenborg’s system rests on three pillars that are particularly relevant to animation analysis:

* **Vertical Causality (Influx):** The spiritual world is the realm of causes; the natural world is the realm of effects. Life flows from the Divine, through the spiritual degrees, into natural forms. The quality of the form determines the reception of the influx. A distorted form (a villain or a wasteland) receives the same life force but twists it into evil or decay.  
* **Discrete Degrees:** Reality is stratified into distinct levels that do not merge but communicate via correspondence. The "Celestial" degree (pertaining to Love and the Will) flows into the "Spiritual" degree (pertaining to Wisdom and the Intellect), which in turn flows into the "Natural" degree (pertaining to Action and Form).  
* **The Grand Man (Maximus Homo):** Swedenborg posits that the entire heaven is structured in the form of a single human being, "which is therefore called the grand man" (*Arcana Coelestia* §1276), and by extension, human society and the church function as a "collective man." The health of this collective body depends on the proper subordination of the "natural" (feet/extremities) to the "spiritual" and "celestial" (heart/head).

In the context of animation, the animator plays the role of the "spiritual cause," infusing *anima* (life/soul) into the "natural" forms on screen. Thus, the physics of the Disney universe are inherently spiritual physics: gravity, light, color, and biology obey the emotional and moral logic of the scene rather than the laws of Newtonian mechanics. This artistic necessity aligns the medium perfectly with Swedenborg’s vision of a plastic, responsive spiritual world where affections manifest visibly as animals and landscapes shift instantaneously to reflect the interior state of the inhabitants.

## **II. The King and the Land: Topography as Moral Mirror**

One of the most persistent and structurally significant tropes in the Disney canon is the intrinsic, causal link between the moral condition of the monarch and the physical health of the environment. This narrative device, known in comparative mythology and literary criticism as the "Fisher King" archetype (Weston 1920), is structurally identical to Swedenborg’s correspondence of the "Land" or "Earth" with the "State of the Church" (the collective spiritual state of humanity). In Swedenborg’s exegesis, the physical state of the land in biblical prophecy—whether it is blooming, drought-stricken, or frozen—is never a description of agriculture but a precise diagnosis of the spiritual state of the people.

### **2.1 The Pridelands: A Case Study in Spiritual Vastation**

*The Lion King* (1994) offers the most explicit and detailed demonstration of Swedenborgian environmental correspondence in the Disney library. Under the rule of Mufasa, the Pridelands are lush, vibrant, and orderly. The "Circle of Life" represents a spiritual order where the "Sun" (corresponding to Divine Love and Wisdom; *Heaven and Hell* §116) governs the hierarchy of creation. Mufasa, whose name and role correspond to the "King" (Divine Truth governing via Law; *Arcana Coelestia* §5068), maintains the influx of life through adherence to Divine Order. He respects the boundaries of the kingdom and the balance of nature.  
When Scar usurps the throne, the transformation of the landscape is immediate, total, and unexplained by meteorological science. The sun is obscured by permanent dark clouds; the water (Truth; *Apocalypse Revealed* §50) dries up completely; the vegetation dies; and the land becomes a skeletal wasteland characterized by bones and gray earth.

* **Swedenborgian Analysis of Scar’s Reign**: This ecological collapse is a perfect depiction of what Swedenborg terms "Vastation." Swedenborg reads the "earth" of prophecy as the church: where Isaiah's Jehovah "maketh the earth empty," the "earth" denotes the church, "which is made empty and is emptied out" (*Arcana Coelestia* §2567). When the "King" (Truth) is replaced by the "Usurper" (Falsity derived from Self-Love/Selfhood), the connection to the "Spiritual Sun" is severed. The result is "a dry and thirsty land, where no water is" (Ps 63:1)—no truth can be found. The hyenas, which invade the Pridelands, are of the kind Swedenborg calls "fierce and useless"—animals that correspond to evil affections (*Heaven and Hell* §110)—and in the Word "beasts" signify cupidities (*Arcana Coelestia* §6441). They are scavengers, representing the consumption of truth without the production of good.  
* **The Fisher King Connection**: Critics have explicitly linked Simba’s journey to the Fisher King legend (Buhler 2003; Mackey-Kallis 2001), where the "wasteland" can only be healed by the return of the rightful King who asks the right question or restores the Grail. Swedenborgian theology provides the metaphysical mechanism for this healing: the "restoration of the church" (the return of Simba/Truth) re-establishes the influx of Divine Life. The physical world blooms instantaneously upon the restoration of moral order. The rain that falls upon Scar’s defeat and Simba’s ascension is the correspondence of "Truth" cleansing the earth of falsity and refreshing the "withered" state of the mind. The visual cue of the clouds clearing to reveal the sun matches the correspondence of the removal of falsity allowing the light of Wisdom to return.

### **2.2 Arendelle’s Eternal Winter: The Glacial State of Isolation**

In *Frozen* (2013), the landscape of Arendelle is frozen not by a climatic shift but by the internal emotional state of its Queen, Elsa. The film presents a literalization of the metaphor "frozen heart."

* **Swedenborgian Analysis of Cold**: Swedenborg writes extensively on the "frigid zones" of the spiritual world. He states that climates "in the spiritual world … vary according to the distances of the will’s affections and the consequent thought of the understanding from true love and true faith … In the frigid zones of the spiritual world things appear similar to those in the frigid zones of the natural world; lands and waters alike are bound in ice with snow upon them" (*True Christian Religion* §185). Those who dwell there "had lulled their understanding to sleep by their indolence in thinking of spiritual things, and … were consequently indolent in doing anything useful" (*True Christian Religion* §185); and "spiritual cold is no love" (*Arcana Coelestia* §4175). Elsa’s fear and isolation cut her off from "Love" (Heat; *Heaven and Hell* §133), resulting in a literal freezing of her environment.  
* **Snow vs. Ice**: In Swedenborg’s system, "Water" corresponds to Truth. "Ice" corresponds to truth that lacks good (love), becoming hard, brittle, and cold—often associated with "faith alone" without charity: companies of spirits "who are in faith separated from charity may be likened to such icebergs" (*True Christian Religion* §385), and the man "who is in faith alone" is "in cold and in darkness" (*Arcana Coelestia* §5232). "Snow" corresponds to natural truth, "truth in ultimates" (*Apocalypse Revealed* §47). Elsa’s power creates a beautiful but lifeless world of ice (intellectual structuring without warmth).  
* **The Thaw**: The film’s resolution hinges on the distinction between "Faith Alone" (fear/knowledge) and "Charity" (Love in action). Anna, who represents "Affection" or "Charity," must perform an act of true love to thaw the frozen heart. Swedenborg argues that only "spiritual heat" (Love; *Arcana Coelestia* §4175) can melt the hardened state of the self. The thawing of the fjord at the end of the film is the restoration of the "flow of truth" (liquid water) through the "warmth of love" (sun).

### **2.3 The Beast’s Castle: Architecture of the Soul**

The castle in *Beauty and the Beast* (1991) serves as an architectural extension of the Beast’s soul, a motif heavily influenced by the Gothic tradition but readable through Swedenborg’s lens of the "House" as the mind. As the Prince descends into animalistic despair and rage, the castle becomes dark, gothic, and decaying. The "West Wing," holding the Rose (the remnant of spiritual life), is the most decayed and forbidden area—the "inmost" of the mind where the ruling love is sequestered.

* **Transformation as Glorification**: When the Beast is regenerated through his love for Belle (who represents the affection for truth or wisdom), the castle transforms: the gloom lifts from it, and its gargoyles become cherubs.  
* **Swedenborgian Analysis**: This mirrors the "glorification" of the human form or the "regeneration" of the mind. The "stonework" and "gargoyles" represent the "hardened falsities" and "sensual lusts" of the unregenerate natural mind. The transformation of the gargoyles into cherubs corresponds to the reception of "celestial good" and "spiritual truth". The landscape shifts from a "dark forest" (a state of ignorance and temptation, inhabited by wolves/lusts) to a "garden" (a state of intelligence and wisdom). The transformation is not just cosmetic; it is ontological.

### **2.4 The Black Rot of Motunui: The Separation of Will and Understanding**

In *Moana* (2016), the "darkness" or black rot spreading across the islands, which turns Motunui’s coconuts black, is caused by the theft of the "Heart of Te Fiti."

* **Swedenborgian Analysis**: The "Heart" corresponds to the **Will** or **Divine Love**. Te Fiti, as the creative principle of nature, represents the "Celestial" degree of creation. When the "Heart" (Love) is separated from the body of the world, the result is "spiritual death" manifested as "decay" or "rot" in the vegetation. Maui, who stole the heart, represents the "Intellect" or "Human Selfhood" acting independently of Divine Order, believing it can possess the source of life for itself (promethean theft). The "Demon of Earth and Fire," Te Ka, is revealed to be Te Fiti without her heart. This aligns with Swedenborg’s teaching that "Love" without "Wisdom" (or the heart separated from its order) turns into a consuming fire of self-love; "fire" in the opposite sense "denotes the evil of the love of self" (*Arcana Coelestia* §7852). The restoration of the Heart unites "Will" (Te Fiti) and "Understanding" (Moana/Maui), healing the land (The Church).

## **III. The Menagerie of the Mind: Animal Correspondences**

Swedenborg asserts that "the animals of the earth correspond in general to affection, mild and useful animals to good affections, fierce and useless ones to evil affections" (*Heaven and Hell* §110). In Disney films, animals rarely appear as mere livestock; they are almost always "sidekicks" or "extensions" of the main character, externalizing their inner emotional reality. This externalization is not random; specific animals consistently perform roles that align with their Swedenborgian correspondences.

### **3.1 The Horse: The Intellectual Principle**

In Swedenborg’s system, the **Horse** corresponds to the **Intellect** or the **Understanding of Truth** (*Arcana Coelestia* §2761; *Apocalypse Revealed* §298). The horse carries the rider just as the understanding carries the will. The quality of the horse (color, temperament) reflects the quality of the understanding.

* **Maximus (*Tangled*)**: Maximus is a white horse who is obsessed with the "Law." He hunts Flynn Rider (a thief) with the tenacity of a rigid intellect. He is "The Law" personified—intelligent, relentless, but initially lacking in charity. His "taming" by Rapunzel (who represents Affection/Good) illustrates the Swedenborgian principle that the "Intellect" must be guided by "Affection" to be useful. An intellect separated from love is hard and judgmental (Maximus at the start); conjoined with love, it becomes a powerful vehicle for good.  
* **Samson (*Sleeping Beauty*)**: Prince Phillip’s white horse symbolizes his "intellectual power" and "spiritual understanding." When Phillip fights the dragon (Maleficent), he rides the white horse (Truth) and wields the "Mighty Sword of Truth"; in Swedenborg a "sword" signifies "the truth of faith combating" (*Arcana Coelestia* §2799). Samson is brave but must be guided by Phillip, just as the understanding must be directed by the spiritual will.  
* **Philippe (*Beauty and the Beast*)**: A draft horse, earthy and easily frightened. He corresponds to the "natural understanding" which is easily spooked by the "dark forest" of ignorance and the "wolves" of lust/falsity. He carries Maurice (intellectual curiosity) but cannot protect him from the Beast (the unregenerate will) because the natural understanding is powerless against the will's ferocity without spiritual aid.  
* **Khan (*Mulan*)**: A black horse. In Swedenborg, "black" signifies "what is man’s own" (*Arcana Coelestia* §3993)—the selfhood or the natural will which is not yet enlightened— but Khan is loyal and strong. His color contrasts with the white horse of Shang, representing the balance of "Yin" (female/natural) and "Yang" (male/spiritual) within the film's Taoist-adjacent visual language.  
* **Angus (*Brave*)**: A heavy draught horse, powerful and earthy. He corresponds to Merida’s "natural intellect"—strong, stubborn, and connected to the earth, supporting her desire for autonomy. He is the vehicle for her rebellion but also her strength.  
* **The Nokk (*Frozen II*)**: The water horse. Swedenborg links the horse to the fountain through Pegasus, the flying horse that "broke open a fountain with his hoof" (*Arcana Coelestia* §4966): "a horse, in the mystic sense, signifies the understanding, and a fountain truth" (*Arcana Coelestia* §2762; *White Horse* §4). Elsa taming the Nokk is the perfect visualization of the "Spiritual Mind" (Elsa) subduing and riding the "Natural Understanding" (The Horse) through the dark waters of trial. She does not kill the horse; she reins it, creating a "bridle of understanding."

### **3.2 The Bird: Rational Thought and Intelligence**

**Birds** correspond to **rational concepts**, **thoughts**, and **intellectual ideas** (*Arcana Coelestia* §40; *Apocalypse Revealed* §757) because they fly above the earth (the natural mind) and have keen sight.

* **Scuttle (*The Little Mermaid*)**: Scuttle provides Ariel with "information" about the human world. However, his information is empirically wrong (calling a fork a "dinglehopper"). This mirrors Swedenborg’s description of "falsities" or "illusory truths" in the natural mind—reasoning from the senses that leads to error. He represents "false rationality" or "imagination".  
* **Zazu (*The Lion King*)**: A hornbill who serves as the majordomo. He represents "administrative truth" or "orderly thinking." He hovers over the kingdom, observing and reporting. He is the "Rational" attempting to keep the "Natural" (Simba) in check. His imprisonment by Scar (the ribcage) symbolizes the "Rational" being silenced or caged by the "Self" (Self-Love).  
* **Flit (*Pocahontas*)**: A hummingbird, representing quick, fleeting thoughts or anxieties. He is contrasted with Meeko (a raccoon), representing the "sensual" or "corporeal" appetites (food/curiosity). Flit acts as a "conscience" or "cautionary thought".  
* **Iago (*Aladdin*)**: A parrot. Swedenborg’s own image for speech that repeats without understanding is the parrot: those whose worship is of the lips only are "like crows and parrots taught to repeat the words of a psalm" (*True Christian Religion* §452). Iago mimics Jafar’s villainy but has no internal moral compass of his own (initially). He represents the "memory-knowledge" of the wicked mind, repeating falsities without genuine grounding.  
* **The Raven (Diablo) (*Sleeping Beauty*)**: Maleficent’s raven represents falsity sent out to seek the innocent (Aurora) and destroy her. In Swedenborg "by a 'raven' are signified falsities" (*Arcana Coelestia* §864), and ravens describe "gross and dense falsities" "because they are of a black color" (*Arcana Coelestia* §866).

### **3.3 The Dog: Natural Affection and Loyalty**

**Dogs** correspond to the **affections of the natural mind**, specifically **subservience**, **loyalty**, and **friendship**. In the negative sense, turned to its own appetites, the dog is the lowest and the unclean (*Arcana Coelestia* §§7784, 9231); faithful, it is the guard, "a guard to prevent man’s passing over from heavenly conjugial love to the love of adultery" (*Arcana Coelestia* §5051). Disney almost exclusively uses the positive correspondence of "faithful affection."

* **Bruno (*Cinderella*)**: Represents the loyal, natural affection that comforts Cinderella when her "spiritual" family (stepmother) rejects her. He dreams of chasing the cat (Lucifer/Evil), representing the natural mind's opposition to malice.  
* **Nana (*Peter Pan*)**: The nursemaid dog. Represents the "natural good" of caretaking and protection of innocence (the children).  
* **Max (*The Little Mermaid*)**: Recognizes the "good" in Ariel and the "evil" in Ursula (Vanessa) before the humans do. This aligns with Swedenborg’s account of why animals know their way untaught: "animals are in the order of their life, and have not been able to destroy what is in them from the spiritual world, because they have no rational faculty," while man has "perverted what is in him from that world by a life contrary to order, which his rational faculty has favored" (*Heaven and Hell* §108). Prince Eric is deceived; his dog is not.  
* **Sultan (*Beauty and the Beast*)**: The footstool (dog). Even when transformed into an object, the "affection" remains—he acts like a dog. This illustrates the "permanence of the ruling love"; the form changes, but the affection remains.

### **3.4 The Cat and Feline: Sensual Knowledge and Power**

**Cats** often correspond to the **sensual man**, obsessed with self and comfort, or **deceit**: in Swedenborg their eyes shine at night "from their burning appetite for mice" (*True Christian Religion* §162), and contempt for others is "like cats lying in wait for birds in their nests" (*True Christian Religion* §455). **Lions**, however, correspond to the **Power of Truth** (*Apocalypse Revealed* §241).

* **Lucifer (*Cinderella*)**: The name itself (Light-bearer/Devil) hints at his role. He represents "sensual malice" and "deceit." He hunts the mice (innocent thoughts/affections). He is the "falsity" allied with the "evil" of the Stepmother. His laziness and gluttony map to the "lusts of the flesh".  
* **Rajah (*Aladdin*)**: A tiger. Tigers are among the fierce animals (*Heaven and Hell* §110; *Arcana Coelestia* §2745), but Rajah is Jasmine’s protector. He corresponds to the "power of the will" defending the "affection for truth" (Jasmine) from "falsity" (suitors/Jafar).  
* **Simba/Mufasa (*The Lion King*)**: The Lion is the "Power of Divine Truth." Mufasa’s roar is the "Voice of Truth." Scar, a lion with "green eyes" (often a symbol of envy/sickness), represents truth perverted into "tyranny".

### **3.5 Marine Life: The Scientifica**

**Fish** correspond to **scientifics** or **facts stored in the memory**: "'fishes' in the Word signify memory-knowledges" (*Arcana Coelestia* §§40, 991). They live in the "water" (truth/knowledge) but are low on the evolutionary scale, representing the "natural mind" submerged in facts.

* **Flounder (*The Little Mermaid*)**: Represents the "simple knowledge" or "childlike affection for truth" that accompanies Ariel. He is not intellectual (like Scuttle) but emotional and supportive. He is "fact" animated by "affection."  
* **Ursula’s Eels (Flotsam and Jetsam)**: Eels with bright yellow eyes. They represent "sensual knowledge" used to ensnare. Their gleaming eyes mimic "spiritual light" but are actually a destructive force. They are the "spies" of the deep abyss (Hell), gathering information to manipulate the soul.

## **IV. The Royal Lineage: Truth, Good, and the Daughter of Zion**

The Disney Princess films almost invariably structure their royal families in a way that maps perfectly onto Swedenborg’s "conjugial" theology. In this system, the **King** represents **Divine Truth** (Intellect/Governance), the **Queen** represents **Divine Good** (Will/Protection), and the **Daughter** represents the **Spiritual Affection** (The Church or the Soul) that must undergo regeneration.

### **4.1 The Good King: The Benevolent Intellect**

In Swedenborg a "king" signifies truth (*Arcana Coelestia* §1672), and the Lord’s royalty "is the Divine truth, from which and according to which judgment is effected" (*Arcana Coelestia* §5068). The "Good King" in Disney is often benevolent but sometimes rigid or naive, representing "Truth" that needs "Good" or "Affection" to be complete.

* **King Triton (*The Little Mermaid*)**: He wields the Trident (Power of Truth). His conflict with Ariel is the conflict between "Rigid Law" and "Free Spirit." He represents "Truth separating from Evil" (hating humans). He must learn to act from "Love" (giving her legs/freedom) rather than just "Law" (forbidding the surface). His evolution is the "Regeneration of the Truth" by Love.  
* **The Sultan (*Aladdin*)**: He represents "Good without Truth" or "Naivety." He plays with toys and is easily hypnotized (manipulated) by Jafar (Falsity). He lacks the "Sword of Truth" to defend his kingdom and needs Aladdin (Action/Practical Truth) to restore order. This mirrors Swedenborg’s teaching that "good without truth is not good" (*Apocalypse Revealed* §97), and that "good without truth is without protection; for truth protects good" (*Apocalypse Revealed* §764).  
* **Mufasa (*The Lion King*)**: The ideal union of "Love" and "Wisdom." He rules with "understanding" of the Circle of Life. He represents the "Internal Man" or the "Celestial" degree.  
* **King Fergus (*Brave*)**: Represents the "Natural" or "Corporeal" power—strong, loud, bear-hunter. He lacks the "spiritual insight" of the Queen but provides the "foundation" of strength.  
* **Chief Tui (*Moana*)**: Like Triton, he represents "Law" based on fear (don't go beyond the reef). He represents "Traditional Truth" that has become stagnant and must be revitalized by the "New Church" (Moana).  
* **The King (*Cinderella*)**: Obsessed with "Succession" (Grandchildren). He represents the "Old Church" seeking to perpetuate itself through a new generation (Prince/Cinderella). He is "Time" and "Tradition".

### **4.2 The Queen: The Internal Will**

The Queen often represents the "Internal" or "Will" aspect—sometimes absent (deceased), sometimes the source of magical power.

* **Queen Elinor (*Brave*)**: She represents "Order," "Tradition," and "Social Good." Her transformation into a Bear (an animal of natural power and maternal protection) forces the integration of her "civilized" will with "natural" instinct. She must become "Natural" to understand her daughter.  
* **Sarabi (*The Lion King*)**: Her name means "Mirage," but she acts as the grounding force of the pride. She endures Scar’s reign with stoic dignity, representing the "Remains of the Church" waiting for restoration.  
* **Queen Athena (*The Little Mermaid: Ariel’s Beginning*)**: Deceased; the 2008 prequel names her, and the 1989 film never mentions her. Represents the "Lost Love" or "Lost Good" that made the King (Truth) hard and bitter.  
* **Queen Iduna (*Frozen II*)**: The source of the "Northuldra" (Nature/Magic) heritage. She represents the "Affection for Truth" that saved the father (Agnarr). Her lullaby is the "Voice of the Spheres" calling Elsa to her destiny.

### **4.3 The Princess: The Affection for Truth**

The Disney Princess is the protagonist because she represents the **Human Soul** (the *anima*) seeking **Regeneration**. In Swedenborg’s terms, she is the "Affection for Truth" wishing to be conjoined with "Truth" (The Prince) to form a "Conjugial Pair" (The Heavenly Marriage).

* **Ariel**: The "Affection for Natural Truth" (the human world). She seeks to rise from the "lower waters" (natural/sensual degree) to the "higher air" (spiritual/rational degree). Her acquisition of legs is the "incarnation" of the soul into a new state. In Andersen’s original, her struggle is the struggle of the soul to acquire immortality.  
* **Belle**: The "Affection for Spiritual Truth" (books/wisdom). She enters the "House of the Natural Man" (Beast) and, through "Conjugial Love" (marriage of Good and Truth), transforms him into a "Spiritual Man" (Prince).  
* **Rapunzel**: The "Light" (Wisdom) locked in a "Tower" (Selfhood/Isolation) by a "False Mother" (Gothel/Hypocrisy). Her hair is the "extension of truth" or "power" (like Samson’s hair; *Doctrine of the Sacred Scripture* §49). Her cutting of the hair is the "vastation" or shedding of the external to find the internal.  
* **Snow White**: The "Innocence of the Soul." She is persecuted by the "Evil Queen" (Self-Love/Vanity). She must "die" (sleep) to the natural world to be awakened by "Divine Truth" (The Prince).

## **V. Transmission Vectors: How Correspondential Patterns Enter Cultural Production**

Given the structural alignment documented above, the question becomes: through what channels did these patterns enter Disney's narrative and visual language?

### **5.1 Direct vs. Indirect Transmission**

No record shows Walt Disney himself reading Swedenborg, but the studio handled Swedenborgian material knowingly. Disney's *Johnny Appleseed* (1948) gives its hero "a holy book" where the historical John Chapman carried Swedenborg's writings and "divided his books into several pieces" (Haley 1871); the film's folklore consultant, Carl Carmer, had already made that substitution in his own retelling of the tale. Yet the film's ending stages Swedenborg's account of death and heaven, and when the Swedenborgian librarian Florence Murdoch wrote to the studio about its angel, Hal Adelquist replied that the studio had treated him "with sincere respect" (Fryeburg New Church Assembly n.d.; Means 2011; *The Apple and the Angel*). The generic packaging makes the structural patterns all the more significant: they reached the screen through **cultural transmission**, as story and craft rather than as doctrine. The library's cumulative case argues that this engagement goes back to the studio's beginnings. The ending of *Snow White* that its first story outline invented in 1934 (the Queen destroyed by her own attack, the kiss, the castle in the clouds) is found in none of the studio's known sources (*The Parsimony Problem*).

### **5.2 The Indirect Transmission: The "Bricolage" of Culture**

The "Swedenborgian pattern" in Disney is undeniable, and most of it came by **indirect transmission**, through the method its artists were taught and the literary sources Disney adapted.

* **The method of expression**: The studio's way of animating—"the mind is the pilot" (Disney 1935)—has a documented route from Swedenborg. Delsarte's American expositors laid a Swedenborgian layer over his law of correspondence; Genevieve Stebbins's standard textbook quotes *Divine Love and Wisdom* (Stebbins 1901); Volkonsky built his Russian manual on Stebbins, Stanislavsky attended Volkonsky's courses, and Don Graham's class of 22 February 1937 recommended *An Actor Prepares* (Graham, 22 February 1937; *The Law of Correspondence in the Arts of Expression*).  
* **Hans Christian Andersen**: The author of *The Little Mermaid* and *The Snow Queen* worked in the Danish Golden Age, where science and spirit were partners; the ideas of his mentor Hans Christian Ørsted's *The Spirit in Nature* (1850), Andersen recalled, "became the subjects of many a talk between us" (Andersen 1871). Andersen’s concept of the mermaid earning an "immortal soul" answers to Swedenborg’s doctrine of "degrees" of life (animal vs. human): by the inmost degree, which animals do not have, a man "lives to eternity" (*Heaven and Hell* §39). When Disney adapted Andersen, the "bones" of this spiritual structure remained.  
* **The Brothers Grimm**: Friends of Clemens Brentano and Achim von Arnim and helpers on *Des Knaben Wunderhorn* (Zipes 1987), they worked within the German Romanticism whose *Naturphilosophie* Schelling led, and its first links to Swedenborg are documented: Oetinger corresponded with Swedenborg (Johnson 2002), and Schelling was "an avid reader of Böhme and Oetinger" (Magee 2001). The Grimms’ tales often feature "talking animals" and "animate landscapes" that function as moral mirrors.  
* **Symbolism and Art Nouveau**: Disney’s "Golden Age" (Snow White, Pinocchio) and "Renaissance" (Beauty and the Beast) visual styles were heavily influenced by 18th- and 19th-century European art (Rococo, Symbolism); the French decorative arts in Disney's films were the subject of the Metropolitan Museum's 2021 exhibition *Inspiring Walt Disney* (Burchard 2021). Symbolism counted Swedenborg among its prophets (Jullian 1977): Baudelaire made his doctrine of correspondences a cornerstone of his aesthetic, and Blake, a Romantic before the Symbolists, read Swedenborg early, attended the first conference of the New Jerusalem Church in 1789 and in 1825 called him a "divine teacher" (Swedenborg Society n.d.). By adopting their visual language (e.g., the anthropomorphized trees in *Snow White*, the ethereal transformations in *Fantasia*), Disney animators imported the *aesthetic* of correspondence.  
* **Eyvind Earle and Medievalism**: For *Sleeping Beauty*, artist Eyvind Earle drew inspiration from the *Très Riches Heures de Jean, Duc de Berry*, a medieval book of hours, from which he took the film's key colors (Solomon 2019). Medieval art is inherently "correspondential," viewing the world as a book of symbols. By adopting this style, Disney adopted the pre-modern worldview of vertical causality.

### **5.3 The Jungian/Campbell Connection**

In the "Disney Renaissance" (1989–1999), **Joseph Campbell**'s monomyth entered the studio's story work, with **Carl Jung**, a reader of Swedenborg, behind it. It entered a studio whose artists were already trained in a method of their own.

* Christopher Vogler, a story consultant at Disney, wrote a practical guide to Campbell’s *The Hero with a Thousand Faces* that circulated among Hollywood script professionals, and applied it in developing *Beauty and the Beast*, *Aladdin* and *The Lion King* (Batty 2011); for *The Lion King* the studio had him make a Hero's Journey analysis of the *Hamlet* plot (Vogler 1998).  
* Jung read Swedenborg closely: as a medical student he read "seven fat volumes of his writings", and he returned to Swedenborg's books after his break with Freud (Swedenborg Society n.d.; Taylor 1991). Campbell drew on Swedenborg's dream journal in *The Hero with a Thousand Faces* (Campbell 1968, 101–102). Jung's concept of the "Collective Unconscious" and "Archetypes" mirrors Swedenborg’s "World of Spirits" (*Heaven and Hell* §421) and "Correspondences".  
* The Renaissance films were made by artists the studio had trained in that method. Walt Stanchfield's programme, set up with Eric Larson in the 1970s, counted Ron Clements and John Musker (*The Little Mermaid*, *Aladdin*), Glen Keane and Andreas Deja among its students, and taught Delsarte by name (Stanchfield 2009); in 1995 the studio reissued Walt's 1935 memo to its artists, with Stanchfield's foreword asking that "the Disney Tradition" be revitalized (Disney 1935).  
* **Conclusion**: Disney movies do not follow Swedenborgian patterns because they follow psychological archetypes. They follow them because correspondence is the structure such patterns have. Where the Renaissance story work used Campbell, its makers named their sources: for *The Lion King*, *Hamlet*, brought in at Jeffrey Katzenberg's direction, read through a Hero's Journey analysis (Vogler 1998). No Swedenborgian carrier appears in that story work. Yet the motif that most marks the film, the land that withers under a false king and revives under the true one, is older than any of these sources. It is the blameless king under whom "the earth yields its wheat and barley … by reason of his virtues" (Homer, *Odyssey* 19), Egypt struck for Pharaoh's hardened heart, and the drought under Ahab that breaks when true worship is restored (Exod 7–12; 1 Kgs 17–18). It is the Fisher King's waste land (Weston 1920), and Hamlet's Denmark under Claudius, "an unweeded garden … things rank and gross in nature / Possess it merely" (Shakespeare, *Hamlet* 1.2). Swedenborg says that among the ancients "the knowledge of correspondences was the chief of knowledges" (*Heaven and Hell* §87), so the motif's age is what correspondence predicts. What reached the 1990s through Campbell was this old content. What the studio's own method supplied was the way to draw it, from inner force to outer form. The "Collective Unconscious" (or Henry Corbin's "Mundus Imaginalis"; *The Hermeneutics of the Mundus Imaginalis* §6.1) names, in a psychological register, the plane Swedenborg describes as real.

## **VI. Case Studies in Correspondence**

### **6.1 *The Lion King*: The Grand Man and the Body Politic**

*The Lion King* serves as a perfect model of Swedenborg’s **Maximus Homo** (The Grand Man; *Arcana Coelestia* §§1276, 2996). The Kingdom is a body. Mufasa is the "Head" (Wisdom/Government). The Lionesses are the "Heart/Lungs" (Vital operations). The Hyenas are the "Intestines/Excretory function" (consuming waste, living in the "Elephant Graveyard" of bones). When Scar (the Cancer/Disease/Self-Love) takes over the Head, the entire Body (Land) sickens. The "circle of life" is broken. The "Rain" that returns with Simba is the "Influx of Truth" that heals the body. Zazu, the bird, is the "Communicating/Nervous System" trying to maintain connection between the parts.

### **6.2 *Tangled*: The Tower as the Self**

Rapunzel represents the **Remains** (innocence stored by the Lord) locked within the **Tower of Selfhood** (the Self). Mother Gothel represents **Hypocrisy**—she feigns love to exploit the "truth/power" (hair) for her own selfish life (youth).

* **The Lanterns**: Correspond to "Heavenly Truths" or "Stars" seen from a distance.  
* **The Hair**: Corresponds to "The Letter of the Word" or "Natural Truth"—it has power and healing, but can be used for evil if controlled by the Self (Gothel).  
* **Eugene/Flynn**: Represents the "Worldly Intellect" (thief) who must be "reformed" by the "Innocence" of Rapunzel. His "dying to self" (cutting the hair/sacrificing his life) is the act that breaks the spell of the Self.

### **6.3 *Beauty and the Beast*: Reformation of the Natural Man**

The Beast is the **Natural Man** ruled by **Lusts** (fur, claws, anger). He is "rich" but "unhappy" (spiritual poverty).

* **Belle**: The **Internal Spiritual Man** (Conscience). She enters the Beast’s domain.  
* **The Rose**: The **Vital Heat** or **Remains of Good**—if it withers completely, the man is spiritually dead (profaned).  
* **The Mirror**: The **Word** or **Self-Examination**. It shows "what is wanted" (truth).  
* **Gaston**: The **Self-Righteousness** or **Pride of Self-Intelligence**. He is "beautiful" on the outside but "beastly" inside (Swedenborg’s description of the hypocrite). The Beast is "ugly" outside but becomes "beautiful" inside through regeneration.

## **VII. Conclusion: Animation as Applied Correspondences**

The analysis confirms that Disney movies follow a pattern matching Swedenborg’s theory of correspondence to a high degree of fidelity. Landscapes reflect the state of the kingdom/mind; animals represent specific affections and intellectual principles; and royal lineages map onto the interplay of Truth and Good.

Two factors account for this:

**First, the method the studio taught.** Animation *requires* externalization: an emotion must be drawn as a storm; a personality must be drawn as an animal; an internal transformation must manifest as visible change in the environment. The studio did not leave this to instinct. From 1935 it taught its artists that "the mind is the pilot" and that the artist "is not drawing a form at all, but indicating a force" (Disney 1935; Graham, 21 June 1937). This is a law of inner cause and outer effect, and its route from Swedenborg, through Delsarte's American expositors and Stanislavsky, is documented (*The Law of Correspondence in the Arts of Expression*). The studio reissued the memo to its artists in 1995, and its artists of the 2010s still reason from force and function to form (Julius 2019; *The Diffracted Light*). Taught as craft rather than as a key of meanings, the method carries the core of the science of correspondences to artists who need not know its source.

**Second, the lineage of the source material.** By adapting fairy tales from Andersen and Grimm, and drawing on Symbolist and medieval visual traditions, Disney inherited narrative and aesthetic frameworks already shaped by Swedenborgian, Neoplatonic, and Romantic influences. Where its story work drew on Campbell's monomyth, it took up motifs older than any of these sources, which is what the correspondences predict. The transmission was indirect but substantial.

The "Magic" of Disney is, in a structural sense, the successful execution of the Doctrine of Correspondences: making the spiritual visible, externalizing the internal, and presenting a world where environment responds to character. Whether this reflects something true about the nature of symbolism itself—that correspondences describe real relationships rather than arbitrary conventions—is precisely what the Swedenborgian framework proposes.
