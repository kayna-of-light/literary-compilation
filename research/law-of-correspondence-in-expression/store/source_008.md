# Source 008 — Ruyter 2012: Delsarte in the United States; Hovey in Los Angeles

**Citation**: Ruyter, Nancy Lee Chalfa. "A Influência do Trabalho de Delsarte nos Estados Unidos a partir do Final do Século XIX." *Revista Brasileira de Estudos da Presença* 2, no. 2 (2012): 457–469. http://www.redalyc.org/articulo.oa?id=463545868009.

**Accessed**: 2026-10-24, PDF read in full (Portuguese).

## Key findings

- **Henrietta Hovey** (earlier Henrietta Russell, author of *Yawning*) learned Delsarte from Lewis B. Monroe and from Steele MacKaye.
- She later settled in **Los Angeles** and taught Delsartism there, including at the **Denishawn** school of Ruth St. Denis and Ted Shawn.
- **Ted Shawn** took private lessons with her in 1915, 1916 and 1917. Shawn became the leading historian and advocate of Delsarte in American dance (*Every Little Movement*, 1954).
- Ruyter traces Delsarte's broad influence on American physical culture, oratory and modern dance from the 1880s.

Teixeira de Souza, "François Delsarte e a Dança Moderna" (same issue, pp. 428–456), covers the same Denishawn line.

## Why it matters

- The Swedenborg-reading line (Monroe) was taught in Los Angeles, in a leading dance school, about two decades before the studio's classes. This is milieu: opportunity, documented.
- Hovey is also the "Mrs. Russell" Volkonsky quotes (store 009), so one teacher stands on both branches of the chain.
