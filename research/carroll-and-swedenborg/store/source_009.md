# Source 009 — Carroll, Preface to *Sylvie and Bruno Concluded* (1893)

**Citation**: Carroll, Lewis. *Sylvie and Bruno Concluded.* London: Macmillan, 1893. Preface. (Project Gutenberg 48795; *Sylvie and Bruno*, 1889, Gutenberg 620 and 48630.)
**Accessed**: 2026-10-23.

## The passage (verbatim)
"It may interest some of my Readers to know the *theory* on which this story is constructed. It is an attempt to show what might *possibly* happen, supposing that Fairies really existed; and that they were sometimes visible to us, and we to them; and that they were sometimes able to assume human form: and supposing, also, that human beings might sometimes become conscious of what goes on in the Fairy-world—by actual transference of their immaterial essence, such as we meet with in 'Esoteric Buddhism.' I have supposed a Human being to be capable of various psychical states, with varying degrees of consciousness, as follows:— (*a*) the ordinary state, with no consciousness of the presence of Fairies; (*b*) the 'eerie' state, in which, while conscious of actual surroundings, he is *also* conscious of the presence of Fairies; (*c*) a form of trance, in which, while *un*conscious of actual surroundings, and apparently asleep, he (i.e. his immaterial essence) migrates to other scenes, in the actual world, or in Fairyland, and is conscious of the presence of Fairies."

## Why it matters
Carroll names his own model for the "immaterial essence" that migrates: Sinnett's Theosophical book, with the psychical research of the SPR behind his "psychical states". The library had called this preface "a direct transliteration of Swedenborg's description of his own states". The parallel with Swedenborg is real — *HH* §§440–441 describe being "withdrawn from the body" in a state "midway between sleeping and waking" and being "carried away by the spirit to another place" while walking, "fully awake" (source_011) — but Carroll's stated source is not Swedenborg.
