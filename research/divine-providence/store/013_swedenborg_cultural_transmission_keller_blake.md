## Helen Keller: Swedenborg Received Without Obstruction

### The Facts
- John Hitz (Swiss consul, friend of Alexander Graham Bell) created Braille translation of *Heaven and Hell* for teenage Helen
- Her response: "my heart gave a joyous leap" — Swedenborg's truths were "to my faculties what light, color and music are to the eye and ear"
- Published *My Religion* (1927), later revised as *Light in My Darkness*
- "Since my seventeenth year, I have tried to live according to the teachings of Emanuel Swedenborg"
- When reported to have embraced Bahá'í, she corrected: "since I was sixteen years old, I have been a strong believer in the doctrines of Emanuel Swedenborg. Why should I change my faith?"

### Why She Matters for the Thesis
Keller is a remarkable vessel: a person whose PHYSICAL senses were almost entirely absent, who therefore perceived more directly through interior faculties. She experienced Swedenborg's teaching not as abstract doctrine but as description of what she already knew from within.

Her physical limitations did not isolate her from reality — they **removed the obstruction** that physical senses create between the interior and the exterior. She is living evidence that spiritual perception does not depend on natural senses. The correspondential framework predicts this: influx flows from interior to exterior, not the reverse. Remove the exterior, and the interior becomes MORE accessible, not less.

**Key quote:** "I believe that in the life to come I shall have the senses I have not had here."

### Keller's Swedenborgian Beliefs
- God as love and wisdom united in the Divine Human
- Heaven and hell as inner states freely chosen — not imposed
- Salvation through regeneration (gradual character transformation)
- Doctrine of Uses — joy of heaven is being useful
- Universal brotherhood — all religious paths have dignity

## William Blake: Swedenborg Absorbed and Contested

### The Facts
- Annotated copies of Swedenborg's *Divine Love and Wisdom* survive with Blake's marginalia
- Signed guestbook at First General Conference of New Jerusalem Church (1789)
- *The Marriage of Heaven and Hell* (c. 1790) is a sustained dialogue with/parody of Swedenborg
- "Memorable Fancies" parody Swedenborg's "Memorable Relations"

### Blake's Critique of Swedenborg
Blake's objection is NOT that Swedenborg perceived incorrectly. It is that Swedenborg SYSTEMATIZED what should remain alive:
- "He conversed with Angels who are all religious, and conversed not with Devils who all hate religion"
- Swedenborg's Heaven is too orderly, too rational, too systematic
- Blake insists on "contraries" — "Without Contraries is no progression"
- The proprium of Enlightenment rationalism constrained what flowed through Swedenborg

### How This Connects to the Library
Blake identified FROM THE OUTSIDE what the library identifies FROM THE INSIDE. The library's correction documents show:
- The Limbus = Cartesian artifact (Swedenborg's scientific training constraining)
- The biological Christology = Aristotelian embryology (18th-century formation shaping)
- The 1757 dating = Enlightenment empiricism demanding temporal coordinates

Blake's critique converges with the library's self-correction: Swedenborg's proprium shaped the vessel, and the vessel constrained what could flow through. The key works. What fails is the application where the man wielding the key had blind spots exactly where his deepest loves were.

### Blake's Relationship to Correspondence
Blake ABSORBED the doctrine of correspondences deeply:
- "Thinking in correspondences" is "of the very nature of poetry" (Thompson)
- Blake used Swedenborg's symbolic vocabulary as raw material
- But Blake insisted that correspondences are CREATIVE ("poetic tales") before they are systematic
- He accused the "Priesthood" of perverting the original creative process into "forms of worship"

This is itself a correspondential insight: the living perception (celestial) gets codified into doctrine (spiritual) which becomes dead taxonomy (natural). Blake saw this happening to Swedenborg's own work within a generation of its publication.

## CHECK AGAINST THE LIBRARY

The library's "Transmission of the Ancient Word" §VIII-IX covers Swedenborg's role as restorer, not originator. It acknowledges his proprium-distortions. The library's correction documents (Limbus, Christology, 1757) confirm Blake's intuition that the vessel constrained the content.

However: Blake's solution — insisting on "Energy" and "Devils" as necessary contraries — is itself a form of the Gnostic Impulse. The library's "Architecture of Autonomy" identifies the proprium's drive for freedom FROM the Lord as structurally identical to Gnosticism. Blake's rebellion against Swedenborg's system may be the proprium rebelling against systematic truth because systematic truth constrains the self.

**Honest assessment:** Blake correctly identified Swedenborg's limitations. But his alternative (the primacy of poetic imagination over systematic revelation) may itself be the proprium wearing the costume of artistic freedom. The library should examine this more carefully.

## Significance for the Thesis
1. Keller: evidence that spiritual perception operates through interior faculties regardless of exterior conditions
2. Blake: evidence that the key entered English culture immediately but was contested by the very minds that received it
3. Both: Swedenborg's teaching produced immediate, powerful responses — one of reception (Keller), one of creative rebellion (Blake). The woman's nourishment in the wilderness operates through BOTH channels.
