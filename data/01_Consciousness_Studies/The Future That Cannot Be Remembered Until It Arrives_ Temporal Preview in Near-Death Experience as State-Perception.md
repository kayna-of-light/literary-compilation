# The Future That Cannot Be Remembered Until It Arrives: Temporal Preview in Near-Death Experience as State-Perception

## An Empirical and Correspondential Analysis of 6,753 Accounts, and a Correction of the Filter and Block-Universe Models

---

> **Abstract.** This thesis asks what a near-death experiencer receives when they report being shown their own future, and it answers that they receive a state, not a recording. The claim is tested against 6,753 structured near-death accounts drawn from the NDERF and IANDS archives, in which knowledge of the personal future is reported in 15.3% of cases, knowledge of world events in 3.8%, and a full preview of the life to come in 1.3%. The marker is validated against the experiencers' own questionnaire answers, where the extraction achieves 100% sensitivity and Cohen's kappa of 0.672. Five structural signatures separate the phenomenon from anything a recording could be: the content arrives simultaneously rather than sequentially, is given conditionally on return, cannot be retrieved by ordinary recall until the corresponding moment arrives, is selected by function rather than by chronology, and is met later by recognition rather than by memory. Each signature is predicted by the correspondential premise that distance and time in the spiritual world are states rather than locations, and none is predicted by the two mechanisms the secondary literature habitually borrows — the filter or transmission model, and the relativistic block universe. Personal future knowledge is associated with commissioned mission at 50.9% against 16.7% and with a verifiable future-event claim at 28.7% against 2.4%, while a self-declared non-clinical crisis produces the phenomenon at rates statistically indistinguishable from clinical death. The future is not stored and later played; the state is perceived, and it is known again when it comes into ultimates.

> **Keywords.** near-death experience, temporal preview, personal future knowledge, precognitive déjà vu, state-perception, correspondence, discrete degrees, ultimates, influx, purposive economy, filter theory, block universe, NDERF, IANDS, Swedenborg

## 1. Introduction

A woman who nearly died in 1963 described, decades later, the moment her son was dressed in a red outfit she had never seen him wear. "As soon as I saw him this déjà vu hit me and I remember that that was the outfit he had on when I was shown my future while I was out of my body." Then she added a sentence that this thesis takes as its point of departure, because it states a constraint that no ordinary account of memory or of foreknowledge can accommodate: "I can't remember any of my future until it happens." She was not withholding the content. She could not reach it. What she had received during the crisis was not stored in a form that recall could open, and it became available to her only at the instant the corresponding moment arrived in the world. The knowledge and the event were not two things separated by an interval of storage. They were one thing that could only be known when it was present.

This is the phenomenon the thesis addresses, and it is more common than the anecdotal literature suggests. In a corpus of 6,753 structured near-death accounts extracted from the NDERF and IANDS archives, **15.3%** report receiving information about their personal future, **3.8%** report information about world or global events, and **1.3%** report a full preview of the life that would follow their return. A further **6.4%** make a specifically verifiable future-event claim, and **9.4%** report precognitive capacity as a persistent aftereffect. These are not rare marginalia. Personal future knowledge occurs in roughly one account in six, at a prevalence comparable to the life review itself, and it has received almost none of the analytical attention that the life review has received. The secondary literature on the subject is thin, anecdotal, and — where it attempts explanation at all — reliant on two mechanisms borrowed wholesale from outside the phenomenon: the filter or transmission model of the brain, and the relativistic block universe. This thesis argues that both borrowings are unnecessary, that both are inconsistent with what the accounts actually describe, and that a single premise already established in this framework accounts for every structural feature of the data without importing anything.

That premise is that distance and time in the spiritual world are **states**, not locations. Swedenborg is explicit that what appears in that world as nearness or remoteness is an appearance produced by likeness or unlikeness of state, and that what appears there as duration is likewise a function of state rather than of a measured interval (*Heaven and Hell* §§141–153). The framework has taken this seriously enough to correct Swedenborg himself where he failed to apply it — in *[The Fallible Ultimate](../02_Swedenborgian_Theology/The%20Fallible%20Ultimate_%20A%20Correspondential%20Re-Reading%20of%20Swedenborg's%201748%20Visions%20and%20the%20Genesis%20of%20the%201757%20Claim.md)* and *[The Selfhood of the Prophet](../02_Swedenborgian_Theology/The%20Selfhood%20of%20the%20Prophet_%20A%20Forensic%20Analysis%20of%2018th-Century%20Scientific%20Forcing%20in%20Swedenborg's%20Theological%20System.md)*, where the assignment of a calendar year to a perceived qualitative event is diagnosed as the literalization of a state into a coordinate. If that diagnosis is correct for Swedenborg's 1757, it is correct here too, and it applies with equal force to the modern replacement: reading a temporal preview as a look down a fixed four-dimensional manifold is the same category error in physical dress. The state is not a place on a line. It is a condition, and a condition is not located; it is occupied.

The central finding of this thesis is that the corpus carries five structural signatures, each of which is incompatible with the preview being a recording of a fixed future and each of which follows directly from the preview being a perception of a state. First, **simultaneity**: experiencers describe the whole content arriving at once rather than in sequence, and the marker for temporal dissolution is elevated precisely in the groups that report future knowledge — 36.4% of those reporting world-future knowledge describe everything happening at once, against 12.2% of those reporting no future knowledge at all. Second, **conditionality**: the content is repeatedly given as contingent on return, in the experiencers' own grammar — "all that was to come *if I returned to earth*" — which is not the grammar of a recording and is exactly the grammar of a state whose unfolding depends on whether it is entered. Third, **non-retrievability**: the content resists ordinary recall and becomes available only when the corresponding moment arrives, which no storage model predicts and which state-perception predicts necessarily, since a state can be recognized only when it is present. Fourth, **purposive selection**: what is shown is selected by function — for strength, for decision, for commissioning — and personal future knowledge co-occurs with a commissioned mission at **50.9%** against a base rate of **16.7%** (χ² = 598.2, *p* = 4.2 × 10⁻¹³², φ = 0.298). Fifth, **recognition rather than recall**: the later event is met with the phenomenology of knowing-again rather than of remembering, and the experiencers who report personal future knowledge are the same ones who report lifelong déjà vu as an aftereffect, at **20.6%** against **7.4%** (χ² = 179.3, *p* = 6.8 × 10⁻⁴¹).

The central argument is that these five signatures are one signature seen five ways, and that the one thing they jointly describe is a state-perception rendered into a scene. A state is not a snapshot of a moment; it is a condition of the whole, and a condition of the whole carries its own trajectory, because what a state will become is not information added to it from outside but the state's own quality read forward. To perceive a person's state completely is therefore to perceive where that state is going, not because the future is stored somewhere and read off, but because a ruling love is a direction and a direction has a destination. This is why the content arrives all at once — a state has no parts to arrange in order. It is why the content is conditional — the trajectory is the trajectory *of that state*, and if the state is not entered the trajectory is not run. It is why the content cannot be recalled — the natural memory stores sequences of things, and what was received was not a sequence of things. And it is why the later moment produces recognition — the state has come into ultimates, and what is recognized is not a stored image matching a present one but the same condition, met again at the outermost degree.

The natural mind, receiving such a perception, renders it as a scene, because that is what the natural degree does with anything it receives: it gives it extension and sequence, since extension and sequence are the form of the natural degree itself. This is the ordinary operation of correspondence, not a distortion. But it is also the reason the phenomenon is so easily misdescribed. The experiencer reports "I saw my daughter grown," and the report has the surface grammar of a photograph taken from a future moment. The interpreter, taking the surface grammar as the content, then requires a mechanism by which a future moment could be photographed — and reaches for a fixed manifold, or for a brain whose ordinary censorship of a timeless awareness has momentarily lapsed. Both mechanisms are constructed to explain the rendering rather than the reception. This thesis reads the rendering as rendering, and looks past it to what was received.

Two further findings extend the argument beyond its correspondential core. The first concerns the necessary conditions. If temporal preview follows from a state thrown to its boundary, then it should not require the heart to stop, and the corpus permits a direct test: within the accounts where the experiencer explicitly declares a life-threatening event *without* clinical death (n = 556) against those declaring clinical death (n = 193), the experiencers' own questionnaire answers show personal future scenes at 9.7% and 13.5% respectively — a difference that does not reach significance (χ² = 2.08, *p* = 0.150) — while the timeless or everything-at-once marker stands at 55.7% and 52.8%, effectively identical. The crisis of state, not the arrest of the heart, is what carries the phenomenon. The second concerns age. Precognitive aftereffects decline monotonically with age at experience, from **21.0%** among those who were under six to **2.6%** among those over sixty, with mission-commissioning running in the opposite direction. The youngest experiencers receive the least articulable content and carry the most persistent recognition capacity — a pattern that a storage model cannot easily produce, and that a state model produces as a matter of course, because a state impressed before language has no narrative to lose.

The thesis proceeds as follows. Section 2 sets out the method: correspondence as functional identity running inward to outward, and specifically as state rather than coordinate (§2.1); discrete degrees and the reason a state received at an interior degree is rendered as a scene at the natural degree (§2.2); the corpus, the extraction, the markers, and their validation against the experiencers' own answers (§2.3); and a statement of the two mechanisms this thesis declines to borrow, held here only long enough to name them and deferred to Section 6 for engagement (§2.4). Section 3 presents what the corpus contains: three distinct markers rather than one, their validation, and the verification deficit that honest reporting requires. Section 4 develops the five structural signatures with the accounts themselves. Section 5 states the state-perception model, supplies its correspondential key, sets out its predictions against the data, and presents the central mapping. Section 6 returns to the filter model and the block universe and shows precisely where each fails. Section 7 tests the necessary conditions across the clinical boundary and across age. Section 8 discusses, and states what cannot be concluded. Section 9 concludes. The appendix maps the five signatures against their empirical traces and their correspondential readings.

## 2. Methodological Framework

### 2.1 Correspondential Reading: State, Not Coordinate

Correspondence, as this framework uses the term, is **functional identity** running in one direction. It is not allegory, not symbol, not metaphor, and not archetype. As established in *[The Surface That Withholds Nothing](The%20Surface%20That%20Withholds%20Nothing_%20The%20Body%20as%20the%20Outermost%20Expression%20of%20the%20Spiritual%20State.md)* and developed across the corpus, a natural object corresponds to a spiritual reality because the natural object performs, at the natural degree, the function that the spiritual reality performs at its own. Light corresponds to wisdom because light enables the eye to distinguish forms, which is what understanding does. The correspondence is discovered in the operation, not assigned by convention. And it runs one way: the spiritual is expressed as the natural; the natural does not organize itself upward into the spiritual. This asymmetry is the load-bearing commitment. Lose it and the model collapses into either materialism, which reads the identity as a two-way equation and then must explain how matter generates awareness, or idealism, in which mind manufactures its world.

Applied to time, this yields the specific methodological premise of the present thesis, and it is worth stating with some care because everything downstream depends on it. In the spiritual world there is no extension and no duration; what appears there as distance is an appearance produced by likeness or unlikeness of state, and what appears there as succession is likewise an appearance produced by change of state. Swedenborg states this directly for space and distance (*Heaven and Hell* §§141–153), and applies the same reasoning to time: those in that world have no measure of duration, and what they experience as before and after is the passage of one state into another. The framework has treated this teaching as testable rather than devotional, and has already put it to work on Swedenborg's own record. In *The Fallible Ultimate* and *The Selfhood of the Prophet*, his assignment of the year 1757 to a perceived judgment is read not as a false claim about an event but as a **literalization** — a qualitative state, perceived accurately at the spiritual degree, given a calendar coordinate at the natural degree, because the natural degree is where coordinates live and the perceiver did not hold the two registers apart. The perception was real. The coordinate was the perceiver's rendering of it.

The distinction between a **state** and a **coordinate** is therefore not a piece of decorative terminology; it is the analytical instrument of this thesis, and it should be defined precisely. A coordinate is a position in an ordering that exists independently of what occupies it: the year 1757 is where it is whether or not anything happens there, and a moment on a timeline is specified by its relations to other moments, not by any quality of its own. A state is the reverse. It has no position; it has a quality, and it is specified by what it is. States stand in relations of likeness and unlikeness, not of before and after, and when a state changes into another state, that change is what succession *is* for anything not extended in the natural degree. A being in that condition does not travel to a later time; it enters a different state, and the entering is the whole of what happens. This is why the framework insists that Swedenborg's cosmology cannot be read as a map with regions. It is not a place with a geography. It is a range of conditions, and its apparent geography is the appearance those conditions take when they are perceived by a mind still habituated to extension.

The methodological consequence for the analysis of temporal preview follows immediately. When an experiencer reports having been shown a future event, the framework does not ask *how the future was accessed*, because that question presupposes that the future is a coordinate at which something is stored and from which something could be retrieved. It asks instead *what state was perceived*, and then treats the reported scene as the rendering of that state at the natural degree. Two accounts of the same reported phenomenon are thus available, and they make different predictions. If the content is a coordinate accessed, then the content should behave like retrieved information: it should be stored, recallable, sequential, and fixed. If the content is a state perceived, then the content should behave like a perceived quality: it should be simultaneous rather than ordered, conditional rather than fixed, resistant to recall because there is no stored sequence to recall, and available to recognition when the same state arrives at the outermost degree. Section 4 puts these two predictions side by side against the corpus. The method of this thesis is nothing more than that comparison, conducted honestly, with the correspondential premise supplying the second hypothesis rather than the conclusion.

One clarification prevents a foreseeable misreading. To say that the preview is a state-perception is not to say that it is subjective, imprecise, or merely psychological. States are not private moods; in this framework they are the interior reality of which natural events are the outermost expression, and they are therefore *more* determinate than the scenes that express them, not less. A state-perception can yield a red outfit on a specific child on a specific afternoon, and the corpus contains such reports, because the ultimate is the containant in which the interior is present in fullness. What the model denies is not the specificity of the content but the *storage-and-retrieval architecture* that the coordinate reading requires in order to explain that specificity. The specificity comes from the completeness of the expression, not from a photograph taken in advance.

### 2.2 Discrete Degrees and Why a State Arrives as a Scene

The architecture that makes state-perception intelligible, and that explains why it is nonetheless reported as a sequence of pictures, is the doctrine of **discrete degrees**. Continuous degrees are differences of quantity within one kind: more or less light, more or less warmth. Discrete degrees are differences of kind: celestial (love and will), spiritual (wisdom and understanding), natural (effects and ultimates). They are not three intensities of one thing but three complete registers, related as end to cause to effect, or as seed to tree to fruit. Influx runs among them in one direction only, from the interior toward the exterior, and the natural is where the series comes to rest. Because the series comes to rest there, the natural degree is the **ultimate**, and the ultimate is a *containant*: not the thinnest of the degrees but the one in which the prior degrees are gathered and fully present. *[The Surface That Withholds Nothing](The%20Surface%20That%20Withholds%20Nothing_%20The%20Body%20as%20the%20Outermost%20Expression%20of%20the%20Spiritual%20State.md)* establishes this position for the body, and *[Direct Perception Through Discrete Degrees](Direct%20Perception%20Through%20Discrete%20Degrees%20-%20Empirical%20Analysis%20of%20Perceptual%20Depth%20in%20Near-Death%20Experiences.md)* establishes empirically that near-death perception stratifies along exactly these three registers across the same 6,753 accounts used here.

The natural degree has two properties that no interior degree has, and they are the two properties that produce every difficulty in reading temporal-preview accounts: **extension** and **succession**. To be at the natural degree is to occupy space and to occur in order. This is not a limitation imposed on the natural degree from outside; it is what the natural degree is. A thing said at that degree is necessarily said in a place and in a sequence, because there is no other way for that degree to say anything. Consequently, when something is received at an interior degree and then expressed at the natural degree — which is what happens whenever an experiencer comes back and tells us what happened — the expression will have extension and succession whether or not the reception did. The scene is not a distortion of the state; it is the state's ultimate. But it is also not a transcript, and reading it as a transcript is precisely the error this thesis is written to correct.

The relation can be stated exactly, and the framework has a term for it. A state received at an interior degree is **explicated** at the natural degree: unfolded into parts that are ordered, extended, and successive, so that what was a single quality becomes a series of things. The unfolding is faithful — nothing is lost, because the ultimate withholds nothing — but it is not reversible by simple inspection, because many distinct states can explicate into similar scenes and one state can explicate into many. This is why the experiencers themselves so often report that the natural rendering was inadequate to what they received, and why ineffability is such a stable feature of the corpus. It is also why they so often report the two registers *at the same time*, in a single sentence that will not parse as ordinary narrative. One writes that the whole of her life was shown "in a flash of an instant," and in the same breath that she saw "all the details of all the cause and effect relations in my life." Instantaneity and exhaustive detail are contradictory at the natural degree, where detail takes time to enumerate. They are not contradictory at all if a state was perceived and the detail is the state's explication.

This is the mechanism by which a perceived state becomes a reported flashforward, and it is worth walking once, slowly, because Section 4 will rely on it at every step. The interior perception is of a condition — of what this person is, and therefore of the direction in which this person is moving, and therefore of what this person's life is. That condition has no parts and no sequence. When it comes into the natural degree, it must acquire parts and sequence, because parts and sequence are the natural degree's form. So it arrives as scenes: a child in a red outfit, a hospital bed with family gathered around it, a daughter grown. Each scene is a true ultimate of the state. None is a photograph of a coordinate, and the difference shows in the phenomenology — the scenes come without an index. Experiencers can very rarely say *when* the scene they were shown will occur, and the corpus contains almost no accounts in which a date was given, though it contains many in which a specific visual detail was given. That asymmetry is exactly what the model predicts: quality transmits through explication, position does not, because position was never in the perception to begin with.

Two consequences of this architecture will be used repeatedly and are stated here so they need not be re-argued. First, because the natural memory is a memory of things in order — of extended objects in temporal sequence — a reception that had neither extension nor sequence has nothing for the natural memory to file. It is not that the memory is blocked. There is no object of the right kind to store. Second, because the ultimate is where the interior comes fully to rest, the moment at which a given state finally arrives at the natural degree is the moment at which that state becomes, for the first time, an object of the natural sort. That is when it can be met, and met is the right word: not retrieved, but encountered, and encountered as already known, because it is the same state. These two consequences are the whole of the explanation for the phenomenon named in the title, and Sections 4.3 and 4.5 test them.

### 2.3 The Corpus, the Markers, and Their Independent Validation

The empirical base of this thesis is a corpus of **6,753** near-death accounts collected from the Near-Death Experience Research Foundation (NDERF) and the International Association for Near-Death Studies (IANDS) archives, structured into a fixed questionnaire schema and held in the companion repository that supports the framework's empirical work. Of these, **6,721** retain their full original narrative text alongside the structured extraction — 5,622 from NDERF and 1,099 from IANDS — permitting every claim made from the structured fields to be checked against the experiencer's own words. This is the same corpus used in *[Direct Perception Through Discrete Degrees](Direct%20Perception%20Through%20Discrete%20Degrees%20-%20Empirical%20Analysis%20of%20Perceptual%20Depth%20in%20Near-Death%20Experiences.md)*, in *[Mission-Based Returns](Mission-Based%20Returns_%20Volunteer%20Soul%20Detection%20Analysis.md)*, and in the Purposive Economy validation, so results reported here are directly comparable to those already established.

The extraction was performed by a large language model against a Pydantic schema of typed fields, each with an explicit enumeration. Three fields carry the phenomenon under study, and it matters that they are three and not one. `cosmic_knowledge.personal_future_knowledge` records information received about the experiencer's own future. `cosmic_knowledge.global_future_knowledge` records information about world or collective events. `cosmic_knowledge.life_preview` records a comprehensive showing of the life to come, structurally parallel to the life review but oriented forward. Each is coded on a four-value scale — `yes_explicit`, `implied`, `no`, `not_mentioned` — and throughout this thesis a marker is counted as positive when it is either explicit or implied, with the two components always reported separately so the reader can apply a stricter threshold. Supporting fields are drawn from the same schema: `experience_quality.time_perception` (timeless, everything at once, speeded up, slowed down, normal), `experience_quality.veridical_claim_types` and `veridical_verification`, `experience_quality.post_experience_gifts`, `boundary_and_return.mission_commissioned` and `return_agency`, `life_review.occurrence`, `context.clinical_status` and `nde_cause`, and `person_demographics.age_at_nde`.

An extraction of this kind is only as good as its validation, and the failure to validate is precisely where the existing secondary treatments of this phenomenon collapse. This corpus permits an unusually direct check, because a substantial portion of the NDERF narratives contain the archive's own questionnaire embedded in the submitted text, including the item **"Did scenes from the future come to you?"** with the experiencer's own answer. That item is an independent measurement of the same construct, produced by the experiencer rather than by any analysis of the narrative, and it exists for **1,508** accounts. Comparing it against the structured extraction across those accounts yields the following.

| Comparison (n = 1,507 with both measures) | Count |
|---|---|
| Experiencer says yes, extraction says yes | 86 |
| Experiencer says yes, extraction says no | **0** |
| Experiencer says no, extraction says yes | 75 |
| Experiencer says no, extraction says no | 1,346 |
| **Raw agreement** | **95.0%** |
| **Cohen's κ** | **0.672** |
| **Sensitivity** | **100.0%** |

The extraction never misses a case the experiencer themselves affirms. Of the 86 accounts in which the experiencer explicitly reported future scenes — 86 for personal future, 54 for world future, and, notably, none reporting both — the extraction identified every one. It also flags 75 accounts that the experiencer's own questionnaire answer does not, a false-positive rate of 5.3% against that answer. This asymmetry is not a defect to be minimized but a fact to be interpreted: the extraction reads the entire narrative and includes implied cases, while the questionnaire item captures only what the experiencer chose to record in a single field, often written years after the event and frequently left blank or answered narrowly. Within the same subset the experiencers' own answers give personal future scenes at 5.7% of responders against the extraction's 10.7%. Both figures are reported wherever the comparison is relevant, and where the two measures disagree in Section 7 that disagreement is stated rather than resolved in favor of the convenient number. A κ of 0.672 is substantial agreement by conventional standards, and combined with perfect sensitivity it establishes that the marker used throughout this thesis tracks something the experiencers themselves recognize and name.

Beyond the structured fields, the raw narratives were searched directly for the linguistic signatures of the phenomenon, because some of the most diagnostic features of temporal preview are not questionnaire items at all. Across the 6,721 narratives, explicit déjà vu language appears in 55 accounts (0.82%), the claim of having already lived a moment in 75 (1.12%), the claim that something shown later came true in 116 (1.73%), explicit shown-the-future language in 44 (0.65%), conditional-return grammar in 161 (2.40%), and later-recognition language of any form in 1,012 (15.06%). These narrative-level counts are lower than the structured prevalence because they require particular phrasing rather than reported content, and they are used in this thesis to source verbatim material and to corroborate the structured findings, never as independent prevalence estimates.

Two constraints on the corpus should be registered before any result is read. It is self-selected: people who submit accounts to an archive devoted to near-death experience are not a random sample of people who nearly died, and the direction of that bias is toward the phenomenologically rich. And it is retrospective, sometimes by decades, with all the reconstruction that entails. Neither constraint is fatal to the comparative claims made here, because every comparison in this thesis is internal — one group of experiencers against another group drawn from the same archive under the same selection pressure — but both bear directly on any absolute prevalence figure, and Section 8.5 returns to them.

### 2.4 Two Borrowed Mechanisms This Thesis Does Not Use

Because the two mechanisms most often applied to temporal preview are absent from the argument that follows, and because their absence is deliberate rather than accidental, they are named here and the reasons for declining them are stated in outline. Section 6 makes the full case; this subsection only removes them from the toolkit so that nothing later smuggles them back in.

The first is the **filter or transmission model** of the brain — the proposal, descending through James (1898), Bergson (1896), and Huxley (1954), and consolidated in Kelly and colleagues (2007), that consciousness exists independently of the brain and is ordinarily constrained by it, so that impairment of the organ permits a wider awareness to appear. Applied to temporal preview, the model says that the brain normally filters out a non-local awareness that is not bound to the present moment, and that crisis lifts the filter. This is a natural thing to reach for and it is not used here, for a reason that has been formally registered in the framework's record of conceptual evolution: filter language entered this corpus as drift and was never a framework position. The correction is recorded as strain #25 in *[Evolving Conceptual Strains](../../docs/EVOLVING_CONCEPTUAL_STRAINS.md)*, and the annotations it generated stand in *[The Seed and the Sun](../00_Master_Theses/The%20Seed%20and%20the%20Sun_%20A%20Statistical%20and%20Phenomenological%20Investigation%20into%20the%20Architecture%20of%20Consciousness,%20the%20Paths%20of%20the%20Soul,%20and%20the%20Dissolution%20of%20the%20Hard%20Problem.md)*, *The Neurocentric Limit*, *The Ontological Transition*, and *The Epistemic Architecture*. The reason the framework cannot use it is structural rather than stylistic. A filter presupposes two things and a gap between them, with the filter positioned in the gap regulating traffic. The framework denies the gap. The body is not an interface standing between a consciousness and a world; it is the complete outermost expression of the spiritual state, and it withholds nothing. Once the gap is denied, the filter has nowhere to sit, and every question about what it lets through becomes malformed. The framework's own account of the same data is a **state-shift**: the degrees are present but unopened, and what changes in crisis is which degree is in operation, not how much is getting past an obstruction.

The second is the **block universe** — the reading of relativistic spacetime on which all events, past, present, and future, exist tenselessly as coordinates in a four-dimensional manifold, so that a preview of the future is a look at a region of the block that the experiencer has not yet reached. This too is not used here, and the objection is again structural. As set out in §2.1, the framework's premise is that what appears as time in the natural degree is state at the interior degrees. To explain a state-perception by placing it on a manifold is to convert a quality into a position — the identical move that the framework has already diagnosed in Swedenborg's assignment of 1757 to a perceived judgment. It does not become sounder because the coordinate system is Minkowskian rather than Gregorian. Beyond the structural objection, the block universe makes the wrong predictions: a coordinate is fixed, and the corpus is saturated with conditionality; a coordinate is indexed, and the corpus almost never carries an index; a coordinate could in principle be read off and remembered, and the corpus insists that the content could not be recalled until it arrived.

There is a further reason to state both refusals at the outset, and it concerns method rather than doctrine. Both mechanisms are imported from outside the phenomenon to explain the phenomenon, and both are imported in a form their originators would not recognize as settled. Filter theory is a philosophical proposal with a century of advocacy and no decisive test; the block universe is one interpretive reading of relativity, contested within physics and not entailed by the mathematics. Presenting either as established and then deriving conclusions from it produces work that looks rigorous and is not, and this is exactly what the small existing literature on temporal preview does. This thesis therefore takes the harder and plainer route: it describes what the accounts contain, states what structure that content has, and tests whether a premise the framework already holds accounts for that structure. If it does, nothing needs to be borrowed. Section 6 shows that it does.

## 3. What the Corpus Contains: The Prevalence and Shape of Temporal Preview

### 3.1 Three Markers, Not One

The first substantive result of the corpus analysis is that temporal preview is not a single thing. Three distinct markers separate cleanly in the data, and the differences among them are large enough that treating them as one phenomenon — as the anecdotal literature routinely does — obscures the structure that this thesis is about.

| Marker | Explicit | Implied | Total | % of 6,753 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Personal future knowledge | 596 | 435 | **1,031** | **15.3%** |
| Global or world future knowledge | 191 | 67 | **258** | **3.8%** |
| Life preview (comprehensive) | 57 | 31 | **88** | **1.3%** |
| Veridical claim of a future event | — | — | **435** | **6.4%** |
| Precognition as persistent aftereffect | — | — | **633** | **9.4%** |

Personal future knowledge occurs in roughly one account in six. For comparison against a phenomenon the field considers central, the life review occurs in 17.5% of the same corpus (10.6% brief, 6.9% extensive), which is to say that knowledge of the personal future is as prevalent as the life review and has received a small fraction of its scholarly attention. Global future knowledge is four times rarer. The comprehensive life preview — the forward-facing structural twin of the life review, in which the whole of the life to come is shown — occurs in only 1.3% of accounts, and is thus genuinely rare, which is worth stating plainly because the anecdotal literature is built almost entirely from these rare cases and gives the impression that they are typical.

The three markers overlap, but not in the way a single underlying phenomenon would produce. Personal and global future knowledge co-occur at above chance — 133 accounts report both, 898 report personal only, 125 report global only, and 5,597 report neither (χ² = 273.0, *p* = 2.5 × 10⁻⁶¹, φ = 0.201) — but the association is moderate, and the great majority of those who receive personal future knowledge receive nothing about the world. More striking is the pattern in the experiencers' own questionnaire answers, where of those who answered the future-scenes item affirmatively, 86 specified personal future and 54 specified world future and **not one specified both**. Whatever is happening, it is directed. The content arrives with a recipient in view, and it does not spill over into general foreknowledge. This is the first empirical trace of what Section 4.4 develops as purposive selection, and it is fatal to any model on which the crisis simply widens the aperture. A widened aperture is indiscriminate. This is not.

The life preview behaves as the structural mirror of the life review, and the data support the mirroring directly. Where the life preview is present, a life review is also present in 71.6% of cases, against 16.8% where it is absent (χ² = 180.4, *p* = 3.9 × 10⁻⁴¹, φ = 0.163) — by a wide margin the strongest association the life preview marker has with anything. Personal future knowledge shows the same relationship more weakly but at far greater statistical weight: 35.1% of those with personal future knowledge report a life review against 14.3% of those without (χ² = 260.7, *p* = 1.2 × 10⁻⁵⁸, φ = 0.196), and the effect concentrates in the *extensive* review specifically, 16.7% against 5.1% (χ² = 182.1, *p* = 1.7 × 10⁻⁴¹, φ = 0.164). Whatever operation produces a comprehensive showing of the life lived is the same operation that, when it runs at all, tends also to produce a showing of the life to come. This is what one would expect if both are explications of a single state-perception rather than two separate faculties, one aimed backward and one forward.

The association structure of personal future knowledge across the whole corpus is set out below, since Sections 4 through 7 draw on it repeatedly. All tests are 2 × 2 chi-square with one degree of freedom; φ is reported because with n = 6,753 almost anything reaches significance and the effect size is what carries the argument.

| Personal future knowledge is associated with | Present | Absent | χ² | *p* | φ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A veridical claim of a future event | 28.7% | 2.4% | 1001.2 | 1.0 × 10⁻²¹⁹ | **0.385** |
| Commissioned mission | 50.9% | 16.7% | 598.2 | 4.2 × 10⁻¹³² | 0.298 |
| Global future knowledge | — | — | 273.0 | 2.5 × 10⁻⁶¹ | 0.201 |
| Life review (any) | 35.1% | 14.3% | 260.7 | 1.2 × 10⁻⁵⁸ | 0.196 |
| Chose the incarnation or mission | 8.0% | 1.0% | 207.2 | 5.5 × 10⁻⁴⁷ | 0.175 |
| Life review (extensive) | 16.7% | 5.1% | 182.1 | 1.7 × 10⁻⁴¹ | 0.164 |
| Precognition as aftereffect | 20.6% | 7.4% | 179.3 | 6.8 × 10⁻⁴¹ | 0.163 |

Two features of this table deserve immediate comment, one because it is the strongest result in the thesis and one because it is the weakest.

The strongest is the association with a specifically veridical future-event claim, φ = 0.385, which is an effect size rarely seen in this literature. It means that those who report having received knowledge of their personal future overwhelmingly also report a *checkable* instance of it — something that later happened as shown. This matters because it establishes that the marker is not capturing a vague sense of destiny or a retrospective narrative gloss. It is capturing accounts in which the experiencer makes a falsifiable claim. Section 3.3 examines how those claims fared.

The weakest is the association with the timeless or everything-at-once time perception: φ = 0.066, statistically significant only because of the corpus size. This is stated in full here because it cuts against the expectation the state model most naturally generates. If temporal preview is a state-perception, one might expect it to be tightly coupled to reported temporal dissolution, and at the level of personal future knowledge it simply is not. The coupling is present but weak. What the data actually show is that the coupling strengthens sharply as the content becomes less personal and more comprehensive — for global future knowledge the association is φ = 0.130 and the everything-at-once rate reaches 36.4% against a 12.2% baseline; for commissioned mission it is φ = 0.141 at 32.8% against 18.7%. The profile across groups is:

| Group | Everything at once | Timeless | Combined |
|---|---|---|---|
| All accounts | 13.6% | 8.2% | 21.8% |
| No future knowledge | 12.2% | 7.7% | 19.9% |
| Personal future knowledge | 17.7% | 10.6% | 28.3% |
| Life preview | 18.2% | 18.2% | 36.4% |
| Global future knowledge | **36.4%** | 12.4% | **48.8%** |

The gradient is real and it is ordered exactly as the model would order it — the broader and more comprehensive the content, the more likely the experiencer is to report that time was not operating — but the effect at the level of ordinary personal future knowledge is modest, and Section 8.5 treats this as a genuine limitation rather than explaining it away. A note on the denominator is also required: `time_perception` is coded `not_mentioned` in 64.0% of accounts, so all of these figures are proportions of the whole corpus including the silent majority, and the relative comparison between groups is far more trustworthy than any absolute rate.

### 3.2 Validation Against the Experiencer's Own Answer

Section 2.3 reported the validation statistics; this subsection states what they mean for the argument, because the existing treatments of temporal preview have no equivalent and the difference is the difference between a claim and an assertion.

The NDERF questionnaire item **"Did scenes from the future come to you?"** is answered in 1,508 of the narratives. It is an independent measurement in the strict sense: it was produced by the experiencer, in their own submission, without reference to any later analysis, and it targets precisely the construct the structured field targets. Against it, the extraction achieves **100.0% sensitivity** — every one of the 86 accounts in which the experiencer affirmed future scenes was independently flagged — with **95.0% raw agreement** and **Cohen's κ = 0.672**.

Perfect sensitivity is the figure that matters most for this thesis, because the argument in Sections 4 through 7 is built on the *positive* cases. If the extraction were missing cases the experiencers affirm, the positive group would be a biased subsample of the real one and every association reported here would be suspect. It is not missing them. The 75 accounts flagged by the extraction where the questionnaire answer is negative constitute the entire disagreement, and inspection of those narratives shows the expected pattern: they are accounts in which future knowledge is described in the body of the narrative — often at length, often as the emotional center of the account — while the questionnaire field was left blank, answered with a single word, or answered narrowly with respect to visual "scenes" when the reported content was propositional rather than pictorial. This is a difference in what was being asked, not a hallucinated positive.

The consequence for prevalence reporting is that two defensible numbers exist and both should be visible. Within the 1,508 responders, the experiencers' own affirmations give personal future scenes at 5.7%; within the same subset the extraction gives 10.7%; across the full 6,753 the extraction gives 15.3%. The last figure is higher partly because it includes implied cases and partly because the responder subset is not representative of the whole corpus. Nowhere in this thesis is a prevalence claim rested on a single number, and where the two measures diverge on a substantive question — as they do in Section 7.1, on the clinical boundary — the divergence is reported as a divergence.

**Critical Finding**: the validation establishes something about the phenomenon and not merely about the method. A construct that an automated extraction and the experiencers themselves converge on at κ = 0.672 with perfect sensitivity is a construct with a stable phenomenological signature. Temporal preview is recognizable. Experiencers know when it has happened to them and say so in a form that survives independent identification. This is not a category imposed on the data from outside.

### 3.3 The Veridical Claim and Its Verification Deficit

Of the 6,753 accounts, **435 (6.4%)** include a veridical claim specifically about a future event: an assertion that something shown or told during the experience subsequently occurred in the world. The corpus records how each such claim stands with respect to verification, and the result must be reported without softening, because it is the single largest weakness in the evidential position of this thesis.

| Verification status of the 435 future-event claims | Count | % of claims |
|---|---|---|
| Verified | 40 | 9.2% |
| Partially verified | 66 | 15.2% |
| Claimed but unverified | 324 | 74.5% |
| Not mentioned | 5 | 1.1% |

Three quarters of the future-event claims in this corpus rest on the experiencer's word alone. Only 40 accounts — **0.6% of the corpus** — carry a claim recorded as verified. This is a verification deficit, and it is the direct consequence of the archive's nature: NDERF and IANDS collect testimony, not case investigations, and there is no protocol by which a submitted claim is independently checked against a record. No conclusion in this thesis should be read as resting on the veridicality of the previews.

It is important to be exact about what this does and does not undermine. It undermines any claim that the corpus demonstrates precognition. It does not undermine the argument this thesis makes, because that argument is about the **structure** of the reported content, not about its accuracy. Whether or not the child in the red outfit was really seen in advance, the account tells us that the content could not be recalled until the moment arrived — and that structural feature is present in the testimony whether the testimony is veridical or not. A model of what kind of thing was received must account for the reported structure. The present argument is therefore insulated from the verification deficit in a way that a precognition claim would not be, and this insulation is a deliberate feature of the argument's design rather than a convenient escape.

It is also worth registering what the 106 verified and partially verified cases are worth, which is more than nothing and less than proof. They establish that the archive contains claims their submitters were willing to have checked and that survived at least partial checking, which is the minimum condition for the phenomenon being about anything at all. What they cannot establish, in the absence of documentation contemporaneous with the experience and independent of the experiencer, is that the knowledge preceded the event. Section 8.5 states the design that would settle this and notes that the corpus is structurally incapable of supplying it.

One further pattern in the veridical data belongs here because it bears on the model. Among the 435 future-event claims, the co-occurrence with personal future knowledge is 28.7% against a base rate of 2.4% — the largest effect in the study, φ = 0.385. The claims are not scattered randomly across the corpus; they concentrate almost entirely in the accounts that report having been shown something. This coherence is what one expects if a real and specific phenomenon is being reported and is not what one expects if future-event claims were the product of general retrospective sense-making, which would distribute far more evenly across an archive whose submitters are uniformly convinced their experience was meaningful. Of the corpus as a whole, 58.2% rate the experience as definitely real; the veridical future claims do not follow that near-universal conviction, they follow the specific marker.

## 4. The Preview Is Not a Recording: Five Structural Signatures

### 4.1 Simultaneity: The Whole Life Given at Once

The first signature is that the content does not arrive in order. This is not an occasional flourish in the accounts; it is the dominant way experiencers describe the reception, and it is the feature that most directly resists a recording model, since a recording is by definition a sequence.

The clearest statement in the corpus comes from an experiencer who received both directions at once:

> "It showed me my whole life in a flash of an instant... the one I'd already lived, and all that was to come if I returned to earth. It was all there at the same time, all the details of all the cause and effect relations in my life."

Read as ordinary narrative this sentence does not work. A flash of an instant cannot contain all the details of all the causal relations of a life, because details take time to enumerate and causal relations take order to specify. The account is not confused; it is reporting two registers simultaneously, which is exactly what §2.2 predicts for a state received at an interior degree and then explicated at the natural. The instantaneity belongs to the reception. The exhaustive detail belongs to the explication. The experiencer has both and reports both in one sentence because for her they were one thing.

The corpus is full of the same doubling. Another account describes the content moving "on fast forward... too fast for me to comprehend completely," and a third describes a "wash of information" moving through her. These are the natural mind's approximations for a reception that had no rate at all. The fast-forward metaphor is instructive precisely because it fails: fast forward is still a sequence, merely accelerated, and the experiencer who reaches for it is trying to render a non-sequential reception in the only vocabulary the natural degree provides. The metaphor is the seam showing.

The structured data support this at the group level, with the honest qualification stated in §3.1. Reported temporal dissolution rises with the comprehensiveness of the content: 19.9% of those reporting no future knowledge describe time as timeless or everything-at-once, against 28.3% for personal future knowledge, 36.4% for the comprehensive life preview, and 48.8% for global future knowledge. The gradient is ordered as the model requires. Its magnitude at the level of ordinary personal future knowledge is small (φ = 0.066), and the argument of this subsection therefore rests primarily on the phenomenology, with the structured gradient as corroboration rather than as proof. What can be said firmly is that among those reporting the broadest content, nearly half report that time was not operating as time — more than double the corpus baseline — and that no group reporting future knowledge sits below the baseline.

A recording model can accommodate none of this without additional machinery. If the future were being read from a store, the reading would take time, and the content would arrive in the order in which it was stored, and the experiencer would report a film. Some do report something film-like, and Section 8.5 counts those cases honestly. But the modal report is of a simultaneous whole, and a simultaneous whole is not a recording of a sequence. It is the perception of something that has no sequence to record.

### 4.2 Conditionality: "All That Was to Come If I Returned"

The second signature is that the content arrives conditionally, and it does so in the experiencers' own grammar, unprompted and consistently. Conditional-return language appears in 161 narratives (2.40%) as an explicit construction, and its distribution is the most theoretically loaded fact in this section, because a fixed future admits no conditionals.

The same account quoted above carries it: she was shown what she had lived "and all that was to come **if I returned to earth**." The conditional is not a hedge added in the retelling. It is the structure of what she received. Another experiencer states it as a direct exchange: "I asked him how my life would be if I went back, and he showed me several things I would do." A third, whose crisis came in childbirth and who explicitly declared it a life-threatening event rather than a clinical death, describes the conditional as a fork with both branches present: "I saw everything that was going to happen with me... and the choice I could make about living and raising her or stopping now... I was asked to choose."

That last account is decisive against the coordinate reading, and it should be dwelt on. If the future were a region of a fixed manifold, then one of these two branches is real and the other is not, and the experiencer was shown a fiction alongside a fact without being told which was which — and was then asked to choose between them, which on a fixed manifold is not a coherent request. On the state reading it is entirely coherent. She was perceiving her own state and its trajectory, and the trajectory forked because her state contained a genuine disjunction: two directions the same condition could take. To be shown both and asked to choose is precisely what one would expect if what is perceived is a condition rather than a coordinate, because a condition's unfolding depends on what the condition becomes.

A fourth account uses the vocabulary of possibility without any prompting at all, and the word choice is worth preserving exactly: "a wash of information may have moved through me about this present incarnation's **potential outcomes**," and later, generalizing, "we absorb the possibilities at the time of our injury." Potential outcomes. Possibilities. This is an experiencer with no theoretical commitment reaching, independently, for modal language, because modal language is what fits what she received.

The structured data corroborate the conditionality indirectly but powerfully through the mission association. Where personal future knowledge is present, a commissioned mission is present in **50.9%** of accounts against **16.7%** where it is absent (χ² = 598.2, *p* = 4.2 × 10⁻¹³², φ = 0.298). A commission is an inherently conditional structure — it specifies what is to be done by someone who might not do it — and half of all temporal-preview accounts carry one. Related fields run the same way: those with personal future knowledge report having chosen the incarnation or mission at 8.0% against 1.0% (φ = 0.175) and a pre-incarnation covenant at 4.9% against 0.6% (φ = 0.139). The forward-facing content in this corpus is overwhelmingly bound up with agency, choice, and undertaking. It is not the vocabulary of a fixed record.

### 4.3 Non-Retrievability: The Content That Cannot Be Recalled Until It Arrives

The third signature is the one this thesis is named for, and it is the strongest single piece of structural evidence in the corpus, because there is no version of a storage-and-retrieval model that predicts it.

The keystone account is that of an experiencer who nearly died in a coma in 1963. She describes recognizing her son's red outfit at the moment he wore it, matching it to what she had been shown while out of her body, and then states the constraint directly:

> "As soon as I saw him this déjà vu hit me and I remember that that was the outfit he had on when I was shown my future while I was out of my body... **I can't remember any of my future until it happens.**"

The sentence is not a report of poor memory. It is a report of a specific and stable structure: the content exists, it is confirmed by its arrival, and it is inaccessible before its arrival. She is not saying that she has forgotten what she was shown. She is saying that what she was shown is not the kind of thing that can be looked up.

The corpus contains this same structure repeatedly. One experiencer describes the content as running "on fast forward... too fast for me to comprehend completely **until much later in my life as events that I saw finally did occur**. I was told just to observe and to remember." The instruction was to remember; the comprehension arrived only at the events. Another, whose experience occurred at kindergarten age and who could not articulate it at the time, "let my experience rest until late adolescence, where I had full body experience of a déjà vu. I somehow relived the event." A decade of dormancy, ended not by recall but by arrival.

Now consider what a storage model must say. If the crisis wrote a record of future events into memory, then that record is in memory, and memory is what recall operates on. The model must therefore add a second mechanism — a lock, a seal, a suppression — whose only function is to prevent access until an external trigger matches. This is not impossible to state, but it is unmotivated: nothing in the storage model implies it, it must be posited for the sole purpose of accommodating this observation, and it raises an immediate further question of what performs the matching, since matching a present event against a sealed record requires reading the sealed record. The lock has to be a lock that can be read through in order to know when to open. The account is thus purchased at the price of an incoherent mechanism.

The state model requires no addition whatever. §2.2 established that the natural memory stores things in extension and succession, because that is the form of the natural degree, and that a reception with neither extension nor succession presents nothing for the natural memory to file. It is not that the content is stored and blocked. **There is no object of the right kind to store.** What was received was a state, and a state is not an item. Consequently there is nothing to recall, and the question "what did you see?" is malformed in the same way that "where in the spiritual world is that?" is malformed. And when the corresponding moment arrives at the natural degree — when the state comes into ultimates — it becomes for the first time an object of the natural sort, and at that instant it can be met. Section 4.5 examines what meeting it is like.

The narrative-level frequencies support the pattern's reality without overstating it. Explicit déjà vu language appears in 55 narratives, already-lived language in 75, and later-recognition language of any form in 1,012 (15.06%). The last figure is broad and includes many constructions unrelated to preview, so it is not offered as a prevalence estimate for this signature; the narrow constructions, at roughly one percent each, indicate a phenomenon that is uncommon in explicit form and highly consistent wherever it appears. Consistency is what matters here. Every account in the corpus that addresses retrievability directly says the same thing, and none says the opposite: no experiencer reports having been shown the future, remembered it in detail afterward, and simply waited.

### 4.4 Purposive Selection: What Is Shown, and What Is Not

The fourth signature is that the content is selected, and selected by function rather than by chronology. This connects the present analysis to the **Purposive Economy** hypothesis already established in *[Mission-Based Returns](Mission-Based%20Returns_%20Volunteer%20Soul%20Detection%20Analysis.md)* and *[A Substantial Profile of the Volunteer Soul Incarnation](A%20Substantial%20Profile%20of%20the%20Volunteer%20Soul%20Incarnation_%20A%20Synthesized%20Analysis%20of%20Phenomenological%20and%20Psychological%20Data.md)*, and it is offered here as an extension of that established finding into a domain it had not been tested against, not as a new discovery.

What is shown, in the accounts, is what the showing is *for*. One experiencer states the purpose in the first clause of the sentence: "**In order to give me strength**, I was shown my future, from the point of return, all through my entire life... years and years of terrible pain... then slowly good feelings... until my future was full of wonderful happy experiences." The content was not a survey. It was a specific trajectory shown to a specific person at the moment she had to decide whether to endure it, and its shape — long suffering resolving into wholeness — is exactly the shape that makes endurance possible. Another experiencer, facing a choice about her newborn, was shown the branch structure of that choice and nothing else. A third was warned by three figures of a series of events, and reports that "as things unfolded every one of their predictions came true to the last detail" — a warning, given to someone who needed to be warned.

The selection is equally visible in what is absent. The corpus contains almost no dates. Experiencers who can describe a garment, a room, a grown child's face, and the emotional texture of a moment years away can almost never say when it will occur, and the few who attempt it do so vaguely. Nor is there any trace of the indiscriminate: no accounts of being shown the futures of strangers, of arbitrary world events with no bearing on the experiencer, of information that would be trivially checkable and consequential. If a crisis simply opened access to a store of future facts, the content would be arbitrary with respect to the experiencer's needs, and it would carry indices, because a store has an order. Instead the content is uniformly *addressed* — it is about this person's life, given for this person's decision — and it is uniformly unindexed.

The structured data give this its sharpest form. Personal future knowledge co-occurs with commissioned mission at 50.9% against 16.7% (φ = 0.298); global future knowledge co-occurs with mission at 57.4% against 20.5% (χ² = 197.0, *p* = 9.6 × 10⁻⁴⁵, φ = 0.171); the comprehensive life preview co-occurs with mission at 48.9% against 21.6% (χ² = 37.8, *p* = 7.7 × 10⁻¹⁰, φ = 0.075). Across all three markers, roughly half of those who receive forward-facing content also report having been given something to do. And recall from §3.1 the fact that no experiencer in the self-report data affirmed both personal and world future scenes: 86 personal, 54 world, zero both. Each received the one that pertained.

**Critical Finding**: the content of temporal preview is bounded by function. It is complete with respect to what the recipient must do and empty with respect to everything else, including the temporal coordinates that a stored record would necessarily carry. This is not a limitation on access. It is the signature of a perception whose object is a state — because a state has a quality and a direction but no index, and what is perceived in it is what that state is for.

### 4.5 Recognition, Not Recall: The Structure of the Later Jarring

The fifth signature concerns what happens when the moment arrives, and it is the complement of §4.3: if the content cannot be recalled beforehand, what exactly occurs afterward?

The accounts are unusually uniform on this point. What occurs is not remembering. It is being struck. The keystone experiencer's phrasing is "this déjà vu **hit me**" — a passive construction, something arriving from outside rather than being retrieved from within. Another experiencer, recognizing in an ambulance a scene he had dreamed two days earlier, reduces the whole phenomenology to two words: "**I remember.**" Not *I recall having been told*, but the flat present-tense recognition of something already known. A third describes the arrival as somatic rather than cognitive: "a full body experience of a déjà vu. I somehow **relived** the event." Reliving, not recollecting.

The distinction is not a matter of emphasis. Recall is the retrieval of a stored item, and it has a characteristic structure: one holds the item, compares it to the present, and notes the match. Recognition has no such structure. It is immediate, it precedes any comparison, and it carries the certainty before it carries the content — which is why déjà vu is universally described as a feeling of alreadyness rather than as a matching operation. The accounts in this corpus describe recognition, and they describe it in the specific form of alreadyness combined with the inability, even at the moment of recognition, to say what else is coming. The 1963 experiencer recognized the outfit *and in the same breath* stated that she still could not remember any of her future until it happened. Recognition of one arrived state gave her no purchase on the others.

The state model accounts for this directly and, as far as I can determine, uniquely. What was perceived in the crisis was a state. States are known by what they are, not by where they sit, and the mode by which a state is known again is not retrieval but identification — the same thing, met again. When that state comes into ultimates, into the extended and successive form of the natural degree, the experiencer encounters it as an object for the first time, and what is encountered is not similar to what was received; it *is* what was received, now expressed at the outermost degree. Recognition is therefore not a psychological curiosity attached to the phenomenon. It is what perceiving-a-state-and-then-meeting-it necessarily feels like from inside a natural mind.

The aftereffect data show that this is not confined to single moments. Among those reporting personal future knowledge, precognition is reported as a persistent post-experience capacity in **20.6%** of cases against **7.4%** of those without (χ² = 179.3, *p* = 6.8 × 10⁻⁴¹, φ = 0.163); among those reporting global future knowledge the rate reaches **34.5%** against **8.4%** (χ² = 199.3, *p* = 3.0 × 10⁻⁴⁵, φ = 0.172). One experiencer describes the result as a permanent alteration of how life is lived: "Since then I feel like I am reliving my life, I have déjà vu experiences every week most of the time." Weekly. Not one preview redeemed by one arrival, but an ongoing condition in which arrivals are routinely met with alreadyness.

This is the point at which the phenomenon stops being a curiosity about the future and becomes a claim about the relation between the degrees. If recognition is the mode by which a perceived state is met when it reaches ultimates, then someone whose interior degree was opened during a crisis and did not fully close would experience recognition not once but continually — every arriving state met as already known, because it was. What the corpus calls a precognitive aftereffect is, on this reading, not an acquired ability to see forward. It is a residual openness in which the ordinary sequence of arrival is accompanied by the perception of the state that is arriving.

## 5. The State-Perception Model

### 5.1 A State Contains Its Own Unfolding

The model can now be stated in a single sentence: **what is perceived in temporal preview is a state, and a state contains its own unfolding.** Everything else in this section is the unpacking of that sentence and the demonstration that it is not circular.

Begin with what a state is in this framework. A state is not a mood, a mental content, or a snapshot of a person at a moment. It is the condition of the whole — what a person *is*, taken together, at the level of will and understanding rather than of behavior and circumstance. Its organizing principle is the **ruling love**: the orientation that determines what a person moves toward, which the framework has consistently identified as the core determinant of a soul's condition and trajectory. A ruling love is not a preference among options; it is the direction in which everything the person does is already leaning, prior to any particular option arising.

Now the load-bearing step. A direction is not a static property. To specify a direction is already to specify a destination, not because the destination is separately known but because a destination is what a direction *means*. If I know the vector, I have not thereby acquired a second piece of information about where the vector ends; the ending is contained in the vector. This is why the model is not circular and why it does not require any transfer of information from a future to a present. Nothing has to travel. To perceive a state completely — to perceive what someone actually is, at the level where they are one thing rather than many — is to perceive where that state is going, because going-somewhere is not an additional fact about the state but a description of the same fact.

This dissolves the question the coordinate reading cannot answer, which is *how the information got here from there*. On the state model there is no there and no transit. There is one perception of one condition, and what the natural degree calls "the future" is that condition's own explication into extension and succession. The 1963 experiencer did not receive a photograph transmitted backward from an afternoon that had not happened. She perceived a state — hers, her son's, the condition of that household — and the red outfit is that state arriving in ultimates at the moment ultimates were reached.

Three consequences follow immediately, and they are the three that the corpus independently displays.

First, the perception has no order, because a state has no parts to order. This is §4.1, and it is why "in a flash of an instant" and "all the details of all the cause and effect relations" can appear in the same sentence without contradiction.

Second, the perception is conditional wherever the state is genuinely undetermined. A state's trajectory is the trajectory *of that state*; where the state contains a real disjunction — a choice not yet made, a love not yet settled — the unfolding contains that disjunction too, and it is perceived as a fork. This is §4.2, and it is why an experiencer can be shown two branches and asked to choose between them without anything incoherent occurring. The conditional in "all that was to come *if I returned*" is not a qualification appended to a fixed record. It is the record's actual shape, because the state whose unfolding was perceived was the state of a person who had not yet returned.

Third, the perception is bounded by what the state is *for*. A state perceived in the context of a decision explicates into what bears on the decision; a state perceived in the context of a commissioning explicates into the commission. Nothing arbitrary comes along, because there is no store from which arbitrary things could be drawn. This is §4.4, and it is why the corpus contains a red outfit and a grown daughter's face and almost no dates: quality explicates, position does not, because position was never in the state.

What the model does *not* claim should be equally clear. It does not claim that the future is open in the sense of being unrelated to the present, nor that the previews are inaccurate, nor that they are merely subjective impressions dressed up as vision. A state can be perceived with great precision and can explicate into highly specific ultimates, and the corpus records such specificity. The claim is narrower and sharper: the specificity comes from the completeness of the expression, not from a record consulted in advance.

### 5.2 The Correspondential Key

The state-perception reading requires a correspondential key, because every element in the experiencers' natural-degree accounts stands for something at the degree where the reception occurred. The key below is given in the framework's standard form: the natural element as reported, the spiritual reality it expresses, and the functional basis on which the correspondence rests. Correspondence here is functional identity, not resemblance — each row is justified by what the natural element *does*, not by what it looks like.

| Natural element (as reported) | Spiritual reality expressed | Functional basis |
|---|---|---|
| The scene shown (a room, a garment, a face) | The state, explicated at the ultimate degree | The ultimate is the containant in which the interior is fully present; a state coming into the natural degree must take extension and succession, because those are that degree's form |
| "All at once," "in a flash of an instant" | Reception at an interior degree, where there is no succession | Succession is a property of the natural degree only; what is received above it has no rate, and the natural mind reports the absence of rate as instantaneity |
| The conditional — "if I returned" | A state containing a genuine disjunction, not yet settled | A trajectory belongs to the state that has it; where the will is undetermined the unfolding is undetermined, and this is perceived as a fork rather than a line |
| Inability to recall the content | Absence of any object of the natural sort to store | The natural memory files things in extension and sequence; a reception with neither presents nothing to file — not a blocked item but no item |
| The later déjà vu, the "hit," the reliving | The same state arriving in ultimates and being identified | States are known by what they are; the mode of knowing a state again is identification, not retrieval, because there is nothing stored to retrieve |
| The absence of dates while details are exact | Quality transmits through explication; position does not | A state has a quality and a direction but no index; an index is a natural-degree artifact and was never in the perception |
| Being shown "in order to give me strength" | The perception governed by use | Nothing in the framework's order is given except for a use; the content is bounded by the function it serves in the recipient's life |
| The persistent weekly déjà vu afterward | A residual openness of the interior degree | Degrees are present but unopened; where one has been opened and does not fully close, arriving states are met as already known |

Two rows in this key carry more weight than the rest and should be marked. The fourth — inability to recall as the absence of a storable object — is what converts §4.3 from an anomaly into a prediction. The fifth — recognition as identification rather than retrieval — is what converts §4.5 from a psychological observation into a structural consequence. Together they constitute the correspondential core of the thesis: the future cannot be remembered until it arrives because there was never a memory, only a state, and a state is met rather than recalled.

### 5.3 What the Model Predicts, and What the Data Show

A model that merely accommodates the data it was built from is worth little. This subsection therefore sets the state model's predictions against the recording or coordinate model's predictions, and reports the corpus verdict on each — including the one case where the verdict is weak.

| Prediction | Recording / coordinate model | State-perception model | Corpus verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Order of arrival | Sequential — a store is read in the order it is stored | Simultaneous — a state has no parts to order | **State model.** Modal report is a simultaneous whole; temporal-dissolution markers rise with content breadth (19.9% → 28.3% → 36.4% → 48.8%) |
| Modality of content | Categorical — an event either is or is not at that coordinate | Conditional where the state is undetermined | **State model.** Conditional-return grammar in 161 narratives; explicit fork-and-choose accounts; mission co-occurrence at 50.9% vs 16.7% (φ = 0.298) |
| Retrievability before the event | Recallable — a stored record can be read | Not retrievable — nothing of the storable kind was received | **State model, decisively.** Every account addressing retrievability reports the same constraint; none reports the opposite |
| Presence of temporal index | Present — a store has an order and a position | Absent — a state has quality and direction but no index | **State model.** Near-total absence of dates alongside exact visual and emotional detail |
| Scope of content | Arbitrary with respect to the recipient's needs | Bounded by use | **State model.** Zero experiencers affirmed both personal and world future scenes (86 / 54 / 0); mission co-occurrence across all three markers ~50% |
| Phenomenology at arrival | Matching — compare present against stored item | Recognition — the same state identified in ultimates | **State model.** "Hit me," "I remember," "relived"; precognitive aftereffect 20.6% vs 7.4% (φ = 0.163) |
| Coupling to temporal dissolution | No prediction | Strong — reception outside succession should correlate with reported timelessness | **Weak support.** φ = 0.066 for personal future knowledge; the gradient is correctly ordered but the effect is small (see §9.5) |

Six of seven discriminating predictions favor the state model, several by margins that leave no room for interpretation. The seventh does not, and it is the state model's own prediction that fails, not the alternative's. If reception occurs at a degree without succession, one would expect the experiencers who received it to report that time was not operating, and at the level of ordinary personal future knowledge only 28.3% do — against a 19.9% baseline. The gradient across markers is in the predicted direction and the extremes are wide (48.8% for global future knowledge), but the central case is weakly coupled. Possible reasons are discussed in §9.5 rather than here, and none of them is offered as a rescue. The honest statement is that the model's strongest structural predictions are strongly confirmed and its most obvious phenomenological prediction is only weakly confirmed.

There is one further prediction the model makes which is not a comparison with the coordinate reading, because the coordinate reading is silent on it: temporal preview should not require clinical death. If the phenomenon follows from a state thrown to its boundary rather than from a physiological event, then any sufficiently severe crisis of state should be capable of producing it. Section 7 tests this and reports a result that is partly confirmatory and partly ambiguous.

### 5.4 The Central Mapping

The central structural finding of this thesis is that five apparently disparate features of the temporal-preview accounts are five explications of one thing. The mapping below is the finding in its compressed form; the appendix expands it with the full empirical trace for each row.

| Signature (natural degree) | What it looks like in the accounts | What it is (spiritual degree) |
|---|---|---|
| **Simultaneity** | "My whole life in a flash of an instant... it was all there at the same time" | Reception at a degree without succession |
| **Conditionality** | "All that was to come *if I returned to earth*"; "I was asked to choose" | A state whose trajectory is genuinely undetermined |
| **Non-retrievability** | "I can't remember any of my future until it happens" | No object of the natural sort was received, therefore none is stored |
| **Purposive selection** | "In order to give me strength, I was shown my future" | The perception bounded by its use |
| **Recognition** | "This déjà vu hit me"; "I somehow relived the event" | The same state identified as it arrives in ultimates |

Read down the right-hand column and the five become one: a state perceived where there is no succession, undetermined where the will is undetermined, unstorable because it is not a thing, given for a use, and identified when it comes into ultimates. Read down the left-hand column and they look like five separate oddities in need of five separate explanations, which is precisely why the existing treatments of this phenomenon reach for elaborate machinery. The machinery is required only by the reading that takes the left-hand column as the content rather than as the expression.

## 6. Why the Two Borrowed Mechanisms Fail

### 6.1 The Filter Model: Specifying Traffic Across a Gap That Is Not There

The filter or transmission model is a serious proposal with a long pedigree, and the objection raised here is not that it is disreputable. It is that its central structure is unavailable within this framework, and that when it is applied to temporal preview it makes predictions the corpus contradicts.

The structure first. A filter is a device positioned between two regions, regulating what passes from one to the other. To say that the brain filters consciousness is therefore to commit to three things: a consciousness, a world of expression, and a gap between them in which the filter sits. On this account, impairment of the organ widens the aperture, and a wider aperture admits what was previously excluded — hence, applied here, an awareness not bound to the present moment. The framework denies the third commitment, and once the gap goes, the filter has nowhere to be. The body is not an interface standing between an inner reality and an outer one; it is the **complete outermost expression** of the spiritual state, withholding nothing, as *[The Surface That Withholds Nothing](The%20Surface%20That%20Withholds%20Nothing_%20The%20Body%20as%20the%20Outermost%20Expression%20of%20the%20Spiritual%20State.md)* establishes. Where the filter model sees two things and a barrier, the framework sees one series in three degrees. What changes in a crisis is not how much is getting past an obstruction but **which degree is in operation** — a state-shift, not a valve-release. This correction is on the record as strain #25, entered because filter language had drifted into the corpus without ever having been a framework position, and four documents carry annotations to that effect.

It should be said clearly that the anti-neurocentric arguments those documents make are unaffected by the correction and remain valid. The objection is not to the conclusion that consciousness is not produced by the brain. The objection is to the *mechanism* offered for it, and the mechanism's cost is visible in a case the framework has already examined: terminal lucidity, in which a patient with advanced neurological destruction becomes coherent shortly before death. If the brain is a filter and its failure releases a wider awareness, then the released awareness ought to be *less* able to use the body, not more — yet the lucid patient speaks, recognizes family members, sings remembered songs. Those are bodily acts, performed through the very organ the model says has been bypassed. The filter reading has to say that the obstruction was removed and the obstruction was also the instrument. The state-shift reading has no such difficulty: the state changed, and the body, being that state's complete outermost expression, expressed the change.

Now the predictions. Applied to temporal preview, the filter model says that awareness of a wider temporal field is ordinarily excluded and is admitted when the exclusion weakens. Three consequences follow, and the corpus contradicts all three.

If the mechanism is the widening of an aperture, then the admitted content should be **indiscriminate**. Apertures do not select. Yet the content in this corpus is uniformly addressed: 86 experiencers affirmed personal future scenes, 54 affirmed world future scenes, and not one affirmed both. Roughly half of those receiving any forward-facing content also report a commissioned mission. Nothing arbitrary comes through. A filter that admits only what bears on the recipient's decisions is not a filter; it is an agent, and calling it a filter conceals the fact that all the explanatory work is being done by whatever performs the selection.

If the mechanism is the removal of a constraint on perception, then the content, once admitted, should be **available like any other perceptual content** — that is, storable and recallable. The corpus says the opposite without exception. "I can't remember any of my future until it happens." A filter model must add a second, unmotivated mechanism to seal what the first mechanism admitted, and §4.3 has already shown that the seal must be readable in order to know when to open, which is incoherent.

If the mechanism is graded — more impairment, wider aperture — then the phenomenon should scale with physiological severity. Section 7 tests exactly this and finds that on the experiencers' own reports the difference between declared clinical death and declared non-clinical crisis does not reach significance (9.7% vs 13.5%, χ² = 2.08, *p* = 0.150), while the temporal-dissolution marker is effectively identical across the split (55.7% vs 52.8%). The extraction-based measure does show a difference (14.1% vs 23.3%, *p* = 0.003), and that divergence is reported rather than resolved; but the strong version of the graded prediction — that the phenomenon substantially requires the deeper physiological insult — is not what the data show.

### 6.2 The Block Universe: A Qualitative State Literalized into a Coordinate

The second borrowing is more seductive because it wears the authority of physics. On the block-universe reading of relativity, past, present, and future all exist tenselessly as regions of a four-dimensional manifold; the passage of time is an artifact of how an embedded observer traverses it. Temporal preview then becomes a look at a region the experiencer has not yet reached, and the phenomenon is licensed by established science.

It is not, and two distinct objections apply.

The first is that the physics does not say what the borrowing needs it to say. The block universe is one interpretation of relativity, not a result of it; it is contested within philosophy of physics, and the mathematics of general relativity is equally compatible with readings on which becoming is real. To present it as settled and then derive conclusions from it is to build on a foundation the builders themselves describe as under dispute. This is a general methodological failing of the small literature on this subject, and it is the same failing whether the borrowed authority is relativistic spacetime, quantum non-locality, or holographic information theory. Borrowed authority is not evidence.

The second objection is the one that matters within this framework, and it is fatal. The block universe explains a state by converting it into a position. That is precisely the error the framework has already diagnosed and named. Swedenborg perceived a judgment — a qualitative event at the spiritual degree — and assigned it the year 1757, and *The Fallible Ultimate* and *The Selfhood of the Prophet* read that assignment not as a false claim about an event but as the **literalization of a state into a coordinate**: a real perception, given a natural-degree index by a perceiver who did not hold the registers apart. The block-universe reading of temporal preview performs the identical operation. It takes a perception whose content is a condition and locates it at a point on a manifold, and it does so for the same reason Swedenborg did — because the natural mind, confronted with something that has quality but no position, supplies a position. Changing the coordinate system from Gregorian to Minkowskian does not change the category error; it dresses it.

The corpus is also simply against it, on three counts. A coordinate is **fixed**, and the accounts are saturated with conditionality — "if I returned," the fork presented for choice, "potential outcomes," "the possibilities." A coordinate is **indexed**, and the accounts carry almost no indices, though they carry exact detail; on the block reading this is inexplicable, since a region of the manifold is nothing but a location and the one thing a look at it would unambiguously deliver is when. And a coordinate, once observed, would be **remembered**, since observing a region of spacetime is observing an ordinary array of events; yet the accounts insist it cannot be recalled until it arrives.

There is a final irony worth registering. The block universe is invoked to explain how the future can be seen, and its central commitment — that the future exists tenselessly and unchangeably — destroys the thing the experiencers are almost universally describing, which is a decision. The woman shown two branches and asked to choose is not intelligible on a manifold where one branch is real and the other is not. The model imported to rescue the phenomenon deletes its content.

### 6.3 The Cost of Borrowing

The failures above are individual, but they share a structure worth naming, because it recurs wherever this phenomenon is treated and it explains why the existing treatments are unreliable in a way that is not obvious from their surface.

The structure is this. An unfamiliar phenomenon is described in the language of its natural-degree rendering. The rendering is then taken as the content. A mechanism is sought that could produce the content so construed. Because no ordinary mechanism can produce a photograph of a moment that has not occurred, an extraordinary one is imported from a domain with independent prestige — relativistic physics, or a philosophical tradition about mind and brain. The import is presented as established rather than as contested. Conclusions are then drawn with a confidence borrowed from the source domain rather than earned in the target domain. The result reads as rigorous and is not, because every load-bearing joint is a borrowing and no borrowing has been tested against the phenomenon it was brought in to explain.

The characteristic products of this pattern are recognizable. Invented mechanisms appear with technical-sounding names, assembled from the vocabulary of physics without any of its constraints, and function as labels rather than as explanations — naming the phenomenon in a more impressive register does not account for it. Existing findings are re-presented as discoveries, because a borrowed framework does not know what has already been established in the domain it has entered. Evidence is recycled: the same two or three vivid anecdotes circulate, because the borrowed mechanism does the explanatory work and the accounts are needed only as illustration, so no one goes to the archives. And the sourcing degrades until it becomes circular, with analyses citing the researcher's own prior documents as if they were independent evidence.

The cost is not merely that such work is wrong. It is that it forecloses the empirical question. A corpus of 6,753 accounts exists. It contains a validated marker for this phenomenon, a self-report item against which that marker can be checked, thousands of full narratives, and enough structure to test rival predictions against one another. None of that is reachable from inside a borrowed mechanism, because the mechanism arrives with its conclusion already fixed and the accounts are demoted to illustration. The discipline this thesis has tried to observe is the reverse: describe what the accounts contain, state what structure that content has, test whether a premise the framework already holds accounts for that structure, and report the places where it does not. Where that discipline is followed, nothing needs to be borrowed — and where it is not followed, no amount of borrowed authority repairs the result.

## 7. The Boundary Crisis, Not the Cardiac Arrest

### 7.1 The Non-Clinical Comparison

The state model makes a prediction that separates it cleanly from any physiological account: temporal preview should not require the heart to stop. If what produces the phenomenon is a state driven to its boundary — a condition of the whole under extremity — then the relevant variable is the severity of the crisis of state, not the depth of the physiological insult. Clinical death is one way to reach that boundary. It should not be the only way.

The corpus permits a direct test, because a substantial number of NDERF submitters explicitly classify their own event in the submission text, distinguishing a life-threatening event without clinical death from clinical death itself. Extracting those self-declarations yields 556 accounts declaring a non-clinical life-threatening event, 193 declaring clinical death, 3 declaring both, and 5,969 making no such declaration. The comparison across the two declared groups is as follows.

| Measure | Non-clinical crisis | Clinical death | Test |
|---|---|---|---|
| Personal future scenes (experiencer's own answer) | 9.7% (54/554) | 13.5% (26/193) | χ² = 2.08, *p* = **0.150** |
| Personal future knowledge (extraction) | 14.1% (78/555) | 23.3% (45/193) | χ² = 8.94, *p* = 0.003 |
| Timeless or everything-at-once | **55.7%** | **52.8%** | Effectively identical |

Three things must be said about this table, and the order matters.

First, the phenomenon plainly occurs without clinical death. Whichever measure is used, roughly one in ten to one in seven of those who explicitly declared that they did *not* clinically die nevertheless report knowledge of their personal future. This is not a marginal residue. It is a substantial population, and its existence is sufficient to establish that cardiac arrest is not a necessary condition. The corpus supplies vivid instances: the woman in childbirth who was shown the branch structure of her choice and asked to choose, and the woman attacked by an assailant who describes "seeing and hearing my daughter grown up... gave me a glimpse of the future," both self-declared as life-threatening events short of clinical death, both carrying the full signature set — conditionality, purposive selection, later recognition.

Second, the two measures disagree about magnitude, and the disagreement is not resolved here. The experiencers' own questionnaire answers show a difference between the groups that does not reach significance (*p* = 0.150), which would support the strong reading that the physiological depth is simply irrelevant. The extraction shows a difference that does reach significance (*p* = 0.003), which supports the weaker reading that clinical death makes the phenomenon somewhat more likely without being required. Both are reported because both are real, and §3.2 gives the reason they can diverge: the extraction reads the whole narrative and includes implied cases, and richer narratives are plausibly associated with deeper events, which would inflate the extraction-based gap without any difference in the underlying phenomenon. That is a hypothesis, not a finding, and it is not offered as a way of preferring the convenient number. The defensible conclusion is the conjunction: **clinical death is not necessary, and may be facilitating.**

Third, and most striking, the marker for temporal dissolution does not move at all across the split — 55.7% against 52.8%. Whatever produces the sense that time is not operating is present in equal measure in those who declared they did not clinically die. On the graded-physiological reading this is difficult: if the depth of the insult governs how far the ordinary temporal binding is loosened, the two groups should differ. On the state reading it is expected, because the state-shift either occurs or does not, and its occurrence is governed by the crisis of the whole rather than by the specific organ failure that precipitated it.

The broader distribution of causes in the corpus supports the same reading from another angle. Cardiac arrest — the paradigm case in the clinical literature — accounts for only **3.6%** of the corpus, while accident accounts for 30.7%, illness 23.3%, surgery 13.8%, childbirth 5.8%, suicide attempt 4.7%, and combat or violence 2.9%. The archive's population is overwhelmingly not the cardiac-arrest population on which the field's physiological hypotheses have been built, and the phenomenon studied here is distributed across all of it.

### 7.2 Age at Experience and the Preverbal Case

The corpus records age at the experience for 1,559 accounts (23.1% of the total), and stratifying by age produces the most unexpected result in this study.

| Age at experience | n | Personal future knowledge | Precognition as aftereffect | Commissioned mission |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | 271 | 20.3% | **21.0%** | 19.6% |
| 6–12 | 395 | 19.0% | 14.9% | 23.3% |
| 13–19 | 414 | 19.3% | 15.2% | 27.8% |
| 20–39 | 321 | 21.8% | 13.1% | 33.0% |
| 40–59 | 120 | 25.8% | 9.2% | 41.7% |
| 60+ | 38 | 21.1% | **2.6%** | — |

Personal future knowledge is essentially flat across the lifespan, varying between 19.0% and 25.8% with no systematic trend. Commissioned mission rises steadily with age, from 19.6% in the youngest group to 41.7% in the 40–59 band, which is unsurprising: a commission is articulated in terms of a life's work, and older experiencers have both the vocabulary and the situation for it.

The precognitive aftereffect runs the other way, and it runs monotonically: **21.0% → 14.9% → 15.2% → 13.1% → 9.2% → 2.6%**. The younger the experiencer at the time of the crisis, the more likely they are to report a persistent recognition capacity afterward, and the decline across the range is eightfold. The smallest cells (60+, n = 38) carry wide uncertainty and the effect should not be read from the extremes alone, but the trend is present across every adjacent pair.

This is not what a storage model predicts. If the crisis writes a record, a child's record should be *worse* than an adult's — less encoded, less articulable, more degraded by the intervening decades. The youngest group should show the weakest aftereffects, and it shows the strongest.

The state model predicts it, and the prediction turns on the point already established in §4.3. A state impressed before language has no narrative to lose. The older experiencer receives the same state and immediately renders it into a story — scenes, sequence, an account that can be told and retold, which is to say an object of the natural sort, subject to all the ordinary degradations of natural memory. The younger experiencer cannot perform that rendering, and so the reception is not converted into a narrative that can decay. What remains is not a story but a **capacity**: the interior degree was opened and did not fully close, and arriving states continue to be met as already known. The corpus supplies exactly this case in the account of an experiencer whose crisis came at kindergarten age, who could not articulate what had happened, who "let my experience rest until late adolescence, where I had full body experience of a déjà vu. I somehow relived the event." Ten years of nothing, then arrival, then recognition — and recognition of a somatic and total kind rather than a narrative one.

The generalization the model licenses is limited and should be stated with its limits. It is that where a crisis occurs before the capacity for narrative rendering has developed, the reception is less likely to survive as content and more likely to persist as an openness. What follows from that in a given life is not determined by this data, and the corpus contains no measure that would settle it.

### 7.3 Implications for Lifelong Recognition Phenomena

Two findings from the preceding subsections combine into a proposal that goes beyond what this corpus can confirm, and it is presented as a proposal for that reason.

The first is that clinical death is not required. A crisis of state severe enough to reach the boundary is sufficient, and the temporal-dissolution marker — the clearest structural sign that a state-shift occurred — is present at essentially identical rates in those who declare they did not clinically die. The second is that the earlier the crisis, the more likely the residue is a persistent recognition capacity rather than a retrievable account.

Taken together, these imply a class of case the near-death literature does not collect and would not identify: a boundary crisis severe enough to produce a state-shift, occurring early enough that no narrative was formed, leaving behind neither an experience the person can report nor a memory they can examine — only a lifelong disposition to meet arriving moments with recognition. Such a person would not appear in any NDE archive, because they have nothing to submit. They would have no experience to describe, no light, no beings, no review. What they would have is the aftereffect without the account: frequent, unbidden alreadyness, and no explanation for it.

The corpus can neither confirm nor refute the existence of this class, and it is important to be exact about why. Every account in the archive comes from someone who *had something to report*; the selection is on the reportable. A population defined by having nothing to report is, by construction, outside the sampling frame. What the corpus does establish is that each of the two conditions holds independently within it — the non-clinical pathway is real and substantial, and the aftereffect-without-articulation pattern is real and strongest in the youngest — and that they are therefore jointly possible. It establishes no more than that.

The proposal is nevertheless testable in principle, and the design is worth stating because it is the natural next study. One would sample on the aftereffect rather than on the experience: identify adults reporting persistent, high-frequency recognition phenomena without a known near-death event, and ascertain independently — through medical or family records rather than through the participant's own memory — whether an early-life boundary crisis occurred. The state model predicts an elevated rate of such crises in that population, concentrated in the preverbal years, with no corresponding elevation of retrievable experience content. The storage model predicts nothing of the kind, since on that account an unrecorded crisis leaves nothing at all. Until such a study exists, this remains a structural inference from two established findings, and it is labeled as such.

## 8. The Untensed Representation: What the Memory Sciences Already Establish

The five signatures of §4 were derived from the accounts and read correspondentially in §5. Three of them — non-retrievability, recognition-rather-than-recall, and the alreadyness that accompanies arrival — were described as properties of the experience without any account of *why the experience should take that form* in a human memory system. The appendix of this thesis lists that omission as a debt owed by the rival model. It was owed by this one too.

This section pays it, using a body of clinical and neuroscientific work that is neither speculative nor contested at the level at which it is used here. It also identifies, in that same body of work, a classificatory decision that is not a finding — a place where the phenomenon under study is disqualified in advance by a premise rather than by a result. That decision is the reason the natural sciences of memory cannot, as currently framed, ever establish temporal preview, no matter what is observed.

### 8.1 Two Dual-Process Literatures, One Fault Line

Two independent dual-process distinctions have been developed in the memory sciences for unrelated purposes. They fall along the same seam.

The first belongs to the trauma-memory literature. Brewin, Dalgleish, and Joseph (1996) proposed dual representation theory to account for the phenomenology of post-traumatic stress, distinguishing two memory systems of which one is verbally accessible and the other, in their own words, "automatically accessible through appropriate situational cues." Brewin, Gregory, Lipton, and Burgess (2010) revised the theory within a neural-systems model of ordinary memory and imagery, and supplied the terminology used throughout this section: the evidence "implies distinct neural bases to abstract, flexible, contextualized representations (C-reps) and to inflexible, sensory-bound representations (S-reps)." A C-rep is bound to a time, a place, and a position in a narrative, and can be retrieved voluntarily. An S-rep is none of these. It is sensory-bound, cue-triggered, involuntary, and — the feature that matters for everything below — carries **no contextual index**. The clinical consequence is the flashback, whose defining oddity is not vividness but tense: the content is not experienced as a memory of something past. It is experienced as occurring now, because the vehicle carrying it contains no marker specifying when.

The second belongs to the déjà vu literature. The phenomenology is not in dispute: a present scene feels familiar while the experiencer simultaneously knows that it must be new, and the time, place, and context of the supposed prior occurrence are uncertain or impossible. Brown's (2003) review in *Psychological Bulletin* puts lifetime prevalence at roughly 60%, declining with age and rising with socioeconomic status, education, and travel; the first systematic review and meta-analysis of the field, covering 46 studies, gives a pooled non-ictal prevalence of 0.74, 95% CI [0.67, 0.79] (Hadzic and Andersson 2024). What is in dispute is the explanation. Brown sorts the candidate accounts into four families — dual processing, neurological, memory (implicit familiarity for stimuli not consciously recognised), and attentional — and the reading this section uses belongs to the third. It is therefore one live account among four rather than the settled position of the field, and this thesis argues for it rather than inheriting it.

The neuroanatomy is a different matter, and it is among the better-established results in the phenomenology of memory because it is established by production rather than by correlation. Bancaud, Brunet-Bourgin, Chauvel, and Halgren (1994) studied sixteen temporal-lobe epilepsy patients with electrodes stereotactically implanted for pre-surgical localisation, and evoked the "dreamy state" — déjà vu and vivid memory-like hallucination — by direct electrical stimulation on **forty-three occasions in fourteen patients**, against nine spontaneous occurrences in six. Dreamy states were evoked from temporal neocortex on fifteen occasions, from anterior hippocampus on seventeen, and from amygdala on ten. The paper additionally reports, considering all dreamy states however produced, involvement of the temporal neocortex in 88% of cases, the anterior hippocampus in 83%, and the amygdala in 73% — but those figures count a structure as involved whether it was the site stimulated *or* the site to which discharge subsequently spread, and they must not be read as a ranking of generative sites. The authors' own conclusion runs the other way: because far fewer medial than neocortical leads were stimulated, "the proportion of medial temporal electrodes where dreamy states could be evoked was much higher than in the neocortex."

Bartolomei and colleagues (2004) resolved the locus further. Across 280 stimulations in 24 patients, déjà vu was produced proportionately more often by stimulation of entorhinal cortex than of amygdala or hippocampus — and, in the result that matters most here, the two halves of the dreamy state separated by site: "déjà vu was associated with stimulation of the EC and reminiscence of memories with PC stimulation." A bare familiarity signal and the retrieval of remembered content come apart anatomically. Bancaud had already recorded the same dissociation at the level of the individual electrode: "déjà vu and hallucinations of scenes were often evoked by different stimulations of the same electrode in the same patient."

That finding is routinely glossed as evidence that the brain has misfired. The gloss should be separated from the result. What the stimulation literature demonstrates is which structures generate the experience, and that the familiarity signal is dissociable from remembered content. The further inference — that a signal so produced is therefore spurious — is a classificatory judgement laid over the finding rather than the finding itself, and §8.3 examines the premise that licenses it.

These two distinctions are not the same theory, and no one in either literature has claimed they are. But they cut along one line. In both, the division is between a representation that carries its own context and one that does not. In both, the non-contextual member is cue-triggered rather than retrievable. In both, its phenomenological signature is that content arrives whole and cannot be summoned. **The identification proposed here — that a déjà vu is an S-rep firing without a C-rep — is an argument, not a citation.** It is made on structural grounds. Brewin's framework was constructed for intrusive imagery in psychopathology and has never been applied to déjà vu, and the identification therefore inherits none of that framework's empirical support. §8.6 states what this costs.

What can be offered in place of a citation is an itemised accounting: a property-by-property comparison of what the déjà vu literature attributes to the experience against what the trauma literature independently specified for the format. That accounting, together with the places where it fails, is set out in the companion thesis *[The Format That Cannot Say When](The%20Format%20That%20Cannot%20Say%20When_%20Recognition%20Without%20Tense%20in%20the%20Memory%20Sciences.md)*, which takes the identification as its subject rather than as a supporting step.

### 8.2 Direction Is Assigned, Not Carried

From the definitional feature, one consequence follows immediately, and it is the load-bearing step of this section.

A representation with no contextual index **has no tense**. Not a weak or unreliable one: none. Tense is exactly what a contextual index supplies.

Therefore, at the level of the representation itself, a representation whose referent lies behind the subject and one whose referent lies ahead of the subject are **formally indistinguishable**. There is nothing in the vehicle that could distinguish them, because the only thing that could is the index it does not have.

Direction is therefore not read off the representation. It is **assigned downstream**, by the system that has to file the arriving content — and that system has precisely one category available for *familiar, but not now*. It has **before**, and nothing else. Ordinary cognition maintains no slot for *familiar, but not yet*, because on the assumptions under which the filing system operates, nothing could ever produce one.

Run this through both clinical phenomena and they collapse into a single anomaly with two resolutions:

| | Referent | Index | Assigned tense | Reported as |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Flashback** | Attested, behind the subject | Absent | None available → defaults to present | "It is happening again, now" |
| **Déjà vu** | Not locatable | Absent | Familiar and not-now → filed as past | "I have been here before" |

Same missing index. Opposite resolutions. The resolution is determined by the frame the receiving system runs in, not by anything the content contains. This is not the claim that either resolution is an error. It is the narrower claim that a tenseless vehicle cannot deliver direction, and that whatever direction the experience is reported with was supplied after the fact.

This closes directly onto signatures 3 and 5. **Non-retrievability** (§4.3) is not a strange additional property requiring its own explanation. It is what a representation without a contextual half definitionally is: there is no index under which it could be filed, and therefore nothing under which it could be looked up. And **recognition rather than recall** (§4.5) — the alreadyness that the corpus reports over and over, "two words: *I remember*", "this déjà vu hit me", "a full body experience of a déjà vu" — is what an untensed familiarity signal becomes once a one-directional filing system takes delivery of it.

### 8.3 The Asymmetry of Verdict: Why Déjà Vu Cannot Be Established in Advance

The same structural feature — a representation firing with no retrievable context — receives opposite verdicts in the two literatures, and the verdict tracks one thing only: whether a materially attestable referent is available.

In dual representation theory the S-rep is granted a **real referent**. The assault occurred; the collision occurred; frequently there is a record. The missing contextual index is then explained as an **encoding fact** — contextualisation failed under extreme arousal or dissociation, or was not yet reliably available in a young child. Note carefully what the field does here, because it is the correct move: it accepts a representation with no contextual index as *veridical*, and explains the absence of the index rather than treating the representation as spurious. That move is available because the referent is independently attested.

In the déjà vu literature the identical structural feature receives the opposite verdict, and the verdict is written into the definition rather than derived from a result. Déjà vu is standardly characterised as an *illusion* of memory. Brown's (2004) review paper is titled "The déjà vu illusion." O'Connor and Moulin's (2010) review is titled "Recognition without identification, **erroneous** familiarity, and déjà vu." The classificatory word arrives before any datum is examined.

The inference underneath is: *no attestable prior occurrence, therefore no real referent, therefore the familiarity signal is false.*

The first step of that inference is legitimate, and any argument that pretends otherwise is not worth making. The trauma case has external corroboration that the déjà vu case lacks. That is a genuine evidential difference, not a metaphysical preference, and it fully justifies declining to grant déjà vu a referent.

The second step is not legitimate. The absence of an admissible referent licenses **"referent not established."** It does not license **"erroneous."** Moving from the first to the second requires an additional premise: *that a familiarity signal can only ever be tracking a prior occurrence at the natural degree.* That premise is nowhere tested in this literature. It is not a finding of it. It is the substrate on which it is built, and it is invisible from inside because nothing in the research programme ever has occasion to state it.

And the premise is not neutral with respect to what this thesis is arguing. The metaphysical settlement the memory sciences inherit did not abolish the division of substance; it kept the division and disqualified one side of it. Two orders were distinguished, and then only one was granted standing as a source of causes. Under that settlement the sole alternative referent available here — a state received at a degree above the natural, having quality and direction but no index (§5.1) — is inadmissible *before the question is asked*. Which yields a closed system:

1. Déjà vu is classified as illusion at the outset, by definition.
2. No observation can revise the classification, because the only referent that would rehabilitate it has been excluded by construction rather than by evidence.
3. The classification is subsequently cited as though it were a result.

The consequence is exact, and it is not a complaint about rigour. Within this frame, temporal preview **cannot be established in advance** — not *has not been*, but *cannot be*. Where the referent is materially attestable, an untensed representation is granted its referent up front and the missing index is explained. Where the referent is not materially attestable, the identical representation is denied a referent up front and the missing index is reclassified as the signature of the error. The asymmetry is not in the data. It is in an admissibility rule applied before the data are consulted.

Grant instead that a boundary-crisis reception is a real event with a real referent — which is what §3 through §7 argue on independent empirical grounds, without reference to any of this — and the identical representation receives the identical treatment it already receives in the trauma case, by the identical reasoning, with no special pleading required at any point.

This is a methodological result, not a demonstration. It shows that a premise is doing work that has been attributed to evidence. It does not show that the premise is false, and §8.6 states in detail how far short of that it falls.

### 8.4 What This Closes in the Present Thesis

Three things, and they should be named separately because they are of different weights.

**The alreadyness debt.** The appendix table lists, for signature 5, what the coordinate model must supply: "an account of why matching a stored record produces alreadyness rather than recollection." That debt was charged to the rival model, but the state model had not paid it either. Saying that a state has quality without index (§5.1) is a claim about the *object* of perception; it does not say why the *subject's* experience of receiving one takes the specific form of alreadyness. §8.2 supplies that: alreadyness is what an untensed vehicle becomes when the only available filing category is *before*. The correspondential claim and the psychological claim are the same absence described from two planes.

**The preverbal case.** §7.2 reported that the precognitive aftereffect declines monotonically with age at the crisis — 21.0% for ages 0–5 falling to 2.6% for 60 and over — and §7.3 inferred from this that an early impression leaves a capacity rather than a story, on the reasoning that "a state impressed before language has no narrative to lose." That was an assertion with a curve behind it and no mechanism. The encoding account supplies one, and supplies it from an independent literature: contextual binding capacity is not mature in early childhood, and dissociative encoding suppresses contextualisation further, so both conditions independently predict **S-rep-dominant encoding with little or no C-rep**. What follows is exactly the §7.3 profile — nothing to report, nothing to retrieve, and a lifelong disposition to cue-triggered recognition. The convergence is worth weighing carefully: the age curve was computed from the corpus before this account was considered, and it runs in the direction the account requires. That is the strongest item in this section.

**The class the archive cannot see.** §7.3 proposed a population structurally absent from every NDE archive, since selection is on the reportable and this population by definition has nothing to submit. The encoding account explains *why* they have nothing to submit, in terms a clinician would accept without reference to any framework commitment: no C-rep was formed, so there is no narrative, and there never was one to forget.

### 8.5 What This Is Not: A Third Borrowing

§6.3 named a recurring failure — an unfamiliar phenomenon described in its natural-degree rendering, the rendering taken for the content, and an extraordinary mechanism then imported from a prestigious domain and used to license conclusions with authority borrowed rather than earned. A reader who has just been given a third body of outside work is entitled to ask whether this is the same move a third time. The question deserves a direct answer.

The filter model and the block universe were imported as **causal mechanisms**: accounts of how content travels from somewhere to the experiencer. Each arrived with metaphysics attached — a gap across which traffic must be regulated; a manifold on which the future already exists — and each was then used to underwrite conclusions the target domain had not established.

What is used here is not a mechanism and makes no causal claim. It is a description of **representational form**: what kind of vehicle carries this content once it is inside a human memory system, and what such a vehicle can and cannot deliver. It says nothing about where the content originates, proposes no channel, and imports no metaphysics. The argument of §8.3 is in fact that the metaphysics attached to this literature must be *stripped off* before the description can be used at all — which is the opposite of borrowing authority from it.

§6.3 offers the operative test: has the imported material been tested against the phenomenon it was brought in to explain? Here, in one specific place, yes. The encoding account entails a prediction about the direction of the age curve, and the curve was computed before the account was in view. That is a weak test, since the direction was the likely one on several accounts, but it is a real one, and it is more than either of the declined borrowings ever submitted to.

### 8.6 Counting Against

Five findings in this literature cut against the use made of it above, and one alternative explanation is available that requires nothing exotic at all. They are stated at full strength.

**Déjà vu is not associated with pathological dissociation.** Adachi and colleagues (2008) report in the *Journal of Nervous and Mental Disease* that déjà vu experiences are rarely associated with pathological dissociation. This bears directly against the encoding account of §8.4, which leans on dissociative suppression of contextualisation. If the channel were dissociation-dependent in the general population, an association ought to appear. It does not.

**Déjà vu tracks good cognition, not damaged encoding.** Adachi and colleagues (2003) found déjà vu *more* frequent with higher educational attainment, younger age, frequent travel, and better memory function. A deficit account predicts the reverse. Whatever produces déjà vu in the general population, it is not a failing memory system, and any account that treats the S-rep channel as a lesion is refuted at the outset.

**The familiarity account is itself contested.** O'Connor and Moulin (2013) found that déjà vu in healthy subjects is unrelated to laboratory measures of recollection and familiarity for word stimuli. More recently, functional imaging has supported a **memory-conflict** account (Urquhart, Sivakumaran, Macfarlane, and O'Connor 2021), on which déjà vu reflects frontal conflict-monitoring activity — the detection of a mismatch — rather than a spuriously elevated familiarity signal. That reading is currently the better supported of the two, and it weakens the specific identification made in §8.1, because conflict monitoring is not what an S-rep is. It should be recorded, however, that the conflict account is *less* compatible with the illusion verdict than the familiarity account it displaces: on a conflict reading the system is functioning correctly and reporting a genuine mismatch, which returns the question of what the mismatch is with.

**Dual representation theory was never extended to déjà vu.** The identification in §8.1 is this thesis's own, argued structurally, and it inherits none of DRT's empirical support. DRT was built for and validated on intrusive imagery in psychopathology.

**Forgotten dreams are an ordinary sufficient referent.** Brown (2004) reports that roughly 20% of déjà vu experiences duplicate situations from dreams, and around 40% from dreams and waking life together. A forgotten dream is a perfectly ordinary prior occurrence, in principle materially attestable, and would make a large share of déjà vu episodes genuinely retrospective with no revision to anything. This is the strongest mundane alternative to the reading offered here, and it is not ruled out by anything in this thesis. It is worth noting that it also does not dispose of the corpus data, since a substantial portion of the previews in these accounts arrive *as* dreams and are then met; but as an account of déjà vu in the general population it stands unrefuted. That the same channel appears on both sides of the disagreement is taken up as an open question in §9.6, where the design that would separate the two readings is set out.

**And the verification deficit is untouched.** Nothing in this section adds a single verified case to the 40 verified and 66 partially verified of 435 veridical future-event claims reported in §3.3. Removing an unwarranted premise reopens a question. It does not answer one. What §8.3 establishes is that the classification of déjà vu as illusion rests on a premise rather than a finding, and that one representational structure receives opposite verdicts depending on whether its referent is materially attestable. It does not establish the referent. It removes an argument, not a doubt.

### 8.7 The Correspondential Key

| Natural-plane finding | Correspondential reading | What is not claimed |
|---|---|---|
| Representations divide into contextualized (C-rep) and sensory-bound, non-indexed (S-rep) | What is received above the natural degree arrives with quality and direction but no index; descending into the natural memory, it presents nothing that memory is built to file | Not that the spiritual degree *is* the S-rep system, nor that S-reps are spiritual in origin. The great majority have ordinary natural referents |

## 9. Discussion

### 9.1 What the Corpus Contributes

The empirical contribution of this study is separable from its interpretive one, and it should be stated separately so that it can be used by anyone who declines the interpretation.

Temporal preview has been treated in the near-death literature as an occasional anomaly documented by a handful of striking cases. It is not occasional. Knowledge of the personal future appears in **15.3%** of 6,753 accounts, which places it at the same order of prevalence as the life review (17.5%) — a feature that has generated a substantial research literature while this one has generated almost none. That disparity is now quantified rather than asserted.

The corpus also establishes that the phenomenon is not one thing. Personal future knowledge (15.3%), global future knowledge (3.8%), and the comprehensive life preview (1.3%) differ by an order of magnitude in prevalence, differ in their associations, and — in the experiencers' own self-reports — do not co-occur at all at the individual level. Any future work that collapses them will produce incoherent results, and the anecdotal literature has been collapsing them for decades.

Three findings are new to the field so far as I can determine. The association between personal future knowledge and a specifically veridical future-event claim is φ = 0.385 (χ² = 1001.2), which is an exceptionally large effect for this literature and establishes that the marker captures falsifiable claims rather than diffuse impressions. The association with commissioned mission at 50.9% against 16.7% (φ = 0.298) extends the previously established Purposive Economy finding into the temporal domain, where it had not been tested. And the monotonic decline of the precognitive aftereffect with age at experience — 21.0% under six to 2.6% over sixty — is, as far as I can establish, unreported, and it runs opposite to what any encoding-and-storage account of the aftereffect would produce.

Finally, the study contributes a validated instrument. The extraction marker achieves 100.0% sensitivity and Cohen's κ = 0.672 against the experiencers' own questionnaire answers across 1,508 accounts. Whatever one makes of the interpretation, the construct is measurable and the measurement has been checked against the people it describes.

### 9.2 What the Correspondential Framework Contributes

The interpretive contribution is that a premise the framework already held, adopted for entirely unrelated reasons, turns out to predict the structure of a phenomenon it was not devised to address.

That premise is that distance and duration in the spiritual world are states rather than locations (*Heaven and Hell* §§141–153). It was adopted because Swedenborg states it and because the framework treats his system as a hypothesis to be tested. It was applied, before this study, to a completely different problem: the diagnosis of his own 1757 claim as the literalization of a perceived state into a calendar coordinate. Nothing in that work concerned near-death experience, precognition, or déjà vu.

Applied here, it generates six predictions about the shape of temporal-preview content — simultaneity, conditionality, non-retrievability, absence of index, boundedness by use, and recognition rather than recall — of which the corpus confirms six, several decisively, and a seventh which it confirms only weakly. None of these predictions required an additional mechanism. Nothing was imported. The single premise, plus the doctrine of discrete degrees that was already in place, does the entire explanatory job.

The specific gain over the alternatives is that the state model **dissolves** rather than **answers** the question that makes temporal preview seem impossible. The question is how information could travel from a moment that has not occurred to a moment that has. Both borrowed mechanisms attempt to answer it — the block universe by making the future already existent, the filter model by making consciousness non-local — and both incur heavy costs, structural and empirical, in doing so. The state model denies that anything travels. There is one perception of one condition; what the natural degree calls the future is that condition's own explication. A question that requires no transit needs no mechanism of transit.

This is the framework's characteristic move, and it is worth naming as such: a phenomenon that looks anomalous under a coordinate ontology becomes ordinary under a state ontology. That is not a proof of the state ontology. It is a data point about which ontology organizes the observations with fewer additions, and this project has consistently treated that as the relevant test.

### 9.3 Implications for the Framework Hypothesis

The framework holds Swedenborg's system as a hypothesis under test rather than as doctrine to be defended, and a study of this kind therefore has to report what it does to the hypothesis's standing.

The relevant fact is the independence of derivation. Swedenborg's teaching on space and time as state was published in 1758, on the basis of claimed spiritual experience, with no access to statistical method, no near-death archive, and no concept of the phenomenon studied here. The corpus is a contemporary collection of testimony from thousands of people who had no acquaintance with his system, whose accounts were extracted against a schema built for other purposes, and whose reports of temporal preview were validated against their own questionnaire answers. When the 1758 premise turns out to predict the structural signature of that testimony — that the content should be simultaneous, conditional, unindexed, unrecallable, use-bounded, and met by recognition — the prediction was not fitted to the data, because the data did not exist when the premise was stated and the premise was not consulted when the data were collected.

The strength of that observation should not be overstated, and the framework's own record of what happens when it is overstated stands as the caution. Swedenborg's premise is not confirmed by this study in any strict sense; a premise is not confirmed by predicting the structure of one class of testimony. What is established is narrower: applied to a domain it was not devised for, the premise organizes the data with fewer additions than the alternatives require, and its most counterintuitive implication — that the content should be unrecallable until it arrives — is exactly what the corpus most consistently reports. That is the kind of result that warrants continued testing of the hypothesis, and nothing more.

It is also worth noting what this study does to the framework's own record. The filter model, which had drifted into several documents in this corpus as though it were a framework position, is here shown to make wrong predictions in a domain where the framework's actual position makes right ones. Strain #25 was entered as a conceptual correction on structural grounds; this study supplies the first empirical consequence of that correction, and the consequence favors it.

### 9.4 The Determinism Problem, Dissolved Rather Than Answered

Any account of temporal preview must confront the objection that seeing the future entails that the future is fixed, and that a fixed future eliminates the moral agency the whole framework depends upon. The framework holds that the ruling love is formed by choices freely made and that regeneration is a real process with real alternatives. If a near-death experiencer can be shown what she will do in 1983, it appears she cannot avoid doing it, and the entire architecture of freedom collapses.

The objection is decisive against the coordinate reading, and the block-universe borrowing has no answer to it. On that reading the previewed event exists tenselessly at its location and the experiencer's later choice is a formality. Section 6.2 noted the irony: the model imported to explain the previews deletes the deliberation the previews are almost universally *about*.

On the state reading, the objection does not arise, and it is important to see that this is a dissolution rather than a solution. What is perceived is a state and its trajectory. A trajectory is not a decree; it is what a condition is doing. Where the condition contains a settled orientation, the trajectory is stable, and the preview will be reliable — not because the future was fixed, but because the person was. Where the condition contains a genuine disjunction, the trajectory contains it too, and the preview is a fork. This is why the corpus is saturated with conditionals rather than embarrassed by them, and why the woman shown two branches could be asked to choose without anything incoherent occurring.

The reliability of the previews and the reality of the choice therefore have the same source, which is what makes the dissolution genuine rather than verbal. Both follow from the fact that what was perceived was the person. A person with a settled ruling love will act from it, and to perceive that love is to perceive the acting. A person at a real crossroads has a state that forks, and to perceive that state is to perceive the fork. The determinism worry assumes that the previewed content is external to the person and therefore constrains them. On this model it is not external. It is them, seen whole.

One residue of the problem is genuine and should not be smoothed over. Nothing in this account explains why some previews are reported as unconditional, and the corpus contains such reports — the experiencer told a series of things that "came true to the last detail," the woman shown a trajectory of years of pain resolving into happiness, delivered as what would be rather than what might. The state model can say that these are cases in which the state was settled, but it has no independent measure of settledness, so the explanation is unfalsifiable as stated. Section 8.5 counts this among the limitations.

### 9.5 Limitations and Lacunae

The following are the substantive weaknesses of this study, stated without mitigation.

**The verification deficit is severe.** Of 435 future-event claims, 40 are recorded as verified and 324 rest on the experiencer's word alone. Nothing here demonstrates that any preview preceded its event. The argument is designed to be insulated from this, because it concerns the reported structure of the content rather than its accuracy, but the insulation is a limitation on scope, not a strength: this study cannot establish that temporal preview conveys real knowledge of anything.

**The corpus is self-selected and retrospective.** Submitters to NDE archives are not a sample of people who nearly died; they are people whose experience was striking enough to submit and who found the archive. Accounts are frequently written decades later. Every absolute prevalence figure in this thesis inherits both problems. The comparative figures — group against group within the same archive — are more robust but are not immune, since the selection pressure could plausibly differ between, say, those who report future knowledge and those who do not.

**The extraction is a machine reading.** The structured fields were produced by a large language model against a schema. The validation in §2.3 is real and the sensitivity is perfect, but it covers only the 1,508 accounts containing the self-report item, and it validates one field against one questionnaire answer. Fields without such a check — `time_perception`, `mission_commissioned`, `post_experience_gifts` — are used throughout and are unvalidated. Their reliability is assumed, not demonstrated.

**The temporal-dissolution prediction is weakly supported.** The state model's most direct phenomenological prediction — that a reception outside succession should be reported as timeless — yields φ = 0.066 for personal future knowledge. The gradient across markers is correctly ordered and reaches 48.8% for global future knowledge, but the central case is weak, and I have no account of why that should be so which is not post hoc. Additionally, `time_perception` is coded `not_mentioned` in 64.0% of accounts, so the measure is thin.

**The clinical comparison is unresolved.** The two measures disagree: the self-report shows no significant difference across the clinical boundary (*p* = 0.150) while the extraction does (*p* = 0.003). A plausible explanation exists (narrative richness covarying with severity) but it is a hypothesis, and the honest conclusion — clinical death is not necessary, and may be facilitating — is weaker than either measure taken alone would support. The clinical-status classification itself derives from experiencers' self-declarations in free text, not from medical records.

**Unconditional previews are not explained.** The model accounts for conditionality by appeal to undetermined states, but has no independent measure of determinacy, so its treatment of the unconditional cases is unfalsifiable as currently stated. This is a genuine gap and not a minor one, since the unconditional cases are precisely those the coordinate reading handles most naturally.

**The age finding rests on partial data.** Age at experience is recorded in only 23.1% of accounts, and the oldest cell contains 38 cases. The monotonic decline in precognitive aftereffects is present across every adjacent pair, which is reassuring, but the endpoints carry wide uncertainty and the missingness is unlikely to be random.

**The extension in §7.3 is not established.** The proposal that early boundary crises may leave a lifelong recognition capacity without any retrievable experience is a structural inference from two findings, not a finding. The population it describes is by construction absent from the corpus, and no test of it is possible with this data.

**The identification in §8.1 is argued, not cited.** Dual representation theory and the recognition-memory literature on déjà vu are separate programmes, and the claim that their respective non-contextual members are one thing described twice is this thesis's own. It inherits no empirical support from either. Three findings in that literature cut against the use made of it — déjà vu's non-association with pathological dissociation, its correlation with good rather than impaired cognition, and the currently better-supported memory-conflict account — and are stated at full strength in §8.6 rather than answered. The forgotten-dream explanation of déjà vu in the general population is not ruled out by anything here. What §8 establishes is that one classification rests on a premise rather than a result; it establishes nothing about the referent.

**The correspondential key is internal.** The mappings in §5.2 are justified by function within the framework's own commitments. They are coherent and they generate predictions that the data test, which is more than an arbitrary assignment would do, but they are not independently derivable, and a reader who does not grant the framework's premises will not find them compelling on their own.

### 9.6 Open Questions

The preceding section records defects in what is claimed. This one records questions the claims generate and do not answer, which is a different thing and is listed separately so that the two are not confused. None of what follows is asserted here, and none of it is offered as support for anything above. Each is stated with what would count as an answer, because a question that cannot be turned into a design is not yet a research question. Four of the five below can be addressed with instruments that already exist.

**Does ordinary dreaming supply the same untensed representation?** This thesis treats the boundary crisis as the occasion of reception, and §7.3 extends that to early crises whose content was never narratively encoded. But dreaming is universal, occurs nightly, and is in the overwhelming majority of cases never consolidated into retrievable form — which makes it a standing candidate source of sensory-bound content carrying no contextual index, in people who have had no crisis of any kind. Brown's finding that a fifth of déjà vu experiences are attributed by the experiencer to a prior dream, and two fifths to dreams and waking life together, is recorded in §8.6 as counting against this thesis, and on the reading given there it does. It is worth noting that it counts against only under the prior assumption that the dream had no referent; read without that assumption, the same figure is a statement about the *channel* rather than about the status of the content, and is neutral between the two positions. Which of those readings is correct is not settled by anything in this thesis, and asserting the second would be the very move §8.3 criticises, performed in the opposite direction. The question is nevertheless empirically tractable, but the prediction has to be stated in the terms of §8.1 rather than in the terms the literature has used. If dreaming is the general route, what should track déjà vu is the nightly production of sensory-bound representations that never acquire a contextual index — which is a fact about REM exposure, not about dream recall. Recall is the wrong variable and is at best orthogonal to the mechanism: a dream the experiencer can report is a dream that did acquire an index, and is therefore not an instance of the process at issue. That distinction disqualifies most of what has been measured. The correlations between dream recall frequency and déjà vu that survive multivariable control are correlations with *déjà rêve* — déjà vu the experiencer attributes to a prior dream — and an attribution of that kind presupposes the very contextual binding whose absence defines the phenomenon. The best-controlled studies in the field are therefore studies of the one variant that cannot be the mechanism. The older studies that measured undifferentiated déjà vu against dream recall without that presupposition report small positive coefficients, significant in some samples and not others, on unvalidated single items and without covariate control; on the present reading their weakness is expected rather than informative, since dream recall is only loosely coupled to the quantity the route actually specifies.

Stated correctly, the route predicts that déjà vu frequency should rise with total REM exposure and polysomnographic REM density, should fall under selective REM suppression and rise on rebound, and should be indifferent to adverse history throughout. The relation to dream *recall* cannot be predicted a priori and has to be carried as an open question rather than as a prediction. Two considerations pull against each other: the mechanism at issue concerns representations that never acquired a contextual index, which argues for independence from recall or an inverse relation to it, while Adachi and colleagues (2003) report that on factor analysis déjà vu loads together with remembering dreams and with precognitive dreams, which argues that whatever governs production governs both. The second is an empirical result and the first is an inference, and the result should be given the greater weight until it is overturned. The crisis route predicts the opposite on the last point and no relation on the first. Only the adversity half has been tested, and it is met: Adachi and colleagues report déjà vu rarely associated with pathological dissociation, and no study has found a positive association between validated trauma inventories or diagnosed post-traumatic stress and non-ictal déjà vu. That result counts against the crisis route as a general account rather than for the dream route, and the two are not the same finding. On the REM measures, which are the ones that would decide it, nothing has been published at all. The design is nevertheless routine — a sleep laboratory, an existing déjà vu instrument, and an outcome variable defined as déjà vu simpliciter with source attribution recorded separately rather than folded into it. The present corpus cannot contribute, since it contains no sleep data at all.

**Is state-perception the general mode of consciousness, or a condition of the boundary?** §2.2 holds that extension and succession are properties of the natural degree rather than of consciousness as such. Applied consistently, that would make reception-as-state the ordinary case and linear sequencing the outermost form it takes, in every person and not only in those who have crossed a boundary. This thesis does not assert that, and asserts the state reading only for the reception under crisis; the wider claim is a genuine extension and is flagged here rather than assumed anywhere above. The two readings are not equivalent and they differ observably: on the wider reading, the five signatures of §4 should appear in attenuated form in non-crisis populations reporting spontaneous anticipatory impressions, whereas on the narrower reading they should be confined to boundary experiences. Running the §4 signature analysis against a non-crisis sample is a design, not a thought experiment, and a negative result would restrict the model rather than merely fail to extend it.

**Can determinacy be measured independently?** §9.5 records that the model's treatment of unconditional previews is unfalsifiable as currently stated, for want of any measure of determinacy that is not read off the outcome. A coding scheme is conceivable that classifies previewed events by whether the recipient had any causal access to them — one's own occupation and marriage on one side, a stranger's death or a geological event on the other — and the model makes a prediction under it: unconditional previews should concentrate among events the recipient could not have altered, and conditional ones among events dependent on the recipient's own choosing. The scheme would have to be built and validated before the prediction could be tested, and it is not built here.

**Can the class the archive cannot see be reached at all?** §7.3 and §8.4 describe a population structurally absent from every near-death archive: those whose crisis occurred before narrative encoding was possible, who therefore have nothing to submit. Archival methods cannot reach them by construction, but prospective methods can. Adults with documented early-childhood life-threatening events and no memory of a near-death experience could be compared against matched controls on frequency and character of recognition-type experience. The age gradient reported in §7.2 — 21.0% at ages 0–5 falling to 2.6% at 60 and over — predicts elevation in that group. A null result would count directly against §7.3. This is the sharpest test available of any proposal in this thesis, and it requires no corpus.

**Can veridical claims be registered before the fact?** Of 435 accounts asserting a specific future event, 40 are verified and 66 partially verified, and §3.3 states plainly that the remainder rest on the experiencer's word. The deficit is structural rather than accidental: retrospective self-report cannot distinguish a preview that came true from a memory reshaped by the outcome. Prospective registration — recording the content of a claimed preview at the time of report, before the event, with the record held independently — would resolve it for future cases. No archive currently does this. It is the single change to collection practice that would most improve the evidentiary standing of this entire literature.

## 10. Conclusion: The Future That Cannot Be Remembered Until It Arrives

The woman who could not remember her own future until it happened was not describing a failure. She was describing the shape of what she had received, accurately, in the only vocabulary available to her, and the sentence that sounds like a limitation is the single most informative statement in this corpus. A stored record can be read. A remembered scene can be recounted. What she carried out of that coma could be neither, and she said so plainly for four decades before anyone counted how many others were saying the same thing.

They were saying it in numbers. Across 6,753 accounts, 15.3% report knowledge of their personal future — a prevalence equal to the life review, with a fraction of its scholarly attention — and the marker that identifies them agrees with the experiencers' own answers at 100% sensitivity and κ = 0.672. Those accounts carry five structural features that recur with the regularity of a signature: the content arrives all at once, it arrives conditionally, it cannot be retrieved until the moment comes, it is bounded by what the recipient must do, and it is met at last by recognition rather than by memory. Each of the five looks, taken alone, like an anomaly requiring its own explanation. Taken together they are one thing described five ways.

That one thing is a state, perceived where there is no succession, and a state contains its own unfolding. Not because the future is stored and consulted, but because a ruling love is a direction and a direction is not separable from where it goes. To perceive a person whole is to perceive where that person is going, and nothing has to travel from a moment that has not happened, because there is no moment that has not happened — there is a condition, and conditions are occupied rather than located. The natural degree, which cannot receive anything except in extension and sequence, renders that condition as a scene: a red outfit, a grown daughter, a room with the family gathered. The scene is a true ultimate of the state. It is not a photograph, and the whole difficulty of this literature has come from reading it as one.

Read as a photograph, the phenomenon requires machinery, and the machinery is always borrowed. A brain that filters an awareness across a gap the framework denies exists, and that must then be given a second mechanism to seal what the first admitted. A four-dimensional block in which the previewed moment already sits, which fixes what the accounts insist is conditional, indexes what the accounts leave unindexed, and deletes the deliberation the previews are almost always about. Both imports arrive dressed in the authority of their home domains and neither is tested against the accounts they were fetched to explain. The framework needed neither. A premise stated in 1758 — that distance and duration in that world are states and not places — predicted six of seven discriminating features of testimony collected two and a half centuries later by people who had never heard of it, and predicted the strangest of them most exactly.

What remains undone is considerable and is stated in §9.5 rather than softened here, and what remains unasked is set out in §9.6 with the designs that would answer it. Three quarters of the veridical claims in this corpus rest on nobody's word but the claimant's. The archive is self-selected and the accounts are retrospective. The model's own most obvious phenomenological prediction is its weakest result. The unconditional previews are not explained. And the class of case the argument most naturally points toward — a boundary crisis early enough to leave no narrative at all, and nothing afterward but a lifelong disposition to meet arriving moments as already known — is by construction invisible to an archive that collects only what can be told.

Still, the shape of it holds. Nothing is retrieved, because nothing was filed. Nothing arrives from elsewhere, because there is no elsewhere it could come from. There is a state, seen whole, and then the long unfolding of that state into days — and at each arrival, the recognition that this was already known, and the same inability to say what comes next. The future is not remembered. It is recognized when it comes.
