# Source 010 — Greyson 2007: NDE accounts are not embellished over two decades

**Citation**: Bruce Greyson, "Consistency of Near-Death Experience Accounts over Two Decades: Are Reports Embellished over Time?" *Resuscitation* 73 (2007): 407–411. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2006.10.013. PMID 17289247.

**Accessed**: 2026-11-03. PubMed abstract read myself.

## What it says

- 72 NDE experiencers who had completed the NDE Scale in the 1980s (63% of the original cohort still alive) completed it again, "without reference to the original scale administration."
- "Mean scores did not change significantly on the total NDE scale, its 4 factors, or its 16 items." The scores on the two occasions correlated at P < 0.001.
- Score changes did not correlate with the time elapsed.
- "Contrary to expectation, accounts of near-death experiences, and particularly reports of their positive affect, were not embellished over a period of almost two decades."

## Why it matters

The content of a spontaneous experience, as measured, stays stable over twenty years. What is acquired later is the name and the framework (source 005). That separates the two layers this project draws: the experience, which holds still, and the literature that names it, which arrives afterwards and can add an address the experience did not give.

## Limits

The NDE Scale measures 16 features. It does not record every detail of an account, nor the interpretive labels an experiencer adopts. A stable scale score is compatible with an interpretation that changes.
