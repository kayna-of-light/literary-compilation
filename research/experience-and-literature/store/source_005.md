# Source 005 — Yuill 1991: years of silence, then reading supplied the name

**Citation**: LaVon E. Yuill, *Near-Death Experiences: An Exploration of Perceived Responses, Effects of Interventions, and Impact* (MS thesis, University of Arizona, 1991; AFIT/CI/CIA-91-080; DTIC ADA243469). archive.org `DTIC_ADA243469`.

**Accessed**: 2026-11-03. Full text read myself at pp. 82 and 138–139.

## What it says

- **Sample and method**: 8 adult NDE experiencers found by network sampling; interviews, content analysis.
- **Delay before full disclosure (p. 82).**
  - "The time span was less than one year for only one subject."
  - Two subjects took "approximately 1–2 years."
  - Two took "three years to find someone with whom to share it."
  - Two "indicated an 8–10 year time span."
  - "The third described ten years of bewilderment before finally reading an article that named the phenomenon, and another two years before finding someone to listen to full disclosure of her experience."
- **Reading after the experience.** "Five subjects stated they had read or 'scanned' at least one other book on NDEs" (p. 82).
- **"Enhancement of Understanding" (pp. 138–139).** "They gained information about the phenomenon, came to recognize that it was something that had a name, and learned what things other NDErs experienced." Two quotations:
  - "The biggest turning point was when I found the Reader's Digest article... it named it so then I could speak in proper terms."
  - "I finally knew what it was that had happened to me."
- **Validation.** "Validation was most effective between experients."

## Why it matters

This is direct evidence of the two-step pattern: the experience comes first and stays unnamed, sometimes for a decade; the vocabulary comes later, from reading and from other experiencers. The name is not part of the experience. It is supplied afterwards by a literature. The sample is very small (8), and the literature here is the NDE literature, not the contact literature. The pattern holds whichever literature supplies the name.
