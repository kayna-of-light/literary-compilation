# Source 002 — *The Diaries of Lewis Carroll*, ed. Roger Lancelyn Green

**Citation**: Carroll, Lewis. *The Diaries of Lewis Carroll.* Edited by Roger Lancelyn Green. 2 vols. London: Cassell, 1953. Reprint, Westport, CT: Greenwood Press, 1971. (The Greenwood reprint was the text searched.)
**Accessed**: 2026-10-23, archive.org FTS, items `diariesoflewisca0000lewi` (1971 reprint) and `diariesoflewisca0000edit` (vol. 2).

## Findings
- **No mention of Swedenborg** in either indexed volume (the items are indexed: "MacDonald" returns many hits). Lancelyn Green's edition is abridged; Wakeling's complete edition (10 vols, 1993–2008) was not searchable.
- **MacDonald** (editor's introduction): it was at Ore, in 1859, "that Hunt introduced him to George MacDonald, then living close by at Hastings". The introduction quotes Greville MacDonald (*George MacDonald and his Wife*, 1924): Dodgson "asked my mother to read his first Alice book to us [the MS. of Alice's Adventures Underground], just to see how we took it and thus to gauge its worth if published. . . . I remember that first reading well, and also my braggart avowal that I wished there were sixty thousand volumes of it!"
- **Maurice** (diary, 1863): "Feb: 21. (Sat). The prosecution of Jowett [for 'heresy'] is beginning to produce its shower of newspaper letters. Maurice has written one, in reply to Pusey, in which there was so much that I thought he had misunderstood, that I wrote him a long letter on the subject" — "Feb: 23. (M). Got a long, earnestly written, answer from Maurice." — "Feb: 24. (Tu). Wrote a second long letter to Maurice."
- **Maurice and MacDonald together** (later entry, undated in the probe): "On the 2nd, (Sunday), I went to Mr. Maurice's church (Vere Street): there was Communion, and as there seemed to be no one to help him, I sent him my card, and offered to help. This lucky accident led to my making his acquaintance: I went back to lunch with him and Mrs. Maurice, and after the afternoon service went to the MacDonalds, where I spent a most pleasant evening. We had some talk about his new novel David Elginbrod, which I have just finished reading."

## Why it matters
It documents the two channels that are real: MacDonald (friend from 1859, first audience of *Alice*) and Maurice (correspondence in 1863; Vere Street). It contains nothing on Swedenborg.
