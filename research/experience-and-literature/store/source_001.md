# Source 001 — Swedenborg moves one group of spirits from Jupiter to "a third earth in the starry heaven" (1748 → 1758)

**Citations**:
- Swedenborg, *The Spiritual Diary*, trans. Bush, Smithson and Buss, vol. 2 (London: James Speirs, 1883), §§1668–1674, entries dated 23 March 1748.
- Swedenborg, *Earths in the Universe*, Standard Edition (West Chester, PA: Swedenborg Foundation, 2009), §§148–151, under the heading "A third earth in the starry heaven, its spirits and inhabitants" (table of contents: "Third earth in the starry heaven (148–156)").

**Accessed**: 2026-11-03, from the text files extracted for the 2026-11-01 audit (the *Spiritual Diary* vol. 2 from the author's Drive; *Earths in the Universe* SE PDF from swedenborg.com).

## What the two texts say

**The *Diary*, 23 March 1748.**
- Heading: "[Spirits] unwilling to admit that they have ever been possessed of a body."
- §1668: "There is a class of spirits who have, during their bodily life, so despised the body that they come to regard it with hatred." Spirits of our earth trouble them, "whence ensues an appearance as if they represented serpents," which "twine round each of their arms."
- §1669: they appeared "in that of a cloud, in which the human form was scarcely discernible … as white clouds, with a rude and hardly perceptible resemblance to the human form." Then the inference: "I was hence persuaded, especially as they spake well of the spirits of the earth Jupiter, that they were, in fact, from thence."
- §1670: "But whether they may not have been from one of the satellites of Jupiter … I am not sure, though they intimated to me something of the kind; for as I could not have an idea of any sort of men except such as live on earths surrounded by atmospheres, therefore, although ignorant of the positive fact, yet I would not decidedly reject the supposition."
- §1672 (bracketed addition): "It seems probable, therefore, that they were born on the planet Jupiter, where some are of this character."
- §1673: "Their opinion during the life of the body had been that they had existed as spirits from eternity … they said that they now knew that they had not existed from eternity, but were born like others … the indications of their penitence … moved me much."

***Earths in the Universe*, 1758, "A third earth in the starry heaven."**
- §148: spirits at a distance unwilling to approach; spirits of our earth, approaching them, "appeared to themselves as if bound hand and foot with serpents," which Swedenborg explains by correspondence: "the spirits of our earth have reference in the greatest man to the external sense … and this sensual is represented in the other life by serpents."
- §149: "they appear before the eyes of other spirits, not like others, in a manifest human form, but like clouds, and for the most part like a dark cloud, in which is mingled something of a bright human appearance. But they said that they are white within." The men of their earth "make no account of their bodies, but only of the spirit in the body." "Many on their earth believe that the spirit of the body has been from eternity … But they added that they now know it is not so, and that they repent having been in such a false opinion."
- §§150–151: they set no value on earthly palaces; their sanctuaries are of living trees.

## Why it matters

The same description of a state appears in both texts:
- cloud-like appearance, white within;
- contempt for the body;
- serpents arising between them and the spirits of our earth;
- the belief that they had been spirits from eternity, and repentance of it.

The address changes:
- 1748: inferred as Jupiter;
- 1748, same day: doubted, perhaps a moon of Jupiter, "I am not sure";
- 1748, added note: settled as "probable … Jupiter";
- 1758: an earth in the starry heaven.

This happens inside Swedenborg's own writings, two centuries before the space probes and with no outside pressure from science. It shows directly that in his reports the quality of the spirits is the stable observation and the location is the variable explanation. It also shows the same pattern *The Selfhood of the Prophet* (`data/02_Swedenborgian_Theology/`, §3.5) documents for the date 1757: private uncertainty in the *Diary* ("I am not sure") becoming a confident placement in the published book.

## Cautions

- That the two passages describe the same group is a reading, not a statement Swedenborg makes. The match rests on four specific shared features listed above; it should be presented with those features so a reader can judge.
- *Earths in the Universe* was compiled from the interchapter sections of *Arcana Coelestia* (1749–1756). The *Arcana* sections corresponding to §§148–151 have not yet been located. They would date the move more precisely.
- Swedenborg does not explain the change. Nothing in either text says why the address moved.

## Connections

- *From Planets to Constellations* §4.2 already cites *Spiritual Diary* §§1669–1670. This source extends that point: the address that was "not sure" in 1748 is a star system in 1758.
- *The Selfhood of the Prophet* §3.6, "the compulsion to anchor continuous spiritual processes in discrete physical explanations": the planetary and stellar addresses are a spatial instance of the anchoring that §3.5 shows for time (1757).
