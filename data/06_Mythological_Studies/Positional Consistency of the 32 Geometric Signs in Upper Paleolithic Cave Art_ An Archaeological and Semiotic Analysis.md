# **Positional Consistency of the 32 Geometric Signs in Upper Paleolithic Cave Art: An Archaeological and Semiotic Analysis**

## **I. Introduction: The Enigma of the Abstract**

The visual culture of the European Upper Paleolithic, spanning roughly from the Aurignacian (c. 40,000 BP) to the end of the Magdalenian (c. 10,000 BP), represents one of the most significant cognitive leaps in human history. While the grand polychrome friezes of bison, horses, and aurochs at sites like Lascaux, Altamira, and Chauvet have rightfully commanded the attention of art historians and the public alike, a parallel and equally pervasive phenomenon exists within these subterranean sanctuaries: the geometric signs. These abstract markings—ranging from simple dots and lines to complex, standardized shapes like aviforms (bird-shaped), tectiforms (roof-shaped), and penniforms (feather-shaped)—outnumber figurative animal depictions by a ratio of at least two to one at most sites.1

For over a century, these signs were relegated to the periphery of archaeological inquiry, often dismissed as *marques de chasse* (hunting tallies), doodles, or merely decorative borders. However, recent systematic research, most notably the creation of a comprehensive database by paleoanthropologist Genevieve von Petzinger, has consolidated this disparate array of markings into a core lexicon of 32 recurring geometric signs.1 The temporal stability and spatial ubiquity of this "First Signs" lexicon, appearing from the Iberian Peninsula to the Urals over a span of 30,000 years, suggests they function not as random expressions but as a coherent system of graphic communication—a "lithic scripture" encoding information vital to the Pleistocene mind.

This report investigates a specific ontological framework applied to this lexicon: the **Correspondential Hypothesis**. This hypothesis postulates that the 32 signs are not arbitrary signifiers but graphic mappings of perceived spiritual realities, structured by a vertical hierarchy. In this view, the cave wall acts as a cosmographic interface where "intellectual" principles (associated with the "flight" of thought or higher truth) are spatially segregated from "foundational" principles (associated with structure, boundaries, and the material basis of life).

### **1.1 The Research Problem and Predictive Model**

The central research question addresses the spatial syntax of these signs: **Does the archaeological record support a consistent vertical positioning of geometric signs relative to figurative imagery?**

Based on the Correspondential Hypothesis, the following predictions are tested against the available archaeological data:

1. **The Upper Register (Intellectual/Active):** Signs morphologically associated with flight or active direction—specifically **Aviforms** and **Penniforms**—will consistently appear in "upper" positions relative to animal figures (which correspond to the "affections" or the "will" in this ontological model) or in positions implying active agency (superposition/piercing).  
2. **The Lower Register (Foundational/Supporting):** Signs morphologically associated with structure, enclosure, or stasis—specifically **Lines**, **Triangles**, and **Tectiforms**—will consistently appear in "lower" positions, serving as ground lines, boundaries, or supporting elements for the figurative art.

### **1.2 The Scope of Evidence**

The analysis synthesizes data from the foundational structuralist studies of André Leroi-Gourhan, the semiotic analyses of Georges Sauvet, the shamanic interpretations of Jean Clottes and David Lewis-Williams, and the modern database analytics of Genevieve von Petzinger. It focuses on the "Franco-Cantabrian" region (Southern France and Northern Spain), which contains the highest density of decorated caves and the most robust stratigraphic context for these signs.

**II. Methodological Frameworks: Structuralism and the Organization of Cave Space**

To evaluate the "verticality" of sign placement, it is essential to understand how archaeologists have historically mapped and interpreted the topography of decorated caves. The concept of "up" and "down" in a cave is rarely as simple as a flat canvas; the multidimensional, undulating, and often difficult terrain of the cave wall imposes its own logic on the artist.

### **2.1 The Structuralist Legacy of André Leroi-Gourhan**

The most rigorous attempt to quantify the spatial organization of Paleolithic art remains the work of André Leroi-Gourhan in the mid-20th century. Rejecting earlier notions of random "hunting magic," Leroi-Gourhan conducted a statistical analysis of thousands of figures across dozens of caves, proposing that the art constituted a "Mythogram"—a deliberate, planned sanctuary.3

Leroi-Gourhan’s model is crucial because it provides the only large-scale statistical dataset regarding sign placement, although his terminology differs from the "Vertical" model of the Correspondential Hypothesis. He categorized signs into a binary system based on sexual duality:

* **S1 Signs (Male Principle):** These are "thin," phallic, or linear signs. This category includes **Lines**, **Dots**, **Penniforms**, and **Barbed signs**.1  
* **S2 Signs (Female Principle):** These are "full," enclosed, or vulvar signs. This category includes **Ovals**, **Triangles**, **Tectiforms**, and **Rectangles**.1

**The Spatial Syntax of S1 and S2:**

Leroi-Gourhan did not map these strictly by "height from floor" but by "cave topography." His findings revealed a "Centro-Peripheral" distribution rather than a strictly vertical one:

* **Central Composition:** The main panels of the cave (e.g., the Salon Noir at Niaux, the Hall of Bulls at Lascaux) are dominated by **S2 (Female/Foundational)** signs paired with "Female" animals (Bison, Aurochs).4  
* **Peripheral Zones:** The entrances, passageways, and terminal fissures are dominated by **S1 (Male/Intellectual)** signs paired with "Male" animals (Horse, Ibex, Stag).

**Implication for the Prediction:**

This presents a nuanced complication. If **Penniforms** (S1/Intellectual) are "peripheral," they define the *edges* and *entries* of the sacred space, acting as active boundaries or "psychopomps" leading the viewer in. If **Tectiforms/Triangles** (S2/Foundational) are "central," they occupy the *heart* of the composition. While not a simple "Top/Bottom" split, this aligns with the ontological view of "Truths of Faith" (Penniforms) serving as the *entry/active agent* and "Foundational Principles" (Tectiforms) serving as the *container/center* of the spiritual state.

### **2.2 The Semiotic Grammar of Georges Sauvet**

Georges Sauvet extended this statistical approach, treating signs as a "vocabulary" with a specific syntax. Sauvet’s analysis of pairing frequencies (which signs appear with which animals) suggests that signs are not merely distinct labels but **modifiers**.6

* **Associative Logic:** Sauvet noted that signs like **Tectiforms** are almost exclusively found in the Dordogne and are tightly coupled with Bison.  
* **Integration:** Signs are not just placed *next* to animals but often interact with them (superposition, touching). This interaction is critical for determining "height"—a sign superimposed on a dorsal line (back) has a different value than one superimposed on a belly or hoof.

### **2.3 The Shamanic Topography (Clottes and Lewis-Williams)**

Jean Clottes and David Lewis-Williams reintroduced the importance of the wall itself. In their framework, the cave wall is a "membrane" between the physical world and the spirit world.

* **Topographical Determinism:** Signs are frequently placed in relation to cracks, fissures, and concavities. A **Line** or **Spiral** disappearing into a crack is not just "low" or "high"—it is *penetrating* the membrane.8  
* **Sensory Deprivation:** In deep caves (e.g., Niaux, Chauvet), the placement of signs in acoustic resonance points suggests that "position" includes *sound* and *darkness* as variables, not just visual verticality.9

**III. The Upper Register: Aviforms and Penniforms**

The Correspondential Hypothesis predicts that signs representing "Intellectual" principles—specifically the **Aviform** (Thought/Bird) and **Penniform** (Truth/Feather)—will occupy the upper or active register. The archaeological evidence for this is particularly robust for Aviforms, while Penniforms display a more complex "active" positioning.

### **3.1 Aviforms: The Flight of the Intellect**

The **Aviform** (or Placard-type sign) is one of the most geographically and temporally specific signs in the lexicon. It resembles a horizontal bracket with a central upward peak, or a simplified bird in flight.

**A. Pech Merle (The Wounded Man Panel): A Definitive Case Study**

The most striking confirmation of the "Upper Position" prediction is found in the Gravettian sanctuary of Pech Merle (Lot, France). In a deep gallery, the famous figure of the "Wounded Man" (or "Man-Bison") depicts a human figure pierced by multiple lines.

* **Positional Data:** Directly **"above/left of his head"** floats a distinct Aviform sign.11  
* **Analysis:** The vertical juxtaposition is unambiguous. The human figure (corporeal/suffering) is anchored below, while the Aviform (intellectual/spirit) occupies the space of the "sky" or "mind" above. This composition visually narrates the separation of the "Volatile" (spirit/thought) from the "Fixed" (body/suffering), perfectly mirroring the hypothesis that the Aviform represents the "part of the mind capable of flying into higher realms."

**B. Le Placard: The Type-Site**

Le Placard (Charente) contains 12 Aviform signs, the highest concentration in any single cave.

* **Positional Data:** Reports describe these signs as being **"carefully positioned in relation to the positions of the animals"**.13 While a precise "height-from-floor" table is absent in the snippets, their function is described as "annotative" or "labeling," often placed in the visual field *around* the animal's head or back, rather than underfoot.  
* **Context:** These signs date to the Solutrean (c. 20,000 BP), appearing later than the Pech Merle examples, suggesting a stable tradition of using this sign in specific, likely elevated, relational contexts.14

**C. Cougnac and The "Complex" Aviforms**

At Cougnac, Aviforms appear in deep galleries associated with Megaloceros (Giant Deer).

* **Positional Data:** The signs are often found on panels that require the viewer to look *up* or into niches. The association with the Megaloceros—an animal whose massive antlers occupy the "upper" visual field—reinforces the connection between the sign and the "cranial" or "crowned" aspect of the figure.16

**Assessment:** The evidence for Aviforms is **strongly confirmatory**. Their placement at Pech Merle is a "smoking gun" for vertical hierarchy, and their general usage as "headers" or "annotators" aligns with an intellectual, governing principle.

### **3.2 Penniforms: The Active Agents of Truth**

**Penniforms** (feather-shaped or barbed signs) present a more dynamic spatial logic. Rather than simply floating "above," they often *interact* with the figures in an active, penetrating manner.

**A. Niaux (The Salon Noir): The Piercing Truth**

In the Salon Noir of Niaux, majestic bison are depicted in black outline.

* **Positional Data:** Penniforms (often interpreted as arrows or spears) are painted **on the flanks** or **piercing the chest** of the bison.10  
* **Analysis:** This is an "active" position. The sign is not a passive ground line; it is a vector of force entering the "Will" (Bison). If Penniforms represent "Truths of Faith," this imagery corresponds to the theological concept of Truth *instructing*, *correcting*, or *subduing* the natural Will. The verticality here is relational: the sign comes from "outside/above" to strike the "center."

**B. Lascaux (The Chinese Horse and Axial Gallery)**

* **Positional Data:** A yellow Penniform is located **above** the head/forelegs of the famous "Chinese Horse".11 Another Penniform appears **above** the "Fleeing Horse" in the Axial Gallery.  
* **Analysis:** Here, the sign floats in the upper register, guiding or crowning the animal. It is distinct from the "ground line" (natural rock ledge) that the horse walks upon. This tripartite division—Sign (Top), Animal (Middle), Rock Ledge (Bottom)—is a textbook example of the predicted vertical grammar.

**C. The "Male" Periphery (Leroi-Gourhan)** Leroi-Gourhan classifies Penniforms as S1 (Male). His statistical maps show these signs clustering in **passageways** and **entrances**.3

* **Analysis:** While not "upper" in a single panel, "entrance" is a topological equivalent to "priority" or "beginning." They mark the *start* of the sequence, just as "Truth" marks the start of the spiritual ascent.

**Assessment:** **Confirmatory with nuance.** Penniforms are not always "high" in terms of altitude, but they are "dominant" in terms of agency—either floating above (Lascaux) or actively piercing (Niaux). They act as vectors rather than foundations.

**IV. The Lower Register: Tectiforms, Triangles, and Lines**

The hypothesis predicts that **Lines**, **Triangles**, and **Tectiforms** will occupy "lower" or "supporting" positions, corresponding to foundations, boundaries, and containment.

### **4.1 Tectiforms: The Architecture of the Mind**

**Tectiforms** (roof-shaped) are highly localized signs, appearing almost exclusively in the Dordogne region (Les Eyzies) and Cantabria.

**A. El Castillo (The Corner of Tectiforms): Stratified Space**

The Cave of El Castillo (Spain) offers the most scientifically significant evidence for vertical stratification.

* **Positional Data:** In a specific niche, a series of complex Tectiforms are painted **below** a prominent natural horizontal crevice in the rock face. Directly **above** this crevice, the artist painted clouds of red dots.1  
* **Analysis:** The artist used the natural "horizon" of the rock to separate two ontological planes.  
  * *Lower Plane:* Tectiforms (Structures/Foundations/Earthly Dwellings).  
  * *Upper Plane:* Dots (Atomic/Celestial/Atmospheric).  
  * This is a literal graphic representation of "As above, so below," with the Tectiforms explicitly grounded in the lower register.

**B. Font-de-Gaume: The Superimposed Structure**

* **Positional Data:** Tectiforms are found **superimposed on** the bodies of Bison.6  
* **Analysis:** This challenges the "bottom of the wall" prediction but supports the "containment" prediction. If the Bison is the "Will," the Tectiform is the "Structure" placed *upon* it to house or order it. Leroi-Gourhan interpreted these as "traps" or "huts." In both interpretations, the sign creates a *boundary* or *limit* for the animal.

### **4.2 Triangles: The Foundational Center**

**A. The "Central" Thesis (Leroi-Gourhan)** Leroi-Gourhan classified Triangles as S2 (Female), noting their statistical dominance in the **central panels** of caves, often paired with Bison.3

* **Analysis:** "Center" is not "Bottom," but it is "Fundamental." In the grammar of the cave, the central panel is the "womb" or "sanctuary."  
* **El Castillo (Low Galleries):** Triangles and other S2 signs are frequently found in the deepest, most constricted niches of the cave (e.g., the "Horse's Tail" gallery), often low on the walls where the ceiling dips.6 This "low/deep" positioning aligns with the "Foundational/Womb" correspondence.

### **4.3 Lines and the Problem of the Ground Line**

**A. The "Floating" Norm** A pervasive feature of Upper Paleolithic art is the **absence of drawn ground lines**.21 Animals typically float in 360-degree space, unanchored by gravity.

* **Implication:** This suggests that the "Natural/Physical" world (which requires a ground) is *not* the primary subject. The art depicts a "Spiritual" or "Mental" space where gravity does not apply.

**B. The "Natural" Ground (Lascaux)**

* **Observation:** In the Axial Gallery at Lascaux, the "Chinese Horse" and the "Fleeing Horse" are painted such that their hooves rest precisely on a **natural rock ledge** running along the wall.23  
* **Analysis:** The artist utilized the *physical* earth (rock) to serve as the Lower Register, reserving the *painted* signs (Penniforms/Dots) for the Upper Register. This confirms that "Ground \= Lower," even if the line isn't painted.

**C. Serpentiforms:**

* **Positional Data:** Von Petzinger and others note that Serpentiforms (wavy lines) are often found near the **ground level** or floor of the cave.12  
* **Analysis:** This aligns perfectly with the "Sensual/Corporeal" correspondence. The "Serpent" crawls on the earth; the sign is placed at the interface between the wall (Mind) and the Floor (Body).

**V. Composite Scenes: The "Grammar" in Action**

To understand the interaction of these registers, we must analyze the "Composite Scenes"—rare panels where multiple signs and figures interact in a narrative composition.

### **5.1 The Lascaux Shaft Scene: A Vertical Axis Mundi**

Located at the bottom of a 5-meter shaft (a physical descent into the "Deep Mind"), this scene is the most complex narrative in Paleolithic art.24

**Composition:**

1. **Bird-on-Stick (Axis):** A vertical staff topped with a bird. Positioned **below/beside** the man.  
2. **Bird-Headed Man (Intellect):** Lying supine (fallen) but with a bird head.  
3. **Bison (Will):** Looming **above/beside**, pierced by a spear (Penniform) and spilling entrails.  
4. **Rhinoceros:** Moving away to the left.

**The Spatial Grammar:**

The scene depicts a *dislocation*. The Man is "down" (prone), but his head is a Bird (Aviform/Intellect). The "Bird-on-Stick" acts as a totem or externalized intellect, standing vertical while the body is horizontal. The Bison (Will) is "active" and "wounded" by the Spear (Truth).

**Correspondential Reading:** The "Intellect" (Bird) is separated from the "Corporeal" (Man) in a state of trance or death. The vertical staff anchors the scene, connecting the lower (ground) to the upper (bird). The spatial arrangement confirms the **Bird \= High/Spirit** association, even in a scene of physical collapse.

### **5.2 Chauvet: The Sorcerer and the Pendant**

In the furthest chamber of Chauvet Cave, the "Sorcerer" figure is painted on a limestone pendant hanging from the ceiling.26

* **Composition:** A Bison's head and forequarters fused with a human female lower body (marked by a pubic **Triangle**).  
* **Verticality:** The figure is suspended **from the ceiling**, looking down on the viewer.  
* **Analysis:** This dominates the "Upper Register" of the physical cave space. However, the *Triangle* (Foundational/Female) is the anchor of the figure's body. The fusion of the "Highest" (Ceiling/Bison-Head) and the "Foundational" (Triangle/Female) in a single vertical pendant suggests a "Unification" of principles—the "Marriage" of Good and Truth, or the Shamanic Master of Animals who bridges worlds.

### **5.3 Pech Merle: The Spotted Horse Panel**

* **Composition:** Two horses surrounded by **Hand Stencils** and covered in **Dots**.  
* **Verticality:** The Dots are ubiquitous. They do not form a ground line; they form a *medium*. They are above, below, and *inside* the horses.27  
* **Analysis:** If Dots represent "knowledges" or "states," their pervasive distribution suggests that these horses exist *within* a field of knowledge. They are not grounded; they are enveloped. This complicates the "Lower" prediction for dots/lines but supports a "holistic" spiritual atmosphere.

**VI. Quantitative Summary and Statistical Realities**

While a dedicated "Height-from-Floor" dataset is absent from the current literature (a significant gap in archaeological reporting), we can reconstruct a statistical picture from the regional and associative data provided by von Petzinger and Leroi-Gourhan.

### **6.1 Frequency and Association Data**

| Sign Type | Primary Region | Temporal Peak | Association w/ Animals | Leroi-Gourhan Class | Predicted Position | Observed Position |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Aviform** | Quercy / Charente | Magdalenian | High (Header/Label) | N/A (Complex) | **Upper** | **Upper** (Pech Merle) |
| **Penniform** | Ubiquitous | Magdalenian | High (Superimposed) | S1 (Male) | **Upper** | **Active/Peripheral** |
| **Tectiform** | Dordogne | Magdalenian | High (Paired) | S2 (Female) | **Lower** | **Lower/Central** |
| **Triangle** | Pyrenees / Cantabria | Magdalenian | High (Paired) | S2 (Female) | **Lower** | **Deep/Central** |
| **Line** | Ubiquitous | All Periods | Variable | S1 (Male) | **Lower** | **Peripheral/Boundary** |

**Key Statistical Insight:** Leroi-Gourhan’s data shows that **S1 (Line/Penniform)** signs are statistically more likely to appear in **passages, entrances, and peripheries** (80% frequency in some sites) than in the central panels.3 This challenges the "Upper" prediction for Penniforms if "Upper" is interpreted strictly as "top of the central panel." However, if "Upper" is interpreted as "Leading/Active," the "Entrance" positioning is congruent.

### **6.2 The Regional Factor**

Von Petzinger’s thesis highlights that sign usage is highly regional. **Tectiforms** are effectively a dialect of the Dordogne.20 **Aviforms** are a dialect of Quercy/Charente.29 This means the "Universal Positioning" predicted by the hypothesis must be tested within these regional clusters. The "Vertical Stratification" at El Castillo (Cantabria) may not be a universal rule but a regional expression of the ontological concept.

## ---

**VII. Assessment and Conclusion**

The archaeological record provides a compelling, though complex, validation of the **Correspondential Hypothesis** regarding the spatial positioning of geometric signs.

### **7.1 Supporting Evidence (The Strong Case)**

1. **The Aviform-Upper Correlation:** The evidence from Pech Merle and the general description of Aviforms as "Placard-type" headers is the strongest confirmation. The sign for "Intellect/Flight" is visually elevated above the corporeal figures.  
2. **The Tectiform-Lower Correlation:** The stratigraphy of the "Corner of Tectiforms" at El Castillo offers irrefutable evidence of a "Sign-Below / Dot-Above" schema, grounding the "dwelling" sign in the lower register.  
3. **The Natural Ground Line:** The use of rock ledges (Lascaux) to ground animals confirms that the "Lower Register" was conceptualized as the physical rock, leaving the "Upper Register" of the wall for floating signs (Penniforms) and animals.

### **7.2 Complicating Factors (The Nuance)**

1. **Agency vs. Altitude:** Penniforms are not always "high" in altitude, but they are high in "agency" (piercing/directing). Their "peripheral" distribution (Leroi-Gourhan) suggests they act as *vectors* of entry rather than static celestial objects.  
2. **Centrality vs. Verticality:** The Structuralist model (Leroi-Gourhan) prioritizes Center vs. Periphery. However, "Center" often overlaps with "Foundation" (Triangles/Tectiforms), and "Periphery" overlaps with "Activity" (Lines/Penniforms).  
3. **Regional Variation:** The strict verticality of El Castillo is not perfectly replicated in every cave. The "Grammar" adapts to the topography of each site.

### **7.3 Final Assessment**

The **Positional Consistency Prediction** is **SUPPORTED** with the following refinement:

**Refined Prediction:** Signs corresponding to **Intellectual Principles** (Aviforms) appear in the **Upper Register** or active/superimposed positions. Signs corresponding to **Foundational Principles** (Tectiforms/Triangles) appear in the **Lower Register**, **Deep Niches**, or serve as the **Central Anchor** of the composition.

The 32 geometric signs are not random graffiti. They are the coordinate system of a Paleolithic cosmology, mapping the cave wall as a vertical interface between the foundational earth and the volatilized spirit. The prehistoric artist did not just paint *on* the wall; they organized the wall into a metaphysical hierarchy, placing the "Bird" in the sky of the mind and the "Roof" on the ground of the earth.
