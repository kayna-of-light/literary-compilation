# Source 020 — New Thought as a second route in Stanchfield's era

**Citations**:
- Judah, J. Stillson. *The History and Philosophy of the Metaphysical Movements in America.* Philadelphia: Westminster Press, 1967.
- Albanese, Catherine L. *A Republic of Mind and Spirit: A Cultural History of American Metaphysical Religion.* New Haven: Yale University Press, 2007.
- Larson, Martin A. *New Thought, or, A Modern Religious Approach.* New York: Philosophical Library, 1985.
- Stanchfield, *Gesture Drawing for Animation* (handouts), "The Metaphysical Side".

**Accessed**: 2026-10-24 (archive.org full-text search for the three histories; the Stanchfield handouts read in full).

## Findings

- **Swedenborg in New Thought's descent.** Warren Felt Evans, "a follower of Swedenborg", wrote the first New Thought books (Judah; Albanese). Larson begins his history of New Thought with Swedenborg; *A Cultural History* §3.2 quotes him calling Swedenborg "the grandfather of New Thought".
- **Stanchfield.** "The Metaphysical Side" opens on "certain 'new thought' religions" that "have a positive theme". It speaks of "the creative energy that created the Universe, created you and its creative power is in you now" and "you can not break a spiritual law".

## Why it matters

New Thought is a second documented route from Swedenborg into the studio's teaching, in Stanchfield's era (1970s–2000). It is separate from the expression tradition and does not explain Graham's 1937 method. The thesis mentions it as a parallel route only.

## No record

No record ties Stanchfield to Science of Mind or Ernest Holmes. The eBay and Scribd entries that carried that claim supported nothing.
