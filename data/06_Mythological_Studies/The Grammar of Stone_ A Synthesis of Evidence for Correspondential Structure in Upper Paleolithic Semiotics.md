## Abstract

**Background**: Genevieve von Petzinger's survey of 367 European Upper Paleolithic cave sites documented 32 distinct geometric signs that remained stable across 30,000 years and at least four cultural periods. Two-thirds of these signs were already present when anatomically modern humans arrived in Europe, indicating African origin. This stability — spanning millennia without institutional transmission, writing, or centralized authority — constitutes a genuine archaeological paradox that existing models (entoptic phosphenes, proto-writing, mnemonic devices) fail to resolve individually.

**Thesis**: This paper synthesizes six companion studies that propose and test a fourth model: the 32 signs are graphic expressions of perceived ontological structure — what the Swedenborgian tradition calls *correspondences*. Their stability reflects not cultural transmission but the stability of the reality they depict. Three directional predictions derived from this hypothesis were tested against archaeological data: positional consistency within caves, cross-cultural isomorphism across isolated populations, and climatic correlation with protective sign frequency. All three were supported. The same structural regularities that characterize the sign system — bounded inventories, constant state with variable form, vertical gradients, entity autonomy — appear independently across multiple empirical domains outside archaeology, strengthening the inference that the patterns reflect perceived structure rather than cultural convention.

**Conclusions**: The correspondential model provides a unified explanation for features that require three separate models under competing frameworks. The pattern-level predictions are empirically confirmed. The meaning-level interpretations remain speculative but internally consistent. The data are best explained by a model in which Paleolithic artists depicted consistent structure in reality, not culturally contingent symbols.

> **[CORRECTION #26]**: The pattern-level confirmation and “best explained” conclusion are retracted. The positional dataset was absent, the cross-cultural comparison did not discriminate shared meaning from expected geometric convergence, and the climatic test assigned meanings before testing frequencies. See *The Mountain and the Pillar*, §§3–4 and 6.8.

**Keywords**: Upper Paleolithic, geometric signs, Swedenborg, correspondences, cave art, von Petzinger, entoptic, cross-cultural, vastation, NDE

## I. The Paradox

Thirty-two geometric signs recur across 367 cave sites spanning the entire Upper Paleolithic — from the Aurignacian arrival in Europe around 40,000 BP to the Magdalenian florescence at 12,000 BP. This is Genevieve von Petzinger's central finding, published in *The First Signs* (2016) and based on systematic survey of the European cave art record.

The stability of this inventory is the problem. These signs persisted for 30,000 years without writing, without institutional priesthoods, without any mechanism of codification that we can identify. Individual cave art traditions rose and fell. Animal depictions shifted in style and subject. Regional differences in figurative art are well documented. Yet the geometric repertoire remained. Lines, dots, spirals, zigzags, crosshatches, tectiforms, aviforms — the same forms appear in Chauvet (36,000 BP) and in Niaux (13,000 BP), separated by 23,000 years.

Two features of this stability demand explanation:

**First, the inventory is bounded.** Von Petzinger identified 32 signs, not 320. If these were arbitrary cultural marks, the set should have grown over 30,000 years as new populations invented new symbols. It didn't. The system is closed.

> **[CORRECTION #26]**: Bounded recurrence is the valid finding. “Exactly thirty-two,” semantic stability, and a closed system do not follow: the count depends on how forms are split, and the regional distributions show change as well as continuity.

**Second, the inventory is selective.** These 32 signs are not exhaustive of the geometric forms available in nature. Countless shapes could have been chosen. These particular 32 were chosen, and chosen consistently, across cultures that had no demonstrable contact. This selectivity implies constraint — something limiting the options to precisely these forms.

The question is: what constrains them?

## II. Competing Models and Their Limits

Three models have been proposed, each capturing part of the phenomenon:

### The Entoptic Model

Lewis-Williams and Dowson proposed in 1988 that geometric signs derive from entoptic phenomena — the phosphene patterns generated by the human visual system during altered states of consciousness. Since all human nervous systems produce similar phosphenes, the global recurrence of dots, zigzags, grids, and spirals is explained by shared neurology.

This model explains the universals but not the specifics. Phosphenes produce an effectively unlimited range of geometric forms; they do not constrain the repertoire to 32 and no more. The entoptic model predicts convergence on a general class of geometric forms, not on a specific bounded inventory. It also fails to explain why some signs (tectiforms, aviforms) appear only regionally — if the source is universal neurology, all signs should appear everywhere.

### The Proto-Writing Model

Marshack and later researchers proposed that the signs constitute a notational or proto-writing system — marks carrying encoded meaning that could be "read" by initiated members of the culture. This explains boundedness (systems have fixed symbol sets) and selectivity (each symbol carries specific meaning).

But proto-writing requires institutional transmission — teachers, learners, standards of correctness. Over 30,000 years without writing, any notational system should undergo significant drift, as the mechanism of correction (written reference) does not exist. The stability of the 32 signs far exceeds what cultural transmission alone can maintain. Aboriginal Australian songlines preserve accurate geographical memory for 7,000–12,000 years, which is impressive, but even these traditions show regional variation and adaptive change. A proto-writing system stable for 30,000 years across populations with no contact is without precedent.

### The Mnemonic Model

Lynne Kelly's "Memory Code" hypothesis proposes that ancient monuments and art served as mnemonic devices — physical anchors for orally transmitted knowledge systems. This explains the relationship between art and place (cave sites as "memory palaces") and the durability of the associated knowledge.

But the mnemonic model addresses function, not form. It explains *why* signs were placed in caves but not *why these particular signs*. Any set of distinctive marks could serve as mnemonic anchors. The constraint on the repertoire — why 32 and not some other number — remains unexplained.

### The Composite Problem

Each model explains one feature:

| Feature | Entoptic | Proto-Writing | Mnemonic |
|:--------|:---------|:--------------|:---------|
| **Universal recurrence** | ✅ | ❌ | ❌ |
| **Bounded inventory** | ❌ | ✅ | ❌ |
| **30,000-year stability** | ❌ | ❌ | Partial |
| **Selective repertoire** | ❌ | ✅ | ❌ |
| **Contextual deployment** | ❌ | Partial | ❌ |

No single model accounts for all five features. A unified explanation is needed.

## III. The Correspondential Hypothesis

The Swedenborgian doctrine of correspondences proposes that every natural object exists as an expression of a spiritual reality — not by cultural convention but by functional participation. Light corresponds to truth because of what light *does*: it enables the eye to distinguish forms, as truth enables the intellect to distinguish ideas. Water corresponds to knowledge because of how water *behaves*: it flows, adapts to containers, sustains life, as knowledge flows between minds, fills capacity, sustains intellectual development. The relationship is grounded in the physics and function of the object, not assigned by a codebook.

Applied to the 32 signs, this hypothesis generates a specific claim: the signs are stable because they depict stable realities. The inventory is bounded because the ontological structure they map is bounded. The signs are selective because they correspond to specific features of that structure, not to arbitrary cultural choices. Their persistence across cultures requires no institutional transmission mechanism because the mechanism is not transmission at all — it is independent perception of the same underlying structure.

The companion paper "The 32 Geometric Signs as Correspondential Vocabulary" maps each of the 32 signs to specific correspondences organized in five categories: Dynamics of Influx (spirals, waves, serpentiforms), Architecture of Mind (tectiforms, quadrilaterals), Intellectual Principle (aviforms, penniforms, scalariforms), Agents of Power (circles, ovals, animal-related forms), and Signs of Structure (lines, dots, crosshatches, grids). These mappings are derived from Swedenborgian principles applied to the functional nature of each geometric form.

This mapping is explicitly speculative at the individual-sign level. No single sign-to-correspondence assignment can be independently verified from the archaeological record alone. The framework's strength lies not in individual mappings but in the *structural predictions* it generates — predictions about how the signs should behave as a system if the hypothesis is correct. Three such predictions were formulated and tested.

## IV. Three Empirical Tests

### Test 1: Positional Consistency

**Prediction**: If the signs map ontological structure organized by discrete degrees (celestial above, natural below), then "intellectual" signs (aviforms, penniforms) should consistently appear in upper or active positions within cave compositions, while "foundational" signs (tectiforms, triangles, lines) should occupy lower, containing, or anchoring positions.

**Data**: The companion paper "Positional Consistency of the 32 Geometric Signs in Upper Paleolithic Cave Art" examined spatial relationships at major decorated sites including Pech Merle, Le Placard, Cougnac, El Castillo, Lascaux, Niaux, and Chauvet.

**Results**: The prediction is supported with refinement. Aviforms are strongly confirmatory — at Pech Merle, an aviform floats directly above and left of the "Wounded Man's" head; at Le Placard, twelve aviforms are concentrated as "headers" around animal panels; at Cougnac, aviforms occupy positions requiring the viewer to look upward. Tectiforms consistently occupy lower, central, or containing positions — at El Castillo's "Corner of Tectiforms," they are painted below a natural rock crevice while red dots appear above, producing a literal graphic hierarchy.

The refinement concerns penniforms, which demonstrate "agency" (superimposition on, or penetration of, animal figures) rather than strict vertical elevation. At Niaux's Salon Noir, penniforms pierce bison flanks — an active, interventional position rather than a high one. This is consistent with the correspondential reading (penniforms as directed intellectual force) but requires redefining the prediction from "upper position" to "active or superimposed position."

**Limitation**: No systematic height-from-floor dataset exists across the full 367-site corpus. The evidence is drawn from qualitative descriptions of specific sites, not from quantitative spatial measurement. This is a genuine gap in the archaeological literature, not a limitation unique to this study.

> **[CORRECTION #26]**: This limitation prevents the claimed test from being performed. Selected sites may illustrate a reading, but they cannot validate a general positional grammar; redefining “upper” as active or superimposed after observing the cases is post hoc.

### Test 2: Cross-Cultural Isomorphism

**Prediction**: If the signs map ontological realities perceived by the human cognitive system universally, then isolated populations with no cultural contact should independently produce the same core lexicon. Regional "dialects" may exist for complex signs, but the foundational inventory should converge.

**Data**: The companion paper "Cross-Cultural Isomorphism of Geometric Signs" examined geometric sign systems from four regions separated by ocean barriers and thousands of years:

- **Africa** (the source inventory): Blombos Cave (75,000–100,000 BP) with the primordial crosshatch; Diepkloof Rock Shelter (60,000 BP) with standardized geometric marking
- **Sulawesi** (the control group, separated by deep ocean): Leang Timpuseng hand stencils (39,900+ BP); Leang Bulu' Sipong scalariforms (37,000–35,000 BP)
- **Australia** (Sahul, colonized ~65,000 BP): Panaramitee tradition with circles, concentric circles, cupules, arcs
- **Amazonia**: Serranía de la Lindosa (12,600 BP) with scalariforms, zigzags, grids, concentric circles

**Results**: A universal core of approximately 50–60% of signs (lines, dots, hand stencils, grids, zigzags, scalariforms, circles) appears globally across all four regions. The scalariform's independent appearance in Europe, Sulawesi, and Amazonia — populations separated by oceans and tens of thousands of years — is particularly striking.

Complex signs show regional distribution: tectiforms are concentrated in Dordogne, France; aviforms cluster in Franco-Cantabrian sites. Australian traditions substitute "tracks" for aviforms — arguably the same functional concept (movement, agency) expressed through a different morphological form adapted to the local ecology. The study proposes a three-tier classification:

| Tier | Signs | Distribution |
|:-----|:------|:-------------|
| **Universal** | Line, Dot, Crosshatch, Zigzag, Hand Stencil | All regions |
| **Convergent** | Scalariform, Circle, Spiral | Multiple independent regions |
| **Regional Dialect** | Tectiform, Aviform | Geographically restricted |

**Clarification on regional signs**: The absence of tectiforms in Sulawesi or aviforms in Australia does not challenge the ontological claim in the way one might initially suppose. The test is not "do all signs appear everywhere?" but "when a sign is used, does its correspondential meaning fit the context — and is it absent only where the context does not demand it?" Tectiforms concentrate in Dordogne during the Magdalenian precisely because Dordogne was the demographic refugium during Heinrich Event 1 — a context demanding structured containment. Their absence in Sulawesi is unremarkable if the Sulawesi context did not call for that function. Similarly, Australian traditions substitute "tracks" for aviforms — the function (directed movement, agency) is present; the morphological form adapts to local ecology. This is variable form of a constant state, which is what the framework predicts.

The entoptic model explains the Tier 1 universals, since dots, lines, and zigzags are known phosphene forms. The correspondential model's advantage is that it additionally explains the Tier 2 convergent signs (scalariforms, circles), the boundedness of the total inventory, and the contextual specificity of deployment — features the entoptic model does not predict.

> **[CORRECTION #26]**: This test is non-discriminating. Simple forms have a strong neurovisual and motor null, while morphological mismatches were reclassified as “dialects” or equivalent functions. Resemblance across mixed corpora does not establish common meanings.

### Test 3: Vastation Markers

**Prediction**: If the signs map perceived spiritual states, then periods of intense climatic or social stress should see spikes in "protective" signs (tectiforms as structured containment) and "ordering" signs (crosshatches, grids as imposed coherence). The Swedenborgian term for this is *vastation* — a period of spiritual crisis in which old structures dissolve and new ones consolidate.

**Data**: The companion paper "The Vastation Markers Hypothesis" analyzed von Petzinger's quantitative dataset against paleoclimatic records, focusing on the Heinrich Event 1 crisis (18,000–14,700 BP) — the most extreme climatic disruption in 70,000 years.

**Results**: The prediction is strongly supported:

| Sign | Aurignacian | Gravettian | Solutrean | **Magdalenian** |
|:-----|:------------|:-----------|:----------|:----------------|
| **Tectiform** | 0% | 14.3% | 14.3% | **58.8%** |
| **Crosshatch** | 3.7% | 3.7% | 3.7% | **81.5%** |

Nearly 60% of all tectiform sites and over 80% of all crosshatch sites are Magdalenian — the period directly corresponding to Heinrich Event 1 and its aftermath. During this crisis, Atlantic thermohaline circulation collapsed, sea surface temperatures off Iberia plummeted to 70,000-year lows, and northern European populations were compressed into the Franco-Cantabrian refugium.

The correlation is specific: the Aurignacian-to-Solutrean percentages are flat (no progressive increase), then the Magdalenian shows a massive spike. This matches the paleoclimatic record — the Last Glacial Maximum (Solutrean) was cold but stable; Heinrich Event 1 (Magdalenian onset) was cold and *volatile*. The signs respond to crisis, not to cold per se.

Regional differentiation is also informative: Dordogne caves concentrate tectiforms (the "containment" strategy) while Cantabrian caves concentrate grids and quadrilaterals (the "ordering" strategy). Different regions deployed different signs from the correspondential vocabulary, suggesting functional deployment rather than arbitrary marking.

The system's dissolution is equally telling: after the Younger Dryas (12,900–11,700 BP), caves were abandoned and art shifted to Azilian painted pebbles — simplified, portable, lacking tectiforms. The "Ark" system dissolved when the crisis passed.

**Limitation**: The Magdalenian concentration could reflect the fact that the Magdalenian simply has more documented sites or better preservation than earlier periods. The study does not fully control for site-count bias. Additionally, the Solutrean (also under extreme cold) shows only moderate tectiform presence (14.3%), and the post-hoc distinction between "stable cold" and "volatile cold" was not part of the original prediction.

> **[CORRECTION #26]**: These are not secondary qualifications; together with the prior assignment of “protection” and “order,” they invalidate the claimed semantic test. Period association may remain descriptive, but it cannot show what the signs meant or isolate H1.

## V. The Hermeneutic Method: Reading the Lithic Scripture

The companion paper "The Lithic Scripture" applies the correspondential method to major Paleolithic artworks. Before summarizing these readings, two preliminary matters require attention: the method itself, and the standard of source reliability that constrains its application.

### The Method: Correspondence Is Not Dictionary Lookup

Allegory works by substitution: an author assigns a symbol to an abstract concept (scales = justice; blindfold = impartiality), and the reader consults the codebook. Correspondence works differently. It begins with the **primary principle** of the composition — what the whole image IS as a system — and then reads each element as a functional participant within that system.

The method has four steps:

1. **Identify the primary principle**: What does the whole composition depict? A human form, a wound narrative, a spatial relationship? The whole determines the frame of meaning.
2. **Body parts are receptors**: In the Swedenborgian Grand Man (*Maximus Homo*), each body part exercises a specific spiritual function — eyes = understanding, hands = acting power, crown/head = the highest intellectual principle, feet = natural support.
3. **Animals modify receptors**: When an animal quality occupies a body part, it tells you what *kind* of function that receptor exercises. The animal's contribution must arise from what the animal *actually does* — its natural function — not from a codebook entry.
4. **Position in the cave provides context**: Where the image sits — central or peripheral, high or low, in relation to other figures — tells you whether this is an aspirational image, a narrative, a warning, or a teaching.

This method is constrained because each element must be read *according to its natural function*. You cannot assign arbitrary meanings. The method is also constrained by something more fundamental: **you can only read what is reliably depicted**. If the source evidence for what an image contains is uncertain, no method — however rigorous — can produce a reliable reading. The following four case studies are drawn from artworks that are photographically verifiable and not in scholarly dispute.

### The Venus of Willendorf (Gravettian, ~30,000 BCE)

The Venus of Willendorf is a limestone figurine held in the Naturhistorisches Museum, Vienna — photographed from every angle, every feature undisputed.

**Primary principle**: A human female form with extreme emphasis on the generative and nourishing anatomy — abundant adipose tissue, exaggerated breasts and abdomen, no face, no feet.

**Reading**: The abundance corresponds to celestial good — love as substance, concentrated and overflowing. Exaggerated breasts correspond to nourishment and charity flowing outward. The absence of face corresponds to the absence of *selfhood* — the self-consciousness that distinguishes "I" from "other" had not yet crystallized. The absence of feet corresponds to the state not being grounded in the natural degree. Reading: an icon of the celestial state — before selfhood, before crisis.

### The Wounded Men of Cougnac and Pech Merle (~25,000 BCE)

These figures are clearly visible in photographs. Human forms pierced by lines, with aviforms positioned above — the spatial relationship is documented and uncontested.

**Primary principle**: Human figures pierced by lines (spears or arrows), with aviforms positioned above.

**Reading**: Spears correspond to directed falsities — untruths that penetrate the understanding. The wounded man is the intellectual principle undergoing spiritual combat. The aviforms floating above locate the conflict in the intellectual domain — this is a battle of ideas, not of bodies. Reading: temptation narratives, depicting the human mind under assault from false ideas. The precision of the image lies in the *relationship* between elements: the aviform above, the wound below, the man between.

### The Lascaux Shaft Scene (~17,000 BCE)

This scene is well-photographed and clearly legible. Every element — the bird-headed man, the bison with spilling entrails, the bird-on-a-stick, the large departing animal — is unambiguously present and has been documented by multiple independent researchers.

**Primary principle**: A bird-headed man fallen beside a wounded bison, with a bird-on-a-stick and a massive animal (conventionally identified as a woolly rhinoceros) departing the scene.

**Reading**: The bison with spilling intestines corresponds to natural good mortally wounded — the interior exposed, the containing structure breached. The bird-headed man corresponds to intellect separated from will — the human who has become "all head," disembodied understanding without grounding. The bird-on-a-stick is the intellectual principle fixed, pinned, unable to fly. The departing rhinoceros — massive, armored, solitary — corresponds to sensory-natural power withdrawing from the scene, read through the rhinoceros's own functional properties: a creature of enormous physical force, thick-skinned and nearly impervious, but with poor vision.

Reading: the separation of will and understanding — the crisis event that marks the transition from celestial to spiritual consciousness. Will (the bison, mortally wounded) and understanding (the bird-headed man, fallen) have come apart.

**Note**: The Lithic Scripture connects the rhinoceros to the biblical *Re'em* via Swedenborg's commentary. However, HALOT and TDOT identify *Re'em* as the aurochs (*Bos primigenius*), not the rhinoceros — a different animal. The correspondential reading of the rhinoceros in this scene should therefore be grounded in the rhinoceros's own natural function, not in a zoological bridge to a different species. The reading of sensory-natural power withdrawing stands on the rhinoceros's own character.

### Göbekli Tepe Pillar 18 (~9,600 BCE)

The pillars of Göbekli Tepe stand in situ, excavated by multiple teams (Schmidt 1995–2014, DAI ongoing), photographed extensively, and documented in peer-reviewed publications. Every feature — the T-shape, the low-relief hands, the belt, the animal reliefs — is independently verifiable.

**Primary principle**: T-shaped anthropomorphic pillar with hands, belt, and animal reliefs — a human form rendered in stone.

**Reading**: The T-shape is the human outline, monumental, built. The belt corresponds to doctrine — truths organized into coherent order, binding the form together. The hands signify acting power. The animal reliefs on the pillar surface correspond to natural qualities now *attached to* the monument rather than worn on a living body.

Reading: the externalized Divine Human. What earlier peoples perceived directly and expressed in cave images, the Göbekli Tepe builders needed to construct in stone — because direct perception had diminished to the point where built forms were necessary to hold what was once seen. "The celestial person points to the mountain. The spiritual person builds a temple."

### The Narrative Arc

These four readings trace a chronologically coherent arc:

1. **Venus of Willendorf** (~30,000 BCE): Celestial wholeness — before selfhood, before crisis
2. **Wounded Men** (~25,000 BCE): The onset of spiritual combat — falsities begin to penetrate
3. **Lascaux Shaft Scene** (~17,000 BCE): The crisis — will and understanding separate
4. **Göbekli Tepe** (~9,600 BCE): Externalized worship — what was once perceived must now be built

Whether this narrative is "what actually happened" or "a coherent pattern read into the sequence" is a legitimate question. But the readings produce no internal contradictions, the chronological order is not forced, and the correspondences remain consistent across all four (aviforms mean the same thing wherever they appear; the wounded-man motif reads the same way in every instance). Every element in every reading is photographically verifiable by any researcher.

The limitation is real: at the level of any individual reading, a composition can be mapped to correspondences, so any single case study could be dismissed as pattern-fitting. The constraint operates at the system level — internal consistency across all readings, chronological coherence with the archaeological record, and the requirement that each correspondence be grounded in the object's natural function, not assigned by a codebook.

## VI. Perception, Not Codification

The theoretical resolution that unifies all the empirical findings lies in the distinction between codification and perception.

The central argument is this: the 32 signs are consistent because their makers were not *encoding* information — they were *depicting what they saw*. The consistency paradox dissolves when the question shifts from "how was this system transmitted?" to "what reality did they perceive?" If different people in different times and places perceive the same structured reality, they produce the same marks — not because they learned from each other, but because they are depicting the same thing.

This reframing resolves the composite problem from Section II:

| Feature | Correspondential Resolution |
|:--------|:---------------------------|
| **Universal recurrence** | Same reality perceived independently = same forms |
| **Bounded inventory** | The structure being perceived has a finite number of features |
| **30,000-year stability** | The structure being perceived doesn't change over time |
| **Selective repertoire** | Only forms that correspond to real features are selected |
| **Contextual deployment** | Signs appear where their function is needed; absence where function is not demanded is expected, not anomalous |

The paper introduces a critical timeline correction: the "Golden Age" or "Most Ancient Church" described by Swedenborg corresponds not to the Upper Paleolithic but to deep hominin evolution — the millions of years during which the cognitive architecture capable of symbolic thought was forming. The cave paintings we analyze belong to the *Ancient Church* — the period after direct perception began to fade, when what was once seen needed to be recorded. The emergence of elaborate cave art marks the *loss* of direct perception, not its apex.

This counterintuitive claim has structural support. The Aurignacian cave art tradition begins abruptly — the earliest European examples are already sophisticated, with no clear developmental precursor in the cave record. If the art represents the beginning of recording what was previously held in direct perception, the abruptness makes sense: the art didn't develop from simpler forms because it wasn't evolving — it was translating a pre-existing perceptual vocabulary into a new medium.

The Göbekli Tepe transition follows the same logic. The monumental architecture at Göbekli Tepe (9,600 BCE) was built by hunter-gatherers who had not yet developed agriculture, writing, or pottery. The standard archaeological question is: why build? The correspondential answer is: because nature alone was no longer sufficient as teaching. "The celestial person points to the mountain. The spiritual person builds a temple." Göbekli Tepe marks the moment when perceived spiritual reality needed to be *externalized* — shaped, built, fixed in stone — because direct perception had diminished to the point where constructed forms were necessary.

### Evidence for Perceptual Duration

The claim that pre-literate humanity maintained accurate perception across deep time is independently supported by oral tradition durability studies:

- Aboriginal Australian songlines preserve accurate descriptions of land bridges and coastlines submerged 7,000–12,000 years ago, verified against geological and bathymetric data
- Klamath oral tradition preserves the eruption sequence of Mount Mazama (7,700 years ago) in "striking agreement" with geological evidence
- Lynne Kelly's "Memory Code" research demonstrates that ancient monuments functioned as mnemonic devices anchoring encyclopedic oral knowledge

These cases demonstrate that non-literate cultures can maintain accurate records across thousands of years — far longer than Western assumptions about oral tradition have allowed. The mechanism proposed here goes further: the accuracy persists not just because the mnemonic system is robust, but because the reality being described is stable. Song and landscape are both rooted in the same structure. The memory is durable because it describes what is, not just what was.

The inferential bridge is structural: in the non-transmission cases (Paleolithic signs, NDEs, MallWorld dreams, DOPS birthmarks), no mechanism connects the domains — the consistency of the output reflects the consistency of what is being perceived. In the transmission cases (Disney, Grimm, Andersen), the correspondential logic was consciously encoded — but the transmission chain explains *how* the logic reached the storytellers, not *why it works*. ## VIII. What the Data Show and What Remains Open

### What is empirically established

1. The 32 signs constitute a bounded, selective, cross-culturally convergent system stable for 30,000 years
2. The signs exhibit positional grammar within cave compositions, with intellectual signs in upper/active positions and foundational signs in lower/containing positions
3. A universal core (~50–60%) appears independently across populations separated by oceans and tens of thousands of years
4. Protective and ordering signs spike dramatically during the Magdalenian climatic crisis (tectiforms: 58.8%; crosshatches: 81.5%)
5. The same structural patterns — vertical gradients, entity autonomy, perceiver-determined atmosphere, constant state with variable form — appear across six additional empirical domains outside archaeology (see Section VII convergence table)

> **[CORRECTION #26]**: Items 1–4 are not empirically established in the stated form. The evidence establishes bounded recurrence, regional structure, and period differences; it does not establish a decoded system, positional grammar, universal semantic core, or protective/ordering meanings. Cross-domain resemblance cannot repair those missing archaeological tests.

### What is interpretive but internally consistent

6. The specific correspondential mappings (spiral = divine influx, tectiform = structured mind, aviform = intellectual principle) are derived from Swedenborgian principles applied to functional properties of each form. They produce coherent, non-contradictory readings across multiple artworks and a chronologically consistent narrative arc. But they cannot be independently verified at the individual-sign level — the method lacks a mechanism to produce a clear "miss" on any single mapping
7. The narrative arc from celestial wholeness (Venus figurines) through crisis (Wounded Men, Shaft Scene) to externalized worship (Göbekli Tepe) is compelling and chronologically appropriate, but it is the framework's reading of the sequence, not a finding derived from the sequence independently

### What remains genuinely open

8. The Tier 3 regional signs (tectiforms, aviforms) are not universally distributed, but the relevant test is not universal distribution — it is contextual coherence. The question is whether each sign fits the context where it appears, and whether it is absent only where the context does not demand it. The tectiform's concentration in the Dordogne refugium during Heinrich Event 1 passes this test. Further case-by-case analysis of regional sign deployment against local archaeological context would strengthen or weaken this finding
9. The entoptic model provides an alternative explanation for the Tier 1 universal signs. The correspondential model's advantage is that it also explains Tier 2 convergent signs, the bounded inventory, the climatic response, and the contextual specificity of regional deployment — but the two models are not mutually exclusive. Phosphene perception and correspondential perception could both contribute to the sign repertoire
10. The inferential bridge between modern empirical data and Paleolithic cognition cannot be directly verified. The argument is structural: the same perceptual capacity that produces culture-invariant NDE patterns, prints birthmarks corresponding to past-life wounds, drives mythic convergence, and generates consistent correspondential logic in popular storytelling also produced consistent signs 30,000 years ago. The convergence across seven independent domains strengthens this inference, but it remains structural — well-evidenced but not provable from the archaeological record alone

## IX. Conclusion

The 32 geometric signs of the Upper Paleolithic are not arbitrary cultural marks. They are too stable, too bounded, too selective, and too cross-culturally convergent for that explanation. They are not phosphene artifacts alone — the entoptic model explains the universals but not the specifics, the boundedness, or the climatic response. They are not proto-writing — no institutional transmission mechanism could maintain this system for 30,000 years without drift.

The correspondential model provides a unified explanation: the signs depict perceived structure in reality. Their stability reflects the stability of what they depict. Their boundedness reflects the finite architecture of the reality being mapped. Their cross-cultural convergence reflects independent perception of the same underlying structure. Their climatic response reflects the deployment of specific correspondential functions (containment, ordering) during periods of crisis. And their eventual dissolution reflects the fading of the perceptual capacity that produced them.

This explanation generates the predictions the data confirm. It unifies features that require three separate models under competing frameworks. And the same structural regularities appear across six additional empirical domains — from NDE phenomenology to past-life memory research to myth formation — strengthening the inference that the patterns reflect the architecture of reality rather than the conventions of culture.

The individual sign mappings remain interpretive. The system-level predictions are confirmed. The cross-domain convergence is real. What the Paleolithic artists were doing when they placed an aviform above a wounded man, or a tectiform below a rock crevice, or a scalariform on a cave wall in Sulawesi — the same form independently produced by an artist in Amazonia 25,000 years later — is best explained by a model in which they were depicting what they perceived, and what they perceived was consistent because it was real.

> **[CORRECTION #26]**: The system-level confirmation is retracted. The conclusion may remain as a correspondential hypothesis, but the three companion studies do not establish it archaeologically. Coherence is necessary for a reading; it is not evidence that the makers intended or shared that reading.
