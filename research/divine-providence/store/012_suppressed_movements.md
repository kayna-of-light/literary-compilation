## The Five Movements

### 1. Cathars (12th-13th c.)
- Radical dualism: benevolent God (NT) vs. malevolent creator (OT)
- The consolamentum: laying-on-of-hands succession claimed back to apostles
- Perfecti lived in extreme apostolic poverty — implicit indictment of the clergy
- Albigensian Crusade (1209-1229): Pope Innocent III launched military campaign against fellow Christians
- Inquisition established 1233 partly to deal with survivors
- Texts systematically destroyed — almost nothing survives in Cathar's own words
- **Key source:** Lambert, *The Cathars* (OUP, 1998); Sennis, *Cathars in Question* (2016)

### 2. Waldensians (12th c. onward)
- Peter Waldo: wealthy merchant who gave everything away and began preaching
- Commissioned FIRST vernacular Bible translation (French, 1175-1185)
- Lay preaching — men AND women — without clerical authorization
- Denied purgatory, indulgences, priestly mediation
- Condemned by Pope Lucius III (1184) despite being theologically orthodox
- **SURVIVED as continuous community** — joined Reformation at Chanforan (1532), exist today
- Hudson: "the most geographically widespread and longest lived of all medieval popular heresies"
- **Key source:** Cameron, *Waldenses* (OUP, 2000); Hudson, *The Premature Reformation* (Clarendon, 1988)

### 3. Meister Eckhart (c. 1260-1328)
- "God's ground and my ground is the same ground" — the soul contains an uncreated element identical with God
- Gelassenheit (releasement): radical detachment, total self-emptying
- "Birth of the Word in the Soul" — the Father's eternal generation of the Son occurs IN the soul
- 28 propositions condemned in *In Agro Dominico* (1329) — BUT Eckhart himself never declared a heretic
- 2010: Vatican said "no rehabilitation needed" — "he is a good and orthodox theologian"
- German sermons survived and influenced Tauler, Suso, Theologia Germanica, entire Rhineland mysticism
- **Key source:** McGinn, *The Mystical Thought of Meister Eckhart* (Crossroad, 2001)

### 4. Early Quakers / George Fox (17th c.)
- "There is one, even Christ Jesus, that can speak to thy condition" — the Inner Light in every person
- Universal scope: Light "extended to all people everywhere" — radical universalism
- Experiential primacy: "What canst THOU say?" — present divine communication over text
- Fox reported being "taken up into the Paradise of God and into the condition of Adam before disobedience" — could "see into all creatures"
- No sacraments, no clergy, no liturgy — silent worship, waiting for the Spirit
- Fox imprisoned 8 times; thousands of Quakers jailed
- **SURVIVED** — Society of Friends exists globally today

### 5. Lollards / John Wycliffe (14th-15th c.)
- First complete English Bible translation (from Vulgate)
- Lay preaching from vernacular scripture ("Poor Preachers")
- Rejected transubstantiation, purgatory, indulgences, papal authority
- *De heretico comburendo* (1401): first English statute authorizing burning of heretics — specifically targeting Lollards
- Council of Constance (1415): Wycliffe posthumously condemned, writings burned, remains exhumed (1428)
- **200+ manuscript copies of Wycliffite Bible survived despite active burning campaigns**
- 40% were luxury copies owned by aristocrats including royals — truth flowing through unexpected vessels
- Hudson: "the premature Reformation" — Wycliffe anticipated Luther by 150 years
- Wycliffe → Hus → Luther: documented transmission chain
- **Key source:** Hudson, *The Premature Reformation* (Clarendon, 1988); Daniell, *The Bible in English* (Yale, 2003)
