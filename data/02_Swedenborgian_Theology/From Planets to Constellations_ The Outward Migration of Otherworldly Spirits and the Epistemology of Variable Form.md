# From Planets to Constellations: The Outward Migration of Otherworldly Spirits and the Epistemology of Variable Form

## Executive Summary and Theoretical Premise

Across the history of Western esotericism, spiritualism, and ufological new religious movements, reports of contact with non-terrestrial intelligences show a striking structural pattern: the interior moral, intellectual, and spiritual qualities attributed to these beings stay recognisable across centuries, while their reported physical coordinates in the cosmos migrate outward. In the eighteenth century, Emanuel Swedenborg's *De Telluribus in Mundo Nostro Solari* (*Earths in the Universe*, 1758) placed distinct spirits from the Moon and from the planets of the solar system — Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, and Saturn — alongside spirits from earths "in the starry heaven" (Swedenborg 1758). Through the late nineteenth and mid-twentieth centuries, Spiritualist mediums, Theosophists, and the early Cold War "contactees" likewise placed their exalted teachers and "Space Brothers" on Mars and Venus (Flournoy 1900; Leslie and Adamski 1953; Melton and Eberhart 1995). From the mid-1970s, the solar system was largely abandoned in contactee and channelled literature, replaced by distant star systems — the Pleiades above all — and by the "starseed" paradigm of extraterrestrial souls incarnated in human bodies (Steiger 1976; Steiger and Steiger 1981; Reece 2007).

The scholarship of new religious movements records this shift and ties it to the disconfirmation of the older claims. The early contactees' "descriptions of Venus, Mars, and the Moon were disconfirmed even in their lifetime" — in the years of the *Mariner*, *Venera*, and *Viking* missions (1962–1976) — while their successors "channel beings who originate on planets far beyond the reach of contemporary science" (Encyclopedia.com, "Contactees"; Reece 2007).

Read through the correspondential framework of *The Living Book* (§§1.3, 3.4) and the tiered model of *A Hybrid Model of Post-Mortem Existence*, this migration is more than a sociological tale of ad hoc apologetics. It illustrates the principle of **constant state, variable form**, and it bears out the library's standing distinction between Swedenborg's spiritual observation and his physical explanation: he was right in what he observed, but not always in how he explained it, above all where the explanation concerned the physical world (*The Living Book* §3.4). Swedenborg's own *Spiritual Diary* of 1748 — written in the months in which he recorded these encounters — supplies the mechanism. Spirits are not in space or time (*Spiritual Diary* §3476); "in respect to material things they cannot judge from themselves," and so they "judged and affirmed from my persuasion" (*Spiritual Diary* §1776); and in at least one case Swedenborg records that he himself inferred a planet of origin and was unsure of it (*Spiritual Diary* §§1669–1670).

## Historical Scholarship on the Outward Shift: From the Solar System to the Constellations

The trajectory by which the reported home of otherworldly beings moved from the planets of the solar system to distant stars runs through the history of the plurality-of-worlds debate, of Spiritualism and Theosophy, and of the UFO religions. It falls into three phases, before and after the space-probe era.

### Phase I: Enlightenment Pluralism, Mediumship, and the Theosophical Solar System (1748–1930s)

Following the Copernican revolution, the "plurality of worlds" debate — charted by the historians of science Steven J. Dick (1982), Michael J. Crowe (1986), and David Dunér (2016) — turned the planets of the solar system into terrestrial globes analogous to the Earth. Popularised by Bernard le Bovier de Fontenelle's *Entretiens sur la pluralité des mondes* (1686) and Christiaan Huygens's *Cosmotheoros* (1698), eighteenth-century natural philosophy widely took it that teleology and physical analogy required the planets to be inhabited by rational beings (Dunér 2016; Crowe 1986). Swedenborg's own statement of the principle is that "where a terrestrial body exists, so does the human being; for the human being is the end for the sake of which a terrestrial body exists" (*Arcana Coelestia* §9237, quoted in Bedford 2006). When Swedenborg recorded his conversations with spirits from Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn, and the Moon in *The Spiritual Diary* (1748), published them in *Arcana Coelestia* (1749–1756), and gathered them into *Earths in the Universe* (1758), he placed his spiritual encounters within this prevailing solar-system cosmology, while also recording spirits from earths "in the starry heaven" (Swedenborg 1758; Dunér 2016).

In the nineteenth century, as telescopic astronomy turned to the inner planets and reported the Martian *canali* (Crowe 1986), both Spiritualist mediumship and Theosophical occultism anchored their cosmic hierarchies on Mars and Venus. In December 1894 the Geneva psychologist Théodore Flournoy began a five-year investigation of the non-professional medium he called Hélène Smith, published as *Des Indes à la planète Mars* (1900; English translation *From India to the Planet Mars: A Study of a Case of Somnambulism with Glossolalia*, 1900). Her "Martian cycle," inaugurated at a séance of 25 November 1894 and resumed in February 1896, brought communications from Martian figures such as Astané and Esenale, drawings of Martian landscapes, and a "Martian" language that Flournoy showed to be built on French (Flournoy 1900).

Concurrently, the Theosophical tradition developed a solar-system spiritual hierarchy whose central figures, the "Lords of the Flame" and Sanat Kumara, were said to have come to the Earth from Venus (Greer 2003). Second-generation Theosophists Annie Besant and Charles W. Leadbeater elaborated this cosmology in *Man: Whence, How and Whither* (1913). The same legend — "about eighteen million years ago" — opens Desmond Leslie's half of *Flying Saucers Have Landed* (Leslie and Adamski 1953), which joined it to the new flying-saucer reports.

### Phase II: The Solar-System Contactees and "Scout Ships from Venus" (1950–1965)

After the flying-saucer reports of 1947, the emerging saucer mythology fused with post-Theosophical occultism to produce the 1950s "contactee" movement, which transposed the Theosophical Masters into technological "Space Brothers" (Rothstein 2013). The dominant contactee literature of the 1950s, catalogued in J. Gordon Melton and George M. Eberhart's bibliography "The Flying Saucer Contactee Movement, 1950–1994" in James R. Lewis's *The Gods Have Landed* (1995), named the planets of the solar system — above all Venus — as the visitors' home worlds (Melton and Eberhart 1995). George Adamski, the archetype of the movement, reported meeting a benevolent, telepathic visitor from Venus in the Californian desert in 1952 (Leslie and Adamski 1953). George Van Tassel's transmissions from "Ashtar" began within the same solar-system frame (Helland 2003). George King founded the Aetherius Society in London in 1955 after his contact with the Venusian Master Aetherius (Lewis 2003).

### Phase III: The Space-Probe Disconfirmations and "Beam Ships from the Pleiades" (1962–1981)

Between 1962 and 1976, the scientific plausibility of inhabited planets within the solar system collapsed under direct robotic exploration (§ Empirical Falsification, below). In the years that followed, the contactee movement changed its cosmography. Gregory L. Reece's *UFO Religion: Inside Flying Saucer Cults and Culture* (2007) builds the pivot into the structure of its Part Two, "Contactees": chapter 5, "Scout Ships from Venus," treats the 1950s solar-system contactees, and chapter 6, "Beam Ships from the Pleiades," the interstellar contactees who followed (Reece 2007). The *Encyclopedia of Occultism and Parapsychology* describes the same turn from the other side: the contactee phenomenon "would experience a significant growth in the 1970s and 1980s from two unexpected sources" — abductees and channellers — and the channellers' beings "originate on planets far beyond the reach of contemporary science" (Encyclopedia.com, "Contactees").

The watershed for the Pleiadian shift is **1975**, when the Swiss contactee Eduard "Billy" Meier began recording contacts with "Semjase," who told him, "I originate in the Pleiades"; his contact notes date the first meetings to January and February 1975 (Stevens n.d.). In tandem, the **starseed** idea — extraterrestrial souls incarnating in human bodies on Earth — was popularised by Brad Steiger in *Gods of Aquarius: UFOs and the Transformation of Man* (1976) and codified by Brad and Francie Steiger in *The Star People* (1981). Through the 1980s and 1990s, channelled literature built an interstellar cartography of human origins — Lyra, Sirius, Orion, and the Pleiades among others (Royal and Priest 1989; Marciniak 1992) — and starseeds today identify themselves as "aliens or alien consciousnesses in human form" (Crockford 2021).

## Empirical Falsification as the Engine of Cosmological Relocation

### The Mariner, Venera, and Viking Closure of the Inner Solar System

The timing of the shift from planets to constellations follows the sequence of planetary reconnaissance by the United States and the Soviet Union. For nearly three centuries, Venus and Mars had served as the twin pillars of solar-system pluralism, widely regarded as Earth-like worlds able to support life (Crowe 1986). That permissiveness ended within some fourteen years.

Venus and Mercury were eliminated as candidate habitats between 1962 and 1978. NASA's *Mariner 2* (1962) and *Mariner 5* (1967), the Soviet *Venera* entry probes and landers of the late 1960s and 1970s, *Mariner 10* (which passed Venus on 5 February 1974 before mapping Mercury), and *Pioneer Venus* (1978) showed that Venus has a crushing carbon-dioxide atmosphere and a surface far too hot for organic life, and *Mariner 10* showed Mercury to be airless and cratered (Dunne 1974; NASA, "60 Years and Counting").

Mars was eliminated as a seat of civilisation between 1965 and 1976. The *Mariner 4* flyby of July 1965 sent back the first close-range photographs of Mars, revealing a heavily cratered landscape and a thin atmosphere; *Mariner 6* and *Mariner 7* (1969) reinforced the finding; and *Mariner 9* (1971–1972), followed in 1974 by the Soviet *Mars 4* and *Mars 5*, mapped the planet and showed that the "canals" had no physical existence. The last blow to classical Martian habitability came in **1976**, when the *Viking 1* and *Viking 2* landers photographed the surface and ran their soil-biology experiments without detecting life (NASA, "60 Years and Counting"; PDS Geosciences Node, "Mariner Missions").

As the inner solar system closed, the scientific horizon opened onto interstellar space. *Pioneer 10* (1972), *Pioneer 11* (1973), and *Voyager 1* and *2* (1977) imaged the gas giants and left the solar system carrying plaques and records addressed to intelligent beings among the stars — *Pioneer 10* in the direction of Aldebaran (Čapová 2021).

### Falsification Resistance: Spatial Outward Shift and Ontological Dematerialisation

The literature on contactee religions identifies the disconfirmation of the older claims as the turning point. The *Encyclopedia of Occultism and Parapsychology* puts it most directly: the channellers of the 1970s and after "channel beings who originate on planets far beyond the reach of contemporary science," and "most importantly, their accounts of life on their home planet is not susceptible to possible falsification, a major flaw of the early contactees whose descriptions of Venus, Mars, and the Moon were disconfirmed even in their lifetime" (Encyclopedia.com, "Contactees").

Two complementary moves preserved the older narratives. The first was **spatial relocation to star systems**: moving the home worlds of the "Space Brothers" from Venus and Mars, within reach of *Mariner*, *Venera*, and *Viking* cameras, to the Pleiades and other stars, far beyond the reach of probes or surface-resolving telescopes (Encyclopedia.com, "Contactees"; Reece 2007). The second was **ontological dematerialisation**: beings once described as physical pilots became channelled, non-physical, or interdimensional beings who needed neither planets in the solar system nor metal craft — the shift Christopher Helland traces in "From Extraterrestrials to Ultraterrestrials: The Evolution of the Concept of Ashtar" (Helland 2003).

## Invariant Spiritual Qualities Across Variable Astronomical Forms

### Swedenborg's *Spiritual Diary* as the Epistemological Key

If the astronomical locations reported by seers, mediums, and contactees were repeatedly falsified and pushed outward, does that invalidate the encounters themselves? Within the framework of *The Living Book* (§§1.3, 3.4), it does not: the underlying spiritual reality is constant, while perceptual forms vary with the receiver's repertoire. By the library's standing principle, Swedenborg was right in his **observations** of spiritual reality, but not always in his **explanations** of them, above all where his explanation concerned the physical world (*The Living Book* §3.4).

Swedenborg's *Spiritual Diary* shows him observing the very mechanism by which a genuine spiritual encounter is clothed in a mistaken natural-world explanation. While conversing with the spirits he associated with Mars, Jupiter, Mercury, Saturn, and Venus in 1748, he recorded four things.

First, the **non-spatiality of the spiritual world**. He wrote "that in the other life there is no notion of time or space … and those who were locally the most remote from me, as in the Indies, or in other earths, compared with the distance of which, all the distances on our earth vanish to nothing, were yet made to be present in a moment of time" (*Spiritual Diary* §3476, autumn 1748). Spiritual proximity is affinity of state, not travel across physical space; meeting a spirit never means tracking a signal from a material planet.

Second, **function in the Grand Man**. In a note appended to *Spiritual Diary* §1558 (19 March 1748), he defined the "planetary" spirits by their functions rather than by astronomy: "the spirits of the earth Jupiter represent rational ideas … The spirits of the earth Saturn correspond to interior sense, or reason; The spirits of the earth Mercury to knowledges; The spirits of the earth Mars to thought," while "those of the planet Venus and of our earth are such as constitute or represent corporeal things and the appetencies connected with them." He later saw that the thought of the spirits of Mars "was not so much of the cerebrum as of the [cerebellum or] will" (*Spiritual Diary* §1550½, 25 September 1748), and he perceived in the sphere of Venus something "truly human" inwardly, though "their exteriors" were of another, animal nature (*Spiritual Diary* §§3345–3346).

Third, and most crucially, **spirits judge from the persuasion of the seer**. Under the heading "That spirits judge from man's persuasion" (29 March 1748), he recorded:

*"In conversing with spirits, I observed that they were persuaded concerning things of which they could have no knowledge; this persuasion evidently arose from my being myself persuaded concerning anything which I was demonstrating. This happened very often, and in regard to a variety of subjects, even scientifics, which they had not known, as also in a multitude of particular things; from whence I was authorized to conclude, as I said to the spirits, that they judged and affirmed from my persuasion. Consequently if I demonstrated anything falsely, they would be persuaded concerning that also, for in respect to material things they cannot judge from themselves, though they still suppose that the knowledges which are in my memory are theirs"* (*Spiritual Diary* §1776).

Fourth, **a planetary address inferred, and held in doubt**. Of a class of upright spirits who loathed their bodies and had once believed themselves to have "existed as spirits from eternity," he writes: "I was hence persuaded, especially as they spake well of the spirits of the earth Jupiter, that they were, in fact, from thence"; and at once qualifies it — they might be from one of Jupiter's satellites, "I am not sure … for as I could not have an idea of any sort of men except such as live on earths surrounded by atmospheres" (*Spiritual Diary* §§1669–1670, 1673). Here the location is explicitly his own inference, shaped by what his natural idea could hold.

Together these passages give a three-level account of why the spiritual qualities stay constant while the physical location shifts with science. At the highest level, the spirits encountered — by Swedenborg, or by any later medium, channeller, or contactee whose experience falls under *Tier 1: Spirit Influence* or *Tier 2, Path B: the Volunteer Soul* in *A Hybrid Model of Post-Mortem Existence* — possess real and distinct spiritual qualities. At the middle level, the receiver's repertoire, the spirits cannot supply material astrography, because "in respect to material things they cannot judge from themselves" (*Spiritual Diary* §1776). At the lowest level, the variable natural form, the receiver's natural mind assigns physical coordinates according to the science of the day. Because Swedenborg's eighteenth-century natural mind was persuaded by the science of his time that the planets were inhabited (Dunér 2016; Goerwitz 1985), his natural memory supplied the planetary addresses, and the spirits "judged and affirmed from [his] persuasion" — as §§1669–1670 show him doing in a single case. Once the probes of 1962–1976 ruled out life in the solar system, the persuasion of later receivers carried the same encounters outward to the stars.

### Comparative Mapping of Spiritual Qualities: From Swedenborg's Planets to the Constellations

When the qualities Swedenborg gives to the spirits of the planets in *Earths in the Universe* (1758) and *The Spiritual Diary* (1748) are set beside the qualities later attributed to Venusian "Space Brothers" and to interstellar beings and starseed lineages, a continuity of four profiles appears. The Swedenborgian column below is established from his texts; the later columns record how the contact and starseed literature describes the beings it places in each location, and the pairing across the columns is this document's reading.

The first profile is **celestial love, harmony, and fellowship without government**. Swedenborg's spirits of **Mars** "live in societies, but not under governments," recognise their associates from face, eyes, and speech, and declare that "the Lord, who is the Only Good, is their holiness" (*Spiritual Diary* §§1539–1540); they are "for the most part celestial men" (*Earths in the Universe* §85), with a tacit speech that reaches the hearer "by a certain internal way … now called the Eustachian tube" (*Earths in the Universe* §87), and they "bear an exceedingly strong resemblance to the Most Ancient Church" (*Spiritual Diary* §1543). In Theosophy and the 1950s contactee movement, **Venus** became the home of compassionate teachers of love, peace, and brotherhood (Greer 2003; Leslie and Adamski 1953; Lewis 2003). After the probes revealed Venus to be uninhabitable, contact and channelled literature located teachers of love and feeling in the **Pleiades** (Stevens n.d.; Marciniak 1992).

The second profile is **the love of knowledges**. Swedenborg's spirits of **Mercury** "love knowledges" (*Earths in the Universe* §29) and have relation in the Grand Man "to the memory … of things abstracted from terrestrial and merely material objects" (*Earths in the Universe* §10). In the same months he met a class of spirits unknown to him who "attend to nothing but to use," following each use to the use beyond it "as far as the ninth use," and was told that "in the earths of the universe" some "progress to the fiftieth use" (*Spiritual Diary* §§3267–3273). Later starseed literature gives a comparable intellectual and systematising temperament to beings it places at **Arcturus**.

The third profile is **wisdom from good, tranquil rationality, and guardianship**. Swedenborg's spirits of **Jupiter** "place wisdom in thinking well and justly of all things" (*Earths in the Universe* §62), represent "rational ideas" in the Grand Man (*Spiritual Diary* §1558, appended note), make the education of their children their greatest concern, turn away from talk of war and plunder (*Earths in the Universe* §§47–49), and send "a herald" before their angels to prepare a man for their coming (*Spiritual Diary* §1648). Later starseed literature gives a comparable profile of guardianship and ancient wisdom to beings it places at **Sirius**.

The fourth profile is **interior reason and aversion to corporeal bondage**. Swedenborg's spirits of **Saturn** "respect the reason," in conflict with spirits of the external senses (*Spiritual Diary* §§3328–3330; §1558, appended note), and the spirits he associated with Jupiter or one of its satellites so despised the body that they had believed themselves to be spirits "from eternity" (*Spiritual Diary* §§1668–1673). Later starseed literature gives a comparable profile of independence and difficulty with embodiment to beings it places in **Lyra** and **Andromeda** (Royal and Priest 1989).

This synthesis also illuminates the starseed profile analysed in *A Hybrid Model of Post-Mortem Existence* (via Steiger and Steiger 1981). Stripped of its post-1975 interstellar vocabulary, the core of the starseed experience — the feeling of not belonging to earthly society, heightened empathy, an innate drive toward service, and an intuitive memory of a pre-incarnate home — maps onto the two explanatory tiers of the Hybrid Model. Under **Tier 2, Path B (the Volunteer Soul)**, a soul that has "already completed its regeneration and achieved a stable, heavenly state" incarnates for a mission of service (*A Hybrid Model* §3.2). Because its interior state belongs to the heavens rather than the unregenerate natural world, its natural mind experiences a deep misalignment with earthly society; and in an era in which popular culture equates "the heavens" with outer space, the natural mind translates a genuine memory of pre-incarnate heavenly origin into a narrative of interstellar origin. Under **Tier 1 (Spirit Influence)**, a living person resonates with a particular society of the Grand Man — the love of knowledges (Swedenborg's "Mercury"), or celestial love (Swedenborg's "Mars") — and receives that society's sphere. By the law of *Spiritual Diary* §1776, receiver and spirits alike clothe that non-spatial state in the astronomical vocabulary of the receiver's era.

## Swedenborgian Scholarship and Commentary on *Earths in the Universe* in the Light of Planetary Science

The disconfirmation of life on the planets of the solar system has produced a body of commentary within the Swedenborgian community, especially in *The New Philosophy*, the journal of the Swedenborg Scientific Association. Commentators fall into several interpretive schools.

### Contextualist and Cognitive Approaches: Goerwitz (1985), Bedford (2006), and Dunér (2016)

The most rigorous scholarship on *Earths in the Universe* places the text within the intellectual history of eighteenth-century science. In "Extraterrestrial Life: A Study of the Intellectual Context of Emanuel Swedenborg's *Earths in the Universe*" (*The New Philosophy* 88, 1985), **Richard L. Goerwitz III** shows that the premise of inhabited planets did not come to Swedenborg from revelation but from his scientific formation and the consensus of the natural philosophers he read (Goerwitz 1985).

Building on Goerwitz, **Allen J. Bedford**, a chemist at Bryn Athyn College, confronts the discrepancy directly in "Planets and Perspectives: New Church Theology and the Plurality of Worlds Debate" (*The New Philosophy* 109, 2006) and returns to it in "Removing Obstacles" (*The New Philosophy* 111, 2008). Quoting *Arcana Coelestia* §9237 — "Where a terrestrial body exists, so does the human being" — Bedford writes: "The trouble we have with this statement is that to date there is not one whit of evidence that any life at all, much less human life, exists anywhere in this solar system, or indeed in the whole universe, except right here on planet earth" (Bedford 2006, 315). Noting that "many Swedenborgians have developed elaborate explanations for this dearth of evidence" (Bedford 2006, 316), he argues that the lasting value of *Earths in the Universe* lies not in eighteenth-century astrobiology but in its answer to the deist challenge exemplified by Thomas Paine's *Age of Reason*: by showing redemption and the Divine Human at work across a boundless cosmos, Swedenborg freed Christian theology from the singularity of the Earth (Bedford 2006).

In "Swedenborg and the Plurality of Worlds: Astrotheology in the Eighteenth Century" (*Zygon* 51, 2016), the Lund historian of science **David Dunér**, also a researcher in cognitive semiotics, reads Swedenborg's planetary accounts through the cognitive tools of his time: teleological argument, the analogy of the planets with the Earth, and metaphorical thinking in the sense of contemporary cognitive semantics. Swedenborg, he shows, carried contemporary natural philosophy — the plurality of worlds among it — into his theology (Dunér 2016).

### Reader Theories and the Exoplanet Relocation Fallacy

Beyond historical contextualisation, the Swedenborg Foundation's introduction to *Other Planets* (*Earths in the Universe*) lists the theories readers have developed to meet the absence of life on the planets: that Swedenborg was being metaphorical; that he met real extraterrestrial spirits but was mistaken, or misled, about which planets they came from; and that life once existed on those planets and died off, or exists still in a form our instruments cannot detect (Swedenborg Foundation n.d.).

The **extinct or masked civilisation view** proposes either that human life existed on Mars, Venus, Jupiter, and Saturn in the remote past, leaving only its departed spirits for Swedenborg to meet, or that it continues in a form undetectable by science (Swedenborg Foundation n.d.; Bedford 2006). Planetary science rules out habitable conditions on the gas giants and on airless bodies such as the Moon and Mercury, and positing "undetectable" physical humans mirrors the unfalsifiable retreats of the 1970s UFO religions.

The **real-encounter, mistaken-attribution view** is put plainly by the Swedenborgian minister **Lee Woofenden**: "Unfortunately, the belief that all of the planets in our solar system are inhabited flies in the face of overwhelming scientific evidence to the contrary. I'll put it plainly. Swedenborg was wrong about this" — and, further on, "I believe that Swedenborg actually did meet the aliens he describes in Other Planets. It's just that they didn't come from the planets he thought they did" (Woofenden 2014). On this view Swedenborg met the spirits of people from planets around other stars, but because the science of his day knew only the planets of our solar system, both he and the spirits took them to come from Mercury, Venus, Mars, and Jupiter (Woofenden 2014).

The **metaphorical view** reads the planetary accounts as teaching stories. As the Swedenborg Foundation notes, it fails to honour Swedenborg's insistence that these were real experiences, meant to be taken literally (Swedenborg Foundation n.d.).

Here the framework of *The Living Book* (§3.4), supported by Swedenborg's own observations in *The Spiritual Diary* (§§1558, 1669–1670, 1776, 3476), offers a synthesis that resolves the tension between literalism and metaphor while exposing a subtle flaw in the exoplanet hypothesis. When Woofenden places the spirits' former homes on planets around distant stars rather than on Mars or Jupiter, he repeats the outward astrographical shift of the contact movements of the 1970s: he keeps a physical explanation by moving the home worlds outward, from the falsified solar system to star systems beyond the reach of falsification.

*The Living Book* §3.4 requires no such relocation to affirm that Swedenborg's encounters were real. Every spirit in the spiritual world is a real person, associated with others according to ruling love and interior quality. When Swedenborg entered the spheres of the **love of knowledges** ("Mercury"), **wisdom from good** ("Jupiter"), **celestial love** ("Mars"), or **interior reason** ("Saturn"), he was conversing with real spirits who embodied those qualities — some of whom may have lived in the earliest ages on this Earth, since he compares the spirits of Mars with the Most Ancient Church (*Spiritual Diary* §1543) and records the same open intercourse with heaven among the inhabitants of other earths as among the Most Ancient and Ancient Churches (*Spiritual Diary* §1587), and others elsewhere in the cosmos. Because neither his spiritual senses nor the spirits themselves had access to physical astrography (*Spiritual Diary* §§1776, 3476), his natural mind mapped that spiritual topology onto the astronomical geography of the solar system.

## Conclusion: Synthesis for the Correspondential Framework and the Hybrid Afterlife Model

Setting the historiography of esotericism and UFO religions beside Swedenborg's primary texts answers the four research questions this study set out to address, and it supports the library's theoretical architecture.

First, the outward migration of reported otherworldly origins — from the solar system (Moon, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn) between 1758 and the mid-1960s to star systems (the Pleiades, Sirius, Lyra, Orion) and non-physical planes afterwards — is documented in the study of new religious movements (Melton and Eberhart 1995; Helland 2003; Reece 2007; Rothstein 2013; Crockford 2021) and turns in the **mid-1970s**, marked by Billy Meier's Pleiadian contacts of 1975 and Brad Steiger's starseed books of 1976 and 1981. Second, the migration tracks the *Mariner*, *Venera*, and *Viking* missions of 1962–1976, which ruled out life on Venus, Mars, and Mercury within the lifetimes of the 1950s contactees; the literature names this disconfirmation as the flaw the later channellers avoided (Encyclopedia.com, "Contactees").

Third, across three centuries, four profiles of spiritual quality — celestial love and fellowship (Mars, then Venus → the Pleiades), the love of knowledges (Mercury → Arcturus), wisdom from good and guardianship (Jupiter → Sirius), and interior reason averse to bodily bondage (Saturn and the spirits of Jupiter's satellites → Lyra and Andromeda) — recur while the physical location shifts; the Swedenborgian side of this pairing is established from his texts, and the later side from the contact and starseed literature as cited. Swedenborg's own *Spiritual Diary* supplies the mechanism: spirits are not in space (§3476), they judge material things from the persuasion of the receiver (§1776), and he records himself inferring, and doubting, a planetary address (§§1669–1670).

Fourth, this is the pattern the library has documented throughout: Swedenborg was right in his observations, and not always in his explanations where they concerned the physical world. His planetary accounts render discrete qualitative societies of the Grand Man as the physical planets of eighteenth-century astronomy (*The Living Book* §3.4) — an explanation that did not hold. His report of the Ancient Word in Great Tartary is another matter: there the observation and the place converge (*The Living Book* §§3.2–3.3, 4.3). When twentieth-century spacecraft closed the solar system, the natural minds of later receivers — and of modern Swedenborgian commentators proposing exoplanet relocation — moved the variable form outward to the stars, while the constant spiritual states, and the Volunteer Soul ("starseed") and Spirit Influence dynamics identified in *A Hybrid Model of Post-Mortem Existence*, continued unchanged.
