## What Happened

### The Diversion
- Pope Innocent III called the Fourth Crusade (1202) to recapture Jerusalem from Muslim control.
- The crusaders contracted with Venice for transport but couldn't pay the full amount.
- Venice diverted them first to Zara (a Catholic city on the Dalmatian coast) — the first time a crusade attacked a Catholic city. The Pope excommunicated the entire army.
- A Byzantine pretender (Alexios IV) promised 200,000 marks, reunion of the Eastern Church with Rome, and provisions — if the crusaders would restore his father to the Byzantine throne.

### The Sack (April 1204)
- When Alexios IV couldn't pay, the crusaders attacked Constantinople — the largest Christian city in the world, the seat of Orthodox Christianity for 900 years.
- Three days of unrestrained looting, rape, and destruction.
- Churches stripped of gold, silver, relics. A prostitute was enthroned on the Patriarch's chair in Hagia Sophia.
- Priceless manuscripts, artworks, and relics from a thousand years of Christian civilization — destroyed or looted.
- The bronze horses of St. Mark's in Venice are Constantinopolitan spoils.

### Scholarly Judgments
- **Runciman (1954):** "There was never a greater crime against humanity than the Fourth Crusade."
- **Martin Arbagi:** "One of the great atrocities of medieval history."
- Pope John Paul II (2001): apologized, calling it "tragic that the assailants, who set out to secure free access for Christians to the Holy Land, turned against their brothers in the faith."
- Ecumenical Patriarch Bartholomew I accepted the apology in 2004 — 800 years later.

## Why This Matters for the Thesis

The Greeks could never forgive what the Latins had done. Every subsequent attempt at reunion (Council of Florence, 1439) failed because 1204 was the lived experience that proved what the institution actually was.

### 4. What Was Destroyed

## The Pattern of the Crusades Generally

The Fourth Crusade is the most extreme case, but the pattern is visible throughout the crusading era:

- **First Crusade (1096-1099):** Rhineland massacres of Jews en route. Crusaders killed more Jews than Muslims in some campaigns. The conquest of Jerusalem (1099) — massacre of Muslim and Jewish inhabitants.
- **Children's Crusade (1212):** Thousands of children set out for the Holy Land. Many sold into slavery. The institution that inspired this had nothing to say about it.
- **Albigensian Crusade (1209-1229):** A crusade against FELLOW CHRISTIANS in southern France (see Source 017).
- **Northern Crusades (12th-15th c.):** Conversion by sword of Baltic and Slavic peoples. The Teutonic Knights — monks who were soldiers — conquering territory in the name of Christ.

Each crusade reveals the same mechanism: sacred language ("Deus vult!") deployed by institutional power for territorial, financial, and political purposes. The outer form (pilgrimage, sacred duty, defense of Christendom) seized by self-love (conquest, profit, power).
