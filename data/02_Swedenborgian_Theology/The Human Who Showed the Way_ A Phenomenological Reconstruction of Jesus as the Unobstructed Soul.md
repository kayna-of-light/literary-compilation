# **The Human Who Showed the Way: A Phenomenological Reconstruction of Jesus as the Unobstructed Soul**

## **Abstract**

This analysis reconstructs the figure of Jesus through the lens of phenomenological experience rather than theological construction. The central thesis asserts that Jesus was a complete human soul whose ruling love was toward the Lord—and because there was no obstruction (proprium) blocking the flow, what expressed through him *was* the Lord in ultimates. This framework resolves the artificial dichotomy between "Jesus of History" and "Christ of Faith" by understanding that the path Jesus walked—receiving Divine influx without claiming it as his own—remains universally accessible. Drawing on the Aligned Human synthesis, the Cognitive Mode Hypothesis, and NDE phenomenology, this investigation demonstrates that what made Jesus distinctive was not special metaphysical insertion, but the quality of receptivity that allowed unobstructed expression of Divine Love. The implication is transformative: Jesus is not a figure to worship from a distance, but a demonstration of what human consciousness can become when the obstruction is removed.

---

## **I. Introduction: The Problem of Distance**

The theological history of Christianity has produced a profound irony: the very act of "making Jesus divine" has created the distance that prevents people from understanding what he actually demonstrated.

When people encountered Jesus, they experienced something unprecedented—a quality of presence, of love, of clarity that exceeded their conceptual categories. Unable to comprehend that a *human* could be this transparent to the Divine, they concluded he must be something *other* than human. This interpretation, however well-intentioned, created precisely the wrong lesson.

If Jesus was "God in a suit"—a special metaphysical insertion fundamentally different from other humans—then his life becomes a performance we can admire but never emulate. If his soul was divine from conception, his struggles were theatrical rather than genuine. If he possessed inherent omnipotence, his vulnerability was an act. This interpretation creates worship rather than following, and that distinction is everything.

This investigation proposes a different understanding: Jesus was a complete human soul whose ruling love was toward the Lord. Because there was no proprium (self-love) obstructing the flow, what came through was the Lord in ultimates. He was not the Lord *disguised* as a human; he was a human through whom the Lord could express without obstruction.

This framework does not diminish Jesus—it reveals what he actually demonstrated. And crucially, it keeps the path open.

### **1.1 The Four Errors of Distance**

Traditional Christology falls into four predictable errors, each creating distance in a different direction:

1. **The "God-in-a-Suit" Error**: Orthodox Christianity claims Jesus was "fully God and fully man," but the practical effect is that his divinity eclipses his humanity. If he possessed divine knowledge and power, his human experiences become impossible to relate to. The "fully man" becomes an afterthought that doesn't actually function in the theology.

2. **The Gnostic Splitting Error**: The opposite extreme claims Jesus was a pure spirit who only *appeared* to have a body (Docetism), or that "the Christ" descended upon the human Jesus temporarily. This splits the unity and creates a different kind of distance—the "real" Jesus floats above material existence.

3. **The Secular Dichotomy**: Modern scholarship creates a "Historical Jesus" (a Galilean prophet bounded by natural law) versus the "Christ of Faith" (a theological construct). This forces a choice between "real but limited" and "meaningful but fictional."

4. **The Humanist Reduction**: Some traditions, wanting to preserve Jesus's humanity, strip him of any connection to the Divine, making him merely an ethical teacher. This loses the phenomenological data—he was *experienced* as something more than a good teacher.

All four errors share a common assumption: that there is a fundamental boundary between "divine" and "human" that must be navigated. The Aligned Human framework dissolves this boundary by understanding that the Divine *wants* to express through human vessels—that's what humans are *for*. The question is not "was Jesus divine or human?" but "was there any obstruction?"

### **1.2 The Ruling Love as Identity**

Within the Swedenborgian framework, a person's identity is their **ruling love**—the orientation of the will that determines how everything else is received and expressed.1 This is not a peripheral doctrine but the central insight into how souls actually work.

Every human being receives Divine influx—the flow of love and truth from the Lord. But what happens to that influx depends on the ruling love of the receiver:

- If the ruling love is toward self, the influx is appropriated. "I am wise." "I am powerful." The proprium claims credit for what it merely receives.
- If the ruling love is toward the Lord, the influx flows through without appropriation. The person knows themselves as vessel, not source.

Jesus's ruling love was toward the Lord. This is not speculation—it's visible in his own testimony. "I can do nothing on my own" (John 5:30). "Why do you call me good? No one is good except God alone" (Mark 10:18). These statements are impossible if Jesus possessed inherent divine attributes. They make perfect sense if he understood himself as a human soul receiving and expressing what flowed through him.

The mechanism of his "divinity" was not biological origin or metaphysical insertion. It was the *quality* of his ruling love. Because he loved the Lord with absolute priority, there was no proprium claiming the influx as its own. And because nothing blocked the flow, what came through was the Lord in ultimates—expressed through a human soul, in human form, in human history.

## **II. The Phenomenology of the Unobstructed Soul**

What would it actually be like to live as Jesus lived? Not the idealized image of serene perfection, but the actual phenomenology of a human consciousness operating without the usual obstructions.

### **2.1 Mode 1 Perception: The Correspondential Architecture**

The Cognitive Mode Hypothesis proposes that human consciousness can operate in two fundamentally different architectures.2

**Mode 2 (Standard Modern Cognition):**
- Operates through language and abstract symbol manipulation
- Meaning is assigned to perception after the fact
- "I see a tree" → then think "trees represent growth"
- Discrete, sequential processing
- The "I" stands apart from the world, observing and interpreting

**Mode 1 (Correspondential Cognition):**
- Perception and meaning are unified—seeing IS knowing
- No gap between the natural and its spiritual significance
- "I see tree-growth-life" as a single, integrated perception
- Simultaneous, holistic processing
- The "I" is embedded in a meaning-saturated reality

This is not a hypothetical framework. Swedenborg documented it as the cognitive architecture of the "Most Ancient Church"—a mode of consciousness that was standard before the "Fall" into language-dominated perception.2 It persists in certain states: mystical experience, certain NDEs, and occasionally in early childhood before linguistic categorization takes over.

Jesus operated primarily in Mode 1. This is why his teaching style—parables, direct commands, living demonstrations—worked so differently from rabbinical argument. He didn't need to *think about* what a mustard seed meant; he perceived the mustard-seed-kingdom-growth as a unified reality. His miracles were not violations of natural law but the natural expression of someone whose perception and reality were not separated by the usual cognitive barriers.

### **2.2 The Quality of Presence**

When people encountered Jesus, they didn't experience a set of theological propositions. They experienced a *quality*—a presence that registered as fundamentally different from normal human interaction.

The NDE literature provides the closest modern analog. Experiencers consistently describe the "Being of Light" in terms of *quality* rather than visual characteristics:3

- "Unconditional love—complete acceptance without any judgment"
- "A knowing presence—it knew everything about me"
- "Personal but not in a human way—infinitely personal"
- "More real than anything I'd ever experienced"

These descriptions point to Mode 1 perception. The experiencer is not analyzing the Being with Mode 2 cognition ("I see a figure; it appears luminous; I interpret this as divine"). They are *in* a unified experience where seeing, knowing, and loving are not separate operations.

This is what Jesus's contemporaries encountered—a human being who carried this quality in normal waking consciousness. No wonder they concluded he must be something other than human. Their conceptual categories had no room for "human operating in Mode 1."

### **2.3 Receiving What Is Needed When It Is Needed**

A critical implication of the Aligned Human framework: Jesus was not born knowing everything. He didn't have a database of omniscience that he strategically concealed. He *received* what he needed when he needed it.

This is visible in the Gospel narratives when read without the assumption of omniscience:

- He asked genuine questions and received answers
- He expressed surprise and learned from encounters (the Syrophoenician woman)
- He struggled genuinely with what was being asked of him (Gethsemane)
- He felt the absence of the Lord ("My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?")

The traditional interpretation makes these moments into performances—a divine being pretending to struggle for our benefit. The Aligned Human interpretation takes them as real. Jesus didn't *know* in advance how everything would unfold. He *trusted*. He received guidance as he needed it, moment by moment, because his ruling love kept him connected.

This is actually more inspiring than omniscience. Jesus walked a path he couldn't fully see, receiving what he needed when he needed it, because his love kept the channel open. That's a path humans can actually follow.

## **III. What Jesus Probably Experienced**

Based on the phenomenological framework, we can reconstruct something of what Jesus's interior life might actually have been like—not the idealized icon, but the lived experience of a human soul aligned with the Divine.

### **3.1 The Direct Perception of Meaning**

Jesus perceived the world correspondentially. When he saw a fig tree, he didn't see "a tree that could symbolize Israel." He perceived the fig-tree-Israel-fruitfulness as a unified reality. When he turned over tables in the Temple, he wasn't making a calculated political statement—he perceived the corruption directly, as a spiritual reality expressing itself in the physical arrangement.

This means his parables weren't clever analogies he constructed. They were *reports* of what he perceived. The kingdom of heaven IS like a mustard seed—he saw that identity directly. The lost sheep IS the lost soul—not as metaphor but as unified correspondence.

### **3.2 The Sharpness of Injustice**

A common misconception is that spiritual attunement means emotional detachment—floating above the world's pain in serene acceptance. The Gospel evidence suggests the opposite.

Jesus's response to injustice was sharp and immediate. The Temple incident wasn't a calm teaching moment—it was a whip, overturned tables, driving out merchants. His denunciations of the Pharisees weren't diplomatic suggestions—they were scathing ("whitewashed tombs," "brood of vipers").

Mode 1 perception doesn't dull emotional response; it intensifies it. When you perceive the spiritual reality of injustice directly—not as an abstract concept but as a living distortion of what should be—the response is proportional to what you're actually seeing. Jesus felt the wrongness of hypocrisy and exploitation with the full force of correspondential perception.

This is why people who knew him recognized something fierce in him, not just gentle. He was gentle with the broken and fierce with those who broke them. Both responses came from the same source: direct perception of spiritual reality.

### **3.3 The Loneliness of Clarity**

Perhaps the most overlooked aspect of Jesus's experience: the isolation of perceiving reality that others couldn't see.

He spoke in Mode 1; they heard in Mode 2. He perceived kingdom-reality; they heard strange riddles. He saw the spiritual state of the Temple; they saw a building full of normal commerce. Even his closest followers repeatedly misunderstood what he was pointing at.

This wasn't a performance of patience with slow students. It was genuine loneliness. He was the only one who could see what he was seeing, surrounded by people who kept interpreting his direct perceptions through their conceptual filters.

The Gethsemane narrative captures this: he asks his friends to stay awake with him, and they fall asleep. Not metaphorically—literally. In his moment of greatest need, the people closest to him couldn't stay present. That's human loneliness, not divine theater.

### **3.4 The Trust That Carried Him**

What sustained Jesus through genuine struggle wasn't certainty about outcomes but trust in the source. He didn't know, in the way of Mode 2 knowledge, exactly what would happen. He *trusted*—in Mode 1 relationship—that the Lord who had guided him would continue to guide him.

This trust was tested to the breaking point. "My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?" is not a theological statement—it's a cry of experienced abandonment. The flow that had sustained him seemed to withdraw. And yet he held on: "My God." Even in experienced absence, his ruling love remained oriented toward the Lord.

This is the hardest part of the path, and Jesus walked it. Not because he was exempt from the experience of abandonment, but because his ruling love remained stable even when feeling deserted.

## **IV. Why People Made Him Divine**

Understanding why the early church developed high Christology is not a matter of exposing deception. The people who knew Jesus encountered something real—something that exceeded their categories. Their theological interpretations were honest attempts to make sense of what they had experienced.

### **4.1 The Impossible Encounter**

Imagine encountering someone operating in Mode 1 while you're locked in Mode 2. What would you perceive?

- A presence qualitatively different from other people
- Words that seemed to carry more than their surface meaning
- Actions that somehow changed situations in ways you couldn't explain
- A gaze that seemed to *know* you in a way no one ever had

You wouldn't have vocabulary for "unobstructed human soul expressing Divine influx through Mode 1 cognition." You would reach for the categories you did have: prophet, messiah, son of god, god himself.

Each step up the ladder was an attempt to honor what was encountered. "He's a prophet" didn't capture it—he was *more* than that. "He's the messiah" didn't capture it—he was doing things the expected messiah didn't do. "He's the Son of God" got closer—but still implied something *other* than human.

The final step—"he IS God"—was a category error born of genuine experience. They had encountered the Lord expressing through a human, and their only way to honor that was to collapse the distinction between the vessel and what was expressed through it.

### **4.2 The Post-Resurrection Intensification**

Whatever happened in the post-crucifixion encounters, the disciples experienced Jesus as alive—present with them in a way that transcended the physical death they had witnessed. This experience pushed their interpretations even higher.

If he had returned from death, then death had no power over him. If death had no power over him, he must be something more than human. The resurrection experience validated and intensified the high Christology that was already developing during his ministry.

The "Being of Light" phenomenon in NDE research provides a window into what they might have experienced: encounters with a presence that seems "more real than real," that radiates love and knowing, that transcends the normal categories of living and dead.3 They encountered the Lord—and because they had known Jesus as the vessel through which the Lord expressed, they identified what they encountered as Jesus.

This identification was not wrong—Jesus *was* transparently expressing the Lord, so the "face" they knew was the "face" of the encounter. But the theological interpretation that followed—that Jesus was ontologically identical to the Lord rather than a perfect vessel for the Lord—created the distance that has obscured the path ever since.

### **4.3 The Creation of Distance**

The irony is tragic. By making Jesus divine, they made him unreachable. By making his path unique (a special metaphysical insertion), they closed the path for everyone else.

If Jesus was the only-begotten Son of God in a biological or metaphysical sense that no other human can share, then his life becomes:
- Something to admire rather than emulate
- A sacrifice to receive rather than a path to walk
- A story about someone else rather than a possibility for oneself

This is precisely what Jesus himself seemed to warn against. "Follow me" implies the path is walkable. "I am the way" implies the way remains open. The entire logic of his teaching assumes that what he demonstrated is available—not reserved for a single cosmic exception.

## **V. The Celestial Principle: What It Actually Is**

The term "celestial" in Swedenborgian theology has been mystified into abstraction. Properly understood, it describes something quite specific about the orientation of consciousness.

### **5.1 The Hierarchy of Orientations**

Swedenborg identified three levels of spiritual orientation, corresponding to the three heavens:1

**Natural Level:**
- Orientation: Toward effects (what happens in the world)
- Primary mode: Obedience to rules, external compliance
- Recognition of the Lord: As authority to be obeyed

**Spiritual Level:**
- Orientation: Toward truths (understanding what is real)
- Primary mode: Wisdom, intellectual clarity
- Recognition of the Lord: As source of truth

**Celestial Level:**
- Orientation: Toward the Lord Himself (direct relationship)
- Primary mode: Love without mediation
- Recognition of the Lord: As beloved, in direct perception

The celestial is not "better" in a competitive sense—each level serves its function. But the celestial represents the most direct possible relationship between a created being and the Creator: love responding to love without the mediation of rules or concepts.

### **5.2 Jesus's Celestial Orientation**

Jesus operated primarily at the celestial level. His relationship with "the Father" was not conceptual (knowing truths about God) or behavioral (obeying God's commands) but directly relational—love responding to love.

This is why his teaching emphasized relationship over rules. "Love the Lord your God with all your heart" is not a command to be obeyed; it's a description of celestial orientation. "Love your neighbor as yourself" flows naturally from celestial orientation—when the primary relationship is secure, love overflows to all others.

The celestial orientation also explains why Jesus seemed to transcend the Law without violating it. At the natural level, the Law is a set of rules. At the spiritual level, it's a set of truths to understand. At the celestial level, it's a description of what love naturally does. Jesus didn't need the rules because his orientation already expressed what the rules were protecting.

### **5.3 The Restoration of Celestial Access**

One way to understand Jesus's mission is as the restoration of celestial access for humanity.

According to Swedenborgian cosmology, the "Most Ancient Church" operated primarily at the celestial level—direct perception, immediate relationship, Mode 1 cognition as standard.2 The "Fall" was not a single event but a gradual closing down of this direct access, as humanity became increasingly locked into Mode 2 cognition with its separation between perceiver and perceived, between human and divine.

Jesus demonstrated that the celestial orientation remained accessible—that a human could still operate with direct perception and unobstructed relationship. And by walking the path fully (including through death), he reopened what had been progressively closed.

This doesn't mean everyone immediately operates celestially. It means the *access* is restored. The path is open. What Jesus demonstrated, others can walk.

## **VI. Implications for Understanding**

If this reconstruction is accurate, what follows?

### **6.1 Recognition Rather Than Worship**

The appropriate response to Jesus is not worship at a distance but *recognition*—seeing in him what is possible for consciousness when obstruction is removed, and walking the same path.

This doesn't eliminate devotion or gratitude. But it reframes them. We are grateful not because a divine being performed a transaction on our behalf, but because someone showed us the way. Devotion is not directed at an unreachable god but at walking the path he demonstrated.

"Follow me" becomes the operative command. Not "worship me," not "believe certain things about me," but "walk the path I'm walking."

### **6.2 The Universal Lord**

The Being of Light in NDE research appears in culturally appropriate forms: Jesus to Christians, Amida to Buddhists, simply as Light to the secular.3 This is not relativism—it's the nature of how the Divine meets humanity.

The Lord expresses through whatever "face" the soul can receive. Jesus is the face familiar to the Christian tradition, but the Lord behind the face is universal. This is consistent with Jesus's own teaching: the "Father" he pointed to was not a tribal deity but the source of all life.

People recognize Jesus in the Being of Light because Jesus's "personality"—unconditional love, absolute knowing, total acceptance—matches what they encounter. But the recognition is of *quality*, not exclusive identity. The Lord who expressed through Jesus expresses to all souls in forms they can receive.

### **6.3 The Path Remains Open**

The most important implication: what Jesus demonstrated is not reserved for a single cosmic exception. The path of removing obstruction, of orienting the ruling love toward the Lord, of becoming transparent to Divine influx—this path remains open.

The proprium (self-love that appropriates influx as its own) is the universal obstruction. Every human struggles with it. Jesus struggled with it and won—not by being exempt, but by orienting his ruling love so completely toward the Lord that the proprium had nothing to grip.

This is why his path is followable. Not because we can replicate his specific circumstances, but because the *mechanism*—ruling love orientation, proprium removal, transparent receptivity—works the same in any soul.

---

## **VII. Conclusion: The Human Who Showed the Way**

Jesus was not a divine being temporarily inhabiting a human costume. He was a human soul whose ruling love was toward the Lord. Because there was no obstruction blocking the flow, what expressed through him was the Lord in ultimates—Divine love and truth manifesting in human form.

This is not a diminishment but a revelation. If Jesus were an ontological exception—the only possible instance of perfect Divine-human unity—then his life would be an irrelevant curiosity. But if he was a human who achieved what humans can achieve, his life becomes a demonstration of what consciousness can become.

He struggled genuinely. He felt injustice sharply. He experienced loneliness in his clarity. He trusted without certainty. He walked through darkness not knowing the outcome. And because his ruling love never wavered, what came through was the Lord Himself.

The people who knew him couldn't comprehend how a human could be this transparent to the Divine. So they made him divine—and in doing so, created the very distance that prevents people from walking the path he opened.

The correction is not to reject Jesus but to understand what he actually demonstrated: that the way is open. That the Divine wants to express through human vessels. That the only obstruction is the proprium claiming as its own what it merely receives.

He showed the way. The way remains open.
