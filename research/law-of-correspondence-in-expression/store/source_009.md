# Source 009 — Volkonsky, *Vyrazitel'nyi chelovek* (1913)

**Citation**: Volkonsky, Sergei. *Vyrazitel'nyi chelovek: Stsenicheskoe vospitanie zhesta (po Del'sartu)* [The Expressive Man: The Stage Training of Gesture, after Delsarte]. St Petersburg: Izdanie "Apollona", 1913.

**Accessed**: 2026-10-24.
- **Copy**: the author's scan on Google Drive (131 spreads).
- **Text**: OCR'd with Tesseract (Russian); key pages read on the image.
- **Status**: not otherwise online.

## Who Volkonsky was

Prince Sergei Mikhailovich Volkonsky (1860–1937): former director of the Imperial Theatres, Dalcroze's champion in Russia, and the leading Russian Delsartean. He taught at the Moscow Art Theatre from 1911 (stores 010, 011).

## Key findings

**His sources** (bibliography, pp. 203–204). Under "Delsartean bibliography", an asterisk marks "the works which the author used in composing the present book":
- Arnaud, *François del Sarte* (Paris, 1882);
- Delaumosne, *Pratique de l'art oratoire* (Paris, 1874);
- Giraudet, *Mimique, physionomie et geste* (Paris, 1895);
- Stebbins, *Del Sarte System of Expression* (New York, 1892);
- Werner's *Delsarte System of Oratory* (1893).

He lists Henrietta Russell's *Yawning* (New York) without an asterisk.

**Borrowings from Stebbins in the body.**
- Gesture tables are "taken from the book of Mrs. Stebbins" ("взяты изъ книги Г-жи Стеббинсъ").
- He quotes "the well-known American 'Delsartean', Mrs. Russell" ("Извѣстная американская «Дельсартанка», г-жа Россель"). Russell is Hovey (store 008).

**Ch. V, "The System"** (p. 49). "Delsarte's followers, in setting out his system, like to begin not only with philosophy but even with religion. The threefold principle he laid at the base of the system is carried through all times — Chaldeans, Egyptians, Druids; through all the philosophical teachings from Pythagoras to Swedenborg; through all religious doctrines, ending with the doctrine of the Holy Trinity." ("сквозь всѣ философскія ученія отъ Пиѳагора до Сведенборга")

He goes on: "But since we are interested in the science of the expressive man, and interested only from the point of view of the manifestation of the inner man in his outward form [проявленія внутренняго человѣка во внѣшнемъ его образѣ], then … I shall lead the exposition not from religion, not from philosophy, but from man."

**Epigraph** (from Delsarte, p. 6): "Art is the finding of the sign corresponding to the essence."

## The new observation (2026-10-24)

Volkonsky's list follows the order of Stebbins's Trinity chapter (pp. 108–110), the chapter in which she quotes Swedenborg:

| Stebbins pp. 108–110 | Volkonsky p. 49 |
|---|---|
| Druids, Egyptians, Greeks, Persians, Serapis, Pythagoras, Hindoos, Plato; "the above ideas agree with Swedenborg" | Chaldeans, Egyptians, Druids; "from Pythagoras to Swedenborg" |
| Stebbins ch. IV, p. 390: the law's "recognition can be found in the cuneiform characters of Chaldean tablets" | |
| Christian Trinity throughout | "ending with the doctrine of the Holy Trinity" |

His one mention of Swedenborg is a summary of the Swedenborgian layer of the American textbook he used. He then sets the metaphysics aside and keeps what it taught: the inner man manifested in the outward form.

## Why it matters

The Russian bridge to Stanislavsky was built from the Swedenborg-citing American textbook, and its author saw the Swedenborg line and named it. What he carried forward was the content of the law, without the names.
