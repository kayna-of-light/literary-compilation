## Background Context

The Correspondential Hypothesis proposes that the 32 geometric signs cataloged by Genevieve von Petzinger are not arbitrary marks but a stable vocabulary mapping ontological realities. In this framework:

- **Aviforms** (bird shapes) = Intellectual Ideas / Thoughts — the part of the mind capable of "flying" into higher realms
- **Penniforms** (feather shapes) = Truths of Faith — knowledge that carries thought upward
- **Lines, Triangles** = Foundational principles, boundaries, limitations
- **Spirals** = Entrance to spiritual world, the gyre of transformation

If these signs correspond to specific spiritual realities with inherent vertical relationships (intellectual principles are "higher" than foundational structures), then their **spatial positioning** relative to animal figures should be consistent.

### The Prediction

> Signs corresponding to "intellectual" principles (Aviforms, Penniforms) will consistently appear in "upper" or "active" positions relative to animal figures (corresponding to affections/will). Signs corresponding to "foundational" principles (Lines, Triangles) will appear in "lower" or "supporting" positions.

### Initial Evidence

- The Lascaux Shaft Scene: Bird-headed man positioned "above/beside" the wounded bison
- Pech Merle: Aviforms reported in elevated positions relative to horse figures
- General cave art convention: Animals occupy central visual field; geometric signs appear in peripheral, boundary, or framing positions

---

## Research Question

**Primary Question**: What does the archaeological literature report about the spatial positioning of geometric signs relative to figurative (animal) imagery in European Upper Paleolithic cave art?

### Specific Sub-Questions

1. **Vertical Distribution**: Are certain geometric sign types consistently found above, below, or at the same level as animal figures? Is there quantitative data on this?

2. **Aviform Positioning**: Where do aviforms (bird-like signs) typically appear in relation to other imagery? Are they predominantly in upper register positions?

3. **Line/Triangle Positioning**: Where do lines, open angles, and triangles appear? Do they function as "ground lines," boundaries, or framing elements below or around figures?

4. **Composite Scenes Analysis**: In scenes with multiple elements (e.g., Lascaux Shaft, Trois-Frères Sorcerer panel), what is the spatial grammar? How do scholars describe the compositional logic?

5. **Statistical Studies**: Has anyone conducted quantitative analysis of sign positioning across multiple sites? What were the findings?

6. **Counter-Examples**: What examples exist where "intellectual" signs appear below or "foundational" signs appear above? How frequent are these?

## What Would Confirm the Prediction

- Quantitative data showing Aviforms/Penniforms cluster in upper registers (>70% of occurrences)
- Qualitative descriptions of Lines/Triangles as "ground lines" or "boundaries"
- Compositional analyses describing a consistent "grammar" of vertical arrangement
- Multiple scholars independently noting the same positional patterns

## What Would Falsify the Prediction

- Random distribution of sign types relative to animals (no statistically significant positioning patterns)
- Aviforms predominantly in lower positions
- Evidence that positioning is site-specific rather than universal
