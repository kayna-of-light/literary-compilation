# The 32 Geometric Signs as Correspondential Vocabulary: An Ontological Analysis of Paleolithic Semiotics

## 1. The Consistency Paradox

Paleoanthropologist Genevieve von Petzinger's systematic survey of 367 European Upper Paleolithic sites (c. 40,000–10,000 BP) identified a core vocabulary of **32 distinct geometric signs** — including aviforms, tectiforms, spirals, and claviforms — that repeat with statistical significance across hundreds of sites.[1] The critical anomaly is not merely their presence but their **stability**: 65% of sign types remained in use throughout the entire Upper Paleolithic, and two-thirds were already present when *Homo sapiens* first arrived in Europe, implying an African origin.[2]

This stability is the paradox. Cultural conventions drift over centuries — the English word "nice" meant "foolish" merely 600 years ago — yet the tectiform sign remained recognizable for over 15,000 years. Standard explanations require mechanisms of fidelity (schools, books, liturgies) that the Paleolithic record does not evidence.

### 1.1 Existing Models and Their Limits

**The Entoptic Theory** (Lewis-Williams) anchors the signs in universal neurology — phosphenes or form constants generated during trance states. This explains why humans *can* see grids or spirals, but not why *these specific 32* were codified and repeated while countless other entoptic patterns were ignored. It reduces meaning to mechanism.

**The Proto-Writing Hypothesis** (von Petzinger) suggests the signs encode data — tags, names, or messages. But the signs lack linear syntax and show no evidence of the combinatorial grammar that characterizes writing systems.

**The Mnemonic Model** (Kelly) proposes the signs served as external memory aids for oral tradition, analogous to Australian songlines or African lukasa boards. This model explains function but not the specific forms chosen.[3]

## 2. The Correspondential Hypothesis

This document investigates a fourth possibility: that the 32 signs are **graphic manifestations of perceived spiritual-natural correspondences** — forms that Paleolithic artists perceived in the relationship between natural objects and their spiritual causes, and rendered in pigment to preserve what they saw.

This hypothesis is derived from Emanuel Swedenborg's Doctrine of Correspondences, which proposes that every natural object corresponds to a spiritual reality through **vertical causality** — not through arbitrary convention but through inherent functional relationship. Light corresponds to wisdom because light *functions* as wisdom functions: enabling distinction, revealing form, dispelling obscurity. The correspondence is grounded in the physics of the object.

**If the correspondential hypothesis is correct**, the consistency paradox resolves naturally: signs depicting real correspondences would remain stable because the spiritual realities they map are stable. Any human perceiving the same spiritual-natural relationships would independently produce the same marks. The consistency would be ontological, not cultural.

**This remains a hypothesis.** The mappings below are speculative — derived from Swedenborgian correspondential principles applied to the archaeological forms. They are offered as a framework for generating testable predictions, not as established fact.

### 2.1 The Cognitive Context: Discrete Degrees

Swedenborg described three discrete degrees of human perception — **celestial**, **spiritual**, and **natural** — each operating with qualitatively different cognitive properties:

| Degree | Perception Mode | Knowledge Type | Characteristic |
|--------|----------------|----------------|----------------|
| **Celestial** | Direct, unmediated | Perception inherent in seeing | No external symbols needed |
| **Spiritual** | Learned correspondences | Knowledge of what things signify | External symbols as mnemonics |
| **Natural** | Literal, external | Knowledge of surfaces | Symbols as arbitrary convention |

In this framework, the emergence of the 32 signs marks a transition from the celestial to the spiritual degree — from direct perception to codified knowledge. The signs are not the *triumph* of abstract thought but the **compensation** for the gradual loss of direct perception. They are mnemonics for truths that were once self-evident, externalized to hold the space where intuition once operated.

The Aurignacian period (fluid, naturalistic art with minimal geometric codification) may represent the tail end of celestial-degree perception. The Magdalenian (peak geometric complexity, rigid codification) represents the full flowering of spiritual-degree knowledge — truth preserved in fixed form because it can no longer be perceived directly.

## 3. The 32 Signs: A Correspondential Catalog

The following catalog maps each of von Petzinger's 32 signs to a hypothesized Swedenborgian correspondence. The mapping principle is **function dictates correspondence**: the shape and behavior of the natural analogue reveal the spiritual reality it would embody under the correspondential framework. The individual sign-to-correspondence mappings below are hypotheses; the archaeological data (distribution, frequency, dating) are empirical.

### Category A: Dynamics of Influx and Process

*Signs hypothesized to represent movement, creation, and the flow of spiritual life.*

**1. Spiral** — *Hypothesized correspondence: Generative process, divine influx.*
Coiled line, clockwise or counterclockwise. Found in Magdalenian French sites, exploding in frequency during the Neolithic (Newgrange). Natural analogues: vine tendrils, shells, whirlpools, galaxies. Swedenborg describes the "flux" of heaven as spiral gyres and regeneration as spiraling ascent — the unfolding of creation from center (Divine) to circumference (Nature).[4]

**2. Zigzag (Chevron)** — *Hypothesized correspondence: Truth descending through discrete degrees.*
Continuous angular line, often in parallel series. Extremely common across all periods; etched on shells by *Homo erectus* 500,000 years ago. Natural analogues: lightning, river meanders, mountain peaks. The zigzag mimics the flash of lightning, which in correspondences signifies sudden illumination of truth — a "stepping down" through successive degrees.[5]

**3. Serpentiform** — *Hypothesized correspondence: The sensual degree of the mind.*
Undulating wavy line, distinct from the sharp zigzag. Widespread, often near ground level. Natural analogues: snake, stream. The serpent corresponds to the sensual/corporeal degree — in order, wisdom ("be ye wise as serpents"); in disorder, persuasive falsity.[4]

**4. Line** — *Hypothesized correspondence: Boundary, terminus, singular truth.*
Single straight stroke, often grouped in parallel series. The most frequent sign, present at >70% of sites.[6] Natural analogues: horizon, path, boundary. A line defines a limit; in the spiritual world, lines mark boundaries between states or societies.

**5. Finger Fluting (Macaroni)** — *Hypothesized correspondence: Flow of perception.*
Parallel lines traced by fingers in soft clay or moonmilk, often meandering. Found in deep chambers (e.g., Rouffignac). Unlike the rigid line, fluting is organic and fluid — possibly representing the continuous flow of spiritual life before codification.

### Category B: Architecture of the Mind

*Signs hypothesized to represent structures, containers, and states of the will.*

**6. Tectiform** — *Hypothesized correspondence: The human will / the structured mind.*
Roof-like shape with central pillar and base. Highly localized to the Dordogne/Lot region (Magdalenian).[6] Natural analogue: house, dwelling. In correspondences, a "house" is the human mind, with rooms representing different affections. Its late appearance (Magdalenian) may mark the shift from fluid perception to fixed doctrine.

**7. Spanish Tectiform** — *Hypothesized correspondence: Systematic doctrine.*
Larger rectangular compartmentalized grid, distinct from the French roof-shape. Found in Northern Spain (El Castillo, La Pasiega). The compartmentalization suggests ordering of the mind into distinct chambers — a more complex doctrinal structure than the French variant.[7]

**8. Quadrangle** — *Hypothesized correspondence: Justice, celestial order.*
Square, rectangle, or trapezoid. Widespread. Swedenborg links the "square" (as in the New Jerusalem) to justice and the union of Good and Truth — completeness and impartiality.[8]

**9. Cupule** — *Hypothesized correspondence: Receptivity.*
Small circular depression pounded into rock. One of the oldest signs, found globally (Lower Paleolithic). Natural analogues: cup, mortar, womb. The "vessel" of the heart or will that receives influx.[9]

**10. Reniform** — *Hypothesized correspondence: Purification, judgment.*
Kidney-shaped. Less common, found in French caves. The kidneys separate pure blood from impure serum — corresponding to the faculty of distinguishing truth from falsity.

**11. Cordiform** — *Hypothesized correspondence: Celestial love, the will.*
Heart-shaped. Rare. The heart corresponds to the will and the vital heat of love.

### Category C: The Intellectual Principle

*Signs hypothesized to represent thought, reason, and the elevation of mind. In Swedenborg, "birds" correspond to things of the understanding.*

**12. Aviform** — *Hypothesized correspondence: Intellectual ideas, thoughts.*
"Placard" shape resembling a bird in flight. Found in Pech Merle, Cougnac, Cosquer. Birds correspond to rational concepts that "fly" above the earth (the will). Frequently associated with "Wounded Men" panels, potentially depicting spiritual temptations — battles of intellect against falsity.[4]

**13. Penniform** — *Hypothesized correspondence: Sensual truths, knowledge.*
Feather-like branching lines. Common (25% of sites).[10] Feathers clothe the bird as factual truths clothe understanding. When superimposed on animals, may signify the "clothing" of that affection with truth.

**14. Flabelliform** — *Hypothesized correspondence: Opening of the mind.*
Fan-shaped radiating lines from a base. Less common. The fan moves air (spirit) — possibly corresponding to the expansion of understanding and reception of new truths.

**15. Y-Sign** — *Hypothesized correspondence: Choice, bifurcation.*
Forked line. Widespread. Natural analogue: path divergence, tree crotch. The point of decision between two paths. Recent research links Y-signs to biological cycles (birthing), suggesting "bringing forth" in act.[11]

### Category D: Agents of Power and Presence

*Signs hypothesized to represent agency, distinctness, and divine-human contact.*

**16. Claviform** — *Hypothesized correspondence: Power of truth.*
Club-shaped, P-shaped. Common in Magdalenian (16% of French sites).[12] A club is a weapon of the hand (power). In correspondences, it may represent the power of the literal sense of the Word — truth used to overcome falsity.

**17. Hand Stencil (Negative)** — *Hypothesized correspondence: Power, presence, witness.*
Outline of a hand created by blowing pigment around it. Universal and ancient. The hand corresponds to power and ability. The *negative* stencil — the self's absence surrounded by the mark of its presence — is striking: possibly acknowledging the self's nothingness before the Divine while marking its witness.

**18. Positive Hand (Handprint)** — *Hypothesized correspondence: Appropriation, will.*
Paint applied to hand and pressed on wall. Touching signifies communication and transfer — an act of will and possession, making the state of the cave one's own.

**19. Cruciform** — *Hypothesized correspondence: Conjunction, temptation.*
Cross (+ or x). Rare (13% of sites). The intersection of Good (vertical) and Truth (horizontal) — also the state of temptation (the cross to be borne) that leads to conjunction.[13]

**20. Dot / Disk** — *Hypothesized correspondence: The beginning, divine good.*
Solid point or circle. The oldest sign (El Castillo, 41,000 BP).[14] The dot is the center from which all geometry unfolds — the first point of creation. A red disk may correspond to the Sun of Heaven (Divine Love).

**21. Triangle** — *Hypothesized correspondence: Fullness of truth, elevation.*
Three-sided polygon. Natural analogues: mountain, arrowhead. The number three corresponds to fullness regarding truth. An upward triangle represents elevation; a downward triangle, influx or receptivity.

### Category E: Signs of Structure and Limitation

*Signs hypothesized to represent order, measurement, and the grid of reality.*

**22. Crosshatch (Grid)** — *Hypothesized correspondence: Systematic order.*
Intersecting parallel lines, lattice. Found from Blombos (75,000 BP) through Lascaux. Natural analogues: net, woven fabric. The grid represents the ordering of truths into a system — the "net" that catches and holds knowledge.[6]

**23. Scalariform** — *Hypothesized correspondence: Discrete degrees, ascent.*
Ladder-shape. Found at La Pasiega (possibly Neanderthal). The ladder is a natural symbol of Swedenborg's discrete degrees — step-wise progression from natural to spiritual to celestial.[15]

**24. Pectiform** — *Hypothesized correspondence: Separation, ordering.*
Comb-shape. To comb is to disentangle — ordering the natural mind, separating tangled affections and aligning them with truth.[16]

**25. Unciform** — *Hypothesized correspondence: Attachment, conjunction.*
Hook-shape. That which connects or appropriates — a "hook" of love or interest that attaches the mind to an object.[17]

**26. Open Angle** — *Hypothesized correspondence: Incomplete state, receptivity.*
V-shape or incomplete triangle. A vessel not yet closed — receptivity open to influx.[10]

**27. W-Sign** — *Hypothesized correspondence: Power in the natural degree.*
Curvilinear W. Found at Chauvet. Natural analogues: mammoth tusks, waves. Often associated with the mammoth (power), possibly representing the "horns" of power in the natural degree.[18]

**28. Circle** — *Hypothesized correspondence: Eternity, unity, heaven.*
Open circle. The circle has no end — representing the form of heaven and the cycle of life.

**29. Oval** — *Hypothesized correspondence: Potential, gestation.*
Egg-shape. The state of containment before birth — life held in potential.

**30. Half-Circle** — *Hypothesized correspondence: Faith (reflected light).*
Arc. The moon corresponds to faith (reflected light of truth). Half-circles may map lunar phases or states of faith.[6]

**31. Segmented Cruciform** — *Hypothesized correspondence: Disjunction, crisis.*
Cross with disconnected center. The intersection is broken — a state where Good and Truth are not yet conjoined.[19]

**32. Asterisk** — *Hypothesized correspondence: Knowledge, illumination.*
Star-shape. Stars correspond to knowledges of good and truth. The asterisk marks a point of intellectual light.[20]

## 4. Pattern Analysis: Observations Worth Testing

Beyond individual sign meanings, the *distribution* and *combination* of signs generate observations that could validate or falsify the correspondential hypothesis.

### 4.1 Temporal Patterns

The archaeological record suggests a trajectory from simplicity to codification:

| Period | Dominant Signs | Hypothesized Cognitive State |
|--------|---------------|------------------------------|
| Aurignacian (40k–28k BP) | Dots, Hands, simple marks | Near-celestial: direct perception still active; signs are simple anchors |
| Gravettian (28k–22k BP) | Hand Stencils, increasing complexity | Transitional: the need to mark presence becomes urgent |
| Magdalenian (17k–10k BP) | Tectiforms, Claviforms, Aviforms, Grids | Spiritual degree: knowledge fully codified into rigid system |

If this trajectory reflects a real cognitive transition across discrete degrees, it should correlate with other markers of increasing symbolic complexity (burial practices, portable art, long-distance trade networks).

### 4.2 Spatial Distribution

Research highlights a distinction between "entrance" and "deep chamber" signs:[4]

- **Entrance/Daylight Zone**: Dominated by large animals, open angles, lines — hypothesized natural-degree truths (external, behavioral)
- **Deep/Dark Zone**: Dominated by complex signs (Tectiforms, Aviforms, Spirals) and composite figures — hypothesized spiritual/celestial truths (interior, initiatory)
- **The physical descent** into the cave may correspond to introspection — the journey inward through the degrees of the mind

### 4.3 Sign Combinations

Certain combinations recur. The Aviform paired with the Spiral (Lascaux Shaft Scene, Pech Merle) may represent the intellect entering the spiral of spiritual transformation. Tectiforms paired with rows of dots may diagram the "organized mind" — states of knowledge contained within a doctrinal structure.

These are observations, not conclusions. They require systematic quantitative study to confirm or refute.

## 5. Framework Comparison

| Feature | Entoptic (Lewis-Williams) | Proto-Writing (von Petzinger) | Correspondential (This Document) |
|---------|---------------------------|-------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| **Origin of Signs** | Neural biology (phosphenes) | Cultural convention | Ontological reality perceived through higher degrees |
| **Explains Consistency** | Shared neurology | Trade/migration | Shared perception of stable reality |
| **Explains Selection** | Weak (why *these* 32?) | Weak (why no syntax?) | Strong — correspondences are grounded in function |
| **Status** | Testable, partially confirmed | Testable, partially confirmed | Hypothesis; awaiting independent empirical test |

## 6. Testable Predictions

If the correspondential hypothesis is correct:

1. **Contextual Rigidity**: Signs mapped to "intellectual" principles (Aviforms, Penniforms) should consistently appear in upper or active positions relative to animal figures. Signs mapped to "foundational" principles (Lines, Triangles) should appear in lower or supporting positions.

2. **Cross-Cultural Isomorphism**: Newly discovered Ice Age art in unconnected regions (Sulawesi, Amazonia, Australia) should utilize the same geometric core lexicon for similar spiritual contexts, even without cultural contact.

3. **Vastation Markers**: Periods of climatic or social stress should see spikes in signs corresponding to "protection" (Tectiforms) and "order" (Crosshatch/Grid) — representing the need to build "arks" for the mind.

4. **Functional Consistency**: Where the same sign appears across cultures and periods, it should appear in functionally similar contexts — spirals at thresholds or generative points, hands at boundaries, grids at organizational or structural locations.
