# Source 013 — Walt Disney to Don Graham, 23 December 1935

**Citation**: Disney, Walt. Inter-office communication to Don Graham, December 23, 1935. Reproduced in Shamus Culhane, *Talking Animals and Other People* (New York: St. Martin's Press, 1986; Da Capo reprint 1998), 117–127, and in Walt Stanchfield, "The Disney Tradition," September 24, 1995, http://www.animationmeat.com/pdf/nineoldmen/MemoFromWalt.pdf.

**Accessed**: 2026-10-24, the animationmeat PDF (text extracted and read).

## Passages

- "I am convinced that there is a scientific approach to this business, and I think we shouldn't give up until we have found out all we can about how to teach these young fellows the business."
- "We should try to show the men ways of visualizing action in their minds, breaking the action so that the men are prepared in advance to begin animation of the action and know thoroughly what they are going to animate."
- "Many men do not realize what really makes things move - why they move - what the force behind the movement is. I think a course along that line, accompanied by practical examples of analysis and planning, would be very good. In other words, in most instances, the driving force behind the action is the mood, the personality, the attitude of the character - or all three. Therefore, the mind is the pilot. We think of things before the body does them."
- "We also do things on the spur of the moment by reaction to stimuli that are telegraphed to the mind by the nerves, etc. There are also things carried out by the subconscious mind - reflexes, actions that have become habit through repetition, instincts."
- "The first duty of the cartoon is not to picture or duplicate real action or things as they actually happen, but give a caricature of life and action."

## Why it matters

The memo asks for a course in "the force behind the movement" and names that force as the mood, the personality, the attitude. This is the order of *DLW* §§218–219 (conatus → forces → motion; will united to understanding → forces in the body → action; store 001), in a studio's words. It is also the order of Northrop's American Law of Correspondence: "every posture of the body … is but an outward expression of an inner emotion or condition of the mind" (store 007).

Walt names no source. The memo tells the teacher what to teach, not where it comes from.
