# **A Library of Biblical Correspondences in Nature and Objects According to Emanuel Swedenborg**

## **1\. Introduction: Emanuel Swedenborg and Biblical Interpretation**

Emanuel Swedenborg (1688–1772), a Swedish scientist, philosopher, and theologian, developed a unique and complex system of biblical interpretation centered on what he termed the "doctrine of correspondences". This doctrine posits a fundamental, dynamic relationship between the spiritual world (the realm of causes, thoughts, and affections) and the natural, physical world (the realm of effects, actions, and objects). According to Swedenborg, everything existing in the natural world is an ultimate effect that corresponds to, represents, and signifies something on a higher, spiritual plane. This includes not only the structure of the human body and the phenomena of nature but also, crucially, the literal text of the Bible.  
Swedenborg asserted that the Bible, or the Word, was written entirely according to these correspondences, meaning its historical narratives, laws, prophecies, and even seemingly mundane details contain an inner, spiritual sense that relates to divine truths, the process of human spiritual development (regeneration), the nature of heaven and hell, and the Lord Himself. He believed the literal sense serves as a necessary foundation and container for these deeper meanings, much like a body contains a soul. Accessing this spiritual sense, he argued, requires understanding the specific correspondences of the words, objects, figures, and events described in the text. His extensive theological works, particularly *Arcana Coelestia* (an exposition of Genesis and Exodus) and *Apocalypse Revealed*/*Apocalypse Explained* (expositions of Revelation), provide detailed, verse-by-verse analyses based on this system.  
This report aims to compile a library of specific correspondences as described by Swedenborg, focusing on natural elements, living creatures, plants, and common objects frequently mentioned in the Bible. The focus is on the inherent symbolic meaning Swedenborg attributed to these items themselves, rather than interpretations derived solely from narrative context. The information is drawn from Swedenborg's own theological writings and authoritative secondary sources compiling his system.

## **2\. The Doctrine of Correspondences Explained**

The core of Swedenborg's hermeneutic is the "doctrine of correspondences," the term he used for the inherent relationship between spiritual causes and natural effects. It is not merely a system of arbitrary symbols but a description of the fundamental structure of reality, where the visible universe is "a theater representative of the Lord’s kingdom," and that kingdom "a theater representative of the Lord himself" (*AC* §3483). Everything in nature, from the grandest celestial bodies to the smallest plant, exists because of a spiritual cause and purpose originating from the Divine.  
**Key Principles:**

* **Spiritual-Natural Link:** There is a constant influx from the spiritual world into the natural world, sustaining its existence and imbuing it with meaning. The natural world is the plane of effects, while the spiritual world is the plane of causes; nothing "can subsist from itself, but only from that which is prior to itself" (*HH* §106).  
* **Love and Wisdom:** The ultimate reality, God, is Divine Love (the source of all affection and life, corresponding to heat) and Divine Wisdom (the source of all form and order, corresponding to light). These two emanate from the Divine and create and sustain all things. The spiritual sun, seen by angels, is the first emanation of this Divine Love and Wisdom, its heat corresponding to love and its light to wisdom. The natural sun corresponds to this spiritual sun (*DLW* §§93, 157; *HH* §§116–118).  
* **Hierarchical Levels (Discrete Degrees):** Reality consists of distinct levels or "degrees"—celestial (love-based), spiritual (wisdom/truth-based), and natural (effect-based). Each lower level corresponds to and is sustained by the level above it (*DLW* §184). The human mind mirrors this structure, possessing internal (spiritual) and external (natural) aspects.  
* **Correspondence in the Word:** The Bible was written using natural language and imagery that correspond perfectly to spiritual and celestial truths. The literal sense provides the necessary base, while the spiritual sense contains the divine wisdom accessible through understanding correspondences. The Lord Himself spoke in correspondences (parables): in the world "He spoke by pure correspondences, because He spoke from the Divine" (*AE* §374).  
* **Double Meanings:** Many symbols in the Word have both a positive (good) sense, relating to heavenly qualities, and a negative (opposite) sense, relating to hellish qualities or perversions of the good—"most things in the Word have also an opposite sense" (*AC* §4236). Context determines which sense applies.  
* **Ancient Knowledge:** Swedenborg claimed this knowledge of correspondences was central to ancient civilizations—among them existed "a Word written entirely by correspondences," the Ancient Word (*SS* §102)—but was gradually lost, leading eventually to idolatry where the representations were worshipped instead of the spiritual realities they signified (*AE* §826; *WH* §12). He saw his work as restoring this lost key to understanding: "it has therefore been granted me from the Lord to unfold these things" (*AE* §5).

Understanding this doctrine is essential for interpreting the specific symbols detailed in the following sections. It provides the framework demonstrating that these meanings are not arbitrary but are rooted in Swedenborg's systematic view of a divinely ordered, interconnected reality.

## **3\. Library of Correspondences: Natural Elements**

Natural elements form a foundational category in Swedenborg's system, often corresponding to the most fundamental aspects of spiritual life, Divine attributes, or states of the church and the human mind. The natural world, sustained by the heat and light of the natural sun, serves as a mirror reflecting the spiritual realities sustained by the heat (Love) and light (Wisdom) of the spiritual sun, which is the Lord's presence in heaven.

* **Sun:**  
  * **Core Correspondence:** The Lord Himself, specifically His Divine Love (as heat) and Divine Wisdom (as light) (*HH* §§116–119).
  * **Opposite Sense:** Self-love, love of the world dominating over love to the Lord; Divine Love obscured or rejected (*AC* §2441).  
  * **Explanation:** As the natural sun is the source of natural life, heat, and light, the spiritual sun (the Lord's presence) is the source of all spiritual life, love, and wisdom. Passages where the sun is darkened signify a state where love to the Lord has perished in the church or individual.  
* **Moon:**  
  * **Core Correspondence:** Faith derived from charity; intelligence; spiritual truth (reflecting the light/wisdom of the sun/love); "the 'moon' signifies faith from the Lord in the Lord" (*HH* §119).  
  * **Opposite Sense:** Faith separated from charity; falsity; intelligence obscured or lost.  
  * **Explanation:** The moon reflects the sun's light, just as true faith and intelligence reflect the light of Divine Wisdom, which originates in Divine Love. When the moon does not give light, it signifies a loss of true faith.  
* **Stars:**  
  * **Core Correspondence:** Knowledges of good and truth derived from the Word; specific concepts and cognitions of spiritual realities (*AC* §§2495, 2849). Around angels below the heavens there appear "little stars in great abundance" (*AR* §51).
  * **Opposite Sense:** Falsities presented as knowledge; knowledges perverted or lost: stars signify truths, "and in the opposite sense falsities" (*AC* §3448).  
  * **Explanation:** Stars provide points of light against the darkness, corresponding to specific knowledges that illuminate the mind regarding spiritual matters. Falling stars signify the loss or perversion of these knowledges within the church.  
* **Earth:**  
  * **Core Correspondence:** The Church, specifically the external church; the external or natural mind of a person; the general human race. The Lord's kingdom (*AC* §§566, 662).
  * **Opposite Sense:** The church devastated or lacking genuine good and truth; damnation; hell: "when there is no church with man, there is damnation" (*AR* §285).  
  * **Explanation:** The earth is the foundation upon which life exists and grows, corresponding to the church as the foundation for spiritual life on the natural plane, or the natural mind as the basis for the spiritual mind. The state of the "earth" in spiritual visions reflects the state of the church or the spirits dwelling there.  
* **Water:**  
  * **Core Correspondence:** Truth, particularly natural truth; truths that cleanse and refresh the mind; knowledge; the literal sense of the Word—"many waters" are "Divine truth in ultimates" (*AC* §2702; *AE* §71).  
  * **Opposite Sense:** Falsity; truth profaned or falsified.  
  * **Explanation:** Water cleanses the body and quenches thirst, corresponding to how truth cleanses the spirit from falsities and satisfies the mind's thirst for understanding. Different bodies of water (seas, rivers) represent different kinds or collections of truth.  
* **Fire:**  
  * **Core Correspondence:** Love, in both good and evil senses; Divine Love (celestial); spiritual love; affection; "in the spiritual sense fire is love" (*HH* §118; *AC* §934).
  * **Opposite Sense:** Evil loves, such as self-love and love of the world; hatred; destructive passions; hellish love (*HH* §§566–575).  
  * **Explanation:** Fire provides warmth, corresponding to the warmth of love and affection in the spirit. Divine Love itself is described as a spiritual fire. Consuming fire often represents judgment on evils or the intensity of evil loves.  
* **Air (or Wind):**  
  * **Core Correspondence:** The activity of the understanding; thought; perception; spiritual influence; influx of truth or falsity. "The 'air' signifies thought because respiration, which is effected by means of the air, corresponds to thought" (*AE* §1012); wind signifies influx from heaven, and in the opposite sense from hell (*AR* §343).  
  * **Opposite Sense:** Influx of falsity; foolish thoughts; spiritual devastation.  
  * **Explanation:** Air is necessary for breath and life, and is the medium for sound (speech/thought), corresponding to the life of thought and understanding. Winds represent spiritual influxes affecting thought; holding back the winds (Rev 7:1) is the Lord's "withholding and restraining … of a nearer and thence more powerful influx into the lower parts, where the good were conjoined with the evil" (*AR* §343).  
* **Mountain:**  
  * **Core Correspondence:** Celestial love; love to the Lord; the highest or inmost state of the church or mind; heaven. The Lord Himself (*AC* §6435).
  * **Opposite Sense:** The evil love of self; pride: "the mountain" can mean "the love of self and the love of the world" (*AC* §1691).  
  * **Explanation:** Mountains are elevated parts of the earth, corresponding to elevated states of love and perception. Worship was often performed on mountains, signifying elevation towards the Lord.  
* **Valley:**  
  * **Core Correspondence:** Lower or external things of the church or mind (*AC* §§1723, 3417); what is lowly, hence humility (*AC* §3527); obscurity of truth—"to walk in a shady valley" is "an obscure understanding that does not see truths from light" (*AE* §727).
  * **Opposite Sense:** Low or base things; ignorance; state of damnation: worship made "more unclean and profane" (*AC* §1292); the valley of Hinnom signifies a hell (*AE* §659).  
  * **Explanation:** Valleys are lower than mountains, corresponding to less elevated spiritual states or the external aspects of the church and life. They can represent humility or states where truth is less clearly perceived.  
* **River:**  
  * **Core Correspondence:** Intelligence; wisdom; flowing truth derived from a higher source (like a mountain/love) (*AC* §108).
  * **Opposite Sense:** Reasoning from falsities; false intelligence: "falsities from reasonings" (*AC* §790).  
  * **Explanation:** Rivers flow and provide water (truth) for the land (church/mind), corresponding to the flow of intelligence and wisdom that enriches spiritual life. The source and quality of the river indicate the nature of the intelligence.  
* **Sea:**  
  * **Core Correspondence:** Collections of truths in the natural mind; general knowledges (scientifica)—not the natural mind as a whole, but "the natural man in respect to its knowledge there" (*AE* §511), "the generals of truth" (*AE* §275); the ultimate boundaries of the church or life, "where truth is in its outmost" (*AE* §342); the external or literal sense of the Word (*AE* §355).
  * **Opposite Sense:** Collections of falsities; worldly and corporeal things limiting the spiritual; hell. When the natural is separated from the spiritual, its knowledge is all that remains, "dead knowledge and false knowledge" (*AE* §511), and the sea "signifies damnation and hell, where all are in their own (proprium), because they are in the natural man separated from the spiritual" (*AE* §355).
  * **Explanation:** Seas are vast collections of water (truth/knowledge) on the natural level; "seas" signify "a collection of knowledges" (*AC* §28). They represent the general store of natural knowledge or the outermost limits of spiritual understanding: in the spiritual world seas appear "about the outmost boundaries where spiritual societies or heaven itself ends" (*AE* §511). The waves of the sea are "disputation and reasoning" (*AE* §511); floods and inundations are temptations (*AC* §705).

## **4\. Library of Correspondences: Living Creatures**

In Swedenborg's system, animals are not merely biological entities but correspond directly to the affections, feelings, thoughts, and principles within the human spirit (the mind or soul). The inherent nature and behavior of each animal reflect specific spiritual or psychological qualities. Good and useful animals generally correspond to good affections and true thoughts, while fierce, unclean, or poisonous animals correspond to evil affections, lusts, and false thoughts. Angelic conversations about specific affections can even manifest as corresponding animals in the spiritual world (*HH* §110; *AC* §3218).  
Analysis of these correspondences reveals a distinct structure mirroring the levels of the human mind described by Swedenborg (natural, spiritual, celestial). For instance, among the gentle creatures lambs are linked to love to the Lord, "which love is one with innocence" (the *celestial* mind), and sheep to "love towards the neighbor, which love is charity" (the *spiritual* mind) (*AE* §314), whereas cattle relate to the affections of the *natural* mind (*AE* §279). Similarly, birds like the eagle, capable of high flight and keen sight, correspond to rational thought and higher understanding (*AC* §3901), while creatures bound to the earth or water, like fish or serpents, relate to more external forms of knowledge or the lowest, sensual level of the mind. This demonstrates that the animal kingdom, in its vast diversity, serves as a corresponding image of the entire landscape of the human psyche, from its highest spiritual loves down to its most basic sensual inputs and reasonings.

* **Lion:**  
  * **Core Correspondence:** The power of truth, especially Divine Truth derived from the Word, in its strength and royalty; the Lord as to the power of Divine Truth: "truth in power from good" (*AC* §§6367, 6441).
  * **Opposite Sense:** The power of falsity destroying truth; destructive evil; reasoning from falsity as if it were true—"the evil of the love of self in its power" (*AC* §6367).  
  * **Related Faculty/Affection:** Power derived from truth (good sense) or falsity (bad sense).  
  * **Explanation:** The lion's strength corresponds to the power inherent in truth, particularly when combating falsities. The "Lion of the tribe of Judah" (Revelation 5:5) represents the Lord's omnipotence through His Divine Truth. In the vision of the Cherubim, the lion's face represents Divine Truth as to power, "Divine truth proceeding from the Lord in respect to power and effect" (*AE* §278). Conversely, the destructive nature of a lion can represent the devastating power of falsity derived from evil (*AC* §6367), or reasoning that appears powerful but is based on falsehood.  
* **Lamb:**  
  * **Core Correspondence:** Innocence, particularly the good of innocence; love to the Lord; the Lord Himself, especially His Divine Human and the good therein (*AE* §314).
  * **Opposite Sense:** Innocence profaned or merely external/feigned: the beast's "two horns like a lamb" signify that they teach from the Word "as if it were from the Lord’s Divine truth, and yet it is truth falsified" (*AR* §595).
  * **Related Faculty/Affection:** Love to the Lord, which is one with innocence (the celestial mind); gentleness; trust.
  * **Explanation:** Lambs embody gentleness and innocence, corresponding to the inmost affection, love towards the Lord, "which love is one with innocence" (*AE* §314). This is a central symbol for Jesus Christ ("Lamb of God") in the New Testament and especially Revelation, signifying His Divine Innocence and role in redemption. Sacrificial lambs represented the purification of the inmost and "the good of innocence in every state" (*AC* §§9990, 10132).  
* **Dove:**  
  * **Core Correspondence:** The goods and truths of faith; the affections of truth and good pertaining to the spiritual person; purification and regeneration (*AC* §870). At the Lord's baptism, "the holy of faith" (*AC* §870).
  * **Opposite Sense:** Falsified truths of faith: Ephraim "like a silly dove, without heart" signifies "no understanding, because there is no truth and no affection of truth and good" (*AE* §654).  
  * **Related Faculty/Affection:** Affections related to spiritual truth and faith; a specific quality of innocence.  
  * **Explanation:** Doves correspond to the gentle affections associated with genuine faith and spiritual understanding. They were used in purification rituals. The "wing of a dove" means "the affection of spiritual truth" (*AE* §282). At Jesus' baptism the dove "signified nothing else than the holy of faith; and the 'baptism' itself, regeneration" (*AC* §870).  
* **Serpent:**  
  * **Core Correspondence:** The sensual degree of the mind; reasoning based on sensory input; the ultimate or lowest plane of natural life; prudence and circumspection (in a good sense) (*AC* §§195–197).  
  * **Opposite Sense:** Evil reasoning based only on senses; deceit; poisonous falsities derived from evil loves; domination by bodily appetites; hellish influence.  
  * **Related Faculty/Affection:** Sensual perception and reasoning; the lowest level of the natural mind.  
  * **Explanation:** Serpents live close to the ground, corresponding to the mind's lowest level, which engages directly with the physical world through the senses. This faculty is essential but becomes dangerous if it detaches from higher spiritual principles and trusts only external appearances, leading to deceit and falsity (as in the Genesis temptation narrative). "Wise as serpents" refers to necessary prudence in worldly matters. The bronze serpent lifted by Moses represented the Lord's Divine Sensual (His glorified ultimate nature)—"the sensuous part in the Lord" (*AC* §197)—which has power to heal those who look to it from faith.  
* **Horse:**  
  * **Core Correspondence:** The understanding; the intellectual faculty, especially regarding the Word (*WH* §2).
  * **Opposite Sense:** Understanding perverted by falsities; reasoning against truth; intellectual pride—"the understanding of the Word and of truth falsified by reasonings, and likewise destroyed; as also one’s own intelligence" (*AR* §298; *AE* §281).  
  * **Related Faculty/Affection:** The intellect; understanding.  
  * **Explanation:** Horses carry people, corresponding to how the understanding "carries" or processes thoughts, doctrines, and knowledge. The quality of the understanding is signified by the horse's color: white for a pure understanding of truth from the Word, red for understanding destroyed regarding good, black for understanding destroyed regarding truth, and pale for understanding destroyed regarding both. The "White Horse" of Revelation 19 signifies the understanding of the Word's spiritual sense being opened (*AR* §§305, 312, 320, 820).  
* **Fish:**  
  * **Core Correspondence:** Knowledges (scientifica) belonging to the natural or external mind; memory-knowledge; natural truths; people characterized by such knowledge (*AC* §991; *AE* §513).  
  * **Opposite Sense:** Knowledges used for evil ends; falsities presented as facts.  
  * **Related Faculty/Affection:** The natural memory and understanding; accumulation of external facts.  
  * **Explanation:** Fish dwell in water (natural truth/knowledge), thus representing the contents of the natural mind's memory and external understanding. "Fishers of men" signified that the disciples "should gather to the church" those who are in natural states of knowledge (*AE* §513).  
* **Eagle:**  
  * **Core Correspondence:** Rational thought; intelligence; knowledge and understanding; perception from a high vantage point (*AC* §3901).  
  * **Opposite Sense:** False reasoning; rationality confirming falsities; intellectual pride.  
  * **Related Faculty/Affection:** The rational faculty; intellectual perception.  
  * **Explanation:** The eagle's high flight and keen vision correspond to the rational mind's ability to survey truths broadly and perceive them acutely. It represents a higher intellectual capacity than the merely natural. In the Cherubim vision, the eagle signifies "the Divine guard and providence in respect to intelligence and as to circumspection on every side" (*AE* §281). In a negative sense, eagles gathering at a carcass can represent rationalizations used to justify dead or false doctrines: "rational things that are not true, and thus are false" (*AC* §3901).

## **5\. Library of Correspondences: Plants and Trees**

Plants and trees, in Swedenborg's system, generally correspond to aspects of human intelligence, wisdom, perception, and knowledge, particularly those related to spiritual life and the church. Gardens signify states of intelligence and wisdom (*AC* §100), hence heaven is called the "Garden of God" (Ezek 28:13; *AE* §717). The type of plant or tree often indicates the *quality* or *level* of the corresponding mental faculty or spiritual principle. Fruits correspond to the goods of life, or good works resulting from love and faith, while leaves often relate to truths that clothe good (*AE* §109; *AC* §885). Grains like wheat correspond to the essential goods that nourish the spirit (*AR* §778).  
A significant pattern emerges in the correspondences of the Olive, Vine, and Fig tree. These three trees represent distinct levels or aspects of the church, mirroring Swedenborg's understanding of the celestial (love-based), spiritual (truth/faith-based), and external/natural planes of reality and the human mind. The Olive tree corresponds to the highest, celestial church, rooted in love to the Lord. The Vine corresponds to the spiritual church, characterized by charity towards the neighbor and faith in truth. The Fig tree corresponds to the external manifestation of both celestial and spiritual good in the natural life, representing natural goodness and practical kindness derived from internal principles. This structure shows that the symbolism is not random but reflects the fundamental order Swedenborg perceived in spiritual reality.

* **Olive Tree:**  
  * **Core Correspondence:** The celestial church; the good of celestial love (love directed primarily to the Lord); internal good: "'olive yard' the celestial church, or the church that is in the good of love to the Lord" (*AE* §638; *AC* §§886, 1069).  
  * **Related Church/Mind Aspect:** Celestial Kingdom/Church; the highest state of love and good in the will.  
  * **Explanation:** Represents the highest form of spiritual life, characterized by love for the Lord above all else. Oil from the olive signifies this celestial good and the delight associated with it. Its presence signifies a connection with the inmost heaven.  
* **Vine:**  
  * **Core Correspondence:** The spiritual church; the good of spiritual love (charity toward the neighbor); the truth of faith: "'vineyard' the spiritual church, or the church that is in the good of charity towards the neighbor, and thence in the truths of faith" (*AE* §638).  
  * **Related Church/Mind Aspect:** Spiritual Kingdom/Church; faith, truth in the understanding, and charity towards others.  
  * **Explanation:** Represents the church and individual focused on understanding truth and living in charity. Wine from the vine signifies spiritual truth or the good derived from faith and charity—"the faith thence derived," "the spiritual of that church" (*AC* §1071). The Lord as the Vine signifies He is the source of all spiritual truth.  
* **Fig Tree:**  
  * **Core Correspondence:** The external good of the celestial and spiritual church; natural good derived from internal principles; good works in the natural life (*AC* §§217, 5113).  
  * **Related Church/Mind Aspect:** External church; natural level of good; practical application of spiritual principles.  
  * **Explanation:** Represents the outward expression of inner love and faith in the form of natural kindness, morality, and useful actions. Sitting under one's fig tree symbolizes the peace and security derived from living a life of natural good rooted in spiritual principles (*AE* §403). A fig tree with leaves but no fruit represents faith or knowledge without corresponding good works (*AE* §109).  
* **Cedar:**  
  * **Core Correspondence:** Spiritual truth; the rational aspect of the mind; intellectual truth: "truth from good in the rational" (*AE* §175), "the spiritual of the church" (*AC* §9472), "spiritual good" (*AC* §10261).  
  * **Related Church/Mind Aspect:** Rational faculty; intellectual understanding of spiritual things.  
  * **Explanation:** As tall, enduring trees, cedars often represent the rational faculty and the perception of spiritual truth from a more intellectual standpoint. Swedenborg identifies shittim wood (used in the Tabernacle) as "the wood of the most excellent cedar" from Isaiah 41:19 (*AC* §9472), and he consistently interpreted shittim wood itself as signifying "the good of the Lord's merit" (*AE* §391). The primary correspondence of Cedar relates to the strength and elevation of rational, spiritual thought.  
* **Wheat:**  
  * **Core Correspondence:** Celestial good; the good of love and charity; the primary nourishment for the soul (*AR* §778; *AC* §3941).  
  * **Related Church/Mind Aspect:** Celestial good; love in the will; nourishment of the spirit.  
  * **Explanation:** Wheat is the source of bread, the staple food. It corresponds to the highest form of good—celestial love—which is the essential nourishment for the human spirit. Represents the goods received directly from the Lord into the will.  
* **Tares (Zizania):**  
  * **Core Correspondence:** Falsities derived from evil; evils manifesting as false thoughts: "'tares' signify the evils of falsity" (Matt 13:24–30; *AE* §374).  
  * **Related Church/Mind Aspect:** Falsity originating from evil; contamination within the church/mind.  
  * **Explanation:** In the parable, tares are sown by the enemy (devil) among the good seed (wheat) sown by the Son of Man: "with natural men the falsities of evil flow in from hell, and are received" (*AE* §374). They represent the evils and resulting falsities that infiltrate the church and the individual mind, existing alongside truths and goods until the time of judgment ("harvest") when separation occurs. They are the direct opposite of the good and truth signified by wheat.

## **6\. Library of Correspondences: Objects and Materials**

Objects and materials mentioned in the Bible also carry specific spiritual meanings according to Swedenborg's system. Metals often form a hierarchy corresponding to descending levels of good and truth, reflecting a perceived decline in the spiritual state of humanity or the church over time. Ritual objects used in worship, such as the Temple, Ark, and Altar, correspond profoundly to the Lord, aspects of heaven and the church, the nature of worship, and the conjunction between the Divine and the human. Basic substances like bread, wine, and oil correspond to the essential elements of spiritual life: good, truth, and love.  
The hierarchy observed in the metals—Gold (celestial good), Silver (spiritual truth), Brass (natural good), Iron (natural truth)—is explicitly linked by Swedenborg to the ancient concept of the Golden, Silver, Copper (Brass), and Iron Ages (*TCR* §762; *AC* §425). He interprets these "Ages" not just as mythical periods but as representative of successive states of the church, tracing a decline from the highest state of love (Golden Age, Most Ancient Church) down to a state characterized by external or literal truth devoid of internal good (Iron Age). Nebuchadnezzar's dream statue with its head of gold, chest of silver, belly of brass, and legs of iron is presented as a direct representation of this spiritual decline. This connection imbues the correspondences of metals with a dynamic historical and spiritual dimension.  
Furthermore, the correspondences of ritual objects like the Ark, Altar, and Temple are not merely symbolic *of* worship, but reveal the inner *nature* of true worship and the spiritual realities involved. The Israelite church's external rituals were designed to be representative of internal spiritual processes and the connection with the Divine. Thus, the Ark containing the Law signifies the Lord's presence through Divine Truth and the covenant of conjunction (*AC* §9485); the Altar represents the reception of Divine Love and the act of worship from the heart (*AC* §§921, 10177); and the Temple represents the Lord's Divine Human as the ultimate dwelling place of God, as well as the church and the regenerated individual built from Divine truths and goods. Understanding these correspondences illuminates the spiritual meaning embedded within the Bible's descriptions of worship.

* **Gold:**  
  * **Core Correspondence:** Celestial good; the good of love to the Lord; the highest or inmost good (*AC* §§113, 425).
  * **Opposite Sense:** Evil love; profaned good: gold signifies "good, and in the opposite sense evil" (*AC* §9146).  
  * **Level/Function:** Celestial (Highest Good).  
  * **Explanation:** As the most precious metal, gold corresponds to the highest good, which is love originating from the Lord and directed towards Him. It represents the good residing in the will.  
* **Silver:**  
  * **Core Correspondence:** Spiritual truth; truth derived from celestial good; rational truth (*AC* §§1551, 2575).  
  * **Opposite Sense:** Falsity, especially rationalized falsity.  
  * **Level/Function:** Spiritual (Truth).  
  * **Explanation:** Corresponds to truth within the understanding, which should proceed from and be conjoined with the good of love (gold).  
* **Brass (or Copper):**  
  * **Core Correspondence:** Natural good; the good of the external or natural person; external charity (*AC* §425).  
  * **Opposite Sense:** Natural evil; external good lacking internal life.  
  * **Level/Function:** Natural (Good).  
  * **Explanation:** Represents good manifested on the natural plane, such as kindness and morality in outward life, subordinate to higher spiritual and celestial goods.  
* **Iron:**  
  * **Core Correspondence:** Natural truth; truth in its lowest or ultimate form; literal truth; memory-knowledge (*AC* §425).
  * **Opposite Sense:** Hard falsity: iron signifies "truth in ultimates, and likewise falsity there, and at the same time what is hard" (*AE* §557).  
  * **Level/Function:** Natural (Truth).  
  * **Explanation:** Represents truth on the most external level, like facts or the literal sense of scripture, which is harder and less adaptable than internal truth. It should serve natural good (brass).  
* **Stone:**  
  * **Core Correspondence:** Truth in general, especially natural or ultimate truth; truths of faith; the literal sense of the Word; the Lord as foundational Truth—"'A stone,' in the Word signifies truth in ultimates" (*AR* §231); "the stone of Israel" (*AC* §6426).
  * **Opposite Sense:** Falsity; hard-heartedness against truth (*AE* §§411, 627).  
  * **Level/Function:** Ultimate/Natural (Truth); Foundation.  
  * **Explanation:** Represents foundational truths, often on the natural level, upon which spiritual understanding rests. Precious stones signify higher spiritual truths.  
  * **Precious Stones (Gems/Crystals):** In general, precious stones correspond to the truths of the Word in its literal sense, made transparent and radiant by the spiritual sense within, revealing Divine Love and Wisdom. They represent holy truths derived from good: "truth transparent from good" (*AR* §231). The appearance of precious stones in heaven corresponds to the intelligence and wisdom of the angels there, appearing "in the exact measure of the wisdom that is with them" (*AE* §874). The foundations of the wall of the New Jerusalem being garnished with precious stones signifies that the doctrine of the New Church is founded upon truths shining from the spiritual sense of the Word (*AR* §915). Specific precious stones correspond to particular qualities of truth derived from good; the two fundamental colours are red, "the good of love," and bright white, "the truth of wisdom" (*AR* §915):  
    * **Ruby (also Sardius, Carbuncle):** Fiery stones corresponding to celestial good, or truths glowing with Divine Love: the ruby, topaz and carbuncle of the breastplate's first row signify "the celestial love of good" (*AC* §9865), and the red sardius "the things which are of the good of love" (*AR* §231).
    * **Sapphire:** Blue stone corresponding to the celestial love of truth, with the chrysoprase and diamond of the second row (*AC* §9868); "a work of sapphire" is "what is translucid … from internal truths" (*AC* §9407).
    * **Diamond:** White, transparent stone corresponding to the celestial love of truth (*AC* §9868), revealing Divine Wisdom with clarity.
    * **Emerald:** The rainbow "in aspect like an emerald" round the throne signifies "the appearance of Divine truth in the heavens about the Lord"; its green signifies "truth obscured" (*AE* §269).
    * **Jasper:** Variously colored (sometimes translated as diamond), often corresponds to Divine Truth in its ultimate form, particularly the literal sense of the Word containing the spiritual sense; forms the foundation of doctrine. Shining white, it signifies "the things which are of the truth of wisdom" (*AR* §231); the city's light "like a jasper stone" signifies that the Word will be understood, "because translucent from its spiritual sense" (*AR* §897); it is the first foundation (*AR* §915).
    * Other stones mentioned in scripture (like topaz, beryl, onyx, etc.) similarly correspond to specific aspects and qualities of truth derived from good; Swedenborg refers each foundation stone to the tribe it answers to (*AR* §915, with §§349–361).  
* **Wood:**  
  * **Core Correspondence:** Good, especially natural or lower-level good; things of the will or affection (*AC* §643); "wood" is sensual good (*AE* §587).
  * **Opposite Sense:** Evil desires; worthless affections; worship based on evil: "wood" signifies good, "and in the contrary sense evil" (*AE* §627).  
  * **Level/Function:** Natural/External (Good); Volitional.  
  * **Explanation:** Represents good pertaining to the will, often on a more external plane than celestial good (gold). Different types of wood signify different qualities of good.  
* **Bread:**  
  * **Core Correspondence:** Celestial good; the good of love; all good nourishing the soul; the Lord Himself as the source of good (*AC* §§276, 2165).  
  * **Opposite Sense:** Evil; profaned good.  
  * **Level/Function:** Celestial (Good); Spiritual Nourishment.  
  * **Explanation:** As the fundamental food, bread corresponds to the fundamental spiritual nourishment, which is the good of love from the Lord. Represents the appropriation of Divine Love.  
* **Wine:**  
  * **Core Correspondence:** Spiritual truth; the truth of faith derived from charity; spiritual good (*AC* §1071).  
  * **Opposite Sense:** Falsified truth; intoxicating falsities.  
  * **Level/Function:** Spiritual (Truth); Spiritual Nourishment/Enlightenment.  
  * **Explanation:** Corresponds to the truth that enlightens the understanding and flows from charity. Represents the appropriation of Divine Wisdom/Truth.  
* **Oil:**  
  * **Core Correspondence:** Celestial good; the good of love (to the Lord and neighbor); delight of love (*AC* §§886, 9780).  
  * **Opposite Sense:** Evil love; profaned good.  
  * **Level/Function:** Celestial (Good); Consecration; Illumination (in lamps).  
  * **Explanation:** Represents the very essence and delight of love. Used for anointing, signifying sanctification by Divine Love.  
* **Temple:**  
  * **Core Correspondence:** The Lord's Divine Human; the Church (heavenly/earthly); the Word; the regenerated human. Divine Truth. "He was speaking of the temple of His body" (John 2:21; *AE* §§220, 630).  
  * **Opposite Sense:** Profaned church; worship from falsity/evil.  
  * **Level/Function:** Representation of Divine Dwelling; Worship.  
  * **Explanation:** Represents the place where the Divine dwells and is worshipped, whether understood as the Lord's glorified Human, the collective church, or the individual receptive soul.  
* **Ark (of the Covenant):**  
  * **Core Correspondence:** Inmost Heaven; the Lord's presence; the Word (inmost sense); Divine Truth (Law within); Conjunction (Covenant) (*AC* §9485).  
  * **Opposite Sense:** Word profaned; broken covenant.  
  * **Level/Function:** Representation of Holiest Presence; Conjunction.  
  * **Explanation:** The most sacred object, representing the immediate presence of the Lord through His Divine Truth (the Law) and the bond of conjunction with humanity.  
* **Altar:**  
  * **Core Correspondence:** Representative of the Lord and worship of Him, specifically from love/good (*AC* §921): burnt offerings signify "worship from the good of love," sacrifices "worship from the truths of faith" (*AC* §10414), and the altar of incense "the grateful hearing and reception by the Lord of all things of worship from love and charity" (*AC* §10177).  
  * **Opposite Sense:** Idolatrous worship; self-worship.  
  * **Level/Function:** Representation of Worship (from Love/Good).  
  * **Explanation:** The focal point for sacrifices and offerings, representing the human heart's reception of Divine Love and acknowledgment of the Lord in worship.  
* **Sword:**  
  * **Core Correspondence:** Truth fighting against falsity; the power of truth to combat/disperse evil/falsity; the Word's power (*AC* §2799; *AE* §131).
  * **Opposite Sense:** Falsity fighting against truth; truth used destructively; spiritual devastation: "in the opposite sense, falsity combating against truth" (*AE* §131).  
  * **Level/Function:** Combat; Defense/Discrimination of Truth.  
  * **Explanation:** Represents truth in its active, combative aspect, used to defend against or destroy falsities during spiritual temptation or conflict.

## **7\. Conclusion**

Emanuel Swedenborg's doctrine of correspondences presents a systematic and deeply integrated vision of reality, wherein the natural world and the literal text of the Bible serve as direct reflections and ultimate expressions of spiritual and divine realities. This system posits that every natural element, creature, plant, and object possesses an inherent spiritual significance, derived from its function and quality within the created order, ultimately stemming from the Divine Love and Wisdom that constitute God. Understanding these correspondences, according to Swedenborg, is the essential key to unlocking the Bible's profound inner meaning, which details the path of spiritual regeneration and the nature of the Lord's kingdom.  
This report has compiled a foundational library of these correspondences, focusing specifically on natural elements, living creatures, plants/trees, and common objects/materials frequently encountered in scripture. The meanings presented are drawn from Swedenborg's theological works (primarily *Arcana Coelestia*, *Apocalypse Explained*, and *Apocalypse Revealed*) and compilations like Nicholson's *Dictionary of Correspondences* (Nicholson 1800) and John Worcester's *Correspondences of the Bible* (Worcester 1884, 1888), adhering strictly to the inherent symbolism attributed by Swedenborg. The analysis reveals consistent patterns within the system, such as the hierarchical relationship between symbols reflecting the discrete degrees of reality (celestial, spiritual, natural) and the human mind, and the association of metals with successive states of the church.  
The implication of Swedenborg's system is vast: it suggests that both the Word and the world are imbued with layers of spiritual meaning, accessible through rational inquiry guided by revealed principles. For Swedenborg, this understanding is not merely intellectual but transformative, offering a way to perceive the Divine operating within all aspects of creation and human experience, thereby facilitating conjunction with the Lord and progress towards a heavenly life.  
For those seeking deeper exploration of this complex system, several avenues are recommended:

1. **Primary Sources:** Direct engagement with Swedenborg's major theological works, such as *Arcana Coelestia*, *Apocalypse Explained*, *Apocalypse Revealed*, and *Heaven and Hell*, offers the most comprehensive understanding.  
2. **Reference Works:** Consulting resources like George Nicholson's *Dictionary of Correspondences* (Nicholson 1800) or John Faulkner Potts's *Swedenborg Concordance* (Potts 1888–1902) can provide quick access to specific symbol definitions.  
3. **Online Databases:** Utilizing searchable online platforms dedicated to Swedenborg's writings, such as eswedenborg.com, New Christian Bible Study, the Swedenborg Foundation website, and the Swedenborg Digital Library, allows for targeted research across his voluminous output.  
4. **Scholarly Analysis:** Exploring academic studies on Swedenborg's theology, hermeneutics, and influence provides critical perspectives and contextual understanding.

By engaging with these resources, one can further investigate the intricate web of meaning Swedenborg perceived woven through the fabric of scripture and existence itself.
