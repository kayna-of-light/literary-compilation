# Source 011 — Checked predictions and borrowed content in channelled and mediumistic material

**Citations** (reading status per item):
- Théodore Flournoy, *From India to the Planet Mars*, trans. D. B. Vermilye (New York: Harper, 1900). Read myself (Gutenberg #63092), chs. 5 and 11, during the 2026-11-02 rebuild. The research agent read the Cornell archive.org copy and reports page numbers.
- Leon Festinger, Henry W. Riecken and Stanley Schachter, *When Prophecy Fails* (Minneapolis: University of Minnesota Press, 1956). archive.org `whenprophecyfail00fest_0`. Agent snippets only.
- Hastings 1991 (see source 008), section "Prophecies That Failed." Agent snippets only.
- Matthew Block, "Some Human Sources of the Urantia Book" (1992/93; postscript 2003), https://squarecircles.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/07/SomeHumanSourcesOfTheUrantiaBook.pdf. Agent read the first pages. Independent, not peer-reviewed.

## What they show

- **Flournoy.** The "Martian" of Hélène Smith is "only a puerile counterfeit of French" (ch. 5, read myself). On her retrocognitions: "Pure cryptomnesia seems to me to furnish a sufficient and adequate explanation" (p. 413, agent). Her "Hindoo" romance he traced, by his own account inferentially, to De Marlès's history of India (pp. 276, 303, 307, agent). He still reports "a little telekinesis and telepathy" (ch. 11, read myself).
- **Festinger et al.** Before her automatic writing began, "Mrs. Keech" had a "background of esoteric knowledge" that included flying saucers. Her channelled prediction of a flood on 21 December 1954 failed. The book is the classic study of what the group did next (agent).
- **Hastings.** A section headed "PROPHECIES THAT FAILED" quotes Cayce, "in the year 1970 there shall be earth changes," and comments: "They are examples of predictions that have failed to be fulfilled" (agent). Cayce's Atlantis-rising prediction for 1968–69 also failed (agent, via the Bimini geology literature: McKusick & Shinn, *Nature* 287 (1980): 11–12).
- **Urantia.** The book itself says it used "more than one thousand human concepts" and "as far as possible I have derived my information from purely human sources." Block lists 19 mostly 1920s–30s books with closely paralleled passages (agent).
- **Semjase and Velikovsky** (already established in *From Planets to Constellations* §2.3, read myself): the 1975 Venus passage combines probe data, inconsistent figures, and Velikovsky's capture story (Stevens 1988, 269–274; Sladek 1974, 23–27).

## Why it matters

Where channelled content can be checked — dated predictions, sources traceable to books, the science of the day — it repeatedly turns out to carry the receiver's library and era, and its dated predictions fail. That is what Swedenborg's rule of *Spiritual Diary* §1776 predicts for material things: spirits "judge from man's persuasion."

## Complications to keep (see also source 012)

- Flournoy's verdict is not wholesale: some telepathy, mostly reconstruction.
- Cayce also has a past-life reading naming an individual later "verified as having historically existed (Cerminara, 1950)" (Hastings, agent). Base rates are not tallied.
- Harrison (*JSPR* 53, 1986) found the Hodgson Report of 1885 against Blavatsky "a highly partisan document" and its case "not proven," and found "significant evidence" that she did not write the Mahatma Letters (agent read Harrison's text). Harrison addresses handwriting and procedure, not the borrowed content.
