## What they say

- **Greyson 1983.** "Eighty-nine survivors of near-death experiences judged values related to material and social success as less important than did 175 control subjects."
- **Groth-Marnat & Summers 1998.** 53 NDE experiencers, 27 controls who had faced similar threats without an NDE, and 45 significant others as outside raters.
  - "The NDE group went through a significantly greater number of changes … the extent and types of changes were mostly corroborated by significant others."
  - Areas of change: "increased concern for others, reduced death anxiety with a strengthened belief in an afterlife, increased transcendental experience, reduced interest in material possessions, increased self-worth, increased appreciation for natural phenomenon, and an enhanced awareness of paranormal phenomenon."
  - Depth of the experience tracked the extent of change.
- **Greyson 2022.** 63 NDE experiencers completed the Life Changes Inventory twice, two decades apart. "LCI scores showed no significant change over that period … NDEs appear to be unusual in the long-term persistence of attitude changes."
- **van Lommel et al. 2001 (secondary).** Prospective follow-up of 23 NDE and 15 non-NDE patients at 2 and 8 years. NDE patients showed larger increases in acceptance of others, love and empathy, and appreciation of ordinary things.
  - Non-NDE cardiac-arrest survivors also changed considerably by 8 years. The cells are small, and significance at 8 years was not reported (Kovoor et al. 2024 scoping review, via the agent).
- **Noyes 1980 (secondary).** Among 215 people who survived life-threatening danger (not screened for NDE), the reported changes included "a reduced fear of death," "a sense of relative invulnerability," "a feeling of special importance or destiny," "a belief in having received the special favor of God or fate," and "a strengthened belief in continued existence."

## Why it matters

The evidence mostly supports a change toward others and away from material and social success, corroborated by people close to the experiencers and lasting two decades. It does not support a simple "humbler self." The same studies report "increased self-worth" (Groth-Marnat & Summers) and, in Noyes's earlier and broader sample, "special importance or destiny."

What the evidence shows is a change in what is valued: less value on success, possessions and others' approval, more on others and on meaning. No study has measured narcissism or grandiosity in NDE experiencers with a validated scale. So the claim "unforced experience makes the self humbler, produced practice inflates it" can be stated only as:

- **supported**: values move away from status and material success after NDEs (this source); superiority feelings are highest where spiritual skills are trained (source 007);
- **untested**: no direct comparison of narcissism between the two populations exists.
