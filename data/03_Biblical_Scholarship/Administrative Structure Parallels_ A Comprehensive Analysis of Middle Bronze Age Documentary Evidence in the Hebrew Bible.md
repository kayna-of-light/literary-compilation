# **Administrative Structure Parallels: A Comprehensive Analysis of Middle Bronze Age Documentary Evidence in the Hebrew Bible**

## **Abstract**

This research report presents a stratigraphic analysis of the Hebrew Bible, specifically the Pentateuch, through the lens of Middle Bronze Age (MBA) administrative, legal, and literary conventions. By correlating biblical structures with the archival practices of Ebla, Mari, and Nuzi, and the diplomatic protocols of the Hittite Empire, this study identifies a systemic preservation of second-millennium BCE "structural fossils" within the Masoretic Text. The findings document that the core narratives of Genesis and the legal frameworks of Deuteronomy contain administrative features—including colophon-like structures, catch-lines, and technical price indices—that match MBA conventions. This report addresses five primary research domains: colophon and tablet practices, legal formulae, narrative structures, administrative vocabulary, and archival methodologies, providing a comprehensive catalog of scholarly documented parallels.

## ---

**I. Introduction: The Stratigraphic Approach to Biblical Historiography**

### **1.1 The Philological and Archaeological Context**

The historiography of the ancient Near East (ANE) has undergone a paradigm shift with the discovery and translation of massive cuneiform archives from the Middle Bronze Age (c. 2000–1550 BCE), most notably at Mari, Nuzi, and Alalakh, and the earlier Early Bronze Age (EBA) archives at Ebla (Tell Mardikh). These discoveries have provided a control data set against which the biblical text can be measured, not merely for thematic resonance but for structural identity. The "Stratigraphy" methodology posits that while narratives can be fluid, administrative structures—the "bones" of a civilization's bureaucracy—are rigid and datable. A legal contract from the Ur III period looks distinctly different from one of the Neo-Babylonian era; a treaty from the Hittite Empire follows a template alien to the Assyrian chancellery of the first millennium.

The central thesis explored here is that the Hebrew Bible, particularly the Primeval History (Genesis 1–11) and the Patriarchal Narratives (Genesis 12–50), contains "administrative fossils" that anchor the text to the MBA horizon. These features are not deliberate archaisms introduced by late scribes to feign antiquity—such forgeries inevitably betray themselves through anachronism—but are surviving structural elements of the source documents themselves.1

### **1.2 The Administrative Horizon of the Middle Bronze Age**

To understand the parallels, one must first characterize the administrative environment of the MBA. This was an era of high literacy and complex bureaucracy, characterized by:

* **The Clay Tablet Medium:** The physical limitations and conventions of writing on clay dictated literary form.  
* **Rigid Legal Formalism:** Contracts, adoption papers, and international treaties adhered to strict, often internationally standardized, templates (e.g., the *covenant* forms).  
* **Listenwissenschaft:** The "science of lists" was the primary intellectual framework for organizing knowledge, moving from lexical lists to encyclopedic categorizations of the cosmos.2

This report systematically excavates these layers within the biblical text, demonstrating that the "editor" (traditionally Moses) functioned as a compiler of extant written traditions—a role analogous to the archivists of Ashurbanipal or the scribes of Ebla—rather than a creative author of oral myths.

**II. Colophon and Tablet Practice: The *Toledot* Formula as Archival Metadata**

### **2.1 The Wiseman Hypothesis: Re-evaluating the Structure of Genesis**

The most critical structural parallel identified in the research is the function of the Hebrew phrase *elleh toledot* ("These are the generations of..."). Standard source criticism (the Documentary Hypothesis) has traditionally identified these phrases as editorial headings (superscripts) belonging to the Priestly (P) source, utilized to structure the narrative into genealogical blocks.4 However, the "Stratigraphy" document and the foundational work of P.J. Wiseman (*New Discoveries in Babylonia About Genesis*, 1936; updated by D.J. Wiseman, 1985\) propose a philological reframing based on Mesopotamian scribal practice: the *toledot* phrase functions as a **colophon**—a concluding subscript used in ancient cuneiform tablets to identify the owner, author, or content of the tablet *after* the text is concluded.6

#### **2.1.1 The Mechanics of the Cuneiform Colophon**

In the archives of Ebla, Mari, and Nineveh, the "title page" of a document did not exist. Instead, the final lines of a tablet contained the metadata necessary for archiving and retrieval. A typical Mesopotamian colophon contained three distinct elements:

1. **The Title/Content Description:** "This is the account of..." (incipit or summary).  
2. **The Date:** "In the year of..."  
3. **The Owner/Scribe:** "Tablet of \[Name\]" or "Hand of".9

Wiseman demonstrated that the *toledot* phrases in Genesis align perfectly with this structure when read as conclusions. For example, Genesis 37:2 ("These are the generations of Jacob") introduces the Joseph cycle, in which Jacob is a passive, often deceived figure. If read as a heading, it is a misnomer. However, if read as a colophon, it signifies that the *preceding* narrative (Genesis 25:19–37:2, covering the rivalry of Jacob and Esau, the sojourn in Haran, and the death of Isaac) constitutes the "Tablet of Jacob"—the family history possessed and finalized by him.7

### **2.2 Comprehensive Analysis of the Eleven Genesis Tablets**

The research identifies eleven specific "tablets" or source documents in Genesis, demarcated by the *toledot* colophon. This structure reveals a compilation process where the redactor stitched together pre-existing written records.

#### **2.2.2 The "Catch-Line" Phenomenon**

A specific feature of multi-tablet series in the ANE (such as *Enuma Elish* or *Gilgamesh*) was the use of **catch-lines**. To ensure the proper ordering of tablets, the scribe would repeat the first few words of the *next* tablet at the very end of the *current* tablet, immediately before the colophon.7 The biblical text preserves clear traces of this practice, often appearing as redundant repetitions surrounding the *toledot* phrase.

* **Genesis 2:4 / 2:7:** Tablet 1 concludes with the completion of the "heavens and earth." The colophon (Gen 2:4) identifies this. The next section (Tablet 2\) begins by recapitulating the creation of man (Gen 2:7) from the dust—a specific detail that links back to the general creation of humanity in Tablet 1, ensuring narrative continuity across the physical break of the tablets.10  
* **Genesis 11:26 / 11:27:** The genealogy of Shem ends with "Terah lived seventy years, and begat Abram, Nahor, and Haran" (v. 26). The colophon follows ("These are the generations of Terah"). The text then immediately repeats: "Now these are the generations of Terah: Terah begat Abram, Nahor, and Haran" (v. 27). This verbatim repetition is a classic scribal catch-line, serving no narrative purpose other than to link the end of Tablet 6 with the beginning of Tablet 7\.9  
* **Genesis 6:9:** "Noah was a just man..." is repeated before and after the *toledot* division, linking the genealogy of Noah (Tablet 3\) with the Flood narrative (Tablet 4).

#### **2.2.3 Witness Limitation and the Scope of Authorship**

The internal evidence of the "tablets" strongly supports the colophon theory through the principle of **witness limitation**. In no instance does a tablet record events that the named owner could not have known personally or received from an immediate ancestor.10

1. **Tablet 2 (Adam):** Contains the history of the Garden and the expulsion—events only Adam and Eve witnessed. It ceases before Adam's death.  
2. **Tablet 4 (Sons of Noah):** Contains the Flood account. The logbook-style precision of dates ("in the six hundredth year... in the second month, the seventeenth day," Gen 7:11) reflects a contemporary diary kept by those on the Ark. It does not record Noah's death, which occurs later in the narrative.7  
3. **Absence of Abraham's Tablet:** There is no "Book of the Generations of Abraham." His history is contained within the *toledot* of Terah (his father) and Ishmael/Isaac (his sons). This aligns with ANE practice where the family head's history is often recorded and finalized by the subsequent generation.10 Abraham plays the central role, but the *archival ownership* passed to his sons.

### **2.3 Archival Practices at Ebla and Nuzi**

The plausibility of this structure is reinforced by the archival practices discovered at **Ebla (Tell Mardikh)** and **Nuzi (Yorghan Tepe)**.

* **Ebla (c. 2350 BCE):** The Royal Archives contained over 17,000 tablets organized on wooden shelves. These texts utilized colophons to tag contents, including lexical lists and administrative records.12 The "Stratigraphy" document highlights that the Ebla tablets attest to the preservation of high-status names like **Ebrium** (cognate to Eber), validating the cultural setting of the Genesis genealogies as a list of historical figures rather than eponymous myths.1  
* **Nuzi (c. 1450 BCE):** Family archives at Nuzi were passed down for generations. The "tablet of sistership" and adoption contracts were vital for establishing inheritance rights. The biblical preservation of the *toledot* formulas mirrors this Nuzi obsession with lineage and legal title deeds.14 The *toledot* was not just a story; it was a **title deed** asserting the family's legitimacy and right to the land, maintained in an archival format identical to the MBA norms.

**III. Legal Formulae: The Jurisprudence of the Middle Bronze Age**

The legal material in the Pentateuch, particularly the Covenant Code (Exodus 21–23) and the Patriarchal narratives, exhibits a "legal stratigraphy" that corresponds to the evolution of law in the ANE.

### **3.1 The Stratigraphy of Price: Slave Economics**

One of the most mathematically rigorous proofs for the antiquity of the Genesis narratives is the price of slaves. Kenneth Kitchen's analysis of ANE economic data establishes a clear inflationary curve for slave prices from the 3rd millennium to the Persian period.1 This data serves as an objective "control" for dating the text.

**Table 2: Ancient Near Eastern Slave Price Inflation vs. Biblical Data**

| Period | Approximate Date | Average Slave Price (Silver Shekels) | Source of Data | Biblical Parallel |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Akkadian** | c. 2300 BCE | 10–15 shekels | Akkad Archives | \- |
| **Middle Bronze** | c. 1800–1700 BCE | **20 shekels** | Code of Hammurabi, Mari | **Gen 37:28** (Joseph) |
| **Late Bronze** | c. 1400–1200 BCE | **30 shekels** | Nuzi, Ugarit | **Exod 21:32** (Mosaic Law) |
| **Iron Age** | c. 800–700 BCE | 50–60 shekels | Neo-Assyrian Archives | 2 Kings 15:20 (Head tax) |
| **Persian** | c. 500–400 BCE | 90–120 shekels | Persian Archives | Neh 5:15 (Inflation context) |

1

* **Analysis:** In Genesis 37:28, Joseph is sold by his brothers to the Ishmaelites for **20 shekels of silver**. This precise figure anchors the narrative to the early second millennium BCE. A writer composing this story in the Persian period (as posited by minimalist scholars) would likely use the contemporary price (c. 100 shekels) or a symbolic number, unaware of the specific economic conditions of the MBA. The preservation of "20 shekels" constitutes a **legal fossil**.1  
* **Mosaic Update:** Later, in Exodus 21:32, the redemption price for a slave gored by an ox is set at **30 shekels**. This corresponds perfectly with the inflation that occurred by the Late Bronze Age (c. 1400 BCE), the traditional time of the Exodus. The text thus accurately reflects the economic shift between the Patriarchal (MBA) and Mosaic (LBA) eras.

### **3.2 Nuzi and Mari: The Legal Logic of the Patriarchs**

The narratives of the Patriarchs (Genesis 12–50) describe social behaviors that often seem bizarre or immoral to modern readers, and were explicitly forbidden by later Mosaic law (e.g., marrying two sisters). However, the **Nuzi tablets** (c. 15th century BCE) and **Mari archives** provide the legal rationale for these actions, proving they were governed by specific MBA customary laws.2

#### **3.2.1 The Wife-Sister Motif (*Ahatu*)**

Three times in Genesis (12:10–20; 20:1–18; 26:6–11), a patriarch presents his wife as his "sister." E.A. Speiser identified a direct parallel in the Nuzi legal documents regarding **fratriarchy** or "sistership" (*ahatu*). In Hurrian society, a man could legally adopt his wife as his sister. This dual status conferred upon the wife greater social protection, prestige, and specific inheritance rights that a mere "wife" did not possess.1

* **Implication:** Abraham and Isaac were appealing to a specific, high-status legal category recognized in the MBA Mesopotamian sphere. The irony within the narrative is that the Egyptian and Philistine kings (operating under different legal systems) misunderstood this legal fiction as a literal biological statement. This reflects a clash of legal cultures preserved in the text.16

#### **3.2.2 Surrogacy and Inalienable Rights**

The narrative of Sarah, Abraham, and Hagar (Genesis 16, 21\) is strictly governed by Nuzi marriage contracts. These contracts stipulated that a barren wife *must* provide a slave-girl to her husband to ensure an heir. Crucially, Nuzi law forbade the expulsion of the slave-girl's son if the primary wife subsequently bore a son of her own.1

* **Biblical Parallel:** This explains Abraham's deep distress and reluctance to send Hagar and Ishmael away in Genesis 21:11. It was not just emotional attachment; it was a violation of the customary law of his time. Divine intervention ("Listen to whatever Sarah tells you," Gen 21:12) was required to override the prevailing legal norm. The text preserves the legal tension perfectly.

#### **3.2.3 The *Teraphim* as Title Deeds**

In Genesis 31, Rachel steals her father Laban's household gods (*teraphim*). Nuzi inheritance texts clarify that possession of the household idols was not merely a religious act but a legal claim. The person holding the *teraphim* held the right to the **primary inheritance** of the estate.1

* **Implication:** Rachel was securing the inheritance for her husband Jacob (or her son Joseph) against her brothers. Laban's frantic pursuit ("Why have you stolen my gods?") was a legal attempt to recover his title deeds, not just his religious icons.

### **3.3 The Covenant Code: Casuistic vs. Apodictic Law**

The legal collection in Exodus 21–23, known as the **Covenant Code**, exhibits a structural identity with MBA law codes such as the **Code of Hammurabi** (c. 1750 BCE) and the **Laws of Eshnunna**.1

* **Casuistic Law:** This is "case law" formulated as "If a man does X... then Y shall happen." This conditional structure dominates the ANE legal corpora (e.g., Hammurabi) and the Covenant Code (Exodus 21–23). It reflects a pragmatic, precedent-based legal system typical of the MBA.19  
* **Apodictic Law:** This is "absolute law" formulated as "Thou shalt not..." or "You shall..." (e.g., the Ten Commandments). Albrecht Alt originally argued this was unique to Israel, but scholars have since found parallels in **Hittite treaty stipulations** (see Section III) and occasionally in other ANE contexts, though it remains a distinct feature of the Covenant structure.17  
* **Socio-Economic Setting:** The Covenant Code deals with goring oxen, open pits, and agrarian disputes—reflecting a pre-monarchic, village-based society. It lacks the complex state apparatus, professional judiciary, and commercial regulation seen in Neo-Assyrian or Persian laws, arguing strongly against a late composition.2

**IV. International Treaty Structures: Deuteronomy and the Hittite Connection**

The book of Deuteronomy has long been a battleground for dating, with critical scholarship often assigning it to the 7th century BCE (Josianic reform). However, structural analysis of international treaties reveals that Deuteronomy follows the **Hittite Suzerainty Treaty** form of the Late Bronze Age (c. 1400–1200 BCE), rather than the Neo-Assyrian treaties of the 1st millennium.

### **4.1 The Six-Point Hittite Structure**

Kenneth Kitchen and George Mendenhall identified a stable six-part structure common to Hittite treaties (e.g., between the Hittite Great King and a vassal) and the biblical covenant.1

| Structural Element | Hittite Treaty (c. 1400–1200 BCE) | Deuteronomy (Biblical Covenant) | Neo-Assyrian Treaty (c. 700 BCE) |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **1\. Preamble** | "Thus says \[King\]..." | Deut 1:1–5 | Present |
| **2\. Historical Prologue** | Recounts history/benevolence | Deut 1:6 – 3:29 | **Absent** |
| **3\. Stipulations** | General & Specific demands | Deut 4 – 26 | Present (focused on loyalty) |
| **4\. Deposit/Reading** | Temple deposit; Annual reading | Deut 31:9–13, 24–26 | **Absent** |
| **5\. Witnesses** | List of gods (Lists of nature) | Deut 30:19; 31:28 (Heaven/Earth) | Present (Gods only) |
| **6\. Blessings/Curses** | Balanced Blessings & Curses | Deut 28 (Blessings & Curses) | **Curses Only** (No blessings) |

1

### **4.2 The "Structural Fossil": The Historical Prologue**

The diagnostic feature for dating is the **Historical Prologue**.

* **Hittite Treaties:** The suzerain always recounts his benevolent acts toward the vassal ("I took you from... I gave you...") to establish a moral obligation for obedience.  
* **Neo-Assyrian Treaties:** These treaties (e.g., Esarhaddon's Vassal Treaties) represent a raw imposition of power. They **lack** a historical prologue entirely. They do not justify the king's rule; they simply demand loyalty.  
* **Deuteronomy:** It devotes three chapters (1–3) to the Historical Prologue, recounting Yahweh's deliverance from Egypt and guidance through the wilderness.

**Conclusion:** If Deuteronomy were a "pious fraud" written in the 7th century BCE to support Josiah's centralization (as argued by De Wette and Wellhausen), the scribe would have likely followed the contemporary legal form—the Neo-Assyrian treaty. The presence of the Historical Prologue, along with the balanced blessings and curses (Assyrian treaties have only curses), structurally anchors the Covenant document to the second millennium BCE.2

**V. Narrative Structure: *Listenwissenschaft* and Literary Conventions**

The literary forms of the Early and Middle Bronze Ages provided the templates for the biblical authors.

### **5.1 Genesis 1 as *Listenwissenschaft* (The Science of Lists)**

The "Stratigraphy" document offers a profound insight into the genre of Genesis 1\. Rather than a poem or a myth in the Greek sense, it is identified as a **taxonomic liturgy** rooted in **Listenwissenschaft**.2 This intellectual tradition originated in Uruk IV (c. 3200 BCE) and flourished in the Early Dynastic period (e.g., Ebla lexical lists).

* **The Logic of Enumeration:** In Sumerian and Eblaite epistemology, to know something was to list it. Knowledge was mastery over chaos through categorization (Lists of Birds, Lists of Trees, Lists of Professions).  
* **Biblical Application:** Genesis 1 categorizes the cosmos into realms (Day 1–3: Light/Dark, Waters Above/Below, Dry Land) and inhabitants (Day 4–6: Luminaries, Fish/Birds, Animals/Man).  
* **The *Min* Classifier:** The recurring Hebrew phrase *le-mino* ("according to its kind") functions exactly like the **determinatives** (classifiers) in cuneiform lexical lists (e.g., GIŠ for wooden objects, LU2 for professions).2 Genesis 1 is thus a "verbalized list," transforming the static academic lists of Ebla into a dynamic narrative of divine ordering.

### **5.2 Narrative Type-Scenes**

Robert Alter and others have identified "type-scenes" in the biblical narrative that share conventions with MBA literature. These repeated narrative patterns signal to the ancient reader the genre and expectations of the story.26

* **Betrothal at the Well:** (Isaac/Rebekah, Jacob/Rachel, Moses/Zipporah). The protagonist travels to a foreign land, meets a maiden at a well, draws water, and is invited to a meal. This motif has parallels in Ugaritic epics (e.g., *Kirta*) and reflects the pastoral reality of the MBA.  
* **Annunciation to the Barren Wife:** (Sarah, Rebekah, Rachel, Hannah). The divine promise of a seed to a barren woman is a recurring motif that underscores the theological theme of divine intervention in lineage, distinct from the fertility cults of Canaan.28

**VI. Administrative Vocabulary: Lexical Fossils of the Archaic**

The "Stratigraphy" document highlights several Hebrew words that are not merely "old" but are technical loanwords from the administrative vocabulary of the Early and Middle Bronze Ages.

### **6.1 Technical Terminology of the River Civilizations**

* **'Ed (Gen 2:6):** Traditionally translated as "mist," this word is a direct cognate of the Sumerian **a.dé** (Eblaite *i-du*). It refers to the specific irrigation technology of the Mesopotamian alluvial plain—the rising of the water table or the flooding of canals.1 Its presence in the Eden narrative suggests the text's geography and hydrology are rooted in the Sumerian south, preserving technical jargon that would be foreign to a highland Israelite scribe of the Iron Age.  
* **Tehom (Gen 1:2):** Often linguistically linked to Tiamat, the Babylonian chaos monster. However, Eblaite texts use *ti-a-ma-tum* as a generic, demythologized noun for "ocean" or "abyss".1 This suggests Genesis 1 utilizes the archaic, common Semitic scientific term rather than engaging in a late polemic against the *Enuma Elish*.  
* **Mabbul (The Flood):** Cognate with Eblaite *ma-ba-lum* and Akkadian *bubbulu*, referring to a specific type of celestial disaster or "destructive rain".1  
* **Qesitah (Gen 33:19; Job 42:11):** An archaic unit of currency used by Jacob to buy land at Shechem. The term is unknown in the Iron Age (where the *shekel* was standard) but is linked to the Eblaite *qestum*.1 Its appearance in Job and Genesis marks these texts as utilizing a currency system that had been obsolete for a millennium by the time of the Exile.

### **6.2 Administrative Titles: *Shoter* and *Mazkir***

While the office of the scribe (*sofer*) is well known, the titles *shoter* and *mazkir* represent specific administrative functions with deep ANE roots.

* **Shoter:** Often translated "officer" or "official," the root relates to "writing" or "recording" in Akkadian (*shataru*). In the MBA and LBA administration (including Egypt), these were the scribal overseers responsible for the logistics of labor and quotas (cf. Exod 5:6).1  
* **Mazkir:** The "Recorder" or "Herald." Parallels in the Egyptian administration (*whm.w*, "speaker/herald") and Mari administration suggest a role that functioned as the royal mouthpiece and keeper of the official annals. The existence of these differentiated roles in the United Monarchy (2 Sam 8:16) and their linguistic roots in the MBA administrative koine point to a sophisticated, pre-exilic state bureaucracy.30

### **6.3 Onomastics: The "Amorite Imperfective"**

The names of the Patriarchs—**Isaac** (*Yitzhak*), **Jacob** (*Ya'aqob*), **Ishmael** (*Yishma'el*)—follow the **Amorite Imperfective** pattern (prefixed with *y-*).

* **Statistical Evidence:** This name type is dominant in the Mari archives (c. 1800 BCE) and the Execration Texts, accounting for a significant percentage of names. By the Iron Age (c. 1000 BCE onwards), this pattern becomes statistically rare.1  
* **Conclusion:** The names are not retrojections. A later scribe inventing names would likely use the "Yahwistic" names common in his own time (e.g., Jeremiah, Hezekiah). The preservation of Amorite names is a strong indicator of authentic MBA traditions.

**VII. Archival Practice: The "Ancient Word" in Clay**

### **7.1 Ebla and the "Doctrinal Cloak" of the Archives**

The discovery of the Ebla archives revolutionized the understanding of third-millennium Syria. It proved that writing was not limited to Mesopotamia and Egypt but flourished in the Levant. The archival practices—tagging tablets, organizing by subject (textiles, metals, diplomacy), and using colophons—provide the physical model for the "Wiseman Hypothesis."

* **Ebrium and Eber:** The appearance of the name *Ebrium* as a king or high official at Ebla establishes that the name *Eber* (the eponym of the Hebrews) was a prestige name in the 3rd millennium, not a late invention.6  
* **Geographic Fidelity:** The "Stratigraphy" document argues that Genesis preserves **"fossil knowledge"** of geography that changed over time. The **Pishon River** (Gen 2:11) is identified with the Kuwait River system, a paleo-river that dried up c. 2000 BCE. Its description in Genesis implies a source text or tradition dating from when the river was still flowing—an "archival memory" of the physical landscape that would be invisible to an Iron Age author.2

### **7.2 Mari: Prophecy and Administration**

The Mari archives provide the closest parallels to biblical prophecy, yet with distinct administrative differences.

* **The Prophetic Mechanism:** Mari prophets (*apilu*, *muhhu*) delivered messages to the king, often demanding cultic care for the deity.  
* **The "Thus Says" Formula:** The messenger formula ("Thus says the god X...") is common to both. However, the Mari letters show that these messages were often treated as administrative data—recorded, checked (via hair and hem samples for divination), and filed.33  
* **Biblical Divergence:** While the *form* is identical (structural parallel), the *function* diverges. Biblical prophets eventually democratized the message, speaking to the people and the king with moral rather than purely ritual demands. However, the *administrative* protocol of recording and archiving these oracles (as seen in Jeremiah 36\) likely has its roots in the scribal habits exemplified at Mari.35

## ---

**VIII. Conclusion: What the Data Show and Do Not Show**

The stratigraphic analysis of the Hebrew Bible through the lens of Middle Bronze Age documents reveals structural features that correlate with second-millennium BCE administrative, legal, and literary conventions:

1. **Colophons:** The *toledot* formula parallels the physical formatting of cuneiform tablets (Wiseman Hypothesis).  
2. **Legal Fossils:** Slave prices and family laws match MBA economic and social realities documented at Nuzi and Mari.  
3. **Literary DNA:** The *Listenwissenschaft* of Genesis 1 and the Treaty structure of Deuteronomy correspond to Uruk and Hittite intellectual traditions.  
4. **Lexical Archaisms:** Terms like *'ed*, *qesitah*, and Amorite names serve as linguistic fingerprints of the MBA.

**What the data demonstrate:**
- The Pentateuch contains administrative structures, legal formulations, and economic values that match the MBA horizon
- These features became obsolete in later periods—a Persian-period author would need to deliberately archaize to reproduce them
- The correlation is structural, not merely thematic

**What the data constrain:**
- A wholly late composition cannot easily account for the density of MBA administrative "fossils"
- The text's structure is consistent with compilation from earlier written sources

**What the data do not determine:**
- The mechanism of transmission
- The motivations or intentions of the compilers
- Whether the narratives describe historical events in any modern sense

The Genesis narratives bear structural features that date to the world from Ebla to Nuzi. The evidence speaks to age and transmission; it does not speak to intention.
