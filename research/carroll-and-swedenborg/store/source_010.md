# Source 010 — Carroll's 1896 preface on the Hatter's riddle

**Citation**: Carroll, Lewis. "Preface to the Eighty-Sixth Thousand of the 6/- Edition" (Christmas 1896). Printed in later Macmillan editions of *Alice's Adventures in Wonderland*. Text as quoted in Wakeling, Edward, ed. *Rediscovered Lewis Carroll Puzzles.* New York: Dover, 1995; checked against a 1927 Macmillan reprint (archive.org `in.ernet.dli.2015.210040`).
**Accessed**: 2026-10-23.

## The passage
"Enquiries have been so often addressed to me, as to whether any answer to the Hatter's Riddle can be imagined, that I may as well put on record here what seems to me to be a fairly appropriate answer, viz. 'Because it can produce a few notes, though they are very flat; and it is nevar put with the wrong end in front!' This, however, is merely an after-thought: the Riddle, as originally invented, had no answer at all."

- Wakeling: "Notice the spelling of 'never' in this extract, which reverses very smartly to give 'raven'!"
- The 1927 reprint prints "never": later printings normalized Carroll's "nevar".

## Why it matters
These are all of Carroll's words on the riddle that the library uses. They establish: no answer was intended at invention; Carroll later offered one as "an after-thought"; the "nevar" reversal is his. They say nothing about a hidden theological meaning, for or against.
