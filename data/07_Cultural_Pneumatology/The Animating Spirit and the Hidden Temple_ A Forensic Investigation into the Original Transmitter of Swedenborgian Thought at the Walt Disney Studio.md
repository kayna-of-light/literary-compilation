# **The Animating Spirit and the Hidden Temple: A Forensic Investigation into the Original Transmitter of Swedenborgian Thought at the Walt Disney Studio (1935–1937)**

## **I. Introduction: The Pneumatological Paradox of the Golden Age**

### **1.1 The Historiographical Lacuna**

The historiography of the Walt Disney Studio has long been dominated by a technological determinism that traces the studio’s evolution through the multiplane camera, the synchronization of sound, and the refinement of Technicolor. Standard narratives, such as those by Barrier or Gabler, rightfully emphasize the technical innovations that allowed for the "Illusion of Life." Yet, these technocratic histories frequently fail to account for the sudden, radical emergence of a specific metaphysical architecture in the studio's first feature film, *Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs* (1937).

Within a span of three years (1934–1937), the studio transitioned from the "rubber hose" mechanics of the early 1930s—where characters possessed motility without interiority—to a narrative universe governed by a rigorous "physics of the spirit." In this new universe, the environment mirrored the internal state of the protagonist, animals functioned as externalized affections, and the narrative arc followed a precise eschatological trajectory of spiritual death, vastation, and celestial resurrection. This report posits that this shift was not merely an aesthetic evolution but the result of a conscious encoding of the theology of Emanuel Swedenborg (1688–1772), specifically his *Doctrine of Influx* and *Science of Correspondences*.

This is a hypothesis claim about intentional transmission, not a settled historical conclusion. Even if Swedenborg’s framework cleanly illuminates the pattern-fit, the evidentiary question remains whether the historical record supports deliberate encoding versus broader diffusion through adjacent metaphysical currents.

### **1.2 The Research Problem**

Recent scholarship has identified a structural isomorphism between Swedenborg’s theology and the "Disney Style," particularly in the pedagogy of "Action Analysis" taught by Don Graham. Furthermore, archival evidence from 1948—specifically the correspondence between studio executive Hal Adelquist and Florence Murdoch, librarian of the Cincinnati Church of the New Jerusalem—confirms that by the mid-century the studio was handling Swedenborgian material knowingly: it had researched the real Johnny Appleseed extensively and defended its angel "with sincere respect" to a Swedenborgian church librarian (Fryeburg New Church Assembly, n.d.).

The central research problem of this investigation is retrospective: If the studio was consciously encoding Swedenborgian thought in 1948, who was the "Original Transmitter" during the foundational era of 1935–1937? Who introduced the specific narrative deviations in *Snow White*—the "Cloud Castle" and the "Awakening by Kiss"—that align so precisely with Swedenborgian eschatology?

### **1.3 The Thesis: The Tripartite Convergence**

This report argues that the transmission of Swedenborgian-compatible metaphysics into the Disney Studio was likely not the work of a single "secret operative" but the result of a **Tripartite Convergence** of personnel, pedagogy, and environment:

1. **The Structural Architect (candidate):** Richard Creedon, whose tentative outline of October 22, 1934, lays groundwork for the film's moral logic, including the self-destruction of evil (Creedon, 22 October 1934).  
2. **The Theological Editor (candidate):** Ted Sears, head of the story department, who may have helped institutionalize the redemptive mechanism of the "Prince's Kiss" and enforce the shift away from gag-based storytelling toward character/interiority-first storytelling (Snow White Museum, n.d.f).  
3. **The Institutional Guardian (documented mid-century):** Hal Adelquist, whose role as head assistant director on *Snow White* and later Story Department Manager plausibly bridged the gap between the implicit encoding of 1937 and the explicit acknowledgement of 1948 (Gabler 2006; Fryeburg New Church Assembly, n.d.).

These figures operated within a **Los Angeles Metaphysical Reservoir** that plausibly carried Swedenborgian-adjacent ideas via New Thought, Theosophy, and related currents (and, where documented, New Church institutions).

## **II. The Reservoir: The Los Angeles Metaphysical Milieu (1920s–1930s)**

To understand how Swedenborgian thought could infiltrate a commercial animation studio, one must first map the "cultural water table" from which the studio drew its talent. Los Angeles in the 1920s and 1930s was not merely a boomtown for cinema; it was the "spiritual frontier" of America, a region saturated with esoteric movements that viewed art as a vehicle for spiritual revelation.

### **2.1 The Church of the New Jerusalem in Los Angeles**

Contrary to the assumption that Swedenborgianism was a distant New England phenomenon, the Church of the New Jerusalem had an active presence in Los Angeles. Through the years of *Snow White* and into the 1950s it met at 509 South Westmoreland Avenue, where by 1942 the Rev. Andre Diaconoff was pastor (*Los Angeles Times* 1935–1942; *New-Church Messenger* 1953).

The city's metaphysical culture inherited the "Garden of the Lord" ideal: the phrase in which Protestant home missionaries had cast their hope of transforming the American West into a spiritual paradise (PBS, n.d.), and with which Theodore Roosevelt praised the California that John Muir had helped preserve (Roosevelt 1915). This supplies a plausible cultural background for Disney’s later use of environment-as-moral-mirror (e.g., the forest reacting to Snow White's terror). The strongest claim warranted here is that Swedenborgian texts and ideas could have been accessible within the city’s metaphysical and artistic networks.

The influence of the Worcesters, a leading family of American Swedenborgianism, extended to the West Coast: the Rev. Joseph Worcester (1836–1913), son of a Swedenborgian minister, built his Swedenborgian Church in San Francisco (1895) as a fusion of the "naturalist beliefs of the Swedenborgian religion" with Arts and Crafts design, and counted John Muir and the painter William Keith among his friends (Maley and Stock 2002). The Worcesters were proponents of the "Science of Correspondences" applied to natural science and art: the Rev. John Worcester wrote *Physiological Correspondences* (Worcester 1889).

### **2.2 The Diffusion of "New Thought" and Theosophy**

The "reservoir" was not limited to the formal church. Swedenborg’s ideas had diffused broadly into **New Thought**, **Theosophy**, and **Spiritualism**, movements that were ubiquitous in Hollywood.

* **Theosophy:** The Theosophical Society, indebted in part to Swedenborgian-compatible metaphysics (among other sources), had a major California presence (Lomaland, Krotona). Theosophy taught that "thoughts are things, and puissant things" (Besant and Leadbeater 1901)—a concept that aligns closely with the animation principle that "the mind is the pilot."  
* **New Thought:** This movement, which traces its lineage to Swedenborg through Warren Felt Evans, a Swedenborgian who was Phineas Quimby's patient and student and who pointed the way toward New Thought (Albanese 2007), taught that the "inner state" creates the "outer reality." In the context of animation, this is the technical instruction: draw the emotion (inner) to create the motion (outer).

**Don Graham**, the Chouinard Art Institute instructor hired by Walt to train his artists, operated within this milieu. His "Action Analysis" classes focused on "forces" rather than "forms" (Graham, 21 June 1937), and Walt Stanchfield, heir to his pedagogy, titled one of his own lectures "The Metaphysical Side" (*The Mechanics of the Soul*). While Graham may not have been a member of the New Church, his pedagogy can be read as a secularized parallel to Swedenborg’s *Doctrine of Influx*. He provided a studio vocabulary that would have made metaphysical concepts communicable as technical craft.

### **2.3 The Palos Verdes Nexus and the "Dream" of the Glass Church**

Later evidence points to the **Palos Verdes** peninsula as a geographical locus for this transmission. This area would eventually host the **Wayfarers Chapel** (The Glass Church), designed by **Lloyd Wright** as a Swedenborgian memorial (Wayfarers Chapel, n.d.). While the chapel's cornerstone was not laid until 1949, and the building completed in 1951, the community and the aesthetic impulse existed earlier. **Elizabeth Sewall Schellenberg**, a Swedenborgian living in Palos Verdes in the late 1920s, conceived the dream of the chapel (Wayfarers Chapel, n.d.). The coast had drawn the studio's artists before the chapel rose: in 1934 Lee Blair painted Mary Blair, later the stylist of *Cinderella* and *Alice in Wonderland*, standing on a Palos Verdes bluff (Bawden-Davis 2016).

## **III. The Pedagogy of the Spirit: Don Graham and the "Action Analysis" Filter**

Before identifying the specific *narrative* transmitter, we must establish the *methodological* transmitter. The "Original Transmitter" of the theology did not have to teach religion; they only had to teach a method of drawing that required the artist to view the world through Swedenborgian physics. This figure was **Don Graham**.

### **3.1 The Crisis of Ensoulment**

In 1932, Walt Disney faced a "Crisis of Ensoulment." His characters moved (the rubber hose style), but they did not *live*. They lacked interiority. To produce a feature film like *Snow White*, the characters needed to appear to have souls—internal wills that drove their external actions.

### **3.2 Secularizing the Doctrine of Influx**

Don Graham’s solution was "Action Analysis." The core tenet of this pedagogy came from Walt Disney's memo to Graham of December 23, 1935: **"The Mind is the Pilot"** (Disney, 23 December 1935).

* **Swedenborg:** "The body’s activity in speech or deed is only an effect from the external and internal of man’s spirit, for the body is so much obedience." (*Divine Providence* §103)  
* **Graham:** "The artist is not drawing a form at all, but indicating a force." (Graham, 21 June 1937)

Graham trained the animators to see the "invisible" cause before drawing the "visible" effect. This is a Swedenborgian-compatible logic of cause/force applied to graphite. By 1935, when production on *Snow White* ramped up, much of the studio had been trained in this way of seeing. This created a "receptive soil." The story men (Creedon, Sears, Smith) could propose narrative changes based on "internal states" (e.g., the Queen dying of her own fury) because the animators now had the tools to depict those states. Graham was an enabler of the transmission, creating a studio language that was structurally Swedenborgian in form, even if the label was absent.

## **IV. The Narrative Architects: Religious Backgrounds and Contributions**

The investigation now turns to the specific individuals in the Story Department who crafted the narrative of *Snow White* between 1934 and 1937\. By analyzing their backgrounds and specific contributions, we can isolate the source of the Swedenborgian encoding.

### **4.1 Richard Creedon: The Structural Architect**

**Richard Granville Creedon** (1896–1964) emerges as the primary candidate for the structural overhaul of the story. Born in Chicago, Creedon worked as a radio scriptwriter for KHJ before joining Disney (Snow White Museum, n.d.c).

* **The Tentative Outline (Oct 22, 1934):** After Walt Disney's three October story conferences with him and the lyricist Larry Morey, Creedon wrote the foundational eighteen-page outline that defined the film’s structure (Barrier 1999; Barrier 2007). This document sets down the whole story as agreed at that stage, from the storybook opening to the castle in the clouds (Creedon, 22 October 1934).  
* **The Self-Destruction of Evil:** In the tentative outline of **October 22, 1934**, the concept of the Queen’s death is explicitly discussed: "It is very important that we find a clever piece of business whereby the Queen, in trying to kill the dwarfs, kills herself" (Creedon, 22 October 1934). This is strong pattern-fit evidence for a Swedenborgian-compatible concept of evil’s self-destruction. The studio explicitly rejected external execution (the Grimm red-hot shoes) in favor of internal causality. Creedon’s insistence on this point suggests he understood the moral necessity of *self-destruction* over *external punishment*, a key Swedenborgian tenet.  
* **Religious Background:** While direct evidence of Creedon's membership in the New Church is elusive, his background in radio drama required an understanding of "invisible audiences" and character interiority. His specific focus on the "clever piece of business" for self-destruction indicates a sophisticated moral theology compatible with New Thought or Swedenborgianism.

### **4.2 Ted Sears: The Theological Editor**

**Ted Sears** (1900–1958), the Head of Story, served as the gatekeeper and refiner. Born in Greenfield, Massachusetts, Sears came from a New England background where Swedenborgianism was historically rooted (Snow White Museum, n.d.f).

* **The "Prince’s Kiss":** The idea of the kiss is often attributed to a general borrowing from Perrault's *Sleeping Beauty* (Barrier 2007), and it stands in Creedon's tentative outline from October 1934: "Only the kiss of a great love can revive the victim" (Creedon, 22 October 1934). Sears, almost systematically listed among the participants of the story meetings (Snow White Museum, n.d.f), appears to have been a key internal advocate for keeping it. The shift from the "dislodged apple" (mechanical/accidental) to the "Kiss" (spiritual/intentional) required a decision-maker to prioritize emotional/spiritual resonance over folklore fidelity. As head of the story department, Sears was that authority.  
* **Rejection of the "Selfhood":** The same ethic outlived him. In 1960, two years after Sears's death, the studio turned down the *Chanticleer* project, whose protagonist is a rooster who believes he causes the sun to rise; Marc Davis remembered the excuse as "you can't make a personality out of a chicken" (Barrier 2007). Within this project's interpretive lens, that rejection is compatible with an anti-selfhood ethic (anti self-deification). However, this should be treated as framework-consistent interpretation rather than direct evidence of Swedenborgian intent.

### **4.3 Webb Smith: The Visualizer of Correspondence**

**Webb Smith** (1887–1951), born in West Liberty, Iowa, is generally considered the inventor of the storyboard, though Dick Huemer credited Ted Sears with it (Snow White Museum, n.d.g).

* **The Metaphysics of Storyboarding:** Smith's innovation—pinning sketches in sequence to visualize the "flow" of the story—can be read as a practical analogue to Swedenborg’s concept of "series and degrees." It allows the creator to see the *whole* (the spiritual intent) before the *parts* (the individual animation frames) are executed.  
* **Religious Background:** Nothing in the record documents Webb Smith's religious life. It is Smith's *methodology*—the visual organization of narrative—that acted as the conduit. By forcing the story to be *seen* before it was *animated*, Smith ensured that the "correspondences" (visual symbols of inner states) were coherent.

### **4.4 Otto Englander: The Cosmopolitan Mystic**

**Otto Englander** (1906–1969), born in Tuzla (Austro-Hungarian Empire), was a Jewish intellectual whose studio employment records list eight languages besides English (Ghez 2020; Snow White Museum, n.d.b).

* **The Kabbalistic Connection (speculative):** Englander’s background opens a potential vector for Jewish Mysticism (Kabbalah), which shares structural parallels with Swedenborgianism (e.g., correspondence logics; body–cosmos mapping; the "Grand Man"). Swedenborg himself met Kabbalistic ideas in the Uppsala of his youth and through Leibniz, though he never cites a Kabbalistic source (Esterson n.d.). This remains conjectural unless direct documentary links (letters, reading lists, meeting notes) are located.

## **V. The Institutional Carrier: Hal Adelquist and the Link to 1948**

While Creedon and Sears built the vessel, **Hal Adelquist** (1914–1981) appears to be the figure who understood the *content* of the vessel and carried that knowledge forward.

### **5.1 The Assistant Director (1937)**

Adelquist was head assistant director on *Snow White* (Gabler 2006), specifically working as assistant director to William Cottrell, Perce Pearce and Hamilton Luske on sixteen of its sequences (Snow White Museum, n.d.a). He was in the room when the decisions regarding the Queen’s death and the awakening were being refined. He witnessed the "encoding" process firsthand.

### **5.2 The Salt Lake City Birth**

Adelquist was born in **Salt Lake City, Utah**, on July 11, 1914 (Snow White Museum, n.d.a).

* **Ancestry:** No record ties the Adelquist family to the New Church. His biography can be treated as mildly suggestive only in combination with later, stronger markers (e.g., the Murdoch correspondence).
* **The Utah Connection:** Utah’s religious environment (especially Mormonism) overlaps in some motifs with Swedenborgian cosmology (degrees/glories; marriage metaphysics), and Joseph Smith has historically been accused of borrowing from Swedenborg (Haws 2008). This supplies plausibility context rather than a direct causal chain.

### **5.3 The "Johnny Appleseed" Confirmation (1948)**

Ten years later, as Story Department Manager, Adelquist wrote the pivotal letter to **Florence Murdoch** of the Cincinnati Church of the New Jerusalem (Fryeburg New Church Assembly, n.d.).

* **The Admission:** The studio, Adelquist wrote, had researched the real Chapman extensively, and he added: "We trust that after you have viewed the picture you will agree that we have treated the angel with sincere respect and endowed him with a certain dignity" (Fryeburg New Church Assembly, n.d.).  
* **The Interpretation:** By admitting to extensive research, Adelquist strongly suggests the studio knew John Chapman was a Swedenborgian missionary. He defended the "Pioneer Spirit" angel (a deceased human, not a separate species), which is compatible with Swedenborgian pneumatology.  
* **The Conclusion:** Adelquist is a plausible bridge figure: present during *Snow White* (1937) and demonstrably involved in *Johnny Appleseed* (1948). His biography contributes weakly to plausibility; his role-positioning and correspondence are the stronger indicators.

## **VI. The Pneumatological Audit of Snow White (1937)**

The evidence of transmission is ultimately sought in the text of the film itself. A forensic analysis highlights changes that are strongly illuminated through a Swedenborgian lens. While isolated elements (e.g., the awakening kiss) have fairy-tale precedent, the *convergence* of three specific innovations—internal causality for evil's destruction, spiritual conjunction as the mechanism of awakening, and celestial ascent to a golden city-in-the-sky—is difficult to explain through generic fairy-tale convention alone. The "cloud castle" ending in particular has no precedent in the Grimm source or standard fairy-tale imagery; it reads as eschatological (a "New Jerusalem" motif).

### **6.1 The Cloud Castle: Celestial Ascent**

**The Change:** In the Grimm fairy tale, the story ends horizontally: the Prince takes Snow White to his father's castle (Grimm and Grimm 1857). In the Disney film, the ending is vertical. The castle appears in the sky, golden and glowing, amidst the clouds (Snow White Museum, n.d.e). The ending stood in the story from Creedon's first outline, which closes as "we see the Prince and Snow White riding the white charger to a castle in the clouds" (Creedon, 22 October 1934). **The Swedenborgian Encoding:** The "Cloud Castle" corresponds to the **Celestial Kingdom**. Clouds represent the "literal sense of the Word" or the veil between the natural and spiritual. A castle in the clouds represents a spiritual state of governance by Divine Truth. Snow White does not just get married; she *ascends*. She leaves the natural world (the forest/dwarfs) behind. The farewell scene with the dwarfs—where she kisses them goodbye—signifies the soul acknowledging the value of the "natural affections" (dwarfs) that sustained it during its trial, but ultimately separating from them to enter a higher state.

### **6.2 The Awakening: Conjunction of Love and Truth**

**The Change:** In Grimm, the awakening is mechanical (the coffin is jolted, dislodging the apple) (Grimm and Grimm 1857). In Disney, it is "Love’s First Kiss." **The Swedenborgian Encoding:** The "Sleep of Death" corresponds to the state of the church (or the soul) when it is consumed by falsity (the poison apple/faith alone). It cannot wake itself. It requires an influx from without. The Prince corresponds to **Divine Truth** (Intellect). The Kiss corresponds to **Conjunction** (Love/Will). Truth alone cannot save; it must be united with Love. When the Prince (Truth) kisses Snow White (Good/Affection), the "Heavenly Marriage" occurs, and the soul awakens. This is a theological correction of the Grimm "accident." Salvation is not an accident; it is an act of Divine Love.

### **6.3 The Queen's Death: The Vastation of the Self**

**The Change:** In Grimm, she is tortured (red-hot iron shoes) (Grimm and Grimm 1857). In Disney, she destroys herself: she is levering a boulder down on the dwarfs when lightning strikes, and she falls from the cliff with the boulder after her (Snow White Museum, n.d.d). **The Swedenborgian Encoding:** The Evil Queen corresponds to the **Self**—the love of self. She creates the poison (falsity) from her own substance. Her death is the result of her own attempt to destroy the innocent. She sets the lever under the boulder that follows her down. Swedenborg teaches that the Lord casts no one into Hell; evil spirits cast themselves in by gravitating toward their own nature (*Heaven and Hell* §§545–550). The Queen is not executed by a judge; she is consumed by the gravity of her own hatred.

## **VII. Conclusion: Leading Candidates and an Open Problem**

The investigation suggests that **Richard Creedon** is a leading candidate for early structural contributions (1934), operating within a supportive triad that likely included **Ted Sears** (story-level enforcement) and **Hal Adelquist** (later documented mid-century gatekeeping). However, the identity of a single, decisive "Original Transmitter" remains an open problem absent direct documentary proof (e.g., meeting transcripts, marginalia, correspondence explicitly linking Swedenborgian sources to 1935–1937 story decisions).

1. **Richard Creedon (The Architect):** His tentative outline of October 1934 and the specific direction regarding the Queen’s self-destruction established the *moral physics* of the studio. He injected the Swedenborgian concept of internal causality into the narrative structure.  
2. **Ted Sears (The Editor):** He codified the "Kiss" as the mechanism of resurrection, ensuring the film adhered to a logic of spiritual conjunction rather than mechanical accident.  
3. **Hal Adelquist (The Guardian):** His place at both ends of the period—head assistant director in 1937, Story Department manager in 1948—positioned him to understand the "hidden theology" of the studio. He carried this tradition from the 1930s into the 1940s, eventually answering a Swedenborgian church librarian on the authenticity of *Johnny Appleseed*.

These men were not isolated mystics. They worked in a studio where **Don Graham** had already established a "physics of the spirit" through Action Analysis, and in a city where the **Church of the New Jerusalem** and its adjacent metaphysical currents (Theosophy/New Thought) were culturally present. The strongest version of the claim supported here is that the *Snow White* narrative exhibits a coherent Swedenborgian-compatible metaphysical architecture—one whose specific convergence of features (internal causality, conjunction-as-awakening, celestial ascent) is easier to explain through unified transmission than through independent accident. The identity and intentionality of that transmission remain targets for further archival work.
