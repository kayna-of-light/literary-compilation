# Source 005 — Madeley, *The Science of Correspondences Elucidated* (1883)

**Citation**: Madeley, Edward. *The Science of Correspondences Elucidated: The Key to the Heavenly and True Meaning of the Sacred Scriptures.* Germantown, PA: Swedenborg Publishing Association, 1883.

**Accessed**: 2026-10-24, archive.org `sciencecorrespon00madeuoft` (full-text search confirmed the sentence).

## What it is

A New Church (Swedenborgian) manual of correspondences by a minister of that church, issued by a Swedenborgian publishing house.

## The sentence that travelled

"Correspondence, then, is no arbitrary relationship, like metaphor or figure, but one founded alike on the inward and outward nature of the things by which we are surrounded."

## Where it went

Stebbins quotes it in her chapter on the legs, where she defines "semeiotics" as "the science of signs, of correspondences". She gives Madeley's etymology in a footnote, naming him ("Science of Correspondences: Rev. Edward Madeley"), and quotes this sentence in the text (Stebbins 1901, 139; store 006).

## Why it matters

- It is the documented point where a Swedenborgian definition of correspondence entered the Delsarte textbook word for word.
- The definition is the same criterion the library uses to separate correspondence from allegory (*Hermeneutics of the Mundus Imaginalis* §2.2: correspondence is not "mechanical substitution" and needs no codebook).
- The Revival filmmakers state it in their own terms: the powers "didn't feel arbitrary, but were dictated by emotions and personality" (store 016).
