# Carroll and Swedenborg: what we know

**Started**: 2026-10-23, at the author's request after the 2026-10-21 audit of *The Animating Spirit: A Cultural History*.

## The question

The library claims, in five documents, that Lewis Carroll was a "systematic Swedenborgian thinker" who owned Swedenborg's works and encoded his doctrine in the *Alice* books and *Sylvie and Bruno*. The 2026-10-21 audit showed the library claim false (Lovett's catalogue lists no work by Swedenborg) and then found that the library record itself is incomplete. The author's instruction: argue what we know, not an argument we no longer know; rewrite everything about Carroll if needed; source it properly; do not rest on earlier knowledge.

So the question has two parts that must be kept apart:

1. **The record.** What do primary sources show about Carroll's contact with Swedenborg, with Swedenborgian thought, and with the ideas the library reads in his books?
2. **The reading.** Which correspondential readings of Carroll's texts stand on the texts themselves and on Swedenborg's own words, whatever the route of transmission?
