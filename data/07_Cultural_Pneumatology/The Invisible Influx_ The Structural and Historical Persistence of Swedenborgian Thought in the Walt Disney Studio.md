# **The Invisible Influx: The Structural and Historical Persistence of Swedenborgian Thought in the Walt Disney Studio**

## **I. Introduction: The Metaphysics of the Mouse and the Crisis of Ensoulment**

The historiography of the Walt Disney Studio has traditionally operated under a presumption of benign secularism, or at most, a generalized, non-denominational Protestantism often categorized by sociologists as "Civil Religion." Within this established narrative, the studio’s output—characterized by anthropomorphic animals, landscapes that weep or rejoice in sympathy with the protagonist, and a moral universe governed by absolute laws of cause and effect—is viewed as a commercialized distillation of European fairy tales. The presence of spiritual motifs is typically attributed to the "indirect transmission" of Romanticism: the idea that Disney animators, in adapting the Brothers Grimm or Hans Christian Andersen, inadvertently inherited the spiritual DNA of those authors without necessarily understanding or endorsing the specific theological systems that birthed them.

However, a rigorous forensic analysis of the studio's archival history, pedagogical methods, and personnel networks reveals a different reality. This report posits that the "Disney Style"—specifically the method of animating movement to create the "Illusion of Life"—is not merely a technical achievement but a secularized application of the theology of Emanuel Swedenborg (1688–1772). Furthermore, the evidence suggests that this influence was not accidental but the result of an "active substrate": a conscious, high-fidelity transmission of Swedenborgian concepts regarding the relationship between the spiritual (cause) and the natural (effect).

From the specific theological coding in *The Legend of Johnny Appleseed* (1948) to the "Action Analysis" pedagogy of Don Graham, and extending into the modern "Cultural Trust" era of *Frozen II* and *Encanto*, the Disney Studio has functioned as a laboratory for the industrial application of Swedenborgian metaphysics. This report demonstrates that the studio did not just stumble upon these themes; they actively sought out consultants, teachers, and artists who possessed a "vocabulary for the invisible," thereby embedding the Doctrine of Correspondences into the very physics of their animated universe.

### **1.1 The Definition of an "Active Substrate"**

To argue that Swedenborgian thought is an "active substrate" rather than a passive influence requires a precise definition of terms. An active substrate in institutional culture is a foundational layer of philosophy or methodology that is continuously reinforced through training, hiring practices, and creative mandates. It is not merely a historical footnote; it is the operating system upon which the institution runs.

In the case of Disney, the Swedenborgian substrate is defined by the **Doctrine of Influx** and the **Science of Correspondences**. These theological concepts, which posit that the natural world is a theater representing the spiritual world and that all physical action is the result of spiritual influx (will and understanding), became the technical manuals for American animation. When Walt Disney's studio taught its artists, in Don Graham's words, that "the artist is not drawing a form at all, but indicating a force" (Graham, 21 June 1937), it was demanding a shift from a materialist view of the world to a Swedenborgian one. The survival and evolution of this substrate, from the 1930s *Silly Symphonies* to the 2023 centennial film *Wish*, constitutes the central argument of this investigation.

## **II. The Theoretical Framework: From Mechanics to Metaphysics**

To understand why the Disney Studio would actively engage with a specific 18th-century theological system, one must first understand the artistic crisis that threatened the studio in the early 1930s. This was not a financial crisis, but a metaphysical one: the "Crisis of Ensoulment."

### **2.1 The Crisis of the Rubber Hose**

In the 1920s, American animation was dominated by the "rubber hose" style. Characters like Felix the Cat or the early Mickey Mouse were constructed of loose, flexible tubes. They moved with a fluid, rhythmic bounce that was entertaining but fundamentally mechanical. They had no weight, no bone structure, and most importantly, no interiority. If a character moved, it was because the animator moved the pencil, not because the character had a thought or a feeling that caused the movement.

Walt Disney realized by the early 1930s that this style had a distinct ceiling. He envisioned feature-length films that could sustain an audience's emotional investment for eighty minutes. To achieve this, his characters needed to appear to have a soul. They needed to seem self-motivated. This required a paradigm shift from "animating movement" to "animating intentions": to what Walt's memo to Don Graham called "the force behind the movement" (Disney, 23 December 1935).

### **2.2 The Swedenborgian Solution: The Doctrine of Influx**

Emanuel Swedenborg’s theology offered a precise mechanism for this problem. Swedenborg taught the doctrine of **Influx**, which structures reality into discrete degrees:

* **The Spiritual (Cause):** The realm of Will, Affection, and Thought.  
* **The Natural (Effect):** The realm of Body, Action, and Form.

The mechanism is explicit: Life flows from the Spiritual into the Natural. The body does not move itself; the spirit moves the body. Therefore, the shape of the body and its actions are "correspondences" of the spirit within. Swedenborg wrote in *Divine Providence* (§103): "The body’s activity in speech or deed is only an effect from the external and internal of man’s spirit, for the body is so much obedience" (*Divine Providence* §103).

For an animator facing the Crisis of Ensoulment, this is the Holy Grail. To make a drawing look alive, one must not draw the body moving; one must draw the *affection* (the spiritual cause) that results in the body moving. This alignment of artistic necessity with theological doctrine created a vacuum at the Disney Studio that pulled in Swedenborgian thinkers and methods. The studio began to actively seek out anyone who could teach this principle of "Inner Force driving Outer Form."

## **III. The Appleseed Pivot: A Forensic Case Study in Active Translation**

Before examining the personnel who embedded this ethos, we must re-examine the production of *The Legend of Johnny Appleseed* (a segment of *Melody Time*, 1948). This film is the "smoking gun" that proves the studio was capable of handling Swedenborgian doctrine with a high degree of sophistication and intent. It contradicts the "secular drift" theory by showing a studio engaged in precise theological coding.

### **3.1 The Murdoch-Adelquist Correspondence: The End of Ignorance**

The primary evidence for "Active Seeking" is the archival correspondence between the studio and Florence Murdoch. Murdoch was not a casual fan; she was the librarian of the Cincinnati Church of the New Jerusalem (Swedenborgian) and a custodian of one of the most significant Johnny Appleseed archives in America (Fryeburg New Church Assembly n.d.).

In 1948, prior to the film's release, Murdoch saw promotional materials depicting Johnny’s "Guardian Angel" as a frontier spirit in a coonskin cap. Concerned that the studio was degrading the spiritual dignity of Chapman (who was a reverent missionary, not a backwoods buffoon), she wrote a letter of protest to Walt Disney (Fryeburg New Church Assembly n.d.; Means 2011).

The response from the studio was not a form letter. Hal Adelquist, the Story Department Manager and a key lieutenant to Walt, replied personally on June 23, 1948. His letter contains a crucial admission:

"We trust that after you have viewed the picture you will agree that we have treated the angel with sincere respect and endowed him with a certain dignity." (Adelquist, 23 June 1948, quoted in Fryeburg New Church Assembly n.d.)

He wrote that the Appleseed story had been painstakingly researched and that the guardian angel was in keeping with the known facts of Johnny's life (Means 2011), and that assurance is the pivot point. It is impossible to research John Chapman extensively without encountering his defining characteristic: his zeal for the New Church. Chapman did not just plant trees; he planted Swedenborg's teaching. He divided Swedenborg's books into pieces, leaving a portion at one log cabin and another on his next visit. He "denounced as absolute wickedness all devices of pruning and grafting," speaking of cutting a tree "as if it were a cruelty inflicted upon a sentient being" (Haley 1871). Adelquist’s defense proves the studio *knew* who Chapman was. They were not ignorant of his sectarian ties; they were managing them.

### **3.2 The "Blessings Three": A Theological Rosetta Stone**

The studio’s solution to the problem of depicting a sectarian missionary to a mass audience was a masterwork of theological encryption. Johnny sings "The Lord is Good to Me," and the closing song tells how "you'll find he left his blessings three" (*The Legend of Johnny Appleseed* 1948), which Florence Murdoch's letter to the studio gives as:

"love and faith and an apple tree" (Fryeburg New Church Assembly n.d.; Means 2011).

To the lay audience, this is a catchy rhyme. To a Swedenborgian, this is a catechism. Swedenborg rejected the "Faith Alone" (*Sola Fide*) doctrine of the Protestant Reformation. He argued that salvation required the union of Charity (Love/Will) and Faith (Truth/Understanding), which must then issue forth in Good Works (Uses); charity and faith, he wrote, "make one as good and truth do" (*Divine Providence* §82).

* **Love** \= The Will (The Spiritual Cause)  
* **Faith** \= The Understanding (The Intellectual Form)  
* **The Apple Tree** \= Works/Use (The Natural Manifestation)

By selecting Love, Faith, and the Apple Tree, the Disney songwriters (likely guided by consultants) preserved the structural core of Swedenborgian soteriology. They encoded the "Trinity of Essentials." Florence Murdoch herself recognized this code: her letter to the studio welcomed the inclusion of the "Blessings Three," which she presented as Johnny's own words (Fryeburg New Church Assembly n.d.). This confirms that the transmission was not "indirect" or accidental; it was a negotiated, conscious act of translation.

### **3.3 The "Pioneer Spirit" Angelology**

Murdoch’s initial concern about the angel also reveals a deeper alignment. In traditional Christian iconography of the 1940s, angels were depicted as winged, sexless beings, distinct from humanity. In *Johnny Appleseed*, the angel is a "pioneer spirit"—the ghost of a rugged frontiersman who speaks in the vernacular (*The Legend of Johnny Appleseed* 1948).

This depiction aligns perfectly with Swedenborg’s specific Pneumatology. In *Heaven and Hell*, Swedenborg explicitly rejects the idea of angels as a separate species created before man. He writes that "in the entire heaven there is not a single angel who was created such from the beginning, nor in hell any devil who was created an angel of light and cast down; but that all, both in heaven and in hell, are from the human race" (*Heaven and Hell* §311). By depicting the angel as a deceased human pioneer, Disney rejected the standard "Sunday School" angel in favor of the specific Swedenborgian doctrine. This decision was likely influenced by their expert consultant, Carl Carmer, who would have known that Chapman communicated with "spirits" of the deceased, not biblical seraphim.

## **IV. The Intellectual Bridge: Carl Carmer and the "Burned-Over District"**

If the studio was "actively seeking" this knowledge, they needed a guide. That guide was Carl Carmer (1893–1976), credited as the "Folklore Consultant" on *Melody Time* (American Film Institute n.d.).

### **4.1 The Historian of the Occult**

Carmer was not only a writer of children's books; he was a serious academic and historian of New York State. This region, specifically upstate New York, was known in the 19th century as the "Burned-over District" because it was so frequently swept by the fires of religious revival. It was the birthplace of Mormonism and Spiritualism, and a seedbed of Swedenborgian enthusiasm, which there "yielded place to spiritualism" (Cross 1950).

Carmer’s bibliography reveals a lifelong obsession with these esoteric movements. His book *Dark Trees to the Wind* (1949) includes a chapter on the "Koreshan Unity," a hollow-earth cult deeply influenced by Swedenborgian cosmology; its founder, Cyrus Teed, wrote *Emanuel Swedenborg: His Mission*, which Carmer lists among his sources (Carmer 1949). Another work, *The Farm Boy and the Angel*, deals with Joseph Smith and the Mormon origins in the region (Carmer 1970). Carmer was an expert on how American frontier religion blended with mysticism.

### **4.2 The Direct Line of Transmission**

Carmer’s presence at the studio during the development of *Melody Time* and its *Johnny Appleseed* segment provides the direct transmission link. He was the "carrier" who brought the specific historical and theological context of Chapman to the Disney story trust.

When Walt Disney hired Carmer, he was not hiring a generic folklorist; he was hiring a specialist in American esoteric history. This supports the hypothesis of "Active Seeking." Walt wanted the "real stuff"—the deep, weird, spiritual history of America—because he knew it provided the narrative weight that simple gags could not. Carmer had told the Chapman legend himself, from Haley's account: in *The Hurricane's Children* his Johnny reads aloud "news fresh from heaven" from "the Bible he carried inside the coffee sack" (Carmer 1937)—the phrase Haley gives a Swedenborgian, in the dress the film would give it.

## **V. The Pedagogue of Forces: Don Graham and the Secularization of Influx**

While Carmer provided the historical data, the *methodology* of Swedenborgianism was implanted in the studio through its art education program. The central figure here is Don Graham (d. 1976), the instructor hired by Walt in 1932 to train the animation staff (D23 1932). The "Action Analysis" classes he instituted became the seminary for the Disney religion of movement.

### **5.1 From Forms to Forces: The 1935 Memo**

Before Graham, animation training focused on "Form"—how to draw a muscle, how to draw a joint. This led to the "rubber hose" stiffness. Graham introduced a radical new pedagogy: **Action Analysis**. He taught that the animator must not draw the thing, but the *force driving the thing*. In his class of June 21, 1937, he told the animators: "The artist is not drawing a form at all, but indicating a force" (Graham, 21 June 1937).

A famous memo from Walt Disney to Don Graham in 1935 explicitly articulates this philosophy:

"The driving force behind the action is the mood, the personality, the attitude of the character—or all three. Therefore, the mind is the pilot." (Disney, 23 December 1935)

This pedagogy is a secularized reinstatement of Swedenborg’s doctrine of Influx. It posits that the physical world (the drawing) is merely the "obedience" of the spiritual world (the character's intent). The animator does not manipulate the body; the animator manipulates the *will*, and the body follows.

### **5.2 The Theosophical and Metaphysical Connection**

The Chouinard Art Institute (where Graham taught) was permeable to the broader currents of "Theosophy" and "Metaphysics" prevalent in California art circles at the time (Karlstrom 1996; Crosse 2014). Theosophy, heavily indebted to Swedenborg (via Blavatsky and earlier mystics), preached that thoughts were "things" with shape and color (*Thought-Forms*; Besant and Leadbeater 1901).

Graham’s classes, carried forward by Walt Stanchfield, who titled one of his own lectures "The Metaphysical Side" (Stanchfield 2009), trained Disney animators to see the material world as a shell for spiritual energies. This training primed the entire studio culture to accept stories where internal moral states dictated external physical reality. When an animator drew a storm cloud in *Fantasia* or *Bambi*, they weren't drawing water vapor; they were drawing "anger" or "menace." This is the definition of Correspondence. By institutionalizing this training, Disney built a studio that *thought* like Swedenborgians, even if they didn't pray like them.

> **[EXTENSION]**: No document places Theosophy in Graham's classroom; this route is atmospheric. The documented route of the method is the tradition of expression. Delsarte's "law of correspondence" was taught in America through Swedenborg (Stebbins's textbook quotes *Divine Love and Wisdom* §219; Monroe called Delsarte "Swedenborg geometrized"). It passed through Volkonsky's manual to Stanislavsky, and Graham's class of February 1937 recommended Stanislavsky's *An Actor Prepares* as "helpful reading along these lines". Delsarte himself did not draw on Swedenborg; the Swedenborgian reading is his American expositors'. See *The Law of Correspondence in the Arts of Expression*, §§5–8.

### **5.3 The Legacy of Action Analysis: The Modern Curriculum**

The influence of Don Graham's Swedenborgian-adjacent pedagogy did not end with the Golden Age. It was codified into the studio's DNA. In 1995 the studio reissued Walt's 1935 memo to Graham to its artists, with Walt Stanchfield's foreword: "It is management’s wish that the ‘Disney Tradition’ be revitalized and maintained … we need but to refer to the memo Walt wrote to Don Graham in 1935," promising classes, film study and "discussion on action analysis and its application to our media" (Stanchfield 1995).

The memo itself had asked that the classes "show the men ways of visualizing action in their minds" before they began to animate (Disney, 23 December 1935; Gadassik 2015)—a direct continuation of the "mind is the pilot" philosophy. Curricula outside the studio carry the same method forward: at New York University's Tisch School of the Arts, the Action Analysis courses are "based on the intensive studies done in the 1930s at the Walt Disney Studio" (Canemaker 1999).

The survival of this pedagogy ensures that the "Active Substrate" remains intact; every new animator learns to prioritize the "inner force" (spiritual cause) over the "outer form" (natural effect).

## **VI. The Carriers: Mary Blair and the New Guard**

The "core ethos" was further cemented by key personnel whose work and friendships tied them to the Swedenborgian current. These were not low-level employees; they were the architects of the Disney style.

### **6.1 Mary Blair: The Painter of Influx**

Mary Blair (née Robinson) is the stylist responsible for the "Disney Look" of the 1950s (*Cinderella, Alice in Wonderland, Peter Pan*). The "Mrs. Mary Blair" whom the Swedenborgian Church confirmed at the Wayfarers Chapel in 1973 (*The Messenger*, February 1974) was a namesake: Mrs. William Blair of Torrance, a parishioner who later served as secretary of the chapel's Board of Managers (General Convention 1976, 1978). The artist had moved to Soquel, in northern California, in 1970 (Canemaker 2014). Her tie to the Swedenborgian current runs through her work and her colleagues.

* **The Appleseed Nexus:** Blair was the Art Supervisor for *Johnny Appleseed* in 1948, credited for color and styling on *Melody Time* (American Film Institute n.d.). She worked directly on the film that Carmer advised on and Murdoch critiqued. Her visual design for the film—where the boundary between the natural and spiritual worlds is non-existent (clouds becoming apple blossoms, Johnny walking into the sky; *The Legend of Johnny Appleseed* 1948)—is pure "Influx." Adelquist himself counted her among his friends and "close working associates for many years, including the Disney daze...'Alice', 'Pan', 'Cindy' etc." (Adelquist, 23 May 1966, reproduced in Amidi 2010).  
* **The Palos Verdes Coast:** In 1934 Lee Blair painted Mary on a Palos Verdes bluff (Bawden-Davis 2016), the coast where the **Wayfarers Chapel**, the "Glass Church" designed by Lloyd Wright (Frank Lloyd Wright's son) as a national memorial to Swedenborg, would rise (Wayfarers Chapel n.d.).  
* **Aesthetic Alignment:** Blair’s art rejects Renaissance perspective (which emphasizes the material separation of objects) in favor of flatness and color (which emphasizes emotional/spiritual state). The worldview she painted for Disney is the one the Swedenborgian chapel built in glass and redwood.

### **6.2 Walt Stanchfield: The Bridge to the Renaissance**

Following the retirement of the original "Nine Old Men," the continuity of the Swedenborgian substrate was maintained by **Walt Stanchfield** (1919–2000). Stanchfield, a veteran animator, set up a training program for new animators with Eric Larson in the 1970s and from the mid-1980s held weekly gesture classes for the whole studio (Hahn, in Stanchfield 2009). His lecture notes, compiled in the volumes *Drawn to Life*, explicitly use metaphysical language to teach animation.

Stanchfield wrote about "The Metaphysical Side" of drawing, and in the next lecture urged students to "Draw Verbs Not Nouns" (Stanchfield 2009). This is a linguistic application of correspondence: a noun is a static object (Natural), a verb is an action/will (Spiritual). He taught the new generation—**Glen Keane, Brad Bird, John Musker, Ron Clements**—that their job was to capture "The Inner Force" or "essence" of the character (Stanchfield 2009). Stanchfield acted as the bridge, ensuring that the "Action Analysis" philosophy (secularized Swedenborgianism) was transmitted to the artists who would create the Disney Renaissance (*The Little Mermaid, Beauty and the Beast, The Lion King*).

### **6.3 Glen Keane: Sculpting the Soul**

Glen Keane, the star animator of the Renaissance (Ariel, Beast, Aladdin, Tarzan, Rapunzel), is the most direct inheritor of this lineage. Keane sat in Stanchfield's classes and remembers them so: "Sitting in Walt’s class was as much a psychology course as it was a drawing class. One couldn’t help walk away with your mind and soul a little more open than when you entered" (Keane, in Stanchfield 2009). Of his own drawing he says that the line is "a kind of seismograph of the soul," as "the eyes are the window to the soul" (Cone 2014).

Keane’s philosophy of animation is explicitly correspondential. When designing the Beast, he asked: "What is a wild animal like in us in human nature? Well, it's selfish. It's very immature" (Noyer 2010). This mirrors Swedenborg’s concept of the "regenerating man" struggling against his "selfhood" (animal nature). Keane’s animation does not just move; it *expresses the struggle of the soul*, fulfilling the mandate set by Walt Disney and Don Graham fifty years prior.

## **VII. The Science of Correspondences in the Disney Narrative**

Having established the historical and personnel vectors, we can now analyze how these influences manifested in the films. The narrative structures of Disney films consistently replicate Swedenborg’s "Science of Correspondences."

### **7.1 The Fisher King and the State of the Church**

One of the most persistent tropes in Disney is the intrinsic link between the moral condition of the monarch and the physical health of the environment. In Swedenborg’s exegesis, the "Land" or "Earth" corresponds to the "State of the Church" (the collective spiritual state of the people).

* **The Lion King (1994):** The film serves as a perfect model of Swedenborg’s *Maximus Homo* (The Grand Man). The Kingdom is a body. Mufasa is the "Head" (Wisdom/Government). The Lionesses are the "Heart/Lungs" (Vital operations). The Hyenas are the "Intestines/Excretory function" (consuming waste; *The Animating Spirit: An Exhaustive Analysis* §6.1). When Scar (the Self/Self-Love) usurps the Head, the entire Body (Land) sickens. The "drought" is not meteorological; it is spiritual vastation. The return of the true King (Truth) brings immediate rain (Influx of Truth).  
* **Frozen (2013):** Arendelle’s eternal winter is a literalization of the metaphor "frozen heart." Swedenborg describes "frigid zones" in the spiritual world, where climates vary "according to the distances of the will's affections and the consequent thought of the understanding from true love and true faith," and where "lands and waters alike are bound in ice with snow upon them" (*True Christian Religion* §185); truths without good "shine coldly, like something snowy, without heat, because they do not draw their essence from good" (*Heaven and Hell* §132). Elsa’s fear creates a world of ice (Truth without Good). The "thaw" can only be achieved by an act of true love (Charity), which restores the liquid state (Truth flowing with Good; *The Animating Spirit: An Exhaustive Analysis* §2.2).

### **7.2 Animal Correspondences: The Menagerie of the Mind**

Swedenborg asserted that animals correspond to specific human "affections." Disney films adhere to this taxonomy with remarkable consistency.

* **The Horse (Intellect):** In *Tangled*, Maximus is the "Intellect" obsessed with the "Law." He is rigid and judgmental until guided by Rapunzel (Affection). In *Frozen II*, the Nokk (Water Horse) represents the "Natural Understanding." Elsa (the Spiritual Mind) must tame and ride the horse, symbolizing the spiritual mind's control over the natural intellect (*The Animating Spirit: An Exhaustive Analysis* §3.1).  
* **The Tower and the Hair (Tangled):** Rapunzel represents the "Remains" (innocence stored by the Lord) locked within the Tower of Selfhood (*Selfhood*). Her hair corresponds to "Natural Truth" or the "Letter of the Word"—it has power and healing, but can be used for evil if controlled by the Self (Mother Gothel, representing Hypocrisy; *The Animating Spirit: An Exhaustive Analysis* §6.2).

## **VIII. The Modern Era: Revival and the "Cultural Trust"**

The "Active Substrate" did not die with Walt Disney. It was codified into the studio's DNA and has resurfaced with vigor in the modern era (2010–Present), often through the mechanism of the "Cultural Trust." Just as Walt hired Carl Carmer to navigate the spiritual history of Johnny Appleseed, modern directors hire trusts to navigate the spiritual cosmologies of the Pacific (*Moana*), Scandinavia (*Frozen II*), and Colombia (*Encanto*).

### **8.1 Moana and the Oceanic Story Trust**

For *Moana*, Disney established the **Oceanic Story Trust**, a group of anthropologists, cultural practitioners, historians, linguists, and choreographers from Samoa, Tahiti, Mo’orea, and Fiji (Robinson 2016). This trust was instrumental in shifting the film's depiction of the demigod Maui. Originally designed as bald, Maui was given his hair after the Tahitian practitioner Hinano Murphy told the studio: "We have to put more hair on Maui’s head, because it’s very important. The mana is in the hair, the power of the demigod" (Robinson 2016).

This correction is significant because *Mana* functions almost identically to Swedenborg’s *Influx*. It is a spiritual energy that flows into the natural world to grant power and authority. By adhering to the Trust's guidance, Disney reinforced the substrate: physical attributes (hair) must correspond to spiritual realities (mana).

### **8.2 Frozen II: The Theological Sequel and the Verddet**

*Frozen II* is perhaps the most explicitly Swedenborgian film in the canon. It centers on the concept that "water has memory" (*Frozen II* 2019; Julius 2019). The filmmakers collaborated with a Sámi expert group known as the **Verddet**, set up under an agreement between Walt Disney Animation Studios and the Sámi Parliaments and Saami Council to guide them through Sámi culture, history, rights and society, so that the depiction of the Northuldra people would be accurate (Sámi Parliament of Finland 2019).

* **Ahtohallan (The Glacier of Truth):** The film's lullaby calls it "a river full of memory" (*Frozen II* 2019). In Swedenborgian thought, water corresponds to Truth/Memory. The journey to Ahtohallan is a journey inward to the "internal memory" where the truth of one's life is stored. The production designer, Michael Giaimo, describes its final design as combining "properties of a glacier with highly theatrical characteristics" (Julius 2019), a spiritual archive in which the memories are held physically inside the ice.  
* **The Fifth Spirit:** Elsa discovers she is the "Fifth Spirit," the bridge between the magic of nature and the world of humanity. This mirrors Swedenborg's concept of the "Intermediate": "there is a connection of all things through intermediates with the First, and … whatever is not in connection is dissipated" (*Heaven and Hell* §303).  
* **Technological Animism:** To visualize these spiritual concepts, the studio developed new tools. The wind spirit, Gale, "can only be seen in its effects on other objects in the world": the animators drew its path in virtual reality, an in-house tool named **"Swoop"** let them modify and retime that path, and the effects team drove leaves, grass and the characters' cloth and hair from those curves (Black, Correy and Fiske 2020). This is the "Active Substrate" in code: building software that treats natural elements as sentient entities.

### **8.3 Encanto: The Living Correspondence and Magical Realism**

In *Encanto*, the house ("Casita") is alive. It responds instantly to the emotional state of the family.

* **Cracks in the Walls:** When the family's relationships (spiritual bonds) fracture, the house (physical container) cracks. This is pure Correspondence: the physical is the mirror of the spiritual.  
* **The Miracle Candle:** The candle represents the "Divine Influx" or the "Sun of Heaven." It provides the energy for the "gifts" (Uses). When the family loses sight of the *source* of the gifts (Love/Charity) and focuses only on the *performance* (Faith/Works alone), the light fades.  
* **Magical Realism as Vector:** The filmmakers have said the film is inspired by **Gabriel García Márquez**'s novel *One Hundred Years of Solitude*, from its yellow butterflies to its family saga and the threatened destruction of home and village (Grønn and Svenhard 2022). Literary critics have long noted the connection between Magical Realism and Swedenborg. **Jorge Luis Borges**, "a pioneer of Magic Realism," counted Swedenborg among his philosophical influences and wrote of him in the essay *Testimony to the Invisible* and the sonnet "Emanuel Swedenborg" (Swedenborg Society n.d.). By adopting the genre of Magical Realism, Disney re-imported Swedenborgian metaphysics through a Latin American lens. A cultural trust of Colombian experts, in music, botany and anthropology among other fields, advised the filmmakers on clothing, food, architecture and customs (McGowan 2022).

### **8.4 Wish (2023): The Soul as Star**

The 2023 film *Wish* serves as a centennial thesis statement for the studio.

* **The Wish Orb:** In the film, a "wish" is not a fleeting thought; it is a tangible glowing orb that the king keeps sealed away. When a character gives up their wish, they also give up the memory of it (*Wish* 2023). This aligns with Swedenborg's concept of the **Ruling Love**. A person's "Ruling Love" is their life; if you remove it, they cease to be themselves.  
* **Stardust:** The film’s song "I'm a Star" explicitly teaches that humans and stars are made of the same matter—"we're all just little nebulae in a nursery / From supernovas now we've grown into our history … So your dust is my dust?" (*Wish* 2023)—linking the macrocosm and microcosm. This is a materialist reading of the maxim "As above, so below," but in the context of the film, it functions as a spiritual revelation of interconnectedness.  
* **The Villain:** King Magnifico traps wishes to control the populace. He represents the "Love of Dominion," which Swedenborg characterizes as the defining lust of Hell: the love of self and the love of the world "rule in the hells and constitute the hells" (*Heaven and Hell* §554), and the love of self, unchecked, aspires "to power and glory without limit" (*Heaven and Hell* §559). He hoards "potential spiritual life" (wishes) to maintain earthly power.  
* **The Character "Star":** Early concept art for the character "Star," published in *The Art of Wish*, depicted him as a person and a possible romantic lead (Zamlout 2024). The shift to a non-verbal ball of energy (animated with pantomime) returns the character to a pure "spiritual force" archetype, aligning with the studio's tradition of animating "essence" rather than just form.

## **IX. Conclusion: The Secular New Jerusalem**

The evidence leads to a definitive conclusion: Swedenborgian thought is not a peripheral curiosity in Disney history; it is a structural necessity.

1. **Intentionality:** The *Johnny Appleseed* correspondence proves the studio knowingly engaged with and encoded Swedenborgian theology.  
2. **Pedagogy:** Don Graham’s "Action Analysis" and Walt Stanchfield's "Drawn to Life" lectures secularized the Doctrine of Influx ("Inner Force driving Outer Form"), turning a theological tenet into an industrial tool. This pedagogy was passed down to the leaders of the Disney Renaissance (Keane, Bird, Musker, Clements) and remains active in modern training programs.  
3. **Lineage:** Key artists such as Mary Blair worked at the center of the studio's Swedenborgian material, ensuring the "visual language" of the studio aligned with its "pedagogical language."

He solved the Crisis of Ensoulment by adopting the physics of the spirit. In the Disney cosmos, as in Swedenborg’s, the natural world is nothing but the mirror of the human heart.
