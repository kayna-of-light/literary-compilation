# Source 004 — Prospective hidden-target tests of perception during cardiac arrest (AWARE I and II)

**Citations**:
- Parnia, S., et al. "AWARE—AWAreness during REsuscitation—a prospective study." *Resuscitation* 85, no. 12 (2014): 1799–1805. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2014.09.004. PMID 25301715.
- Parnia, S., et al. "AWAreness during REsuscitation – II: A multi-center study of consciousness and awareness in cardiac arrest." *Resuscitation* 191 (2023): 109903. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.resuscitation.2023.109903. PMID 37423492.

**Accessed**: 2026-11-03. PubMed abstracts read myself.

## What it says

**AWARE (2014).** Among 2,060 cardiac-arrest events, 140 survivors completed stage-1 interviews and 101 completed stage 2.
- "46% had memories with 7 major cognitive themes: fear; animals/plants; bright light; violence/persecution; deja-vu; family; recalling events post-CA."
- "9% had NDEs, while 2% described awareness with explicit recall of 'seeing' and 'hearing' actual events related to their resuscitation."
- "One had a verifiable period of conscious awareness during which time cerebral function was not expected."

**AWARE II (2023).** 567 in-hospital arrests at 25 sites. 53 survived (9.3%) and 28 completed interviews.
- 11 of the 28 (39.3%) reported memories or perceptions suggestive of consciousness. Six (21.4%) had a "transcendent recalled experience of death."
- "Nobody identified the visual image, 1/28 (3.5%) identified the auditory stimulus."
- "Normal EEG activity (delta, theta and alpha) consistent with consciousness emerged as long as 35–60 minutes into CPR."

## Reported by the research agent, not read myself (secondary)

- In AWARE I, shelves with hidden images were installed, but "as many as 78%" of arrests happened in rooms without shelves. Neither of the two detailed visual-awareness cases occurred in a room with a shelf (SPR Psi Encyclopedia, "AWARE NDE Studies").
- Greyson, Holden & Mounsey, *JNDS* 25(2) (2006): in 52 induced arrests during defibrillator testing, no patient reported an NDE or an OBE, so the hidden targets were never put to the test.
- Holden (2009), the *Handbook* chapter on veridical perception, reviewed 107 anecdotal cases from 39 publications. The commonly quoted figure is 92% "completely accurate." The chapter reportedly counts five hidden-target studies, none with a hit. Not read; see the open items below.

## Why it matters

The one test designed to rule out overhearing, guessing and later contamination is a hidden target seen only from above. So far it has not produced a hit, and in most studies no positive case occurred within view of a target. Point 5 of this project ("experience pointing to checkable things") therefore cannot rest on veridical NDE perception as proof. What these studies do show is:

- awareness and recalled experiences during cardiac arrest occur in a measurable share of survivors;
- in AWARE I, one case's account of real events was verified;
- in AWARE II, EEG consistent with consciousness appeared long into CPR.

The retrospective veridical cases remain anecdotes with published alternative explanations.
