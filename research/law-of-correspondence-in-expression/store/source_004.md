# Source 004 — Waille 2015: "François Delsarte, an atypical Catholic"

**Citation**: Waille, Franck. "François Delsarte, un catholique atypique du XIXe siècle." *Chrétiens et sociétés* 22 (2015): 185–219. https://doi.org/10.4000/chretienssocietes.3903.

**Accessed**: 2026-10-24 via WebFetch. OpenEdition blocks curl and headless Chromium with its Anubis check; the page text was read paragraph by paragraph.

## Key findings

**¶62**: « Delsarte n'était pas plus un disciple éloigné du mystique suédois Emmanuel Swedenborg qu'un tenant de l'occultisme » (Delsarte was no more a distant disciple of the Swedish mystic Emmanuel Swedenborg than an adherent of occultism). Note 142 cites Stebbins (pp. 109–110, the Trinity chapter with the Swedenborg quotations; store 006) and Ruyter as the places where the filiation was asserted.

**¶36**: his training in Christian and Hebrew Kabbalah came through "illuminist freemasonry" (n. 71: Ligou, *Dictionnaire de la franc-maçonnerie*, 1998, art. "Illuminisme"). His principal theological referent is Thomas Aquinas.

## Why it matters

The manuscript scholar rejects a Swedenborgian filiation for Delsarte himself, and he names the American pages that asserted it. The thesis therefore does **not** claim that Delsarte drew on Swedenborg. The claim it makes is the one the record supports: Delsarte's American expositors read his law through Swedenborg and taught it that way (stores 005–007). Northrop's "Tradition affirms that he … studied Swedenborg" (store 007) is that American tradition. Waille answers it.
